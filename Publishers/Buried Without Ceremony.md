# Buried Without Ceremony

**Founded**: 2005
**Active Era**: OSR Revival through Modern Era (2005-Present)
**Historical Significance**: ⭐ 4/5
**Headquarters**: Brooklyn, New York
**Status**: Active

## Company History

Buried Without Ceremony emerged as influential voice in experimental indie roleplaying game publishing, demonstrating how radical mechanical innovation combined with focused thematic exploration could create unforgettable gaming experiences. Founded and led by Epidiah Ravachol, the publisher built reputation for games integrating physical components, experimental mechanics, and thoughtful design supporting specific emotional experiences. The company's journey from publishing Dread—revolutionary Jenga-based horror game—through continued experimentation with innovative mechanics represents sustained commitment to pushing RPG boundaries and exploring what games could become through unconventional approaches.

Epidiah Ravachol brought distinctive perspective to game design, combining theatrical background, deep engagement with indie gaming theory, and willingness to question every RPG assumption. Ravachol participated actively in The Forge's theoretical discussions, absorbing narrativist philosophy while developing personal design sensibilities emphasizing physical engagement, emotional resonance, and mechanical innovation serving thematic goals.

The company name "Buried Without Ceremony" evoked both horror aesthetics and indie publishing's scrappy ethos—operating outside mainstream recognition, creating works of passion rather than commercial calculation. From inception, the name signaled commitment to experimental design unconcerned with mainstream acceptance.

Buried Without Ceremony's founding centered on publishing Dread (2005), Ravachol's breakthrough design. This horror game replaced dice with Jenga tower, creating unique physical tension supporting horror atmosphere. The game's radical departure from traditional RPG mechanics—no stats, no dice, just tower and narrative—demonstrated how unconventional components could create specific emotional experiences impossible with traditional systems.

Dread's mechanical framework proved elegantly simple. Character creation used questionnaires developing backstory while establishing personalities and relationships. During play, when characters attempted risky actions, players pulled Jenga blocks from tower. Success meant block removed without collapse. Tower collapse meant character's doom—death, transformation, or removal from story. This simple mechanic created extraordinary tension.

The Jenga tower's physical presence transformed play experience. Unlike dice providing momentary tension, tower's instability created sustained anxiety increasing throughout session. Players literally felt tension in hands as blocks loosened and tower wobbled. This physical manifestation of danger created horror atmosphere traditional verbal description couldn't match.

The game's questionnaire-based character creation departed from traditional stat assignment. Questions like "What are you afraid to tell your companions?" or "Who depends on you?" created nuanced characters while establishing relationships and potential complications. This approach demonstrated character creation needn't involve numerical optimization to create compelling personas.

Dread achieved remarkable success and influence. The game won multiple awards, became indie gaming staple, and introduced thousands to experimental mechanics. More significantly, Dread demonstrated that replacing traditional RPG components with unconventional alternatives could enhance rather than diminish play experiences. The game influenced broader acceptance of physical components and experimental mechanics across indie gaming.

The game's horror focus proved perfectly suited to mechanical innovation. Dread captured horror's escalating tension through tower's increasing instability. Players experienced characters' fear physically through their own nervousness pulling blocks. This alignment between mechanics and theme demonstrated Ravachol's design sophistication—mechanics didn't just simulate horror situations but created actual emotional experiences.

Following Dread's success, Buried Without Ceremony continued exploring experimental mechanics through smaller releases. Rather than repeating Dread's formula, Ravachol pursued varied mechanical experiments exploring different aspects of roleplaying and storytelling.

Wolfspell (2011) explored transformation and identity through game about humans becoming werewolves. The game's mechanics emphasized transformation's psychological aspects—changing perspectives, shifting loyalties, gradual loss of humanity. This thematic focus on transformation as identity crisis rather than just power fantasy distinguished Wolfspell from typical werewolf games.

Swords Without Master (2013) presented sword-and-sorcery gaming through highly structured scene-based framework. The game alternated between bombastic action (Glorious) and atmospheric mood (Perilous) phases, creating distinctive pacing and tonal variety. This formal structure demonstrated how constraining creative freedom through framework could paradoxically enhance rather than limit creativity.

The game's phase structure proved influential. By explicitly alternating between action and atmosphere, Swords Without Master created rhythm supporting sword-and-sorcery fiction's characteristic flow between explosive combat and brooding dread. This conscious pacing influenced later games exploring structured scene types and tonal management.

The Mustang (2018) explored Western frontier themes through game about wild horse and various humans attempting to tame, exploit, or free them. The game's structure rotated protagonist role among players, with spotlight shifting between Mustang and humans pursuing their own agendas. This rotating perspective created complex narrative examining relationship between freedom and civilization from multiple viewpoints.

The game's thematic sophistication addressed colonization, freedom, exploitation, and competing worldviews without heavy-handed messaging. By presenting multiple perspectives sympathetically while examining their conflicts, The Mustang created space for meaningful exploration of Western mythology's complications and contradictions.

Vast & Starlit (2020) returned to horror territory through game about desperate space crew facing cosmic threats. The game combined Dread's Jenga mechanics with expanded frameworks supporting science fiction horror. This revisiting of successful mechanics demonstrated how core innovations could evolve and expand rather than remaining static.

Throughout catalog, Buried Without Ceremony maintained commitment to small-press ethos. Games appeared as modest print runs, itch.io releases, and convention sales rather than major retail distribution. This approach prioritized creative freedom and experimental risk-taking over commercial maximization.

Ravachol's broader influence extended beyond published games. The company's periodical "Worlds Without Master" showcased fiction, essays, and games exploring similar experimental territory. This publication created space for literary and theoretical exploration complementing game design work.

The company's business model reflected indie publishing realities and values. Small print runs reduced financial risk while maintaining physical presence. Digital distribution through itch.io provided accessibility and sustainable revenue. Convention sales created personal connections with customers while supplementing online sales. This multifaceted approach enabled sustainable operation without compromising experimental vision.

Ravachol's public presence and community engagement influenced broader indie gaming discourse. Thoughtful writing about design philosophy, generous support of other creators, and active participation in indie gaming community built reputation extending beyond direct commercial success. Buried Without Ceremony became respected voice in discussions about experimental design, physical components, and what games could explore.

Today, Buried Without Ceremony continues operating as boutique publisher committed to experimental mechanics and thoughtful design. The company exemplifies how small-press publishers can achieve cultural influence and sustainable operation through innovative design, community engagement, and unwavering commitment to creative vision.

## Company History Expansion

Buried Without Ceremony's development paralleled story games movement's evolution from theoretical discussions to practical implementations. Ravachol participated in The Forge's online community where Ron Edwards, Vincent Baker, and others developed narrativist theory questioning traditional RPG assumptions.

The Forge's influence shaped Ravachol's design philosophy but didn't determine it. Where some Forge-influenced designers focused on specific mechanical innovations like currency systems or authority distribution, Ravachol explored how physical components and sensory experiences could create emotional resonance supporting thematic goals.

Early financial challenges tested commitment to publishing. Small print runs required upfront capital without guaranteed returns. Distribution challenges limited mainstream visibility. However, indie gaming community's enthusiasm for experimental work provided sustainable market enabling continued operation despite modest commercial scale.

Brooklyn's creative scene influenced company development. Surrounded by experimental theater, independent music, and alternative art communities, Buried Without Ceremony absorbed broader experimental culture's willingness to prioritize artistic vision over commercial calculation. This cultural context supported risk-taking uncommon in commercial gaming publishing.

The company's relationship with conventions proved crucial for visibility and sales. Gen Con, PAX, and smaller indie-focused conventions provided direct customer access, press connections, and community engagement opportunities. Convention presence built reputation translating to online sales while creating memorable personal interactions.

Ravachol's design process emphasized playtesting and refinement. Games underwent extensive development before publication, with mechanics tested rigorously to ensure they created intended experiences. This commitment to craft distinguished Buried Without Ceremony from publishers prioritizing rapid release over careful development.

The company's editorial standards maintained professional quality despite small-press scale. Clear writing, thoughtful editing, and intentional design ensured products competed on quality rather than just novelty. This professionalism built credibility supporting experimental mechanics—innovations served design goals rather than just seeking attention through gimmickry.

Buried Without Ceremony's influence extended through Ravachol's generous mentoring and support of other designers. Sharing knowledge, providing feedback, and promoting others' work built collaborative community culture. This generosity created network effects benefiting entire indie gaming ecosystem.

## Publishing Philosophy

Buried Without Ceremony's publishing philosophy centers on several core principles:

**Physical Component Integration**: Games often integrate physical components—Jenga tower, specialized dice, cards—creating tactile experiences traditional RPGs neglect. These components aren't gimmicks but carefully chosen elements creating specific emotional or experiential effects supporting thematic goals.

**Experimental Mechanics**: Willingness to explore unconventional mechanics distinguishes Buried Without Ceremony from conservative publishers. Ravachol questions every assumption about how RPGs should function, exploring alternatives to dice resolution, traditional stats, and standard narrative structures.

**Thematic Coherence**: Every mechanical choice serves thematic goals. Components, procedures, and structures align to create specific emotional experiences and explore particular themes. This coherence ensures mechanics enhance rather than distract from intended play experience.

**Horror Expertise**: Multiple games explore horror effectively through mechanics creating actual tension and fear rather than just describing scary situations. Understanding how mechanics generate emotional experiences informs horror design's sophistication.

**Small Press Values**: Operating as small press enables experimental risk-taking impossible for larger publishers requiring broad commercial success. Limited print runs, direct sales, and modest budgets create freedom to pursue artistic vision over market calculation.

**Narrative Focus**: Games prioritize compelling narratives and emotional engagement over tactical complexity or simulationist accuracy. Mechanical simplicity serves narrative clarity rather than limiting depth.

**Character Development**: Character creation and development emphasize personality, relationships, and psychological depth over numerical optimization. Questionnaires, thematic questions, and narrative-driven advancement create nuanced characters.

**Accessible Complexity**: Games balance mechanical simplicity with emergent complexity. Core rules remain accessible while play generates sophisticated situations through interactions between simple elements.

**Community Engagement**: Active participation in indie gaming discourse, generous support of other creators, and thoughtful engagement with players builds community extending beyond commercial transactions.

**Design Transparency**: Sharing design thinking and development process educates other designers while building appreciation for games' intentional construction.

## Major Releases

### Dread (2005)

Dread revolutionized horror gaming by replacing dice with Jenga tower, creating unique physical tension perfectly capturing escalating horror atmosphere. The game's radical mechanical innovation demonstrated how unconventional components could create emotional experiences impossible with traditional systems.

The core mechanic proved brilliantly simple: when characters attempted risky actions, players pulled blocks from Jenga tower. Successful pull meant success; tower collapse meant character's doom. This binary outcome—succeed or die—created stakes far beyond typical dice rolls' statistical graduations. Every pull risked everything.

The tower's physical presence transformed play psychology. Unlike dice providing momentary tension resolved by roll, tower's growing instability created sustained anxiety increasing throughout session. Players watched tower wobble, felt blocks loosening, experienced visceral tension as structure threatened collapse. This physical manifestation of danger created horror atmosphere verbal description couldn't match.

The escalating difficulty proved perfect for horror pacing. Early pulls felt relatively safe with stable tower. As session progressed and tower destabilized, simple actions became terrifying risks. This natural escalation mirrored horror fiction's structure where situations grow progressively worse until inevitable catastrophe.

Character creation used questionnaires replacing traditional stat generation. Questions like "What are you running from?" or "Why do you distrust authority?" developed nuanced backstories while establishing relationships and potential complications. This approach created well-rounded characters quickly while avoiding numerical optimization's distraction.

The questionnaire format served dual purpose: creating characters and establishing story hooks. Facilitator used answers to craft situations exploiting characters' fears, secrets, and relationships. This integration between character creation and plot development ensured personal stakes driving drama.

Tower collapse consequences varied based on narrative context. Death proved most common, but transformation, betrayal, or removal from story also occurred. This flexibility meant doom took form appropriate to situation rather than just generic failure.

The game's structure supported one-shot horror effectively. Setup proved quick, escalating tension drove toward climax, and sessions concluded decisively when tower inevitably collapsed. This format suited horror's emphasis on complete dramatic arcs rather than indefinite campaigns.

Dread achieved remarkable cultural penetration. The game became indie gaming staple, won multiple awards, appeared in mainstream media coverage, and introduced countless players to experimental mechanics. More importantly, Dread demonstrated physical components could enhance rather than gimmick-ify RPGs.

The game's influence extended far beyond direct imitators. Dread proved that radically reconsidering core RPG components could create superior experiences for specific purposes. This validation encouraged broader experimentation with alternative resolution mechanics, physical components, and unconventional game structures across indie gaming.

### Wolfspell (2011)

Wolfspell explored transformation and identity through game about humans becoming werewolves, focusing on psychological and social aspects of transformation rather than just physical changes and combat prowess. The game examined transformation as identity crisis, relationship disruption, and fundamental challenge to self-understanding.

The game's structure tracked transformation's progression. Characters began human, gradually experienced werewolf changes, and eventually faced choice between retaining humanity or embracing wolf nature. This progression created narrative arc focused on identity rather than power acquisition.

Mechanical systems emphasized transformation's psychological dimensions. Characters' perspectives shifted as wolf nature emerged—human concerns faded while pack bonds strengthened, civilized restraints weakened while primal instincts intensified. This gradual perspective shift created compelling character arcs.

Relationship mechanics tracked bonds with human contacts and emerging pack connections. As transformation progressed, human relationships strained while pack bonds deepened. Characters navigated competing loyalties, struggling to maintain human connections while drawn toward pack belonging.

The game explored transformation's social dimensions—how others reacted to changes, how characters concealed or revealed their nature, how communities responded to werewolf presence. These social complications created drama beyond just transformation's physical aspects.

Wolfspell distinguished itself from typical werewolf games emphasizing combat prowess and supernatural power. Instead, game focused on transformation's existential dimensions—what happens to identity when fundamental nature changes? This thematic sophistication elevated game beyond typical monster fantasy.

### Swords Without Master (2013)

Swords Without Master brought highly structured approach to sword-and-sorcery roleplaying, alternating between bombastic action (Glorious) and atmospheric mood (Perilous) phases creating distinctive pacing and tonal variety characteristic of the genre.

The phase structure proved central innovation. Glorious phases featured explosive action, heroic declarations, and dramatic combat. Perilous phases emphasized brooding atmosphere, ominous portents, and creeping dread. This explicit alternation created rhythm mirroring sword-and-sorcery fiction's characteristic flow between excitement and tension.

The game's formal constraints paradoxically enhanced creativity. By defining clear phase types with specific tones and purposes, structure channeled improvisation productively. Players knew what each phase should accomplish, creating focus preventing aimless wandering while supporting spontaneous creativity within framework.

Oracle mechanics provided prompts and complications driving narratives forward. Players consulted oracles for inspiration when uncertain how to proceed, receiving cryptic guidance requiring interpretation. This mechanic balanced structure with unpredictability, ensuring narratives remained surprising while maintaining coherent direction.

The game's sword-and-sorcery focus demanded specific tone—world-weary rogues, eldritch horrors, ancient civilizations, moral ambiguity. Mechanics supported this tone through phase structure emphasizing alternation between glory and peril, reflection and action, triumph and doom.

Swords Without Master influenced later games exploring structured scene types and tonal management. The concept of explicitly defined phases with characteristic tones proved useful for maintaining genre conventions while supporting improvisation.

### The Mustang (2018)

The Mustang explored Western frontier themes through game about wild horse and various humans attempting to tame, exploit, or free them, rotating protagonist role among players to examine conflicting perspectives on freedom, civilization, and land.

The game's rotating protagonist structure proved innovative. Players portrayed Mustang during some scenes, humans pursuing various agendas during others. This shifting perspective created complex narrative examining relationships between freedom and control, wilderness and civilization, indigenous and settler worldviews from multiple angles.

When portraying Mustang, players experienced freedom, fear, and resistance to captivity. When portraying humans—rancher seeking work animal, idealist dreaming of wild nobility, hunter seeing commodity—players understood varied motivations without simplistic villainy. This multiperspectival approach created nuanced exploration of Western themes.

The game addressed colonization, exploitation, and competing worldviews without didactic messaging. By presenting perspectives sympathetically while examining their conflicts, The Mustang created space for meaningful exploration of Western mythology's complications. The Mustang's freedom represented indigenous land claims, environmental preservation, and resistance to exploitation—themes resonating beyond literal horse story.

Mechanical systems supported thematic exploration. Mustang's actions emphasized freedom, survival, and resistance. Human actions pursued various goals—profit, romance, duty, ideology—each understandable within character's context while potentially harmful from other perspectives.

The game's emotional power came from genuine conflicts without clear villains. Players understood why rancher needed work horse, why hunter sought income, why idealist romanticized wildness—while simultaneously experiencing Mustang's terror at captivity. This complexity created moral weight absent from simpler good-versus-evil narratives.

The Mustang demonstrated how Western themes could be explored thoughtfully, acknowledging genre mythology's complications while creating engaging play experiences. The game influenced approaches to addressing problematic genre elements through mechanical frameworks supporting nuanced exploration.

### Vast & Starlit (2020)

Vast & Starlit returned to horror territory through space exploration framework, combining Dread's Jenga mechanics with expanded systems supporting science fiction horror's distinctive characteristics—isolation, cosmic dread, technological failure.

The game maintained Jenga tower as core tension mechanic while expanding character creation, situation frameworks, and thematic elements for science fiction. This evolution demonstrated how successful core mechanics could adapt to new genres while maintaining essential emotional effects.

Space setting's isolation amplified horror elements. Crews faced threats far from help, surrounded by hostile vacuum, dependent on fragile technology. This vulnerability created different horror flavor than Dread's typical scenarios while maintaining escalating tension structure.

The game explored cosmic horror themes—incomprehensible aliens, reality-warping phenomena, humanity's insignificance against universe's vastness. These science fiction horror staples received mechanical support through tower's physical tension and narrative frameworks emphasizing dread and isolation.

Character creation adapted Dread's questionnaire approach for science fiction crews. Questions established roles, relationships, secrets, and fears appropriate to space setting while maintaining psychological depth and narrative hooks characteristic of original game.

Vast & Starlit demonstrated Buried Without Ceremony's willingness to revisit and evolve successful designs. Rather than treating Dread as finished work, the company explored how core innovations could expand while maintaining essential characteristics.

## Business Model and Innovation

Buried Without Ceremony developed sustainable small-press model suited to experimental publishing:

**Small Print Runs**: Limited physical editions reduce financial risk while maintaining tangible products for collectors and convention sales. This approach enables experimentation without requiring mass-market success.

**Digital Distribution**: Itch.io and PDF sales provide accessible, low-overhead revenue streams supplementing physical sales. Digital formats reduce barriers for international customers and budget-conscious players.

**Convention Sales**: Direct sales at Gen Con, PAX, and indie conventions create personal customer connections while generating revenue. Convention presence builds visibility and community relationships beyond just commerce.

**Experimental Risk-Taking**: Small-press economics enable pursuing innovative designs requiring niche audiences. Freedom from mass-market requirements permits exploration impossible for larger publishers.

**Careful Development**: Extended playtesting and refinement ensure quality justifying experimental mechanics. This craft focus builds reputation supporting sales despite limited marketing budgets.

**Community Building**: Active participation in indie gaming discourse, generous support of other creators, and thoughtful engagement build reputation and goodwill translating to customer loyalty.

**Modest Operations**: Solo operation with occasional collaborators keeps overhead minimal while maintaining creative control and authentic vision.

**Physical Components**: Including specialized components—Jenga towers aren't included but games require them—creates distinctive products commanding premium pricing while delivering unique experiences.

**Periodical Publishing**: "Worlds Without Master" zine provides additional revenue stream while building community around company's aesthetic and thematic interests.

**Long-Tail Sales**: Quality experimental games continue selling years after publication as new players discover indie gaming and seek innovative experiences.

This model demonstrates sustainable small-press publishing through careful cost management, community engagement, and consistent quality enabling modest but stable operation supporting creative vision.
