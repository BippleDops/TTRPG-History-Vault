# MCDM Productions

**Founded**: 2015
**Active Era**: Modern Era (2015-Present)
**Historical Significance**: ⭐ 3/5
**Headquarters**: Renton, Washington
**Status**: Active

## Company History

MCDM Productions emerged from Matthew Colville's popular YouTube channel "Running the Game," transforming from content creator platform into successful game publisher through community-funded supplements and original RPG development. Founded in 2015, the company exemplifies the modern creator-to-publisher pipeline where engaged online communities enable direct-to-audience business models bypassing traditional publishing infrastructure. The journey from YouTube videos about D&D game mastering to developing original RPG systems demonstrates how digital platforms, crowdfunding, and passionate communities can create viable independent publishing businesses.

Matthew Colville brought diverse gaming background to MCDM's founding. His career included video game writing (Turtle Rock Studios, working on Evolve), tabletop gaming since childhood, extensive game mastering experience, and thoughtful engagement with game design theory. This combination of professional game industry experience and passionate hobbyist perspective positioned him uniquely for creating gaming content.

The "Running the Game" YouTube series launched in 2016, providing game mastering advice, campaign design philosophy, encounter building techniques, and D&D discussion. Colville's articulate presentation, theoretical depth, practical examples, and genuine enthusiasm resonated with viewers seeking game mastering improvement. The series rapidly built substantial following—eventually hundreds of thousands of subscribers.

The content focused on actionable advice over abstract theory. Rather than purely philosophical game design discussion, Colville provided specific techniques, house rules, example scenarios, and practical tools immediately applicable to campaigns. This pragmatic approach distinguished the series from more theoretical gaming discourse.

The community engagement proved central to success. Colville responded to comments, incorporated viewer questions, acknowledged community contributions, and genuinely participated in discussions. This authentic engagement built relationships beyond typical content creator-viewer dynamics.

The Patreon launched in 2016 enabling community financial support. Subscribers received early video access, exclusive content, community Discord access, and ability to financially support content creation. The Patreon quickly became financially significant, enabling Colville to pursue content creation full-time rather than as hobby alongside other employment.

The transition from content creator to publisher emerged organically from community demand. Viewers requested Colville's house rules, custom content, and design ideas in publishable form. Rather than merely sharing free PDFs, professional publication became logical next step.

Strongholds & Followers (2018) represented MCDM's first major publishing project. This 5th Edition D&D supplement addressed perceived gap in official content—rules for player characters establishing strongholds, managing followers, and engaging domain-level play beyond individual adventuring.

The Kickstarter campaign became phenomenon, raising over $2.1 million from nearly 14,000 backers. This massive success demonstrated passionate community support, market appetite for domain-level rules, and viability of creator-driven publishing. The campaign positioned MCDM as legitimate publisher rather than just content creator producing casual supplement.

The development process maintained community engagement. Regular updates, design discussions, playtesting opportunities, and transparent communication kept backers invested beyond financial contribution. This collaborative approach created ownership feeling beyond typical product purchase.

Strongholds & Followers provided comprehensive domain management rules. Different stronghold types (keeps, towers, temples, establishments) offered class-specific options. Followers provided mechanical benefits and story opportunities. The rules integrated with standard 5E gameplay while adding strategic layer typically absent from individual character focus.

The warfare rules enabled mass combat resolution at strategic rather than tactical level. Rather than moving individual soldiers, players commanded units achieving objectives. This abstraction prevented combat bog-down while maintaining meaningful tactical decisions.

The production quality matched professional publishers. Full-color printing, professional art, sophisticated layout, comprehensive editing, and quality binding created premium product justifying high backing levels. The physical object demonstrated serious publishing commitment beyond amateur production.

The success validated creator-to-publisher model. Rather than pitching traditional publishers or self-publishing marginally, Colville leveraged community directly funding professional production. This disintermediation became increasingly common as successful creators recognized communities enabled direct publishing.

Kingdoms & Warfare (2020) expanded domain-level rules, focusing on organizational management and large-scale conflicts. The supplement addressed kingdoms, organizations, and warfare at scales beyond individual adventures.

The organization rules enabled players to develop institutions—kingdoms, churches, mercenary companies, thieves' guilds. These organizations featured statistics, abilities, and development systems creating mechanical framework for domain play.

The warfare expansion deepened mass combat rules from Strongholds & Followers. The comprehensive system enabled strategic battles integrating with standard 5E while maintaining distinct scale and pacing.

The intrigue system provided social conflict resolution at organizational level. Diplomacy, espionage, and political maneuvering received mechanical framework beyond individual Charisma checks.

The Kickstarter raised over $2.2 million, exceeding even Strongholds & Followers' success. This demonstrated sustained community support and appetite for MCDM's design approach. The consistent crowdfunding success positioned company financially for ambitious future projects.

Flee, Mortals! (2023) addressed another perceived 5E gap—comprehensive monster manual with tactically interesting creatures designed for dynamic combat. The book provided over 400 creatures emphasizing action-oriented design.

The action-oriented monster design philosophy emphasized dynamic combat over static stat blocks. Rather than creatures simply making attacks, the designs featured special actions, reactions, triggered abilities, and tactical considerations creating interesting encounters beyond hit point attrition.

The villain actions concept provided legendary actions framework specifically designed for single powerful foes facing parties. These abilities enabled villains to remain threatening and interesting without overwhelming action economy disadvantages.

The companion rules integrated animal companions, summoned creatures, and followers into combat without overwhelming bookkeeping. This addressed common 5E complaint about companion management complexity.

The Kickstarter raised over $4.5 million from 34,000+ backers—MCDM's most successful campaign. The massive support demonstrated growing community, proven track record, and market appetite for quality monster design. The success positioned MCDM among most successful crowdfunding RPG publishers.

The MCDM RPG development represented strategic evolution from supplements to original core system. Rather than continuing 5E supplementation indefinitely, Colville envisioned creating independent RPG incorporating years of design philosophy and community feedback.

The design goals emphasized tactical gameplay, heroic fantasy, interesting combat, and removing D&D sacred cows that don't serve play. Rather than revolutionary mechanics, the game refines traditional fantasy RPG concepts based on extensive play experience and theoretical consideration.

The heroes' focus on tactical combat distinguishes it from narrative-focused alternatives. While storytelling matters, the system prioritizes mechanically interesting combat creating tactical challenges and meaningful decisions. This design philosophy serves specific audience preferring tactical depth.

The development transparency continued MCDM's community engagement tradition. Regular updates, playtest packets, design discussions, and feedback incorporation maintain backer investment. The collaborative development creates community ownership in success.

The ambitious scope represents significant risk. Creating original core RPG requires enormous development investment, competes against established systems with decades of content, and risks fragmenting community between 5E supplements and original system supporters. However, success could establish MCDM as core system publisher beyond supplement creator.

Throughout history, MCDM maintained unusual business model—funded primarily through Kickstarter campaigns and Patreon rather than traditional publishing revenue. This direct community support enabled independence from publishers, distributors, and traditional retail channels while requiring sustained community engagement and delivery reliability.

The talent recruitment built small but capable team. James Introcaso, Celeste Conowitch, and other designers brought professional experience and community credibility. The team expansion from solo creator to collaborative design studio demonstrated business maturation.

The Where Evil Lives (2024) adventure compilation provided ready-to-play content supporting MCDM's mechanical supplements. The adventures showcased action-oriented design philosophy through practical examples.

Today, MCDM operates as established independent publisher with successful supplement line, original RPG in development, engaged community, and proven crowdfunding success. The company represents successful creator-to-publisher evolution enabled by digital platforms and direct community support.

## Business Model and Philosophy

MCDM Productions developed distinctive model based on community funding and engagement:

**Crowdfunding Primary Revenue**: Rather than traditional publishing with advance print runs and distributor relationships, MCDM funds projects through Kickstarter campaigns. This provides upfront capital, validates market interest, and builds community investment before production.

**Patreon Supplementary Support**: Ongoing Patreon subscriptions provide consistent revenue funding operations between major projects. This subscription model creates financial stability while rewarding committed supporters with exclusive content and access.

**Content Marketing Foundation**: The "Running the Game" YouTube series functions as extended marketing, building community and demonstrating design philosophy. Free content creates awareness and trust enabling commercial success.

**Community Collaboration**: Extensive backer engagement, playtesting opportunities, feedback incorporation, and transparent communication treat community as creative partners rather than mere customers.

**Premium Production Values**: Professional art, quality printing, sophisticated layout, and comprehensive editing create products justifying premium pricing and competing with major publishers' presentation standards.

**Supplement-First Strategy**: Rather than immediately creating original core system, MCDM established credibility through high-quality supplements for existing popular system (5E). This built audience and experience before attempting more ambitious original system.

**Action-Oriented Design Philosophy**: Emphasizing tactically interesting combat, dynamic encounters, meaningful decisions, and mechanical engagement distinguishes MCDM from narrative-focused design trends.

**Heroic Fantasy Focus**: The games celebrate heroic protagonists, epic adventures, and triumphant narratives over grimdark alternatives. This optimistic approach serves specific audience preferences.

**Independence Priority**: Maintaining operational independence from traditional publishers, investors, or corporate ownership preserves creative control while limiting resources.

**Direct Community Relationship**: Bypassing traditional retail and distribution enables direct creator-audience connection but requires sustained community engagement and delivery excellence.

**Transparent Development**: Regular updates, design discussions, and honest communication about challenges build trust and investment beyond simple purchase transactions.

**Talent Collaboration**: Recruiting experienced designers creates collaborative design environment producing better products than solo efforts while maintaining consistent vision.

This philosophy creates sustainable independent publisher funded directly by engaged community rather than traditional publishing infrastructure.

## Major Releases

### Strongholds & Followers (2018)

Strongholds & Followers addressed perceived 5th Edition D&D gap—domain-level play rules enabling characters to establish strongholds, manage followers, and engage strategic-scale activities beyond individual adventuring.

The stronghold types provided class-specific options. Keeps suited fighters and paladins, towers served wizards, temples supported clerics, establishments enabled rogues and bards. This class differentiation created mechanical and narrative appropriateness for different character types.

The construction rules enabled customization through modular design. Rather than predetermined strongholds, players allocated resources across features creating personalized domains. This customization served optimization preferences while maintaining reasonable balance.

The follower system provided mechanical benefits and narrative opportunities. Characters attracted followers—soldiers, scholars, acolytes, servants—providing capabilities beyond personal abilities. Followers enabled domain management, military operations, research projects, and other activities.

The warfare rules resolved mass combat at strategic scale. Rather than tactical miniatures battles with hundreds of figures, the system abstracted combat to unit actions achieving objectives. This maintained meaningful decisions while preventing overwhelming complexity.

The special units provided tactical variety in warfare. Artillery, cavalry, infantry, magic users, and specialty units featured distinct capabilities creating combined-arms tactical considerations.

The integration with standard 5E maintained compatibility. Rather than replacing core gameplay, domain rules added strategic layer between adventures. This preserved standard play while expanding scope.

The practical examples demonstrated implementation. Sample strongholds, followers, and warfare scenarios showed how rules functioned during play rather than leaving implementation entirely to groups.

The production featured beautiful full-color artwork, professional layout, comprehensive content, and quality binding. The physical book created desirable object beyond mere mechanical resource.

The crowdfunding success ($2.1M) demonstrated market appetite for domain-level play and MCDM's community trust. The campaign positioned MCDM as legitimate publisher capable of professional production.

The cultural impact influenced subsequent 5E design. Official products like Acquisitions Incorporated and later supplements incorporated stronghold concepts, demonstrating MCDM's influence on broader 5E evolution.

### Kingdoms & Warfare (2020)

Kingdoms & Warfare expanded domain-level play, focusing on organizational management, mass combat, and political intrigue at scales beyond individual adventuring.

The organization rules created mechanical framework for institutions. Kingdoms, churches, mercenary companies, thieves' guilds, and other organizations featured statistics—Domain Power, Size, Defenses, and special abilities. This systemic approach enabled organizations as campaign elements with mechanical substance.

The organization actions provided domain-turn activities. Diplomacy, warfare, construction, research, and other actions occurred at domain scale alongside individual adventures. This dual-scale play created campaign depth beyond pure adventuring.

The domain turns structured large-scale time passage. Rather than tracking days, domain turns represented months enabling strategic decision-making between adventure arcs. This pacing integrated personal and strategic play.

The warfare expansion comprehensively detailed mass combat. Building on Strongholds & Followers' foundation, the system featured extensive unit types, battlefield terrain, magical support, and tactical considerations creating sophisticated strategic battles.

The unit customization enabled tailored armies. Rather than standardized troops, organizations developed unique forces reflecting culture, resources, and strategic doctrine. This customization created mechanical differentiation matching narrative identity.

The intrigue system provided social conflict resolution at organizational scale. Diplomacy, espionage, propaganda, and political maneuvering received mechanical framework. This addressed criticism that D&D handles combat mechanically but treats social conflict as pure roleplay.

The integration challenges required careful campaign management. Balancing individual adventures with domain management, ensuring all players remained engaged during strategic play, and preventing one scale from overwhelming the other demanded thoughtful implementation.

The practical advice helped groups implement complex systems. Rather than just mechanical rules, the book provided campaign structure guidance, pacing advice, and examples demonstrating successful integration.

The crowdfunding success ($2.2M) exceeded even Strongholds & Followers, demonstrating sustained community support and MCDM's growing reputation.

### Flee, Mortals! (2023)

Flee, Mortals! The Monsters Know What They're Doing provided comprehensive monster manual emphasizing action-oriented design philosophy creating tactically interesting combat encounters.

The action-oriented design distinguished creatures from traditional static stat blocks. Rather than monsters simply attacking, designs featured special actions, reactions, triggered abilities, and tactical considerations. This created dynamic encounters where monsters felt distinct and interesting.

The villain actions framework enabled solo powerful foes to challenge parties. Rather than legendary actions (somewhat underpowered), villain actions occurred at specific initiative counts creating dramatic moments and maintaining threat despite action economy disadvantage.

The minion rules addressed numerous weak creatures. Rather than tracking hit points for dozens of enemies, minions died from any damage while dealing meaningful attacks. This maintained threat of numerous foes without overwhelming bookkeeping.

The companion rules integrated animal companions, summoned creatures, and NPC allies without excessive complexity. Simple action options and streamlined bookkeeping maintained companions' tactical relevance while preventing overwhelming detail.

The creature variety covered classic fantasy monsters comprehensively. Dragons, demons, undead, beasts, and other staples received action-oriented treatment demonstrating design philosophy across creature types.

The tactical guidance helped DMs use creatures effectively. Rather than just stat blocks, entries included tactical advice, roleplaying notes, and encounter suggestions maximizing interesting play.

The encounter building advice provided framework for creating challenging, interesting combats. Mixing creature types, using terrain, incorporating objectives, and dramatic pacing created memorable encounters beyond simple combat attrition.

The production quality featured beautiful artwork, professional layout, and comprehensive content across 400+ creatures. The physical book created substantial, desirable product.

The crowdfunding phenomenon ($4.5M from 34,000+ backers) positioned Flee, Mortals! among most successful RPG Kickstarter campaigns. This demonstrated MCDM's growing community and market position.

The design influence extended beyond direct sales. Action-oriented design philosophy influenced how DMs approach encounter design and how other designers create monster stat blocks.

### The MCDM RPG (In Development)

The MCDM RPG represents strategic evolution from 5E supplementation to original core system development. Rather than continuing supplements indefinitely, Colville envisioned creating RPG incorporating years of design philosophy and community feedback.

The design philosophy emphasizes tactical heroic fantasy. The system prioritizes mechanically interesting combat creating meaningful tactical decisions while maintaining heroic tone where players feel powerful and capable.

The heroes over balanced encounters approach designs for party success rather than mathematical encounter balance. Rather than potentially losing every fight being equally possible, the system assumes heroes generally triumph while creating interesting challenges and meaningful victories.

The simplified resolution maintains familiar d20 foundation while streamlining subsystems. The goal balances accessibility with sufficient tactical depth for sustained play.

The class design creates distinct mechanical and narrative identities. Rather than similar classes with cosmetic differences, each class plays distinctively with unique mechanics reinforcing thematic identity.

The removing sacred cows philosophy questions D&D assumptions that don't serve play. Rather than preserving mechanics purely from tradition, the design evaluates whether each element enhances gameplay.

The tactical combat emphasis creates encounters as interesting challenges. Terrain, objectives, special actions, and dynamic developments prevent fights from becoming mere hit point attrition.

The development transparency maintains community engagement. Regular playtest packets, design discussions, feedback incorporation, and honest updates treat backers as collaborative partners in development.

The ambitious scope represents significant risk. Creating original core system requires enormous development investment, competes against D&D's market dominance, and risks fragmenting MCDM's community between 5E and original system.

The success potential could establish MCDM as core system publisher rather than supplement creator. This represents qualitative business transformation with corresponding opportunities and challenges.

**Action-Oriented Design**: The monster design philosophy influenced how designers and DMs approach creating tactically interesting combat encounters.

**Domain-Level Play**: Strongholds & Followers and Kingdoms & Warfare renewed interest in domain management and mass combat systems for 5E.

**Community Collaboration**: The extensive backer engagement and transparent development influenced publisher-community relationship models.
