# Renegade Game Studios

**Founded**: 2014
**Active Era**: Modern Era (2014-Present)
**Historical Significance**: ⭐ 4/5
**Headquarters**: El Segundo, California
**Status**: Active

## Company History

Renegade Game Studios emerged as dynamic publisher bridging accessible family gaming, nostalgic licensed properties, and sophisticated narrative RPGs—most notably becoming primary publisher for World of Darkness 5th Edition games. Founded by Scott Gaeta in 2014, the company evolved from board game publisher into significant RPG force through strategic licensing, partnership publishing, and commitment to accessible design. The journey from Lanterns and Clank! to publishing Vampire: The Masquerade 5th Edition demonstrates how versatile publishing strategy, licensing acumen, and production quality can build diverse gaming catalog serving multiple markets simultaneously.

Scott Gaeta founded Renegade Game Studios after experience in gaming retail and distribution sectors. His industry knowledge provided understanding of market dynamics, retailer relationships, and distribution logistics many creative-focused publishers lacked. This business sophistication positioned Renegade strategically from inception.

The company initially focused on board games, publishing titles across various mechanics and themes. Lanterns: The Harvest Festival, Clank! In! Space!, and other board games built catalog and industry credibility. The board game focus provided revenue foundation enabling subsequent RPG expansion.

The licensing strategy distinguished Renegade from competitors. Rather than primarily developing original properties, the company pursued licensed board game adaptations of popular properties. This approach leveraged existing IP recognition while serving fans seeking tabletop experiences of beloved media.

The production quality emphasis created market positioning. Renegade games featured professional art, quality components, sophisticated graphic design, and careful development. This premium positioning justified higher prices while creating desirable products competing with industry leaders.

The RPG expansion began with Kids on Bikes (2018), designed by Jonathan Gilmour and Doug Levandowski. This nostalgic RPG captured 1980s youth-focused adventure aesthetic popularized by Stranger Things, E.T., The Goonies, and similar media. Players portrayed kids investigating mysteries in small-town America, confronting supernatural threats through courage, friendship, and ingenuity.

The Powered by Kids on Bikes system used simple mechanics accessible to families and younger players. Each player controlled kid character using basic stats and aspects. One player controlled shared Powered Character—mysterious figure with supernatural abilities supporting group. This collaborative approach encouraged teamwork while maintaining accessibility.

The game's accessibility proved strategically important. Rather than targeting traditional RPG demographic, Kids on Bikes served families, younger players, and newcomers intimidated by complex systems. This market expansion aligned with broader industry accessibility push.

The success spawned "Powered by" ecosystem. Kids on Brooms (magical school setting), Teens in Space (young adult science fiction), and other variants adapted core mechanics to different genres. This ecosystem approach created recognizable system family while exploring diverse themes.

The critical breakthrough came through partnership with Paradox Interactive for World of Darkness 5th Edition publishing. Paradox acquired White Wolf IP in 2015, bringing tabletop publishing in-house before licensing to third-party publishers. Renegade secured publishing rights for Vampire: The Masquerade 5th Edition and subsequent World of Darkness games.

Vampire: The Masquerade 5th Edition (2018) represented franchise reboot with modernized mechanics and controversial setting updates. The game addressed contemporary themes—surveillance states, social media, refugee crises—while updating mechanics for modern sensibilities. The Hunger system replaced traditional Blood Pool, mechanically expressing vampiric nature more viscerally.

The controversial elements sparked community debate. The Chechnya content in early printings (discussing real-world persecution) created backlash leading to revision. The mechanical changes from previous editions divided longtime fans. However, production quality and design sophistication attracted new audiences while most veterans appreciated refinements.

The success positioned Renegade as primary World of Darkness publisher. Hunter: The Reckoning 5th Edition (2022) continued franchise revival, presenting modern monster hunters confronting supernatural threats. Werewolf: The Apocalypse 5th Edition (2023) addressed environmental themes through shapeshifter lens. This systematic franchise revival demonstrated sustained commitment beyond single product.

The licensed property strategy expanded aggressively. Power Rangers RPG (2022) adapted iconic children's television franchise into accessible gaming experience. G.I. JOE Roleplaying Game (2021) brought military action figures to tabletop. Transformers RPG development continued licensing strategy. These nostalgic properties targeted audiences combining childhood affection with adult purchasing power.

The licensed RPG approach required careful mechanical choices. Rather than creating bespoke system for each property, Renegade developed Essence20 system—unified framework adaptable across properties. Power Rangers, G.I. JOE, and Transformers all used Essence20 with property-specific modifications.

The Essence20 system provided accessible resolution using d20 roll plus modifiers against difficulties. The system emphasized competence—heroes succeeded regularly while challenges remained meaningful. Skills, perks, and character abilities created customization without overwhelming complexity. This balanced accessibility with sufficient depth for sustained play.

The production values maintained premium standards across properties. Full-color books, professional art, quality printing, and sophisticated design created desirable products regardless of license. This consistent quality built brand reputation exceeding any single property's recognition.

The partnership publishing model distinguished Renegade from traditional publishers. Rather than purely in-house development, the company frequently partnered with designers and smaller publishers bringing projects to professional publication. This curator role enabled diverse catalog without requiring extensive in-house design staff.

The board game catalog continued expanding alongside RPG growth. The company maintained dual focus—board games providing consistent revenue while RPG prestige built industry recognition. This diversification reduced risk from any single product category's market fluctuations.

The convention presence reinforced market position. Gen Con, PAX, and other major events featured Renegade prominently with demos, panels, and sales. The visible presence built industry relationships while introducing products to potential customers.

The COVID-19 pandemic challenged traditional business model dependent on retail sales and conventions. However, Renegade adapted through increased digital presence, online events, and direct-to-consumer sales. The established catalog and distributor relationships enabled weathering disruption better than less established publishers.

The community engagement balanced corporate professionalism with genuine enthusiasm. Social media presence, responsive customer service, organized play programs, and community support built relationships beyond transactional sales. This engagement particularly important for World of Darkness properties with devoted existing fanbases requiring respectful stewardship.

The strategic flexibility demonstrated adaptation capabilities. The transition from board games to RPGs, pursuit of diverse licenses, partnership publishing model, and system development showed willingness to evolve based on opportunities and market conditions rather than rigid adherence to original vision.

Today, Renegade operates as significant publisher with diverse catalog spanning board games, family-friendly RPGs, licensed properties, and prestigious World of Darkness franchise. The company occupies unusual position—simultaneously serving casual family market and sophisticated horror gaming audiences through carefully targeted product lines.

## Business Model and Philosophy

Renegade Game Studios developed versatile approach serving multiple markets:

**Licensing Focus**: Pursuing licensed properties rather than primarily original IP leverages existing recognition while serving fan demand for official tabletop adaptations. This strategy requires securing profitable licenses while executing quality adaptations.

**Dual Board Game/RPG Strategy**: Maintaining both board game and RPG catalogs diversifies revenue streams and market presence. Board games provide accessible entry point while RPGs offer deeper engagement.

**Partnership Publishing**: Collaborating with designers and smaller publishers through partnership model enables diverse catalog without extensive in-house development. This curator role builds industry relationships while reducing development risk.

**Accessible Design Philosophy**: Emphasizing accessible mechanics, family-friendly themes, and low barrier to entry expands market beyond traditional gaming demographics. Kids on Bikes and licensed properties serve this accessibility focus.

**Premium Production Values**: Professional presentation, quality components, sophisticated design, and careful development create products justifying premium pricing while competing with industry leaders.

**Unified System Development**: Creating Essence20 as flexible framework for multiple licensed properties enables development efficiency while providing mechanical consistency across catalog.

**Nostalgic Property Targeting**: Focusing on licenses combining childhood affection with adult purchasing power (Power Rangers, G.I. JOE, Transformers) serves demographic with emotional connection and financial means.

**World of Darkness Stewardship**: Managing prestigious franchise under Paradox license requires respectful continuation, mechanical modernization, and community engagement. This high-profile stewardship builds industry credibility.

**Multi-Demographic Strategy**: Simultaneously serving family market (Kids on Bikes), nostalgic adults (licensed properties), and sophisticated gamers (World of Darkness) requires careful product line management.

**Retail and Distribution Expertise**: Founder's background in retail and distribution creates operational advantages in market positioning, retailer relationships, and logistics.

**Hybrid Crowdfunding**: Using Kickstarter selectively for major projects while maintaining traditional distribution for regular releases balances crowdfunding benefits with retail presence.

**Organized Play Support**: Providing organized play programs, convention support, and community resources builds engagement beyond individual sales.

This multifaceted approach creates sustainable business serving diverse markets through carefully targeted products rather than single-focus strategy.

## Major Releases

### Vampire: The Masquerade 5th Edition (2018)

Vampire: The Masquerade 5th Edition represented franchise reboot after years of dormancy, modernizing mechanics and setting while attempting contemporary relevance. Published by Renegade under Paradox license, the game sparked controversy while attracting new audiences.

The Hunger system replaced Blood Pool mechanics. Rather than tracking numerical blood points, vampires experienced Hunger—escalating drive represented by special dice potentially triggering Beast manifestations. This visceral mechanic made vampiric nature more prominent in gameplay.

The mechanical streamlining simplified resolution. The d10 dice pool system maintained familiar structure but reduced complexity. Character creation became more accessible, conflicts resolved more quickly, and bookkeeping decreased. These changes prioritized playability over simulation detail.

The Disciplines received substantial revision. Rather than extensive power lists, each discipline featured branching power trees. Characters developed signature powers reflecting individual expression rather than predetermined advancement. This created customization while reducing choice paralysis.

The thin-blood concept introduced vampire subspecies with weaker but more flexible powers. These second-class vampires faced discrimination while accessing unique abilities. The option provided alternative play style while addressing setting questions about vampire population.

The contemporary themes addressed modern concerns. Surveillance states, social media's impact, refugee crises, and political polarization appeared in setting material. The Second Inquisition—modern hunters using government resources and technology—presented existential threat to Kindred society.

The Chechnya controversy demonstrated licensed property challenges. Early printings included content referencing real-world Chechen persecution of LGBTQ+ individuals. Community backlash led to content revision and apology. The incident highlighted sensitivity required when addressing real-world atrocities in games.

The mechanical changes divided community. Veterans accustomed to previous editions debated whether simplifications improved accessibility or reduced depth. The Hunger system received mixed reception—some appreciated visceral vampirism representation, others preferred numerical tracking.

The production quality achieved professional standards. Full-color artwork, sophisticated layout, comprehensive content, and quality printing created premium product. The physical presentation matched or exceeded previous editions.

The success attracted new players while retaining substantial veteran audience. The game's accessibility lowered barriers for newcomers interested through video games, television, or general vampire fiction. Simultaneously, many longtime fans appreciated modernization even while debating specific changes.

The extensive supplement line supported comprehensive setting exploration. Camarilla and Anarch books examined sect politics. Companion provided additional character options. Chicago by Night and other setting books detailed specific locations. Cults of the Blood Gods explored religions. This systematic development built comprehensive game line.

The cultural timing proved fortunate. Vampire fiction's continued popularity, nostalgia for 1990s properties, and interest in sophisticated horror gaming created receptive market. The game capitalized on World of Darkness's cultural recognition while offering modern implementation.

### Kids on Bikes (2018)

Kids on Bikes captured 1980s nostalgic adventure aesthetic through accessible RPG mechanics suitable for families and younger players. The game channeled Stranger Things, The Goonies, E.T., and similar media presenting kids confronting mysteries in small-town America.

The character creation used simple stats—Brains, Brawn, Fight, Flight, Charm, and Grit—rated by dice type (d4 through d20). Higher dice indicated greater capability, creating intuitive character differentiation. The limited stats enabled quick creation while providing meaningful mechanical differences.

The collaborative Powered Character represented shared mysterious figure with supernatural abilities. Rather than each player controlling individual character, one player at a time directed Powered Character supporting group. This rotational control encouraged collaboration while introducing supernatural elements.

The Trope-based character creation streamlined development. Rather than complex point allocation, players selected character tropes—Popular Kid, Nerd, Loner, etc.—receiving appropriate stat assignments and narrative hooks. This accelerated creation while establishing clear character identities.

The adversity tokens created collaborative storytelling currency. Players earned tokens describing character failures and complications. These tokens could later be spent for successes, creating narrative balance where setbacks enabled future triumphs.

The episodic structure supported campaign play mimicking television seasons. Individual episodes addressed immediate mysteries while overarching plot developed across season. This structure provided satisfying short-term resolution while building toward climax.

The nostalgic setting evoked specific period aesthetics—1980s small towns, kid freedom and danger, mystery and wonder, and coming-of-age themes. While explicitly 1980s-focused, the system supported other periods or contemporary settings.

The accessibility proved commercially and culturally important. Families played together, introducing children to roleplaying. New gamers found approachable entry point. Traditional gamers discovered lightweight system for casual play.

The Powered by Kids on Bikes ecosystem expanded system through variants. Kids on Brooms brought magical school adventures. Teens in Space offered young adult science fiction. Altered States presented '90s video store employees. These variants demonstrated system flexibility while building recognizable brand.

The cultural relevance capitalized on nostalgic entertainment popularity. Stranger Things' success created broad audience for 1980s kid adventure narratives. Kids on Bikes provided participatory experience of beloved genre.

### Power Rangers RPG (2022)

Power Rangers Roleplaying Game adapted iconic children's television franchise using Essence20 system. The game faced unique challenge—serving nostalgic adult fans while maintaining family-friendly accessibility matching source material.

The Essence20 system provided accessible d20-based resolution. Characters rolled d20 plus modifiers against difficulties, with perks and abilities providing advantages. The familiar structure eased learning for players experienced with D&D-derived systems.

The Ranger creation process included civilian identity and Ranger form. Characters developed normal capabilities alongside powered abilities, supporting storylines involving secret identities and civilian complications. This dual character addressed franchise's civilian/hero dichotomy.

The color-based role system captured Power Rangers' team structure. Red Leaders, Blue brains, Yellow hearts, etc. provided mechanical differentiation while honoring franchise conventions. The roles created team synergies encouraging collaboration.

The zord (giant robot) rules enabled climactic mecha combat. Characters piloted individual zords potentially combining into Megazord for enhanced power. The tactical mecha combat system captured franchise's signature battles while remaining accessible.

The threat escalation mechanics mirrored television episode structure. Conflicts escalated from civilian encounters through powered battles to zord combat. This pacing recreated show's typical progression while providing varied gameplay.

The Grid as power source provided setting framework. Rangers drew power from Morphin Grid—multiversal energy field enabling transformation and abilities. This metaphysical concept explained diverse Ranger teams across franchise history.

The multi-season support enabled campaign variety. The game supported original Mighty Morphin Power Rangers alongside numerous subsequent series. This flexibility let groups play favorites while maintaining mechanical consistency.

The family-friendly approach maintained source material's tone. Despite martial arts combat, the game avoided graphic violence or mature themes. This accessibility enabled family play while serving nostalgic adults wanting authentic franchise experience.

The production featured extensive franchise imagery. Licensed art from various series, recognizable characters and locations, and faithful adaptation created official product matching franchise aesthetic.

The success demonstrated licensed property adaptation's viability. Power Rangers combined nostalgia, family appeal, and tactical gameplay creating market success while serving devoted fanbase.

### Hunter: The Reckoning 5th Edition (2022)

Hunter: The Reckoning 5th Edition continued World of Darkness revival, presenting modern hunters confronting supernatural threats. The game explored ordinary humans discovering monsters and choosing to fight despite terrible odds.

The Conviction system drove hunter motivation. Rather than mechanical power source, Conviction represented moral certainty fueling hunters' resistance to supernatural influence and ability to perceive hidden threats. Different Creeds embodied distinct philosophical approaches to hunting.

The Desperation mechanic captured hunters' mortal fragility. Hunters could push beyond normal capabilities through desperation, risking consequences for enhanced performance. This created tension between capability needs and personal costs.

The Edges provided supernatural advantages without making hunters superhuman. Rather than magic powers, Edges represented heightened perceptions, resistance to influence, and subtle advantages enabling survival against superior foes.

The Chronicle structure supported varied campaign styles. Hunters could operate as isolated cells, organized groups, or connected to larger organizations. The flexibility accommodated different play preferences and themes.

The danger emphasis maintained horror atmosphere. Hunters remained mortal facing creatures with supernatural advantages. Combat proved deadly, investigation crucial, and preparation essential. This created tense gameplay where careful planning proved necessary for survival.

The contemporary themes addressed modern concerns. Conspiracy theories, surveillance, extremism, and social fragmentation appeared in setting material. The game explored how ordinary people react discovering monsters hiding in society.

The organization variety provided factional options. Cells represented different philosophical approaches, resources, and methods. This created character diversity while enabling intra-hunter conflicts beyond simple monster hunting.

The moral complexity avoided simple heroism. Hunters committed violence, invaded privacy, and operated outside legal constraints. The game questioned whether ends justified means and cost of fighting monsters while maintaining humanity.
