# Goodman Games

**Founded**: 2001
**Active Era**: Modern Era (2001-Present)
**Historical Significance**: ⭐ 4/5
**Headquarters**: Chicago, Illinois
**Status**: Active

## Company History

Goodman Games emerged in the d20 System boom of the early 2000s, initially publishing third-party Dungeons & Dragons supplements before creating one of the Old School Renaissance's most distinctive and successful games: Dungeon Crawl Classics RPG. Founded by Joseph Goodman, the company evolved from supplement publisher to creative force defining modern old-school gaming. The journey from producing adventure modules to publishing acclaimed original RPG systems demonstrates how focus, quality, and understanding audience can transform a company's trajectory.

Joseph Goodman entered RPG publishing during the d20 System explosion following Wizards of the Coast's Open Gaming License release in 2000. The OGL allowed third-party publishers to create D&D-compatible content legally, spawning dozens of companies. Goodman recognized opportunity: quality adventure modules addressing perceived gap in official D&D support. While Wizards focused on core rulebooks and setting books, adventure modules received less attention. Third-party publishers could fill this niche.

Goodman Games launched in 2001 with clear vision: publish high-quality adventure modules evoking classic D&D's spirit. The early products emphasized dungeon exploration, dangerous encounters, mysterious locations, and deadly challenges—elements Goodman felt contemporary D&D deemphasized. The company name itself signaled straightforward quality focus—good games from Goodman.

The early product line focused on d20 System adventure modules. These weren't simple dungeon maps with monster stats but fully developed scenarios with backgrounds, NPCs, interesting locations, and multiple resolution approaches. Production values exceeded many competitors—professional editing, quality art, useful maps, and careful playtesting. This quality focus distinguished Goodman Games in crowded d20 market.

Several product lines emerged during d20 era. The Dungeon Crawl Classics line (originally just adventure modules, predating the RPG) became signature series—adventures emphasizing exploration, danger, and classic fantasy themes. These modules explicitly evoked early D&D's spirit while using modern d20 mechanics. The name "Dungeon Crawl Classics" captured nostalgic appeal while clearly communicating content.

Each DCC module received distinctive cover featuring painted art in classic fantasy style, establishing visual identity. The trade dress—purple borders, bold title fonts, evocative taglines—made modules instantly recognizable. This branding created collectible series identity beyond just individual products.

The modules gained reputation for quality, challenge, and old-school sensibility. Dungeons proved dangerous, puzzles required thought, and character death remained real possibility. This differentiated them from contemporary adventures critics felt were too gentle or story-focused. Goodman Games served audience wanting challenging tactical gameplay.

The company expanded beyond adventures. The Aerial Adventure line explored sky-based scenarios. The Cthulhu Mythos line brought Lovecraftian horror to d20 System. Various setting-specific adventures explored different fantasy environments. This diversification reduced dependence on single product type.

When d20 boom cooled in mid-2000s, many third-party publishers collapsed. Market oversaturation, decreasing sales, and audience fatigue with generic d20 content killed numerous companies. Goodman Games survived through quality reputation, conservative business practices, and genuine audience connection. The company avoided overextension, maintained quality standards, and built loyal customer base.

The transition to Dungeons & Dragons 4th Edition (2008) created crisis for d20 publishers. Wizards replaced OGL with more restrictive Game System License, and 4th Edition's radical mechanical changes alienated many fans. Some publishers adapted to 4E, others shifted to Pathfinder (Paizo's 3.5E continuation), many simply disappeared. Goodman Games faced critical decision about future direction.

Rather than merely following D&D editions or Pathfinder, Joseph Goodman envisioned creating original RPG capturing old-school spirit with modern design. The Dungeon Crawl Classics RPG (DCC RPG) began development as completely new game evoking classic fantasy gaming while incorporating innovative mechanics. This represented enormous gamble—creating original RPG from scratch required massive development investment with uncertain returns.

Development took several years, involving extensive playtesting and refinement. Goodman wanted game feeling simultaneously familiar and fresh—recognizable to D&D veterans but offering novel experiences. The mechanical innovations needed justifying separate game rather than just another retroclone.

Dungeon Crawl Classics RPG launched via Kickstarter in 2012, raising over $100,000 and validating market enthusiasm. The game's release transformed Goodman Games from supplement publisher to RPG creator. DCC RPG became the company's defining product, overshadowing earlier d20 work.

The game's mechanical innovations distinguished it from OSR contemporaries. The dice chain (d3, d4, d5, d6, d7, d8, d10, d12, d14, d16, d20, d24, d30) created granular modifier system where bonuses/penalties shifted die types rather than adding numbers. This seemingly simple change created visceral, swingy resolution feeling different from standard d20 roll.

The zero-level funnel approach started characters as random peasants—multiple characters per player, most dying during brutal first adventure. Survivors reached level 1 having earned advancement through trial by fire. This encouraged creative problem-solving over mechanical optimization and created memorable emergent stories.

Magic functioned unpredictably through spell tables. Rather than automatic effects, casters rolled dice consulting tables for results ranging from spectacular success to catastrophic failure. Critical failures caused corruption or worse. This made magic feel wild and dangerous rather than merely another resource.

The corruption mechanics transformed wizards over time, marking them with physical and mental changes reflecting reality manipulation. Warriors relied on mighty deeds—attempting cinematic actions beyond normal combat. Clerics performed divine appeals rather than merely selecting spells. Each class felt mechanically and thematically distinct.

The aesthetic embraced gonzo sword-and-sorcery over high fantasy. Appendix N literature (Leiber, Moorcock, Vance, Howard) provided inspiration rather than Tolkien-derived high fantasy. This created grittier, weirder tone with more moral ambiguity and fantastical weirdness.

DCC RPG achieved critical and commercial success beyond expectations. The game won multiple awards, spawned extensive adventure module line, built devoted community, and established Goodman Games as major RPG publisher. The success validated gamble on original system.

The adventure module line continued DCC RPG's success. Modules like Sailors on the Starless Sea, The Chained Coffin, and Intrigue at the Court of Chaos became beloved classics. The distinctive purple trade dress continued, creating collectible series. Third-party publishers created DCC-compatible content, expanding ecosystem beyond Goodman's output.

Mutant Crawl Classics (2017) adapted DCC mechanics to post-apocalyptic science fantasy inspired by Gamma World and Metamorphosis Alpha. The game demonstrated system's flexibility while serving nostalgic science-fantasy audience. The same core mechanics—dice chain, spell/tech tables, zero-level funnels—worked effectively in different genre.

The Original Adventures Reincarnated series (2018-present) presented classic D&D modules with both original text and 5th Edition conversion. These deluxe editions included historical context, designer interviews, classic art alongside new illustrations, and both original rules and modern conversion. The series preserved gaming history while making classics accessible to contemporary audiences.

Temple of Elemental Evil, Into the Borderlands, Expedition to the Barrier Peaks, and other classics received this treatment. Each became substantial hardcover featuring extensive historical material beyond just adventure conversion. This demonstrated Goodman's continued commitment to preserving and celebrating RPG history.

The Fifth Edition Fantasy line provided 5E adventures in Goodman's signature style. These modules served D&D 5E market while maintaining company's design philosophy—challenging, dangerous, old-school-inspired. The series achieved strong sales, demonstrating market appetite for this approach within modern D&D.

DCC Lankhmar (2018) licensed Fritz Leiber's Fafhrd and Gray Mouser setting, bringing sword-and-sorcery classics to DCC. The game modified core DCC rules for urban intrigue and thievery while maintaining system's gonzo spirit. The license secured one of fantasy literature's most prestigious properties.

Goodman Games maintained steady release schedule across multiple product lines. Annual DCC releases, Fifth Edition Fantasy modules, Original Adventures Reincarnated volumes, and various special projects created diverse revenue streams. The company avoided overextension while maintaining market presence.

Convention presence became important for community building. Gen Con events—especially DCC tournaments using specially created adventures—built community and showcased products. The distinctive DCC aesthetic (purple trade dress, Russ Nicholson art, gonzo themes) created recognizable brand identity at conventions.

The community embraced DCC enthusiastically. Online forums, actual play streams, fan sites, and third-party publishers created vibrant ecosystem. Goodman Games supported this through open licensing allowing third-party DCC content, similar to OGL approach enabling broader community involvement.

Joseph Goodman's personal involvement remained significant. His creative vision and business leadership provided continuity across company history. The relatively focused product line reflected deliberate choices about sustainable growth over empire-building.

Today, Goodman Games operates as established publisher with signature game system, diverse product portfolio, and devoted community. The journey from d20 supplement publisher to OSR leader demonstrates how understanding audience, maintaining quality, and creative innovation can build lasting success.

## Business Model and Philosophy

Goodman Games developed focused approach emphasizing quality and audience understanding:

**Quality Over Quantity**: Rather than flooding market with mediocre products, Goodman Games maintains selective release schedule. Each product receives substantial development, extensive playtesting, professional production, and careful quality control. This creates reputation justifying premium prices and building customer trust.

**Old-School Audience Focus**: The company identified and served audience wanting challenging, dangerous, exploration-focused gaming. While mainstream D&D evolved toward different priorities, Goodman Games maintained focus on particular play style, building loyal customer base.

**Distinctive Visual Identity**: The purple trade dress, painted fantasy art, bold fonts, and consistent aesthetic create instantly recognizable products. This branding builds collectible series identity and market visibility.

**Gonzo Sword-and-Sorcery Tone**: Embracing Appendix N literature over high fantasy creates distinctive tonal identity. Weird magic, morally ambiguous characters, dangerous worlds, and fantastical elements distinguish DCC from Tolkien-derived fantasy.

**Innovative Mechanics with Familiar Foundation**: DCC RPG feels simultaneously fresh and familiar—recognizable fantasy RPG structure with innovative subsystems. This balance attracts veterans seeking novelty without completely abandoning familiar frameworks.

**Community Engagement**: Tournament adventures, convention presence, organized play support, and engagement with fan community build relationships. Third-party publisher licensing creates ecosystem beyond company's direct output.

**Historical Preservation**: Original Adventures Reincarnated series preserves RPG history, introducing classics to new audiences while providing historical context. This demonstrates reverence for gaming heritage.

**Licensing Selectivity**: When pursuing licenses (Lankhmar, Dying Earth), Goodman targets properties matching company aesthetic and audience interests rather than just commercially popular franchises.

**Multi-Edition Support**: Publishing both DCC original content and 5E-compatible Fifth Edition Fantasy serves diverse markets without abandoning core identity. This pragmatic approach balances creative vision with commercial reality.

**Hybrid Distribution**: Combining traditional distribution through game stores, direct sales, PDF distribution, and Kickstarter campaigns creates diverse revenue streams while serving different customer preferences.

**Conservative Financial Management**: The company avoids overextension, maintains sustainable release schedules, and operates within means. This conservative approach enabled survival through industry downturns that killed competitors.

**Playtest Emphasis**: Extensive playtesting before publication ensures products work as intended. This quality control reflects commitment to functional products over rushed releases.

This philosophy creates sustainable business serving specific audience exceptionally well rather than attempting broad appeal with mediocre execution.

## Major Releases

### Dungeon Crawl Classics RPG (2012)

Dungeon Crawl Classics RPG represented Joseph Goodman's vision of old-school fantasy gaming with modern design sensibilities. The game sought capturing early D&D's spirit—dangerous dungeons, mysterious magic, high mortality, and emergent storytelling—while incorporating innovative mechanics impossible or impractical in 1970s.

The core resolution mechanic—roll die, add modifiers, meet target number—remained familiar to D&D players. However, the dice chain innovation created distinctive feel. Rather than adding numbers to d20 roll, bonuses/penalties shifted die type along chain: d3, d4, d5, d6, d7, d8, d10, d12, d14, d16, d20, d24, d30. A +3 bonus changed d20 roll to d24; -2 penalty shifted d20 to d16. This created visceral, swingy resolution with dramatic variance.

The dice chain required unusual dice (d5, d7, d14, d16, d24, d30), creating barrier to entry but also distinctive ritual. Rolling weird dice made DCC feel special, different from standard RPGs. The unusual dice became brand identity—DCC dice sets with complete chains became desired accessories.

Zero-level character funnels started campaigns with peasant characters. Each player controlled 3-4 randomly generated level-0 characters with basic occupations—farmer, blacksmith, fortune teller, grave digger. The initial adventure killed most characters, with survivors becoming level 1 adventurers. This approach created several benefits:

Players became invested in survivors who earned advancement through actual play rather than just point allocation. Multiple characters enabled experimentation and risk-taking impossible with single precious character. Random generation removed optimization focus, encouraging creative problem-solving. Character death felt acceptable—losing one of four peasants mattered less than losing sole campaign character. Emergent stories arose from unexpected survivor combinations.

The funnel structure became DCC signature, distinguishing it from games starting with competent heroes. Many modules explicitly designed as funnels, featuring brutal challenges and high lethality. Surviving the funnel created visceral accomplishment.

Magic operated through spell tables rather than automatic effects. Wizards rolling spells consulted tables with results varying by roll. Low rolls produced minimal effects or failures; high rolls created spectacular outcomes; critical failures caused corruption, patron taint, or worse. This made magic unpredictable and dangerous.

The corruption mechanics marked wizards permanently. Failed spells might cause physical mutations, personality changes, or supernatural stigmata. Over time, wizards accumulated visible signs of reality manipulation. This created visual and mechanical differentiation from mundane classes while emphasizing magic's costs.

Clerics functioned through divine appeals rather than spell selection. Rather than preparing spell lists, clerics appealed to deities for aid. Success depended on deity approval, disapproval risked divine disfavor. Lay on Hands healing worked through faith rather than guaranteed effect. This made clerics feel spiritually connected rather than just divine-flavored wizards.

Warriors gained Mighty Deeds—attempting cinematic combat actions beyond basic attacks. Describing impressive deed and rolling attack, warriors could simultaneously deal damage and achieve special effect (disarm, trip, blind, etc.). This encouraged creative combat description over mechanical optimization.

Each class felt mechanically and thematically distinctive rather than just different power sources. Wizards courted corruption for arcane power. Clerics relied on divine favor. Warriors performed heroic deeds. Thieves relied on luck. Elves connected to magic differently than humans. Dwarves' inherent toughness manifested mechanically.

The patron bond system let wizards ally with otherworldly entities for power. Demons, elemental lords, Great Old Ones, or other patrons granted unique spells and abilities in exchange for service. This created Faustian bargains driving stories while mechanically differentiating wizards.

The game's tone embraced gonzo sword-and-sorcery. Appendix N literature—Fritz Leiber's Lankhmar, Michael Moorcock's Elric, Jack Vance's Dying Earth, Robert E. Howard's Conan—provided inspiration. This created grittier, weirder fantasy than Tolkien-derived high fantasy. Moral ambiguity, strange magic, dangerous quests for survival rather than world-saving heroism characterized adventures.

The rulebook's production quality matched content ambition. The massive hardcover featured Russ Nicholson's evocative art, comprehensive rules, extensive spell descriptions, monster entries, and Goodman's distinctive voice throughout. The writing balanced mechanical clarity with atmospheric flavor.

The game spawned extensive adventure line. Modules like Sailors on the Starless Sea, The Chained Coffin, Intrigue at the Court of Chaos, and dozens more provided ready-to-play content. Third-party publishers created additional adventures and supplements, expanding ecosystem.

DCC RPG achieved remarkable success critically and commercially. The game won ENnie Awards, built devoted community, and established Goodman Games as major publisher. The success validated gamble on original system while proving old-school aesthetics could combine productively with innovative mechanics.

### Original Adventures Reincarnated: Into the Borderlands (2018)

The Original Adventures Reincarnated series preserved classic D&D modules while making them accessible to contemporary audiences. Into the Borderlands combined two Gary Gygax classics—B1: In Search of the Unknown and B2: The Keep on the Borderlands—in deluxe hardcover format.

The book included complete original modules with facsimile reproduction—original text, maps, art, and even typeface. This preserved historical artifacts exactly as published, allowing modern audiences to experience classics authentically. The production quality exceeded original printings while maintaining fidelity.

Alongside original content, the book provided 5th Edition conversions. Every encounter, trap, treasure, and NPC received modern statistics compatible with current D&D edition. This allowed playing classics with contemporary rules without requiring conversion work.

The historical context enriched appreciation. Interviews with original designers, development history, cultural context, and analysis explained why modules mattered and how they influenced RPG development. This scholarly approach treated adventures as culturally significant artifacts worth preserving and studying.

New art supplemented historical illustrations. Contemporary artists created additional visual material while respecting original aesthetic. This balanced historical preservation with modern presentation expectations.

The Keep on the Borderlands held particular significance as one of D&D's most influential modules. Included in Basic Set for years, it introduced countless players to RPGs. The sandbox structure—home base (Keep) with nearby adventure locations (Caves of Chaos)—influenced adventure design for decades. Making this accessible to modern audiences preserved critical gaming history.

The production quality justified premium price. The hardcover book exceeded 300 pages with extensive content beyond just adventures—essays, interviews, historical context, beautiful production. This created desirable object for collectors and historians beyond functional game product.

Subsequent volumes preserved other classics: Temple of Elemental Evil, Expedition to the Barrier Peaks, Isle of Dread, and others. Each received similar treatment—original text, 5E conversion, historical context, deluxe production. The series became important preservation project alongside commercial product line.

The series served multiple purposes: preserved gaming history, introduced classics to new audiences, demonstrated evolution of adventure design, and provided playable content for modern groups. This multifaceted value justified Goodman's investment in historical preservation.

### Mutant Crawl Classics (2017)

Mutant Crawl Classics adapted DCC RPG mechanics to post-apocalyptic science fantasy inspired by Gamma World, Metamorphosis Alpha, and similar gonzo post-apocalyptic games. The game demonstrated system flexibility while serving audience nostalgic for particular science-fantasy aesthetic.

The setting presented far-future Earth after "the Great Disaster"—technological civilization collapsed, survivors reverted to primitive tribalism, and dangerous mutants, ancient technology, and environmental hazards filled world. Plantients (intelligent plants), manimals (uplifted animals), mutants, and pure-strain humans explored Terra A.D. (After Disaster) seeking survival and understanding lost ancients.

The core mechanics directly adapted DCC structure. Dice chain created swingy resolution. Zero-level funnels started campaigns with tribal primitives, most dying before survivors reached level 1. Character death remained common, encouraging creative problem-solving over mechanical optimization.

Technology replaced magic thematically while functioning similarly mechanically. Ancient artifacts operated through tech tables—rolling to activate produced varying results from catastrophic malfunction to spectacular success. This captured mystery and danger of incomprehensible ancient technology.

Mutations provided character differentiation. Physical mutations altered appearance and granted abilities. Mental mutations provided psychic powers. Plantient and manimal characters featured species-specific mutations. Rolling random mutations during character creation created unpredictable combinations similar to DCC's random zero-level generation.

The Patron AI system adapted DCC's patron bonds. Artificial intelligences from ancient world granted knowledge and abilities to followers. These relationships paralleled wizard-patron bonds while fitting science-fantasy context.

The aesthetic embraced gonzo weirdness—intelligent plants, psychic animals, impossible technology, environmental dangers, and moral ambiguity. This tone matched inspirational material's spirit while allowing creative freedom.

The game served multiple audiences: DCC fans wanting familiar mechanics in new setting, Gamma World veterans seeking modern implementation of beloved game, and post-apocalyptic science-fantasy enthusiasts. This positioned MCC strategically within gaming market.

The production matched DCC quality standards. The core rulebook featured Jim Holloway's distinctive art (known from original Gamma World), comprehensive rules, extensive equipment and mutation lists, and ready-to-play content. The purple trade dress and visual identity connected it clearly to DCC line.

Supplements explored setting further—adventures, equipment guides, mutation compendiums, and setting material. The line never achieved DCC RPG's prominence but maintained steady support for dedicated audience.

MCC demonstrated Goodman Games' ability to leverage successful mechanics across genres while maintaining quality and thematic coherence. The game showed DCC system's flexibility beyond just fantasy application.

### DCC Lankhmar (2018)

DCC Lankhmar licensed Fritz Leiber's iconic sword-and-sorcery setting, bringing Fafhrd and the Gray Mouser's adventures to DCC mechanics. This represented Goodman's most prestigious literary license, securing one of fantasy literature's foundational works.

Lankhmar—grimy city of thieves, corrupt nobles, mysterious sorcerers, and gutter-level survival—perfectly matched DCC's gonzo sword-and-sorcery aesthetic. Leiber's influence on Appendix N literature made Lankhmar natural fit for game explicitly drawing from those sources.

The game modified DCC rules for urban intrigue focus. While core mechanics remained—dice chain, spell tables, corruption—new subsystems supported thievery, patron manipulation, urban adventure, and street-level survival. This demonstrated system's adaptability while respecting setting specifics.

The Fleeting Luck mechanic captured Leiber's protagonists' tendency toward narrow escapes and last-minute fortune. Characters gained and spent Luck dramatically, creating swingy outcomes matching source material's tone. This encouraged risk-taking and bold action.

Character creation focused on thieves, rogues, and scoundrels fitting Lankhmar's underworld setting. While warriors, wizards, and clerics remained viable, thief-focused mechanics received extensive development. This specialized approach served setting rather than forcing generic fantasy assumptions.

The book included extensive Lankhmar details—city districts, notable NPCs, factions, cultural information, and adventure hooks. This provided comprehensive introduction to setting while respecting Leiber's vision. Long-time fans found faithful adaptation; newcomers received accessible entry point.

Ready-to-play adventures showcased setting and mechanics. These scenarios captured Leiber's tone—gutter-level survival, corrupt power structures, supernatural threats, and protagonists' roguish charm. The adventures demonstrated how to run Lankhmar campaigns successfully.

The production quality honored prestigious license. Beautiful art, comprehensive content, and careful adaptation respected source material while creating functional game product. The book served both Leiber fans and DCC players, bridging literary and gaming audiences.

The license demonstrated Goodman's literary credibility and ability to secure prestigious properties. Leiber's work influenced D&D's creation; bringing it full circle to modern old-school game represented meaningful cultural moment.

## Impact on Industry

Goodman Games influenced contemporary gaming significantly:

**Old School Renaissance Leadership**: DCC RPG became one of OSR's most successful and distinctive games, demonstrating that old-school aesthetics could combine productively with innovative mechanics. The game influenced subsequent OSR design.

**Dice Chain Innovation**: The dice chain mechanic influenced other games' mechanical innovations, showing how simple changes create distinctive feel. Unusual dice became DCC signature and influenced other games' physical components thinking.

**Zero-Level Funnel Popularity**: The funnel approach influenced numerous other games adopting similar structures. Starting with expendable characters became recognized play style beyond just DCC.

**Gonzo Fantasy Aesthetic**: DCC helped popularize gonzo sword-and-sorcery over high fantasy in OSR community. Appendix N literature gained renewed appreciation and influence.

**Dangerous Magic Systems**: DCC's unpredictable magic with corruption influenced other games' magic systems, demonstrating that wild, risky magic could be more interesting than reliable spell effects.

**Historical Preservation**: Original Adventures Reincarnated series influenced industry approach to classic modules, demonstrating market for deluxe historical preservation alongside functional reprints.

**Quality Third-Party Publishing**: During d20 era, Goodman's quality adventures set standards for third-party publishers, influencing expectations for non-official content.

**Convention Tournament Model**: DCC tournaments at Gen Con and other conventions created community-building model other games adopted.

**Successful Original System Launch**: DCC RPG demonstrated that established supplement publisher could successfully create original system, influencing other publishers' strategic thinking.

**Appendix N Revival**: The game sparked renewed interest in Appendix N literature, influencing gaming culture's literary awareness and diversifying beyond Tolkien-centric fantasy.

## Current Status and Future

Goodman Games continues operating successfully as established publisher:

**Financial Health**: Consistent releases across multiple product lines, successful Kickstarter campaigns, and sustained market presence indicate stable financial position. Conservative management enables sustainable operations.

**Active Product Lines**: Ongoing DCC RPG support (adventures, sourcebooks, supplements), Fifth Edition Fantasy modules, Original Adventures Reincarnated volumes, Mutant Crawl Classics support, and various special projects.

**Community Strength**: Devoted DCC community, active online presence, convention participation, and third-party publisher ecosystem demonstrate healthy engagement beyond just sales.

**Market Position**: Recognized as major OSR publisher and significant 5E third-party content creator. The company occupies distinctive niche serving specific audience exceptionally well.

**Leadership Continuity**: Joseph Goodman continues directing company with consistent creative vision across history.

**Distribution**: Strong relationships with game stores, online retailers, direct sales, and digital distribution through DriveThruRPG create diverse revenue streams.

**Future Prospects**: Strong position for continued operation through established brand identity, loyal audience, proven products, and sustainable business model. Challenges include maintaining innovation while preserving core identity and navigating evolving RPG market dynamics.
