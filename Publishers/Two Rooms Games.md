## Company History

Two Rooms Games emerged as UK independent publisher championing accessibility, diverse creator voices, and inclusive design practices within indie roleplaying game publishing. Founded by Banana Chan and Grant Howitt, the company built reputation for games centering marginalized perspectives, implementing accessibility features thoughtfully, and demonstrating commitment to inclusive gaming extending beyond token representation to structural design choices and business practices. The company's journey represents conscious effort to address indie gaming's diversity and accessibility challenges through publisher-level advocacy combined with quality game production.

Banana Chan and Grant Howitt brought complementary perspectives and existing reputations to partnership. Chan's background in accessibility advocacy, game design, and community organizing combined with Howitt's established indie gaming credentials through Rowan, Rook and Decard created partnership balancing idealism with practical publishing experience.

The company name "Two Rooms Games" evoked both partnership structure and physical gaming spaces while suggesting intimacy and accessibility—games for small groups in comfortable settings rather than massive convention halls or commercial gaming spaces.

Two Rooms' founding in 2019 centered on creating publisher actively addressing accessibility and diversity rather than treating these as afterthoughts. The company established accessibility standards for releases, prioritized diverse creators, and implemented business practices supporting marginalized game designers systematically.

Jiangshi: Blood in the Banquet Hall (2021), designed by Banana Chan, demonstrated company's commitment to centering marginalized cultural perspectives. This game explored Asian diaspora experiences through Chinese vampire (jiangshi) framework, creating space for examining cultural identity, family expectations, and immigrant experiences through horror-comedy lens.

The game's cultural specificity proved crucial strength. Rather than generic vampire game with superficial Asian aesthetics, Jiangshi engaged meaningfully with Chinese cultural traditions, diaspora experiences, and generational conflicts. This authentic cultural grounding created resonance with Asian players while educating others about experiences outside mainstream gaming's typically Western focus.

Mechanically, the game combined light party game elements with meaningful character development. Players portrayed family members dealing with jiangshi threat while navigating interpersonal relationships and cultural expectations. This balance created accessible gameplay supporting thematic depth.

The game's reception demonstrated appetite for culturally-specific games centering marginalized perspectives. Rather than limiting market through specificity, authentic cultural engagement expanded audiences to players hungry for representation and fresh perspectives.

Beyond original publishing, Two Rooms distributed and promoted games from diverse creators, using publisher platform to amplify marginalized voices. This curatorial role proved important for visibility—publishers' distribution and marketing resources could elevate games that might otherwise remain obscure despite quality.

The company's accessibility standards represented systematic commitment rather than occasional effort. Games included content warnings, safety tools discussion, accessibility considerations in layout and format, and guidance for inclusive play. These standards influenced other publishers adopting similar practices.

Banana Chan's accessibility advocacy extended beyond Two Rooms through broader gaming community engagement, writing about inclusive design, and consulting on accessibility issues. This thought leadership positioned Two Rooms as authority on accessibility in indie gaming.

Flotsam (2020) demonstrated company's range beyond single creator or cultural perspective. This game about gig economy workers surviving in near-future economic precarity explored class issues and economic marginalization through accessible framework. The game showed Two Rooms' commitment to marginalized perspectives extended beyond cultural representation to class and economic justice themes.

The company's business model prioritized creator-friendly practices—fair revenue sharing, transparent communication, support for marginalized designers navigating industry. This ethical approach built reputation attracting diverse creators seeking publishers aligned with their values.

Community engagement proved central to company identity. Rather than just commercial entity, Two Rooms positioned itself as community advocate using publisher platform for broader movement-building around accessibility and inclusion. This activism distinguished company from purely commercial publishers.

The company's UK base provided perspective outside American indie gaming's dominance while connecting to British indie scene's growing international presence. This positioning enabled contributing to both UK gaming culture and broader international indie movement.

While Two Rooms' commercial scale remained modest compared to larger indie publishers, cultural impact exceeded size through advocacy, accessibility standards, and centering marginalized voices. The company demonstrated that publishers could prioritize ethics and inclusion while maintaining quality and sustainability.

Today, Two Rooms Games continues operating as advocate for accessibility and diverse creator voices within indie gaming, demonstrating how publishers can advance inclusivity through systematic practices beyond just rhetoric.

### Flotsam (2020)

Flotsam explored gig economy workers' survival in near-future economic precarity, addressing class issues and economic marginalization through accessible game framework.

The setting presented future where traditional employment collapsed, replaced by precarious gig work and economic survival struggles. This near-future extrapolation examined contemporary economic issues through speculative lens.

Character struggles centered on economic survival, maintaining dignity under exploitation, and finding solidarity amid precarity. The game explored class issues often neglected in gaming's typically middle-class assumptions.

Flotsam demonstrated Two Rooms' commitment to marginalized perspectives extended beyond cultural representation to economic justice themes.
