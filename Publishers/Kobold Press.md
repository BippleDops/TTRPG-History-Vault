# Kobold Press

**Founded**: 2006
**Active Era**: Modern Era (2006-Present)
**Historical Significance**: ⭐ 4/5
**Headquarters**: Kirkland, Washington
**Status**: Active

## Company History

Kobold Press emerged as premier third-party publisher for Dungeons & Dragons and Pathfinder, earning reputation for exceptional monster design, folklore-inspired dark fantasy settings, and production quality rivaling official products. Founded by industry veteran Wolfgang Baur, the company evolved from modest supplement publisher to creator of comprehensive campaign settings and independent RPG systems. The journey from publishing magazine adventures to developing original game systems demonstrates how focused expertise, community engagement, and creative excellence can build sustainable independent publishing business.

Wolfgang Baur brought extensive RPG credentials to Kobold Press's founding. His career included work at TSR during AD&D 2nd Edition era (designing for Planescape and Al-Qadim), freelance design for multiple publishers, editing Dungeon magazine, and establishing Kobold Quarterly magazine. This experience provided deep industry knowledge, extensive creative network, and understanding of what constituted quality RPG content.

Kobold Press began in 2006 as primarily magazine publisher. Kobold Quarterly magazine featured articles, adventures, interviews, and gaming resources for multiple RPG systems—primarily D&D 3.5, Pathfinder, and later D&D 4th Edition. The magazine built community, showcased quality content, and established Kobold Press as serious publisher rather than amateur operation.

The magazine's open-system approach served diverse gaming communities. Rather than focusing exclusively on single game, Kobold Quarterly provided useful content across systems. This pragmatic approach maximized readership while demonstrating versatility—same design principles and creative approaches worked across different mechanical frameworks.

The transition to Pathfinder support proved strategically important. When Wizards of the Coast released D&D 4th Edition (2008), many fans resisted radical changes, creating market opportunity. Paizo's Pathfinder RPG continued D&D 3.5's mechanical legacy, and substantial community migrated to new system. Kobold Press recognized this shift, providing extensive Pathfinder support through magazine and supplements.

The Open Design model pioneered innovative publishing approach. Wolfgang Baur created patron system where supporters funded projects during development, receiving input on content direction while backing financially. This crowdsourced design predated Kickstarter, creating collaborative development process with community investment. Patrons received finished products while contributing to creation, building engagement beyond simple purchase.

Early Open Design projects produced adventure modules and sourcebooks for Pathfinder and other systems. These collaborative projects tested concepts, built community relationships, and demonstrated market demand before full production commitment. The model reduced financial risk while engaging passionate fans in creative process.

The Midgard Campaign Setting project represented major milestone. This dark fantasy setting drew heavily from European folklore, Norse mythology, Slavic legends, and Central European fairy tales rather than typical Tolkien-derived high fantasy. The rich cultural specificity created distinctive identity distinguishing Midgard from generic fantasy worlds.

The initial Midgard releases came through Open Design patrons, building setting through collaborative development. Geography, pantheons, cultures, and conflicts emerged from patron input shaped by Baur's editorial vision. This grassroots development created community investment in setting's success.

The Midgard Campaign Setting book (2012) consolidated years of development into comprehensive sourcebook. The setting presented Crossroads region connecting various cultural zones—Norse-inspired Northlands, Byzantine-influenced Magocracy of Allain, Slavic Margreve forest, Arabian-themed southern kingdoms, and more. This geographic and cultural diversity enabled varied campaign styles within cohesive framework.

The dark fantasy tone distinguished Midgard from optimistic high fantasy. Dangerous magic corrupted users, political conflicts threatened civilians, darkness literally spread from Shadow Realm, and moral ambiguity complicated heroic action. This sophisticated approach appealed to audiences seeking mature fantasy beyond black-white morality.

Kobold Quarterly magazine ended in 2012, transitioning focus to book publishing. While magazine built community and reputation, economics favored larger projects with longer shelf life. The decision concentrated resources on substantial products rather than quarterly deadlines.

The Kickstarter adoption transformed Kobold Press's business model. Rather than Open Design's patron system, Kickstarter provided broader crowdfunding platform with established infrastructure. Beginning in early 2010s, Kobold Press launched campaigns for Midgard supplements, adventure modules, and monster books.

The Tome of Beasts Kickstarter (2016) became watershed moment. This monster manual for 5th Edition D&D featured over 400 creatures inspired by folklore, mythology, and dark fantasy rather than typical fantasy bestiary tropes. The campaign raised over $100,000, demonstrating passionate community support and market appetite for quality third-party 5E content.

The book's success stemmed from several factors: 5th Edition's popularity creating hungry market for supplements, distinctive folklore-inspired creatures offering fresh alternatives to standard monsters, exceptional art and production quality, and Kobold Press's reputation for design excellence. The creatures featured mechanical innovation and thematic coherence—these weren't mere stat blocks but thoughtfully designed encounters with narrative potential.

Tome of Beasts established Kobold Press as premier monster designer. The book achieved widespread use—DMs incorporated creatures into campaigns, the monster designs influenced other supplements, and the book became go-to resource for fresh creature options. This positioned Kobold Press strategically within 5E third-party market.

The success spawned monster book series. Creature Codex (2018), Tome of Beasts 2 (2020), and Tome of Beasts 3 (2022) expanded creature catalog, each featuring hundreds of new monsters. This comprehensive coverage created near-essential resource for DMs seeking diverse encounters beyond official content.

The Midgard expansion continued through 5E era. Midgard Heroes Handbook (2018) provided player-facing content—new races, subclasses, backgrounds, and character options grounded in setting. Midgard Worldbook (2018) updated campaign setting for 5E. Southlands (2015) expanded setting southward into Arabian-inspired territories. Courts of the Shadow Fey and other major supplements built comprehensive campaign setting rivaling Forgotten Realms or Eberron in depth.

The adventure design received particular acclaim. Kobold Press adventures featured strong narratives, interesting encounters, political intrigue, moral complexity, and production quality matching official products. Rather than simple dungeon crawls, adventures presented sophisticated scenarios requiring thought, investigation, and meaningful choices.

Tales of the Old Margreve (2020) exemplified this approach—anthology of adventures set in haunted Slavic-inspired forest. The scenarios combined folklore horror, dark fairy tales, political conflict, and moral ambiguity. The adventures worked standalone or as connected campaign, demonstrating design sophistication.

The community engagement remained central to operations. Kobold Press maintained active social media, regular blog posts, responsive customer service, and genuine appreciation for supporters. This authentic engagement built loyalty beyond transactional relationships.

The design excellence attracted top talent. Wolfgang Baur's industry reputation helped recruit exceptional designers, artists, and editors. Products featured contributions from industry veterans and rising talents, creating quality consistently exceeding typical third-party supplements.

The OGL crisis of 2023 created existential challenge. Wizards of the Coast attempted modifying Open Gaming License, potentially restricting third-party publishers' ability to create D&D-compatible content. This threatened Kobold Press's business model built largely on 5E supplements.

The community backlash forced Wizards to retreat, but damage was done—trust eroded, and reliance on potentially revocable license proved dangerous. Kobold Press responded strategically: committing to developing independent RPG system not dependent on Wizards' permission.

Project Black Flag (later renamed Tales of the Valiant) began as Kobold Press's answer to OGL uncertainty. Rather than merely creating OGL alternative, the project aimed at producing standalone 5E-compatible system with distinct identity. The development involved community input, extensive playtesting, and careful balancing of compatibility with innovation.

The Tales of the Valiant Kickstarter (2023) raised over $4.6 million from nearly 25,000 backers—one of tabletop RPG industry's most successful campaigns. The massive support demonstrated community desire for 5E alternative, trust in Kobold Press's design capabilities, and market appetite for quality fantasy gaming independent of Wizards.

The system maintained 5E compatibility while introducing refinements: rebalanced classes, streamlined subsystems, refined mechanics based on years of 5E play experience. This positioned Tales of the Valiant as both familiar to 5E players and mechanically improved based on community feedback.

The successful launch transformed Kobold Press from third-party supplement publisher to independent RPG publisher competing directly in core rulebook market. This represented enormous strategic shift with corresponding risks and opportunities.

Today, Kobold Press operates as established publisher with diverse product lines: Tales of the Valiant core system, extensive 5E supplements, Midgard campaign setting, monster books, adventures, and resources. The company occupies unique position—respected third-party publisher that successfully transitioned to creating competing system.

## Business Model and Philosophy

Kobold Press developed sophisticated approach balancing third-party publishing with original development:

**Design Excellence Focus**: The company prioritizes exceptional design quality over rushed publication. Products receive extensive development, thorough playtesting, professional editing, and design refinement. This creates reputation justifying premium pricing and building customer trust.

**Folklore and Mythology Inspiration**: Rather than generic fantasy tropes, Kobold Press draws from European folklore, world mythology, and cultural traditions. This creates distinctive identity while providing rich narrative and mechanical inspiration.

**Monster Design Expertise**: Specializing in creature design created market differentiation. The comprehensive monster books position Kobold Press as essential supplement publisher for DMs seeking diverse encounters.

**Dark Fantasy Aesthetic**: Midgard and other properties embrace darker, morally complex fantasy over optimistic high fantasy. This sophisticated tone appeals to mature audiences seeking nuanced narratives.

**Community Collaboration**: From Open Design patrons through Kickstarter backers to social media engagement, Kobold Press treats community as partners. This builds investment beyond mere customer relationships.

**Crowdfunding Primary Model**: Kickstarter campaigns fund major projects, providing upfront capital, market validation, and community building. Consistent delivery record maintains backer trust essential for continued success.

**Premium Production Values**: Professional art, quality printing, sophisticated layout design, and careful editing create products rivaling or exceeding official publications. This quality justifies premium pricing while creating desirable objects.

**Strategic Adaptation**: The transition from Pathfinder to 5E support, then to independent system development, demonstrates strategic flexibility responding to market conditions and threats.

**Comprehensive World-Building**: Midgard receives sustained development creating deep, coherent campaign setting. This comprehensive approach builds value beyond individual supplements.

**Multi-System Versatility**: Experience creating content for D&D 3.5, Pathfinder, 4E, and 5E demonstrates mechanical versatility and broad gaming knowledge.

**Talent Recruitment**: Wolfgang Baur's industry reputation attracts exceptional designers, creating consistent quality across projects.

**Risk Management**: The shift toward independent system development reduces dependency on potentially revocable licenses, managing existential business risk.

This philosophy creates sustainable business producing exceptional quality while maintaining independence and community connection.

## Major Releases

### Tome of Beasts (2016)

Tome of Beasts established Kobold Press as premier monster designer through over 400 creatures inspired by folklore, mythology, and dark fantasy. The book addressed perceived gap in 5E monster options—while official Monster Manual covered classics, room existed for fresh creatures beyond standard repertoire.

The folklore inspiration distinguished Tome of Beasts from generic monster compilations. Rather than slight variations on standard creatures, the book featured beings from world mythology and European folklore: alkonost (Slavic bird-women), ala (storm demons), bukavac (Balkan swamp horrors), and dozens of culturally specific creatures rarely appearing in fantasy gaming.

The mechanical design demonstrated sophistication beyond simple stat blocks. Each creature featured interesting abilities, tactical considerations, and narrative potential. The designs encouraged creative encounter building beyond straightforward combat—creatures had motivations, ecological niches, and story hooks.

The Challenge Rating range served diverse needs. Low-CR creatures provided options for early adventures; mid-range threats suited typical adventuring; high-CR monsters challenged powerful parties. This comprehensive coverage made book useful across campaign levels.

The thematic organization grouped related creatures, facilitating encounter and adventure design. Fey creatures appeared together, enabling Feywild adventures. Demons and devils received extensive coverage. Dragon variants explored alternatives to standard chromatic/metallic dichotomy.

The production quality rivaled official products. Beautiful full-color art brought creatures to life. Clear stat blocks, professional layout, and useful organization created functional reference. The physical book served both practical tool and attractive collectible.

The lore sections provided narrative context. Beyond mechanics, creatures received descriptions of behavior, ecology, society, and narrative roles. This helped DMs use creatures meaningfully rather than as mere combat encounters.

The success proved transformative for Kobold Press. The book became near-essential resource for 5E DMs, building reputation and revenue enabling subsequent projects. The creature designs appeared across gaming communities, influencing how DMs approached encounter design.

The spawn of sequels demonstrated ongoing demand. Creature Codex, Tome of Beasts 2, and Tome of Beasts 3 expanded catalog, eventually featuring well over 1000 creatures. This comprehensive coverage created definitive third-party monster resource.

The cultural impact extended beyond sales. Tome of Beasts demonstrated third-party publishers could create essential resources rivaling official content in quality and utility. The folklore focus encouraged other publishers to explore non-standard inspirational sources.

### Midgard Campaign Setting (2012-present)

Midgard Campaign Setting represented Wolfgang Baur's vision of dark fantasy world grounded in European folklore rather than Tolkien-derived high fantasy. The setting drew from Norse mythology, Slavic legends, Germanic fairy tales, Arabian Nights, and diverse cultural traditions creating rich, distinctive world.

The Crossroads geography organized diverse regions coherently. The central location enabled characters to explore Norse-inspired Northlands, Byzantine-influenced Magocracy, Slavic Margreve forest, Arabian southern kingdoms, or other cultural zones. This diversity supported varied campaign styles within unified setting.

The dark fantasy tone distinguished Midgard from optimistic alternatives. Magic corrupted users through shadow taint, political conflicts endangered civilians, ancient evils threatened civilization, and moral ambiguity complicated heroic action. This sophisticated approach created mature setting beyond simple good-versus-evil narratives.

The gods and religions reflected cultural inspirations. Norse-inspired pantheon dominated Northlands, Slavic deities influenced Margreve, unique religious systems appeared in each region. Rather than generic fantasy pantheon, religions felt culturally grounded and thematically appropriate.

The political complexity created adventure opportunities. City-states competed, kingdoms jockeyed for power, ancient empires declined, and new forces emerged. This dynamic political landscape provided intrigue, conflict, and meaningful stakes beyond dungeon crawling.

The magic system emphasized danger and corruption. Shadow magic offered power at cost of potential corruption. Ley lines created geographic magical significance. Rune magic provided alternative spellcasting tradition. These magical variations created mechanical and narrative interest.

The races included distinctive options beyond standard fantasy species. Ravenfolk (intelligent ravens), kobolds (presented as playable rather than mere cannon fodder), minotaurs, centaurs, and other unusual options created diverse character possibilities.

The deep magic concepts explored sophisticated magical theory. Rather than simple spell lists, the setting developed magical philosophies, competing traditions, and theoretical frameworks. This depth appealed to players interested in magic beyond mechanical effects.

The comprehensive development built setting depth rivaling major official campaign worlds. The Midgard Worldbook, Heroes Handbook, regional supplements, adventure collections, and numerous sourcebooks created thoroughly developed setting supporting extended campaigns.

The adventures integrated setting meaningfully. Tales of the Old Margreve, Courts of the Shadow Fey, and other adventure collections didn't just use Midgard as backdrop but engaged setting elements—politics, religions, cultures, and conflicts drove narratives.

The community support demonstrated devotion. Many groups ran extended Midgard campaigns, third-party creators developed additional content, and setting developed passionate fanbase. This community investment built sustainable market for continued development.

The 5E conversion expanded accessibility. While originally developed for Pathfinder, the comprehensive 5E Midgard products introduced setting to larger audience, building recognition and market presence.

The setting demonstrated that carefully developed third-party campaign worlds could compete with official settings in depth, quality, and player devotion. Midgard proved viable alternative to Forgotten Realms or Greyhawk for groups seeking distinctive setting.

### Tales of the Valiant (2024)

Tales of the Valiant (originally Project Black Flag) represented Kobold Press's strategic response to OGL crisis—creating independent 5E-compatible core RPG system not dependent on Wizards of the Coast's potentially revocable license. The project aimed at producing familiar-but-improved fantasy gaming experience.

The development emerged from existential threat. The OGL 1.1 controversy demonstrated reliance on Wizards' license created dangerous dependency. While community backlash forced retreat, trust eroded. Kobold Press recognized need for independent alternative.

The design goals balanced compatibility with improvement. The system needed feeling familiar to 5E players—easing transition and enabling content compatibility—while incorporating refinements based on years of 5E play experience and community feedback.

The core mechanics maintained d20 resolution, ability scores, proficiency bonuses, and familiar structures. Players accustomed to 5E could transition smoothly without completely relearning game. This compatibility proved essential for market success.

The class design refined 5E's options based on community feedback. Underperforming features received improvements, overpowered elements faced rebalancing, and gaps in options got filled. The result maintained class archetypes while addressing known issues.

The lineage and heritage system replaced traditional race mechanics, reflecting contemporary design thinking about character ancestry versus culture. This approached diversity more sophisticatedly than simple racial stat blocks.

The magic system maintained spell slot structure while refining problematic spells and introducing new options. The goal balanced familiar spellcasting with mechanical improvements addressing community critiques.

The monster design leveraged Kobold Press's expertise. Rather than just converting existing creatures, the system featured refined stat blocks, interesting abilities, and tactical depth drawing on years of monster design experience.

The GM tools reflected Wolfgang Baur's extensive experience. Advice on pacing, encounter design, campaign development, and world-building drew from industry veteran's deep knowledge.

The Kickstarter campaign raised over $4.6 million from nearly 25,000 backers—massive success demonstrating community trust in Kobold Press and desire for 5E alternative. The campaign positioned Tales of the Valiant as legitimate competitor in core rulebook market.

The development involved extensive community input. Playtesting, surveys, feedback incorporation, and transparent communication built community investment in success. This collaborative approach created product reflecting player desires.

The strategic significance transformed Kobold Press from supplement publisher to system publisher competing directly in core game market. This represented enormous business evolution with corresponding opportunities and risks.

The compatibility approach enabled existing 5E content use—both official and third-party. This reduced transition friction while enabling Kobold Press's extensive 5E supplement catalog to remain relevant.

The success demonstrated market appetite for quality 5E alternatives. Rather than monopoly, D&D market could support competing compatible systems with distinct identities.

### Creature Codex (2018)

Creature Codex continued Tome of Beasts' success, providing additional 400+ creatures expanding Kobold Press's comprehensive monster coverage. The book maintained folklore inspiration while exploring new thematic territories.

The fey creatures received extensive development. Various courts, seasonal alignments, and creature types created comprehensive fey resource beyond scattered official content. This enabled sustained Feywild campaigns with diverse encounters.

The undead section explored creative variations beyond standard zombies and skeletons. The creatures featured interesting abilities, narrative hooks, and tactical considerations making undead encounters mechanically and thematically interesting.

The elemental and construct creatures provided alternatives to standard elementals. These beings featured unique abilities reflecting specific elemental combinations or unusual construction methods.

The demon and devil expansions offered fiendish options beyond Monster Manual coverage. These creatures enabled extended Lower Planes adventures with diverse infernal encounters.

The dragon variants continued exploring alternatives to standard dragons. These creatures maintained draconic themes while offering fresh mechanical and narrative options.

The production quality maintained standards established by Tome of Beasts. Beautiful art, professional layout, clear stat blocks, and useful organization created functional reference.

The success demonstrated sustainable market for quality monster books. The consistent demand across multiple volumes proved DMs valued diverse creature options supporting creative encounter design.

## Impact on Industry

Kobold Press influenced contemporary gaming significantly:

**Third-Party Quality Standards**: Consistently excellent products demonstrated third-party publishers could match or exceed official content quality, raising industry expectations.

**Monster Design Excellence**: The comprehensive creature catalogs influenced how designers approached monster creation, emphasizing folklore inspiration and mechanical interest.

**Folklore Fantasy**: Midgard and creature books encouraged industry exploration of diverse cultural mythology beyond standard Tolkien-derived fantasy.

**Dark Fantasy Viability**: Demonstrated commercial appetite for morally complex, darker fantasy over typical heroic high fantasy.

**Crowdfunding Third-Party Publishing**: Successful Kickstarter campaigns proved viability of crowdfunding major third-party projects, influencing publisher business models.

**Community Collaboration**: Open Design and subsequent community engagement demonstrated benefits of treating audience as creative partners.

**5E Third-Party Market**: Kobold Press helped establish robust third-party 5E supplement market, demonstrating opportunities beyond official content.

**OGL Alternative Development**: Tales of the Valiant influenced industry response to OGL crisis, demonstrating feasibility of compatible independent systems.

**Campaign Setting Development**: Midgard proved third-party settings could achieve depth and quality rivaling official campaign worlds.

**Professional Production Values**: Consistent premium quality raised independent publisher presentation standards.

## Current Status and Future

Kobold Press continues operating successfully with expanded focus:

**Financial Health**: Successful Tales of the Valiant launch, continued supplement sales, and strong crowdfunding record indicate robust financial position.

**Dual Product Lines**: Supporting both Tales of the Valiant core system and 5E supplements creates diverse revenue streams and market positions.

**Market Position**: Recognized as premier third-party D&D publisher and emerging independent system publisher.

**Tales of the Valiant Development**: Significant resources supporting new system through core books, supplements, adventures, and ecosystem development.

**Ongoing Midgard Support**: Continued setting development across multiple game systems.

**Community Strength**: Devoted fanbase, successful crowdfunding, and active engagement demonstrate healthy community relationships.

**Leadership Continuity**: Wolfgang Baur continues creative direction with consistent vision.

**Challenges**: Balancing Tales of the Valiant development with existing 5E business, competing against D&D in core system market, and managing expanded operations.

**Future Prospects**: Strong position through successful system launch, proven design capabilities, devoted community, and diversified product lines. Risks include system market competition and managing operational scaling.
