# Rowan, Rook and Decard

**Founded**: 2014
**Active Era**: Modern Era (2014-Present)
**Historical Significance**: ⭐ 4/5
**Headquarters**: London, United Kingdom
**Status**: Active

## Company History

Rowan, Rook and Decard emerged as leading voice in UK independent roleplaying game publishing, demonstrating how British sensibilities—dark humor, political awareness, urban decay aesthetics, and working-class perspectives—could create distinctive contribution to indie gaming dominated by North American publishers. Founded by Grant Howitt and Chris Taylor, the company built reputation for evocative dark fantasy settings, innovative Resistance system mechanics, and remarkably successful one-page RPG format that proved experimental design could achieve viral mainstream success. The company's journey from publishing small games through creating Spire and Heart—critically acclaimed dark fantasy games about revolution and ruin—represents British indie gaming's maturation into internationally recognized creative force.

Grant Howitt and Chris Taylor brought complementary skills to partnership. Howitt's background in journalism, comedy writing, and game design combined with Taylor's illustration, graphic design, and worldbuilding talents created complete creative package. Together they could design mechanics, develop settings, write compelling text, and create gorgeous visual presentations without relying on extensive freelance teams.

The company name "Rowan, Rook and Decard" evoked both British pub names and mysterious conspiratorial organizations—perfectly capturing the aesthetic territory their games would explore. The name suggested working-class spaces, ancient traditions, and secrets hidden in plain sight—themes pervading their major releases.

Rowan, Rook and Decard's founding in 2014 reflected both creators' desire for creative independence and recognition of opportunities in growing indie gaming market. The UK independent RPG scene remained small compared to American counterparts, creating space for publishers establishing distinctive British identity rather than imitating American indie gaming aesthetics.

Early releases established company identity through small-scale experiments. These initial projects developed design sensibilities, built audience, and refined approaches that would characterize later major releases. However, the company's breakthrough came through unexpected avenue: one-page RPGs.

Honey Heist (2017) presented deceptively simple premise: players portrayed bears attempting to steal honey from HoneyCon while maintaining balance between bear and criminal tendencies. The entire game fit on single page with clear rules, character creation, and premise. The game's combination of absurdist premise, accessible mechanics, and complete package in minimal format proved lightning in bottle.

Honey Heist achieved viral success unprecedented for RPG of any scale. The game spread through social media, appeared in mainstream gaming coverage, became convention staple, and introduced countless non-gamers to roleplaying through immediately accessible premise and minimal rules barrier. The success demonstrated that experimental format and absurdist premise could achieve mainstream penetration.

More importantly for Rowan, Rook and Decard's development, Honey Heist's success provided financial resources, visibility, and confidence to pursue ambitious projects. The one-page RPG proved both valuable format in itself and marketing tool building audience for larger releases.

However, the company's most significant creative achievement came with Spire: The City Must Fall (2017), designed by Grant Howitt and Chris Taylor. This dark fantasy game presented aelfir (dark elves) revolutionary cell attempting to overthrow high elf occupation of mile-high city of Spire through subversive operations, terrorism, and covert resistance.

Spire's setting proved immediately compelling: vertical city divided by class and species with wealthy high elves ruling from upper reaches while subject peoples—drow, humans, gnolls, and others—struggled in lower districts. This explicitly political framework addressed colonization, class oppression, and revolutionary violence without heavy-handed allegory.

The Resistance system created for Spire proved elegant innovation. Instead of traditional success/failure resolution, actions generated stress which accumulated in categories—Blood (physical harm), Mind (psychological damage), Silver (financial cost), Shadow (compromised position), Reputation (public exposure). When stress filled category, characters suffered fallout ranging from minor complications to permanent harm or death.

This stress-and-fallout structure created escalating tension without binary success/failure. Characters could accomplish goals while suffering consequences, or fail while avoiding serious harm. The system mechanized difficult revolutionary reality—small victories came at costs, setbacks didn't mean total failure, and accumulated damage eventually became unsustainable.

Character classes—Ministry (masked infiltrators), Bound (body-modification cultists), Midwives (street medics), Carrion-Priests (corpse-worshipping clergy), and others—combined evocative concepts with mechanical frameworks. Each class offered distinctive approaches to revolutionary work while embodying Spire's dark aesthetic.

The game's approach to revolutionary violence proved thoughtful. Rather than glorifying terrorism, Spire presented revolutionary violence as desperate response to oppression, personally damaging to perpetrators, and morally complex. Characters fought for liberation while risking becoming as monstrous as oppressors—difficult subject matter handled with sophistication.

Spire achieved critical acclaim, winning multiple awards and demonstrating British indie gaming could compete globally. The game's combination of political sophistication, mechanical innovation, and gorgeous presentation established Rowan, Rook and Decard as major indie publisher.

Following Spire's success, the company published Heart: The City Beneath (2020), set in nightmarish ever-changing dungeon beneath Spire. While mechanically similar using Resistance system, Heart shifted focus from political revolution to personal quests through surreal, deadly environment. The game explored what people would risk for desires—redemption, wealth, knowledge, revenge—in world literally trying to kill them.

Heart's setting proved equally evocative as Spire but tonally distinct. Where Spire presented vertical class stratification and political conflict, Heart explored horizontal depths, personal obsession, and reality's malleability. The ever-changing dungeon reflected characters' psychological states while posing lethal challenges.

Character classes in Heart—Junk Mage (trash magic practitioners), Vermissian Knight (train-haunted warriors), Heartsong (reality-warping musicians), and others—embodied game's surreal aesthetic. Each class pursued distinctive goals through dangerous environment while managing personal obsessions driving them into Heart.

Heart achieved commercial and critical success matching Spire, validating Resistance system's versatility while demonstrating company's worldbuilding range. Together, Spire and Heart created connected setting with distinct tonal territories appealing to different player interests.

Beyond major releases, Rowan, Rook and Decard maintained one-page RPG output. These games—Crash Pandas (racing raccoons), Honey Heist 2 (more bear crime), The Witch is Dead (woodland creatures avenging witch's death)—provided accessible entry points, convention games, and creative experiments between larger projects.

The company's business model centered on Kickstarter funding major projects while distributing through PDF and physical retail. This approach provided development capital while building community investment. Successful Kickstarters for Spire and Heart demonstrated strong audience enthusiasm and provided resources for premium production.

Rowan, Rook and Decard's visual aesthetic distinguished them from American indie publishers. Taylor's illustrations combined clean line art with evocative details, while layouts balanced information density with visual appeal. This professional presentation competed effectively with larger publishers while maintaining indie creative freedom.

The company's British identity influenced content significantly. Settings explored class conflict, urban decay, and political resistance through distinctly British lens. Humor maintained dry, dark quality characteristic of British comedy. Worldbuilding reflected British cultural touchstones while remaining accessible to international audiences.

Today, Rowan, Rook and Decard operates as respected UK indie publisher demonstrating British creative perspectives contribute distinctively to global indie gaming. The company exemplifies how regional publishers can establish international presence through strong creative vision, mechanical innovation, and professional execution.

## Company History Expansion

Rowan, Rook and Decard's development reflected broader UK indie gaming scene's growth. British tabletop gaming traditionally focused on wargaming and imported American RPGs, with limited indigenous RPG publishing. The company helped establish UK indie gaming's distinctive identity separate from American indie movement.

Geographic location in London influenced creative perspectives. Urban environment's class stratification, historical layers, and cultural diversity informed Spire's vertical city and Heart's archaeological depths. British political culture's class consciousness shaped games' approaches to oppression and resistance.

The company's collaborative structure between Howitt and Taylor proved crucial. Rather than single auteur or larger team, partnership enabled rapid iteration, integrated design and art, and complete creative packages without extensive freelancing. This efficiency suited indie economics while maintaining high quality.

Financial sustainability required balancing creative ambitions with commercial realities. One-page RPGs generated visibility and modest revenue while requiring minimal investment. Major projects like Spire demanded significant upfront costs justified through Kickstarter pre-funding. This portfolio approach enabled sustainable operation.

The company's relationship with UK gaming community provided local support while international reach came through digital distribution. British convention presence built domestic audience while online sales accessed global market. This dual focus prevented over-dependence on either market.

Howitt's public presence through social media, one-page RPG releases, and writing built personal following translating to company visibility. His accessible communication style and generous sharing of design ideas created community goodwill beyond commercial transactions.

## Publishing Philosophy

Rowan, Rook and Decard's publishing philosophy centers on distinctive principles:

**British Sensibilities**: Games reflect British cultural perspectives—class consciousness, dark humor, urban aesthetics, political awareness—creating distinctive contribution to global indie gaming.

**Political Gaming**: Settings explicitly address political themes—colonization, class oppression, revolutionary violence—without reducing complexity to simple allegories. Games create space for exploring political questions through play.

**Resistance System Innovation**: Stress-and-fallout mechanics create escalating tension and meaningful consequences without binary success/failure. This system supports thematically appropriate play across varied settings.

**Evocative Worldbuilding**: Settings combine vivid details with space for player creativity. Spire and Heart provide rich contexts without overwhelming detail, enabling groups to make settings their own.

**Class-Based Characters**: Character classes combine evocative concepts with mechanical frameworks, creating immediate character concepts while allowing personalization.

**One-Page Format**: Commitment to one-page RPGs demonstrates belief that complete games can fit minimal formats, making gaming accessible while showcasing design constraints' creative potential.

**Integrated Design and Art**: Close collaboration between mechanics and visuals creates cohesive packages where art and text reinforce each other.

**Dark Fantasy Focus**: Embracing dark fantasy aesthetics—urban decay, body horror, political violence—creates distinctive tonal territory different from heroic fantasy or grimdark excess.

**Accessible Complexity**: Games balance mechanical depth with clarity, creating sophisticated systems remaining learnable and playable without extensive study.

**Professional Presentation**: Premium production values—quality art, thoughtful layout, solid editing—demonstrate indie games can compete aesthetically with major publishers.

## Major Releases

### Spire: The City Must Fall (2017)

Spire revolutionized political fantasy gaming by creating framework for revolutionary resistance against colonial oppression through dark elves' guerrilla war against high elf occupiers in mile-high city. The game combined explicit political themes with innovative Resistance system and evocative setting.

The setting presented Spire—vertical city stretching mile high with wealthy high elves ruling from upper districts while subject peoples struggled below. This explicit class stratification provided immediate political context while vertical geography created distinctive worldbuilding.

Players portrayed drow (dark elves) belonging to Ministry—secret revolutionary organization working to overthrow high elf occupation through terrorism, subversion, propaganda, and covert operations. This framing created moral complexity—fighting legitimate liberation struggle through violent means with personally damaging consequences.

The Resistance system proved elegant innovation. Actions generated stress accumulating in five categories: Blood (physical injury), Mind (psychological trauma), Silver (financial cost), Shadow (compromised operational security), Reputation (public exposure). When stress filled category, characters suffered fallout—complications, injuries, or death depending on severity.

This stress-and-fallout structure meant actions had consequences without binary success/failure. Characters could accomplish objectives while suffering wounds, or fail while escaping serious harm. The accumulation created rising tension—stress inevitably built until fallout became unsustainable.

Character classes combined evocative concepts with mechanical frameworks. Bound practiced body modification as liberation theology. Midwives provided street medicine while managing revolutionary networks. Carrion-Priests worshipped corpse-god while handling Ministry's dead. Each class offered distinctive approach to revolution while embodying Spire's dark aesthetic.

The game's political sophistication distinguished it from typical fantasy adventure. Rather than simple good-versus-evil, Spire explored revolutionary violence's moral complexity, liberation struggle's personal costs, and question of whether freedom justified methods required to achieve it.

The vertical geography enabled class metaphor—literal elevation corresponded to social status, with oppressed peoples' ascent representing revolutionary success. This spatial representation of class conflict provided immediate comprehensible political framework.

Spire achieved critical acclaim winning multiple awards including Best Game at ENnies. The game demonstrated political gaming could achieve commercial success while maintaining thematic complexity. International success established Rowan, Rook and Decard as major indie publisher.

### Heart: The City Beneath (2020)

Heart explored personal obsession and surreal horror through game set in ever-changing nightmare dungeon beneath Spire. While using Resistance system mechanically similar to Spire, Heart shifted thematic focus from political revolution to personal quests through deadly environment.

The Heart presented ancient mass transit system transformed into living, malevolent dungeon consuming reality around it. The ever-changing environment shifted based on delvers' psychological states, fears, and desires—literal externalization of internal struggles.

Characters descended into Heart pursuing personal obsessions—wealth, knowledge, redemption, revenge, forbidden love. The game explored what people would risk for desires, how obsession drove self-destructive behavior, and whether goals justified suffering required to achieve them.

Character classes embodied game's surreal aesthetic. Junk Mages practiced magic through trash and discarded objects. Vermissian Knights bonded with ghost trains haunting abandoned subway. Heartsongs warped reality through music. Each class pursued distinctive goals through increasingly dangerous depths.

The Resistance system's stress-and-fallout mechanics suited Heart's themes perfectly. Descending deeper accumulated physical, psychological, and metaphysical damage. Characters achieved objectives but paid costs in sanity, body, and soul. The system mechanized obsession's toll.

The game's approach to dungeon crawling departed from traditional treasure-seeking adventure. Heart presented psychological landscape where external dangers reflected internal struggles. The dungeon literally fed on delvers' fears and desires, creating personal horror rather than generic threats.

Heart achieved commercial success matching Spire while attracting players interested in surreal horror over political gaming. Together, the games demonstrated Resistance system's versatility and company's worldbuilding range across connected setting's different aspects.

### Honey Heist (2017)

Honey Heist achieved viral success through deceptively simple premise: bears attempting to steal honey from HoneyCon while managing tension between bear instincts and criminal discipline. The entire game fit on single page with complete rules, character creation, and scenario.

Character creation required two choices: bear type (grizzly, polar, panda, etc.) providing descriptor, and criminal role (brains, muscle, hacker, etc.) providing skills. Characters tracked two stats on simple scale: Bear (animal instincts) and Criminal (heist professionalism). Actions shifted balance between stats—too much Bear meant reverting to simple animal, too much Criminal meant forgetting you're bear and attempting impossible feats.

The setup proved immediately accessible: players understood premise instantly, rules fit single page eliminating learning barriers, absurdist humor reduced performance anxiety. This combination enabled non-gamers to play successfully within minutes.

The game's viral success proved unprecedented. Social media sharing, mainstream gaming coverage, convention ubiquity, and celebrity actual plays introduced countless people to roleplaying through accessible package. Honey Heist demonstrated experimental format and absurdist premise could achieve mainstream penetration.

More importantly for indie gaming, Honey Heist validated one-page RPG format commercially and culturally. The success proved complete games could exist in minimal formats, making game design accessible to broader creators while demonstrating constraints could enhance rather than limit creativity.

### Havoc Brigade (2016)

Havoc Brigade presented players as troubleshooters for wizard's dimensional empire, solving problems across multiple realities through violence, magic, and questionable decisions. The game combined dark humor with pulp adventure through episodic mission structure.

The setting presented empire spanning multiple dimensions with players as Brigade—expendable troubleshooters sent to handle problems imperial bureaucracy couldn't. This setup created consistent adventure framework while enabling varied scenarios across different realities.

Character creation offered class and species combinations creating distinctive characters. The system emphasized competent characters facing problems through creative solutions, violence, or spectacular failures. This power level suited episodic troubleshooting adventures.

The game's humor maintained dark quality characteristic of Rowan, Rook and Decard. Comedy emerged from competent characters facing absurd situations, bureaucratic dysfunction, and unintended consequences rather than incompetent slapstick.

Havoc Brigade demonstrated company could create lighter adventure gaming while maintaining signature aesthetic and British sensibilities.

### Unbound (2018)

Unbound explored magical school gaming through lens of teenage rebellion and self-discovery. Players portrayed students at academy learning magic while navigating social hierarchies, personal relationships, and institutional oppression.

The game addressed young adult themes—identity formation, authority resistance, found family, personal growth—through magical school framework. Rather than celebrating institutional education, Unbound examined how institutions shaped and constrained individuals.

Character creation emphasized relationships, personal goals, and magical approaches over numerical optimization. The system supported social conflict, magical experimentation, and personal development through school year structure.

The game's British perspective on education influenced tone—more critical of institutional authority, more conscious of class implications, more willing to examine education's role in reproducing social hierarchies than American magical school games.

Unbound demonstrated Rowan, Rook and Decard could address young adult themes through sophisticated lens while maintaining mechanical innovation and evocative worldbuilding.

## Business Model and Innovation

Rowan, Rook and Decard developed sustainable model suited to UK indie publishing:

**Kickstarter Funding**: Major projects funded through Kickstarter campaigns providing development capital while building community investment. Successful campaigns for Spire and Heart demonstrated strong audience support.

**One-Page Portfolio**: Regular one-page RPG releases generate visibility, provide convention demos, and create accessible entry points requiring minimal investment while showcasing design creativity.

**Digital and Physical**: Dual distribution through PDFs and print versions serves different market segments. Digital provides accessible pricing and global reach, physical offers premium collectible versions.

**Integrated Production**: Partnership between designer and artist enables complete production in-house, reducing freelance costs while maintaining creative control and cohesive vision.

**Supplement Strategy**: Focused supplements for major releases extend product lines while exploring setting aspects. District guides, adventure collections, and sourcebooks provide ongoing content.

**Retail Distribution**: Professional distribution channels ensure retail presence complementing direct sales and convention sales.

**International Focus**: While UK-based, digital distribution and English language enable global market access beyond domestic British audience.

**Premium Production**: Investment in quality art, layout, and printing creates products competing with major publishers while justifying premium pricing.

**Community Engagement**: Active social media presence, generous sharing of one-page RPGs, and community interaction build goodwill translating to customer loyalty.

**Lean Operations**: Partnership structure keeps overhead low while maintaining output quality through efficient collaboration.

This model demonstrates sustainable UK indie publishing through smart funding, format diversity, and professional execution.
