# Fantasy Flight Games

**Founded**: 1995
**Active Era**: d20 Era through Modern Era (1995-Present)
**Historical Significance**: ⭐ 4/5
**Headquarters**: Roseville, Minnesota (now subsidiary of Asmodee)
**Status**: Active

## Company History

Fantasy Flight Games (FFG) emerged in 1995 as board game publisher, eventually becoming one of gaming's most recognizable brands through innovative design, high production values, and strategic license acquisition. While board games remain FFG's primary focus, the company's RPG contributions—particularly the narrative dice system and approach to licensed properties—significantly influenced modern gaming. FFG's journey from small Minnesota publisher to international gaming corporation demonstrates how innovation, production quality, and strategic licensing can build industry dominance.

Christian T. Petersen founded Fantasy Flight Games in 1995 in his parents' basement in Roseville, Minnesota. The company initially focused on board games, with Twilight Imperium (1997) establishing FFG's reputation for epic, high-production-value games. Petersen's vision emphasized premium components, extensive playtesting, and production quality rivaling or exceeding industry leaders.

FFG's growth accelerated through strategic acquisitions. The company purchased several gaming properties and smaller publishers, building catalog and market presence. This aggressive expansion strategy characterized FFG's approach—identify valuable properties or companies, acquire them, then apply FFG's production expertise and marketing resources.

The company's RPG involvement began with licensed properties. FFG acquired licenses for Warhammer Fantasy Roleplay, Legend of the Five Rings, and other established games. Rather than simply reprinting existing materials, FFG completely reimagined properties with innovative mechanics and premium production.

Warhammer Fantasy Roleplay Third Edition (2009) represented FFG's first major RPG innovation. The game abandoned traditional dice mechanics for proprietary narrative dice system using special symbols. Cards represented actions, conditions, and resources. The approach polarized audiences—some loved the innovation and production quality, others resented component requirements and departure from traditional WFRP.

WFRP Third Edition demonstrated FFG's design philosophy: premium components, innovative mechanics, and complete reimagining rather than incremental evolution. The edition achieved moderate success but never matched earlier WFRP versions' popularity. The polarized reception taught FFG valuable lessons about balancing innovation with fan expectations.

Star Wars Roleplaying Games represented FFG's most successful RPG venture. Beginning with Edge of the Empire (2012), FFG released three interconnected core books exploring different aspects of Star Wars universe: Edge (scoundrels and fringe), Age of Rebellion (2014, military campaigns), Force and Destiny (2015, Force users).

The Star Wars games refined FFG's narrative dice system from WFRP. Instead of simple success/failure, rolls generated success, advantage, triumph, failure, threat, and despair results. This created nuanced outcomes—you might succeed but generate complications, or fail but create advantages. The system supported Star Wars' cinematic action perfectly.

Star Wars RPG achieved commercial and critical success. The games' production values, innovative mechanics, and faithful Star Wars feel attracted audiences. Multiple sourcebooks, adventures, and supplements supported extensive campaigns. The success validated FFG's approach and established narrative dice as viable alternative to traditional resolution.

Legend of the Five Rings (2018) continued FFG's licensed property strategy. The game used custom dice creating narrative outcomes while maintaining L5R's focus on honor, duty, and samurai drama. FFG's production quality and mechanical innovation refreshed property while maintaining core identity.

Genesys (2017) genericized FFG's narrative dice system, creating standalone universal framework. This followed industry pattern (like Savage Worlds from Deadlands, AGE from Dragon Age) of extracting licensed game mechanics for broader application. Genesys supported any setting using narrative dice mechanics, expanding system accessibility.

In 2014, Asmodee acquired Fantasy Flight Games. While FFG maintained brand identity and operational independence initially, Asmodee ownership brought resources for continued expansion. The acquisition placed FFG within larger board game conglomerate alongside Days of Wonder, Z-Man Games, and others.

FFG's approach to RPG publishing differed from traditional publishers. The company emphasized premium components—cards, tokens, maps, and proprietary dice—creating physical experiences beyond just books. This component-heavy approach increased production costs but justified premium pricing and created distinctive products.

However, component requirements created barriers. Proprietary dice meant players couldn't use standard gaming dice. Card and token requirements increased entry costs. Some players loved the premium experience; others resented component dependencies. This tension characterized FFG's RPG reception.

Throughout 2010s, FFG balanced board game dominance with selective RPG development. Star Wars remained flagship RPG, while other properties received varying support levels. The company demonstrated that premium production and innovative mechanics could succeed in RPG market, though not displacing traditional book-centric publishing.

Recent years saw FFG focusing on established properties while reducing new RPG development. Star Wars support continued, Genesys received supplements, and Legend of the Five Rings maintained release schedule. However, FFG's primary identity remained board games, with RPGs representing smaller though significant portion of business.

Today, Fantasy Flight Games operates as major gaming company under Asmodee ownership. The company's RPG legacy centers on narrative dice innovation, premium production standards, and successful licensed property management. While not primarily RPG publisher, FFG's contributions influenced modern game design and raised production value expectations.

## Publishing Philosophy

Fantasy Flight Games' RPG publishing philosophy emphasized:

**Premium Production**: From inception, FFG prioritized production quality. Games featured full-color books, custom dice, cards, tokens, maps, and other premium components. This component focus distinguished FFG from traditional book-centric publishers.

**Mechanical Innovation**: FFG consistently innovated mechanically rather than relying on established systems. Narrative dice, card-based mechanics, and other innovations showed willingness to challenge conventional design assumptions.

**Licensed Property Focus**: FFG concentrated on licensed properties with established audiences rather than creating original IPs. Star Wars, Warhammer, and Legend of the Five Rings provided built-in markets and recognizable brands.

**Complete Reimagining**: When acquiring licenses, FFG completely reimagined properties rather than incremental updates. This approach created distinctive products but sometimes alienated existing fans.

**Component Integration**: FFG designed games around physical components rather than treating them as supplements. Cards, dice, and tokens were integral to gameplay, not optional enhancements.

**Cinematic Experience**: Games emphasized cinematic, narrative-rich experiences over tactical simulation or gritty realism. The narrative dice system particularly supported dramatic, story-focused gameplay.

**High Price Points**: Premium components justified premium pricing. FFG products typically cost more than competitors but offered distinctive physical experiences.

**Systematic Support**: FFG supported released games through regular supplements, adventures, and expansions following planned release schedules.

**Cross-Media Integration**: The company leveraged board games, card games, miniatures, and RPGs around same properties, creating integrated product ecosystems.

## Major Releases

### Star Wars Roleplaying Games (2012-2015)

FFG's Star Wars line comprised three interconnected core books:

**Edge of the Empire** (2012) focused on fringe characters—smugglers, bounty hunters, colonists—operating on galactic margins. The game emphasized scraping by, moral ambiguity, and underworld adventures. The setting's focus on non-Jedi characters differentiated it from previous Star Wars RPGs.

**Age of Rebellion** (2014) centered on military campaigns against the Empire. Players controlled Rebel Alliance members fighting tyranny through military operations, espionage, and guerrilla warfare. The setting emphasized heroism, sacrifice, and organized resistance.

**Force and Destiny** (2015) explored Force-sensitive characters during dark times when Jedi hunted and Force tradition fractured. The game balanced power fantasy with persecution, creating morally complex campaigns.

The three books shared mechanical compatibility—characters from different books could play together—while maintaining distinct tones and focuses. This approach served diverse Star Wars campaign preferences.

The narrative dice system created nuanced outcomes. Success with threat meant achieving goals while generating complications. Failure with advantage meant missing objectives but creating opportunities. Triumph and despair generated dramatic critical effects. This system supported Star Wars' cinematic feel perfectly—dramatic successes, costly victories, and dramatic reversals.

The games achieved significant commercial success. Production quality, faithful Star Wars feel, and innovative mechanics attracted audiences. Extensive supplement line explored factions, locations, and campaign frameworks.

### Warhammer Fantasy Roleplay Third Edition (2009)

WFRP Third Edition represented FFG's boldest reimagining. The game abandoned WFRP's traditional percentile system for narrative dice mechanics. Cards represented actions, careers, conditions, and resources. Tokens tracked stress, fatigue, and other states.

The component-heavy approach created distinctive physical experience. Players built hands of action cards representing available options, selected actions strategically, and resolved outcomes with narrative dice. The system emphasized tactical choices and narrative flexibility.

The innovation polarized audiences. Some loved the fresh approach, premium components, and tactical depth. Others resented losing traditional WFRP mechanics, proprietary component requirements, and departure from established feel.

The edition achieved moderate commercial success but never matched previous WFRP versions' popularity. When FFG's license ended, the property returned to traditional percentile-based Fourth Edition, suggesting market preferred familiar mechanics.

Despite mixed reception, WFRP Third Edition influenced FFG's later designs. The narrative dice system refined for Star Wars, and lessons about balancing innovation with fan expectations guided future projects.

### Genesys (2017)

Genesys extracted FFG's narrative dice system into generic framework. The game provided core rules applicable to any setting, with supplements exploring specific genres.

The system maintained narrative dice mechanics while removing Star Wars-specific elements. Character creation, skills, talents, and combat rules worked across settings. This flexibility let groups use familiar mechanics for custom campaigns.

Genesys faced challenges convincing players to adopt generic system requiring proprietary dice. Unlike Star Wars' built-in audience, Genesys competed against established universal systems not requiring special components. The system's quality and innovation attracted some, but component barrier limited adoption.

Supplements explored science fiction (Shadow of the Beanstalk), fantasy (Realms of Terrinoth), and other settings. These demonstrated system flexibility while providing ready-to-play options.

Genesys represented FFG's attempt to build original RPG beyond licensed properties. While achieving moderate success, the game never matched Star Wars' popularity, suggesting FFG's strength lay in licensed properties rather than original systems.

### Legend of the Five Rings RPG (2018)

FFG's L5R acquisition included card game and RPG rights. The RPG edition used custom dice creating narrative outcomes while maintaining L5R's focus on honor, duty, and samurai drama.

The "Roll and Keep" mechanic—choosing which dice to keep from larger pool—created meaningful decisions. Players balanced success likelihood against narrative opportunities. The system supported L5R's themes of internal conflict between duty and desire.

The game's emphasis on social interaction, courtly intrigue, and personal honor distinguished it from combat-focused RPGs. Mechanics supported political maneuvering, maintaining honor, and navigating complex social hierarchies.

FFG's production quality elevated L5R presentation. Full-color artwork, premium components, and attention to detail created immersive experience faithful to setting's aesthetic.

## Cultural Impact

Fantasy Flight Games influenced gaming through several channels:

**Narrative Dice Innovation**: FFG's narrative dice system demonstrated alternative to binary success/failure resolution. The mechanic influenced later designs incorporating nuanced outcomes.

**Production Value Standards**: FFG's premium components raised expectations for production quality. Other publishers elevated standards to compete with FFG's presentation.

**Licensed Property Management**: FFG demonstrated how to successfully adapt licensed properties through complete reimagining rather than conservative updates. This approach influenced industry licensing practices.

**Component Integration**: FFG showed physical components could be integral to gameplay rather than supplements. This influenced how designers thought about physical game design.

**Cinematic Gaming**: The narrative dice system and design philosophy emphasized cinematic, dramatic play over simulation. This influenced broader movement toward narrative-focused mechanics.

**Star Wars RPG Success**: Successful Star Wars games introduced many to RPG hobby, particularly younger players drawn by familiar property.

## Current Status

Fantasy Flight Games remains active under Asmodee ownership:

**Corporate Structure**: Operates as Asmodee subsidiary with operational autonomy.

**Product Lines**: Continued support for Star Wars RPG, Genesys, and Legend of the Five Rings. Primary focus remains board games.

**RPG Position**: RPGs represent smaller portion of business compared to board games and miniatures, but maintain active development.

**Production Quality**: Maintains reputation for premium components and high production values.

**Digital Presence**: Online store, PDF sales (through distribution partners), and community support.

**Market Position**: Recognized as premium publisher, particularly for licensed properties and component-rich games.

## Legacy

Fantasy Flight Games' RPG legacy centers on several achievements:

**Narrative Dice System**: Creating influential resolution mechanic demonstrating alternatives to traditional dice systems. Many later games cited FFG's approach as inspiration.

**Production Standards**: Raising industry expectations for component quality and presentation. FFG proved premium production could succeed commercially.

**Licensed Property Success**: Demonstrating that licensed RPGs could achieve both commercial success and design innovation. The Star Wars games particularly showed how to honor properties while innovating mechanically.

**Component-Based Design**: Pioneering integration of cards, tokens, and custom dice as core gameplay elements rather than supplements.

**Cinematic Mechanics**: Advancing mechanics supporting narrative, dramatic gameplay over detailed simulation. The narrative dice system particularly influenced story-focused design.

**Star Wars Contribution**: Creating successful Star Wars RPG that introduced new audiences to hobby and demonstrated license's ongoing viability.

While FFG never became primarily RPG publisher, the company's innovations influenced modern design significantly. The narrative dice system, premium production approach, and licensed property management created lasting impact extending beyond FFG's specific products.

## Business Model and Distribution

Fantasy Flight Games developed distinctive business model:

**Premium Pricing**: Component-rich products commanded premium prices, maintaining margins supporting production costs.

**Licensed Properties**: Focus on established licenses provided built-in audiences reducing marketing requirements.

**Planned Product Lines**: Systematic release schedules for supplements and expansions created predictable revenue streams.

**Cross-Platform Integration**: Leveraging same licenses across board games, card games, miniatures, and RPGs created synergies and market efficiencies.

**Retail Focus**: Strong relationships with game retailers through board game success supported RPG distribution.

**Component Dependencies**: Proprietary dice and components created ongoing sales opportunities (replacement dice, additional sets) while creating entry barriers.

**Beginner Boxes**: Starter products with simplified rules and components introduced new players while creating upgrade paths to full games.

**Corporate Resources**: Asmodee ownership provided capital for major licensed property acquisitions and production investments.

**International Distribution**: Asmodee's global reach ensured international availability and localization.

This model leveraged FFG's strengths in production, licensing, and retail relationships while creating distinctive market position.
