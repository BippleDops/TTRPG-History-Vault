# Dog Eared Designs

**Founded**: 2017
**Active Era**: Modern Era (2017-Present)
**Historical Significance**: ⭐ 3/5
**Headquarters**: Baltimore, Maryland
**Status**: Active

## Company History

Dog Eared Designs emerged as experimental small press offshoot allowing Fred Hicks—co-founder of Evil Hat Productions—to explore design directions separate from Evil Hat's established Fate focus. Founded in 2017, Dog Eared provided creative space for developing Pip System and publishing smaller experimental projects without Evil Hat's brand expectations or production scale requirements. The company's journey represents established designers' need for creative outlets beyond successful main ventures, demonstrating how side projects enable experimentation impossible within larger operations' constraints.

Fred Hicks' position as Evil Hat co-founder and Fate system steward created both opportunities and limitations. Evil Hat's success provided financial security and industry reputation, but company identity centered strongly on Fate. Projects departing significantly from Fate sensibilities risked confusing Evil Hat brand or requiring resources better spent on core products. Dog Eared Designs solved this creative tension by providing separate outlet for non-Fate experimentation.

The company name "Dog Eared Designs" evoked well-used books, casual accessibility, and unpretentious charm—signaling different approach from Evil Hat's professional polish. Dog Eared suggested projects pursued for creative satisfaction rather than commercial calculation, though maintaining quality standards.

Dog Eared's founding centered on developing Pip System—minimalist dice pool mechanic using d6s to resolve actions through counting pips showing specific results. The system departed from Fate's aspect-based narrative focus toward streamlined traditional resolution supporting varied genres without extensive mechanical framework.

Pip System's core mechanic proved elegantly simple: roll pools of d6s, count specific results (usually 5-6), compare totals. This straightforward approach enabled quick resolution without complicated rules while supporting varied difficulty levels and opposed rolls. The system's simplicity suited pickup games, convention demos, and scenarios where learning complex rules proved impractical.

The system's design philosophy emphasized "narrative-light" approach. Where Fate explicitly supported collaborative storytelling through aspects and fate points, Pip System provided mechanical resolution without prescribing narrative structures. This created space for traditional gamemaster authority and player-driven improvisation alike without assuming specific creative agenda.

Psychic Detective (2019) demonstrated Pip System applied to supernatural investigation genre. Players portrayed psychics solving crimes through paranormal abilities, mundane investigation, and navigating skeptical authorities. The game showed Pip System could support investigative play through straightforward mechanics.

Eldritch Hack (2018) applied Pip System to Lovecraftian horror adventure, creating accessible entry to cosmic horror gaming. The game combined traditional dungeon crawling with sanity mechanics and eldrich threats, demonstrating system's genre flexibility.

Beyond Pip System games, Dog Eared occasionally published other small experimental projects exploring mechanics or genres outside Evil Hat's focus. These releases remained modest in scope—digital-first releases, minimal art, straightforward production—prioritizing design exploration over production spectacle.

The business model reflected experimental side project nature. Digital-first releases minimized production costs. Print-on-demand provided physical options without inventory investment. Modest marketing emphasized projects to existing audience rather than seeking mainstream breakthrough. This conservative approach enabled sustainable operation without requiring commercial success.

Dog Eared's relationship to Evil Hat proved symbiotic rather than competitive. The companies maintained clear separation—different brands, different systems, different audiences—while Hicks' involvement connected them. Dog Eared provided experimental outlet preventing Evil Hat from diluting brand through non-Fate projects, while Evil Hat's success provided security enabling Dog Eared's experimental risks.

The company's output remained deliberately modest. Rather than prolific release schedule, Dog Eared published selectively when projects warranted release. This quality-over-quantity approach suited side project nature and prevented overextension.

Community reception proved generally positive if modest in scale. Pip System attracted players seeking simpler alternative to Fate while maintaining some narrative focus. Games found niche audiences appreciating accessible mechanics and straightforward presentation. However, Dog Eared never achieved commercial scale matching Evil Hat—nor apparently sought to.

The company demonstrated successful pattern for established designers maintaining creative outlets beyond main ventures. Rather than forcing all creative impulses through single brand, separate imprints enabled experimentation while preserving main brand coherence. This model influenced other designers considering similar arrangements.

Today, Dog Eared Designs continues operating as modest experimental publisher allowing Fred Hicks to explore mechanics and genres outside Evil Hat's Fate focus. The company exemplifies how side projects enable creative satisfaction and experimentation complementing rather than competing with main ventures.

## Publishing Philosophy

Dog Eared Designs' philosophy reflects experimental small press nature:

**Narrative-Light Mechanics**: Pip System provides resolution without prescribing narrative structures, creating space for varied play styles from traditional to improvisational.

**Minimalist Design**: Streamlined mechanics prioritize accessibility and quick learning over comprehensive simulation or complex narrative frameworks.

**Genre Flexibility**: Pip System's simplicity enables application across genres without extensive modification, demonstrating versatile core framework.

**Digital-First Economics**: Emphasizing PDFs reduces production costs enabling experimental releases without requiring commercial hits.

**Quality Selectivity**: Publishing selectively when projects warrant release rather than maintaining aggressive schedule ensures quality focus.

**Complementary Positioning**: Operating as Evil Hat offshoot rather than competitor creates clear market positioning and brand separation.

**Experimental Outlet**: Providing space for design exploration outside main brand enables creative risks impossible within larger operation's constraints.

**Accessible Entry**: Games target accessible entry points for genres through straightforward mechanics and clear presentation.

## Business Model and Innovation

Dog Eared developed model suited to experimental side project:

**Digital-First Publishing**: PDFs as primary format reduces overhead enabling experimental releases without significant capital investment.

**Print-on-Demand**: POD provides physical options without inventory costs or risk, serving collectors while maintaining accessibility.

**Modest Marketing**: Relying on existing audience and organic discovery rather than aggressive marketing campaigns suits side project economics.

**Selective Publishing**: Quality-focused selective releases prevent overextension while maintaining standards.

**Brand Separation**: Clear differentiation from Evil Hat prevents brand confusion while enabling independent identity.

**Pip System Focus**: Developing coherent system family creates recognizable product line without requiring extensive diversity.

**Conservative Budgets**: Minimal art, straightforward production, and contained scope enable sustainable operation without commercial pressure.

This model demonstrates viable approach for established designers maintaining experimental outlets beyond main ventures.
