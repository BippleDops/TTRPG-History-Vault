# Fronts (Dungeon World)

**First Appeared**: [[Apocalypse World]] (2010), refined in [[Dungeon World]] (2012)
**Designers**: [[D. Vincent Baker]], [[Sage LaTorra]], [[Adam Koebel]]
**Category**: Threat Tracking / Campaign Management

## Overview

Fronts provide structured GM tool for tracking multiple ongoing threats with each Front having Dangers (specific threats), Grim Portents (warning signs progressing toward doom), and Impending Doom (what happens if unchecked)—creating campaign framework where various dangers advance toward catastrophes unless players intervene, giving GMs organized way to manage multiple simultaneous storylines while maintaining world responsiveness, becoming essential PbtA GM technique for "play to find out" philosophy where threats advance logically rather than waiting for player attention.

**Structure**:
- **Front**: Major threat category (The Goblin Invasion, The Spreading Plague, Political Coup)
- **Dangers**: Specific threats within Front
- **Grim Portents**: Steps progressing toward doom (clock-like advancement)
- **Impending Doom**: What happens if unchecked
- **Stakes**: Who/what is threatened

**Purpose**: Organizing campaign threats, tracking advancement, maintaining world dynamism

**Play to Find Out**: Fronts advance whether players engage or not—world is alive

**Impact**: Became standard PbtA GM technique. Influenced threat-tracking and campaign management across narrative gaming.

## Mechanics

### Creating Fronts

**Identify Threat**: What's the danger?

**Define Dangers**: Specific elements within threat

**Establish Grim Portents**: 3-7 steps toward doom
- Each represents progression
- Like countdown clock
- Triggers based on logic/time/player inaction

**Determine Impending Doom**: What happens at final Portent?

**Identify Stakes**: What's threatened (people, places, ideals)

**Example Front**:
- **Name**: The Dragon Cult's Rising
- **Danger**: Cult summoning ancient dragon
- **Grim Portents**:
  1. Cultists kidnap first sacrifice
  2. Ritual site discovered and cleansed
  3. Three sacrifices completed
  4. Dragon stirs beneath mountain
  5. Dragon emerges, devastates countryside
- **Impending Doom**: Dragon destroys region
- **Stakes**: Everyone in the valley

### Advancing Fronts

**Grim Portents Progress**:
- When fiction demands (players ignore threat, time passes, logical progression)
- Mark next Portent
- Describe in-world manifestation
- Create visible consequences

**Player Impact**: Players can derail, delay, or stop Fronts through intervention

**World Dynamism**: Fronts advance independent of player attention—world doesn't wait

### Multiple Fronts

**Campaign Fronts**: Long-term major threats

**Adventure Fronts**: Immediate shorter dangers

**Simultaneous**: Multiple Fronts active creating complex situation

**Interconnected**: Fronts can affect each other

**Management**: GM tracks all, advances as logic dictates

## Design Philosophy

**Play to Find Out**: Don't predetermine outcomes; see how Fronts and player actions interact

**Living World**: Threats don't wait for player engagement

**Organized Threats**: Structure preventing forgotten storylines

**Logical Progression**: Dangers advance based on in-world logic

**Visible Consequences**: Portent advancement shows in fiction

**Player Agency**: Can intervene, derail, or ignore (with consequences)

## Cultural Impact

**PbtA GM Standard**: Expected tool for PbtA campaign management

**Threat Organization**: Template for tracking multiple dangers

**Living World**: Demonstrated responsive world creation

**Campaign Structure**: Influenced how narrative games organize ongoing threats

**Play to Find Out**: Embodied principle mechanically

## Reception

**Praise**: Organized campaign management, living world creation, logical threat advancement, visible progression

**Criticism**: Can feel like railroad if Fronts advance regardless of intervention, requires GM tracking discipline, some Fronts need more flexibility

## Legacy

Became essential PbtA GM technique. Influenced threat-tracking across narrative gaming. Demonstrated how to manage complex campaigns with multiple storylines while maintaining world responsiveness.
