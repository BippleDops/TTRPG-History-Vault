# Session Zero

**First Appeared**: Practice evolved 2000s-2010s, formalized in indie gaming
**Category**: Pre-Game Practice / Social Contract Establishment

## Overview

Session Zero refers to pre-game session (before actual play begins) where group discusses expectations, establishes boundaries, creates characters/world collaboratively, and aligns on themes/tone/content—becoming standard practice in modern gaming (especially indie/narrative scenes) for preventing mismatches, establishing safety, building investment through collaborative creation, and ensuring everyone wants similar gaming experience, transforming from optional planning into expected foundational practice.

**Purpose**: Prevent problems through upfront discussion rather than discovering incompatibilities mid-campaign

**Contents**: Safety tools, expectations, character/world creation, social contract

**Now Standard**: Expected practice in indie gaming, increasingly common mainstream

## Common Session Zero Elements

**Safety Discussion**:
- Establish Lines and Veils
- Introduce X-Card or other safety tools
- Discuss sensitive content boundaries
- Establish comfort levels

**Expectations Alignment**:
- Desired tone (serious/comedic, dark/light)
- Preferred balance (combat/roleplay/exploration)
- Campaign length and commitment
- Attendance expectations
- Rules adherence vs. Rule of Cool

**World/Character Creation**:
- Collaborative worldbuilding
- Character creation together (ensuring party cohesion)
- Establishing relationships
- Setting creation/customization

**Logistics**:
- Scheduling
- House rules
- Snacks/breaks
- Recording/streaming policies
- Communication preferences

**Social Contract**:
- Behavioral expectations
- Conflict resolution
- Player agency
- GM authority/limits

## Cultural Impact

**Standard Practice**: Became expected in indie gaming

**Mainstream Adoption**: Increasingly common in D&D and traditional games

**Problem Prevention**: Upfront discussion prevents later conflicts

**Safety Integration**: Connected to safety tool movement

**Collaborative Culture**: Emphasized group buy-in over GM dictation

## Reception

Widely adopted and praised for preventing problems. Some see it as unnecessary formality but proponents argue it saves more time than it costs.

## Notes

- Standard in safety-conscious gaming
