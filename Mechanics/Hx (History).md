# Hx (History)

**First Appeared**: [[Apocalypse World]] (2010)
**Designer**: [[D. Vincent Baker]]
**Category**: Relationship Stat / Shared History

## Overview

Hx (History), created by Vincent Baker for Apocalypse World (2010), rates how well characters know each other numerically (-3 to +3)—higher Hx means deeper history/understanding—used when reading people, helping them, or interfering, creating mechanical representation of interpersonal familiarity where knowing someone well provides bonuses when interacting with them, earning XP when Hx with someone reaches +4 (then resets), mechanically rewarding character interaction and relationship development through advancement tied to connections.

**Rating**: -3 (strangers/enemies) to +3 (intimate knowledge)

**Usage**: Add Hx when Reading someone, Helping them, or Interfering

**Advancement**: Hx +4 earns XP and resets to +1

**Development**: Hx increases through certain Moves and relationship development

**Impact**: Influenced relationship stats in PbtA. Demonstrated connections could be mechanical ratings.

## Mechanics

**Starting Hx**: Determined during character creation through playbook prompts

**Using Hx**: Modifier when:
- Reading another PC (understanding their motives)
- Helping another PC (assisting their action)
- Interfering with another PC (opposing their action)

**Increasing Hx**: Certain Moves, shared experiences, or end-of-session

**Reset and Advance**: At Hx +4, mark XP and reset to +1

**Asymmetric**: Your Hx with them may differ from their Hx with you

## Design Philosophy

**Relationships as Stats**: Mechanical ratings for connections

**Know Them Well**: Familiarity provides bonuses

**Advancement Through Connection**: XP from developing relationships

**Asymmetric Knowledge**: Can know them better than they know you

## Cultural Impact

Influenced relationship stats in PbtA games. Demonstrated connections could be numerical ratings. Some PbtA games adopted; others used different relationship mechanics.

## Reception

**Praise**: Mechanical relationship development, advancement through connection

**Criticism**: Math overhead, resets feel artificial, some prefer descriptive relationships

## Legacy

Influenced PbtA relationship mechanics. Some games kept Hx; others developed alternatives (Bonds, Strings, etc.). Demonstrated relationship-stat viability.
