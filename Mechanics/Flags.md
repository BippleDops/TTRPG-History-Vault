# Flags

**First Appeared**: Concept evolved across 2000s indie design
**Category**: Player Signaling / Content Negotiation

## Overview

Flags provide mechanic where players signal content they want/don't want in game—placing "flags" indicating desired scenes, themes, or interactions so GM and other players can provide flagged content, creating consent-and-desire communication system ensuring everyone gets content they enjoy while avoiding unwanted material, influencing safety tool design and demonstrating how explicit communication about preferences improves play.

**Positive Flags**: "I want scenes about..."
**Negative Flags**: "I don't want content involving..."

**Communication Tool**: Makes implicit preferences explicit

**Consent Framework**: Negotiating content boundaries

**Influence**: Informed safety tools (X-Card, Lines/Veils) and desire-signaling mechanics

## Types of Flags

**Content Flags**: Specific themes/situations wanted
**Character Flags**: Aspects inviting interaction (Primetime Adventures Issues)
**Relationship Flags**: Desired interpersonal dynamics
**Tone Flags**: Preferred atmosphere/intensity

## Cultural Impact

Influenced safety tools broadly. Demonstrated explicit communication improves gaming. Became standard in safety-conscious gaming communities.
