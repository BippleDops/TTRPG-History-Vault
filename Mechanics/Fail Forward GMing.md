# Fail Forward GMing

**First Appeared**: Evolved concept through 2000s narrativist design, formalized in PbtA
**Category**: GM Technique / Resolution Philosophy

## Overview

Fail Forward represents GM technique where failed rolls advance story through complications rather than creating dead-ends—on 6- in PbtA, "you fail to pick lock AND guards hear noise, forcing new situation" not "you fail, try again?"—ensuring every roll changes circumstances whether successful or not, maintaining narrative momentum by making failure interesting instead of repetitive, becoming standard narrativist principle that even mainstream games increasingly adopt, demonstrating how every outcome can drive story forward rather than stalling on failure.

**Traditional Failure**: "You fail to pick lock. What do you do?" → potential dead-end requiring repeated attempts

**Fail Forward**: "Lock resists AND guards approach—what do you do now?" → story advances through complication

**PbtA Codification**: 6- triggers GM Move introducing complication/consequence

**Momentum**: No static loops—every roll changes something

**Impact**: Became narrativist standard, influenced mainstream gaming, changed how GMs handle failures

## Implementation

**PbtA Structure** (when player rolls 6-):
- Failure triggers GM Move
- Move advances story through complication
- No repetition—situation has changed

**GM Moves** include:
- Separate them
- Announce future badness  
- Take away their stuff
- Inflict harm
- Make them pay a price
- Turn their move back on them
- Reveal unwelcome truth

**Example**:
- Traditional: "You fail Stealth check. Try again?"
- Fail Forward: "You slip, alerting guard who investigates—what do you do?"

## Design Philosophy

**Momentum Over Realism**: Story advancement trumps simulation

**Interesting Over Accurate**: Complication beats nothing-happens

**Player Respect**: Failure doesn't mean wasted action/time

**GM Creativity**: Requires interesting complication generation

**No Whiffing**: Avoiding "I swing and miss" tedium

**Every Roll Matters**: Success AND failure both significant

## Cultural Impact

**PbtA Standard**: Expected in Apocalypse World games

**Mainstream Influence**: Even traditional GMs adopting

**Design Vocabulary**: "Fail Forward" recognized term

**GM Technique**: Changed how failure is handled

**Pacing Tool**: Maintaining story momentum

## Reception

**Praise**: Maintains momentum, respects player time, creates drama, no dead-ends

**Criticism**: Can feel railroaded, removes retry choice, some situations should allow repetition, not all failures warrant complications

## Design Lessons

**Failures Can Be Interesting**: Complications more engaging than nothing

**Momentum Matters**: Forward movement maintains engagement

**Respect Player Actions**: Even failures should matter

**Complication Generation**: Requires GM skill creating interesting failures
