# Stress and Trauma

**First Appeared**: [[Blades in the Dark]] (2017)
**Designer**: [[John Harper]]
**Category**: Resource Management / Psychological Tracking

## Overview

Stress and Trauma, created by John Harper for Blades in the Dark (2017), revolutionize resource management by making stress (psychological/physical composure) the primary spendable resource—players mark Stress to resist consequences they want to avoid, creating tactical choice architecture where every dangerous outcome can be resisted at cost of depleting stress pool, with filled stress track leading to Trauma (permanent psychological scars) that eventually force retirement, modeling criminal lifestyle's psychological toll while creating elegant resist-or-accept decision framework that became foundational Forged in the Dark mechanic.

**Stress as Currency**: Unlike hit points (depleted by enemies), Stress is spent by PLAYERS choosing when to resist consequences. This player-agency shift transforms resource management from passive-defensive (avoid damage) to active-tactical (choose which consequences to accept/resist).

**Resistance Mechanics**: When facing consequence, can resist:
- Roll relevant Action rating (d6 pool)
- 6 reduces consequence one level; multiple 6s eliminate it
- Mark Stress equal to 6 - highest die
- Choose whether consequence worth the Stress cost

**Trauma**: When Stress fills completely, clear all Stress BUT take Trauma (permanent condition: Haunted, Paranoid, Ruthless, Unstable). Characters can accumulate up to 4 Traumas, then must retire—can't mechanically continue as scoundrel.

**Psychological Toll**: Trauma models how criminal lifestyle psychologically scars practitioners. Most scoundrels don't die—they burn out, break down, and retire traumatized. This creates realistic arc where successful careers end not in death but in psychological unsustainability.

**Massive Influence**: Stress/Trauma became core FitD mechanic appearing in every Forged in the Dark game and influencing stress/consequence systems broadly. The active-choice resist framework demonstrated how resource management could create meaningful tactical decisions while serving thematic purposes (showing psychological costs).

## Mechanical Framework

### The Stress Track

**9 Stress Boxes**: Standard track with 9 segments

**Marking Stress**: Happens when:
- Resisting consequences (primary use—cost equals 6 minus highest resistance die)
- Pushing yourself (2 Stress for bonus die or improved effect)
- Flashbacks (0-2 Stress based on complexity)
- Assisting allies (1 Stress to give bonus die)
- Special abilities (various playbook moves cost Stress)

**Strategic Resource**: Finite pool requiring allocation decisions—spend on resisting this consequence or save for later?

### Resistance Mechanics

**When Facing Consequence**: Player can choose to resist

**Resistance Roll**: Roll dice equal to most relevant Action rating
- Insight actions (Hunt, Study, Survey, Tinker) resist mental/social consequences
- Prowess actions (Finesse, Prowl, Skirmish, Wreck) resist physical consequences
- Resolve actions (Attune, Command, Consort, Sway) resist supernatural/social consequences

**Resistance Outcomes**:
- **Critical (multiple 6s)**: Fully avoid consequence, Stress cost reduced
- **6**: Reduce consequence severity one level (severe→moderate→minor→avoided)
- **4-5**: Reduce consequence slightly
- **1-3**: No reduction but still mark Stress

**Stress Cost**: Mark Stress equal to 6 - highest die
- Rolled 6: Mark 0 Stress (free avoidance!)
- Rolled 5: Mark 1 Stress
- Rolled 4: Mark 2 Stress
- Rolled 3 or less: Mark 3+ Stress

**Player Choice**: Every consequence can be resisted—question is whether it's worth the Stress

**Examples**:
- Enemy attacks (Level 2 Harm): Resist with Prowess, maybe reduce to Level 1
- Rival discovers your identity: Resist with Resolve, keep secret at Stress cost
- Heat from illegal activity: Resist with Insight, lower profile but depleted

### When Stress Fills

**Clearing Stress**: When all 9 boxes marked, you're at breaking point

**Two Options**:

**1. Overindulge Vice**: Clear all Stress by indulging vice (drinking, gambling, pleasure, violence)
- Roll dice vs. minimum threshold
- Success: Clear Stress, carry on
- Failure: Overindulgence creates problems (lost time/money, enemies made, obligations incurred, reputation damaged)

**2. Take Trauma**: Instead of overindulging, take permanent Trauma and clear all Stress
- Choose Trauma from list or accept fitting condition
- Trauma is permanent character change

### Trauma Conditions

**Common Traumas**:
- **Cold**: Emotionally detached, unfeeling
- **Haunted**: Plagued by memories/ghosts
- **Obsessed**: Fixated on particular goal
- **Paranoid**: Trusting no one
- **Reckless**: Taking unnecessary risks
- **Ruthless**: Willing to do anything
- **Soft**: Showing mercy/compassion inappropriately
- **Unstable**: Erratic, unpredictable
- **Vicious**: Excessive violence

**Trauma Effects**:
- Fictional: Changes how character behaves and relates
- Mechanical: Some provide minor bonuses/penalties
- Advancement: Trauma accumulation creates character evolution arc
- Retirement: At 4 Traumas, must retire (mechanically can't continue)

**Thematic**: Traumas represent how criminal life psychologically scars you. Few scoundrels die in action—most burn out, break down, accumulate traumas until they can't function, then retire.

## Thematic Purpose

Beyond mechanical function, Stress/Trauma serves themes:

**Psychological Toll**: Criminal lifestyle extracts psychological cost

**Active Choices**: You choose which consequences to resist—complicity in your trauma

**Burnout Over Death**: Most careers end in retirement not death

**Escalating Pressure**: Stress accumulates throughout scores creating tension

**Vice Dependence**: Clearing Stress through vices models coping mechanisms

**Character Development**: Traumas create character evolution not just numerical advancement

**Desperate Measures**: Low Stress forces accepting more consequences—desperate criminals take more risks

## Variations Across FitD

Different FitD games adapt Stress/Trauma:

**Scum and Villainy** (space opera): Largely identical to Blades

**Band of Blades** (military): Stress renamed but similar mechanics

**Wicked Ones** (monsters): Ruin instead of Stress (corruption not psychological)

**Beam Saber** (mecha): Stress affects pilot and mecha differently

**Most FitD Games**: Keep core Stress/Trauma framework with thematic adjustments

## Beyond FitD: Stress Systems

**Alien RPG** (Year Zero Engine): Stress dice added to rolls, creating panic risk—different mechanic but similar name/purpose

**Trophy Dark**: Ruin as corruption version of Stress

**10 Candles**: Hope dice depleting toward inevitable darkness

**Various Horror**: Many games adopted stress tracking for psychological horror

## Cultural Impact

**Resist-or-Accept Framework**: Became template for consequence management

**Psychological Resource**: Demonstrated stress could be tactical resource

**Active Player Choice**: Shifting agency from passive (take damage) to active (choose resistance cost)

**Retirement Mechanics**: Traum accumulation providing natural character end

**Vice Systems**: Coping mechanisms as gameplay

**Burnout Modeling**: Representing how dangerous careers psychologically destroy practitioners

**FitD Signature**: Stress resistance became defining FitD characteristic alongside Position/Effect

## Reception and Criticism

**Praise**:
- Brilliant player-agency shift (choosing consequences vs. passively receiving)
- Stress as tactical resource creates interesting decisions
- Trauma modeling psychological toll perfectly
- Retirement arc creates natural character conclusions
- Resist mechanic allows competent characters to avoid some consequences
- Vice systems model coping realistically

**Criticism**:
- Stress management becomes primary strategy (some dislike this focus)
- Trauma accumulation feels inevitable (death-spiral criticism)
- Mathematical optimization of resistance diminishes fiction
- Some want more hit-point-style durability
- Vice complications can frustrate players
- Retirement-by-trauma forces character end some resist

## Design Lessons

Stress/Trauma demonstrates:

**Resource as Choice**: Spendable resources create more engagement than passive tracking

**Thematic Mechanics**: Systems can model themes (psychological toll) while serving gameplay (tactical resource)

**Natural Endings**: Accumulation mechanics provide character arc conclusions

**Player Agency**: Letting players choose consequences increases investment

**Resist Framework**: Template for handling unwanted outcomes
