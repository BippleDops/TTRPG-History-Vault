# Random Character Generation

**First Appeared**: Original D&D (1974)
**Category**: Character Creation / Random Determination

## Overview

Random Character Generation creates characters through dice rolls rather than player choice—rolling 3d6 in order for D&D ability scores, rolling on occupation tables for background, using lifepath systems for history—producing emergent character concepts discovered rather than planned, with debate between random-determination advocates (creates surprise, forces creativity, levels playing field) and point-buy supporters (player agency, concept realization, balanced fairness) representing fundamental split in character creation philosophy, demonstrating tension between planned characters versus discovered-through-randomness.

**Methods**:
- **3d6 in Order** (OD&D): Roll stats sequentially, accept results
- **4d6 drop lowest** (Modern D&D): Roll better stats, assign where wanted
- **Occupation Tables** (DCC): Random backgrounds
- **Lifepaths** (Traveller, Burning Wheel, Cyberpunk): Sequential random career choices
- **Random Everything** (Some OSR): Class, equipment, background all rolled

**Philosophy Split**:
- **Random Advocates**: Surprise, creativity from constraints, fairness, old-school authenticity
- **Point-Buy Advocates**: Player agency, concept realization, balance, modern design

**Impact**: Continues debated. OSR embraces random; mainstream offers options. Represents design philosophy divide.

## Historical Evolution

**1974-1989: Random Standard**
- Original D&D: 3d6 in order (Str, Int, Wis, Dex, Con, Cha)
- No rerolls, no rearranging
- Class available based on stats rolled
- Equipment random
- Character discovered not designed

**1990s-2000s: Options Emerge**
- Point-buy systems introduced
- 4d6 drop lowest became common
- Arranging stats allowed
- Choice increasing

**2010s OSR: Random Revival**
- Old-school games returning to 3d6 in order
- DCC's random occupations
- Embracing random as feature
- Reaction against character optimization

**Modern Mainstream: Player Choice**
- D&D 5E: Standard array, point-buy, OR rolling
- Pathfinder: Point-buy standard
- Character concepts realized through choice

## Random Methods

### 3d6 In Order (Original D&D)

**Process**:
1. Roll 3d6 for Strength
2. Roll 3d6 for Intelligence  
3. Continue for all six abilities in sequence
4. Accept results—no rearranging
5. Class available based on what you rolled
6. Name character fitting stats

**Results**: Completely random, often unoptimized

**Appeal**: Surprise, creativity from constraints, fairness (everyone rolled)

**Criticism**: Can produce unplayable characters, removes player agency, unfair statistical variation

### 4d6 Drop Lowest (Modern Standard)

**Process**:
1. Roll 4d6, drop lowest die, sum remaining
2. Repeat 6 times
3. Assign results to abilities as desired

**Results**: Better stats, player choice in allocation

**Middle Ground**: Random but player-directed

### Occupation/Background Tables (DCC, others)

**Random Backgrounds**: Roll on table for occupation
- Alchemist, Blacksmith, Cheese Maker, Ditch Digger, Eel Fisher...

**Starting Gear**: Based on occupation

**Emergent**: Discover character through random background

### Lifepath Systems (Traveller, Cyberpunk, Burning Wheel)

**Sequential Careers**: Choose or roll career paths sequentially

**Traveller Example**:
1. Roll or choose career (Navy, Merchant, Scout...)
2. Survive term (roll or die/injured)
3. Gain skills from tables
4. Continue or muster out
5. Character emerges from career history

**Emergent History**: Character's past discovered through process

## Design Philosophy

### Pro-Random Arguments

**Surprise**: Don't know what you'll get creates excitement

**Creativity**: Constraints force interesting solutions

**Fairness**: Everyone subject to same randomness

**Discovery**: Finding character rather than planning

**Old-School**: Authentic to gaming roots

**Anti-Optimization**: Prevents min-maxing

**Egalitarian**: Rich/poor stats both valid

### Pro-Choice Arguments

**Agency**: Players should control characters

**Concept Realization**: Want to play specific idea

**Balance**: Everyone should be competitive

**Modern Design**: Player empowerment standard

**Frustration Prevention**: Bad rolls ruin experience

**Accessibility**: New players shouldn't be handicapped

## OSR Revival

**3d6 In Order Renaissance**: Old-school games embracing

**DCC Occupation Tables**: Random backgrounds creating emergent concepts

**Creativity From Constraints**: Arguing randomness enhances creativity

**Anti-Optimization**: Rejecting min-max culture

**Fairness**: Everyone rolls, accepts results

**Community**: OSR culture values random generation

## Hybrid Approaches

**Roll Then Arrange**: Roll stats, assign to abilities

**Reroll Worst**: Roll stats, reroll if too poor

**Organic Growth**: Roll initially, point-buy improvements

**Optional Random**: Offering both methods
