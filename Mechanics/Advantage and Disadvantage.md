# Advantage and Disadvantage

**First Appeared**: [[D&D 5th Edition]] (2014)
**Designer**: WotC Design Team (Mike Mearls, Jeremy Crawford)
**Category**: Resolution Modifier / Simplification Mechanic

## Overview

Advantage and Disadvantage, introduced in D&D 5th Edition (2014), replace complicated stacking modifiers with elegant binary system: when circumstances favor you, roll two d20 and take higher (Advantage); when circumstances hinder you, roll two d20 and take lower (Disadvantage)—eliminating modifier arithmetic (+2 flanking, +1 bless, -2 cover, +5 magic weapon = +6 total) with simple roll-twice-take-better/worse, creating streamlined tactical play praised for speed and clarity, becoming one of 5E's most celebrated innovations and influencing modifier systems across gaming.

**Revolutionary Simplicity**: Replaced:
```
+2 (flanking)  
+1 (bless)
-2 (cover)
+1 (higher ground)
-4 (darkness)
= -2 total
```

With: "Roll twice, take lower" (Disadvantage from cover/darkness)

**Multiple Advantages/Disadvantages Don't Stack**: Either have Advantage or don't—10 favorable conditions still just "Advantage," preventing arithmetic complexity

**Cancellation**: Advantage and Disadvantage cancel 1:1—if you have both, roll normally regardless of quantities

**Massive Success**: Became 5E's signature innovation, praised for streamlining without losing tactical depth

## Mechanical Framework

### Granting Advantage

**Favorable Circumstances**:
- Attacking prone enemy in melee
- Unseen attacker
- Flanking (optional rule)
- Helpful spells (Bless, Guidance, Faerie Fire)
- Superior positioning
- Environmental advantages
- Ally assistance

**Rule**: Roll 2d20, take higher result

**Statistical Impact**: ~+5 average bonus (roughly equivalent to +4 to +5 modifier)

### Granting Disadvantage

**Hindering Circumstances**:
- Attacking while prone
- Attacking unseen target
- Being restrained/grappled
- Negative spells/effects
- Poor positioning
- Environmental hazards
- Exhaustion

**Rule**: Roll 2d20, take lower result

**Statistical Impact**: ~-5 average penalty

### Stacking Rules

**Multiple Sources Don't Stack**:
- 5 sources of Advantage = Just Advantage (roll twice, take higher)
- 10 sources of Disadvantage = Just Disadvantage (roll twice, take lower)

**Simplification**: Prevents arithmetic tracking

**Cancellation**:
- 1 Advantage + 1 Disadvantage = Normal roll
- 5 Advantages + 1 Disadvantage = Normal roll (cancel regardless of quantities)

**Binary State**: Either have Advantage, Disadvantage, or neither—no in-between

## Design Philosophy

**Simplification Goal**: Remove modifier arithmetic slowing play

**Binary Clarity**: Simple yes/no question: "Do I have Advantage?"

**Streamlined Tactics**: Tactical depth without calculation overhead

**Speed**: Faster resolution maintaining d20 feel

**Accessibility**: Easier for new players

**Preserve Tactics**: Positioning and circumstances still matter without complex math

## Statistical Analysis

**Advantage**:
- Approximately +5 average benefit
- Increases critical hit chance significantly (9.75% vs. 5%)
- More valuable for high DC checks

**Disadvantage**:
- Approximately -5 average penalty
- Reduces critical hit chance (0.25%)
- More punishing for low bonuses

**Not Exactly +5/-5**: Advantage/Disadvantage create bell-curve shift, not flat bonus—mathematical probability differs from static modifier

## Cultural Impact

**5E Signature**: Became defining 5E innovation

**Widespread Praise**: Universally celebrated for elegance

**Adaptation**: Other games adopted roll-twice-take-better/worse

**Simplification Trend**: Influenced movement toward streamlining

**New Player Friendly**: Made 5E more accessible

**Speed Improvement**: Noticeably faster play

**Design Vocabulary**: "Advantage" became standard term

## Reception and Criticism

**Praise**:
- Brilliant simplification
- Speeds play noticeably
- Maintains tactical depth
- Easy to remember and apply
- Feels good (rolling twice exciting)
- Perfect for new players

**Criticism**:
- Binary system lacks gradations (no difference between minor and major advantages)
- Doesn't stack can feel wrong (10 advantages = 1 advantage)
- Mathematical properties different from static bonuses
- Some miss precise modifier stacking
- Cancellation can frustrate (many advantages negated by single disadvantage)

## Comparison to Previous Editions

**D&D 3.5/Pathfinder**:
- Countless stacking modifiers
- Complex arithmetic
- Tactical depth through modifier variety
- Slower but more precise

**D&D 5E**:
- Advantage/Disadvantage binary
- No arithmetic
- Fast tactical play
- Less precise but much faster

**Player Preference**: Split between those valuing precision vs. speed

## Design Lessons

Advantage/Disadvantage demonstrates:

**Simplification Can Work**: Removing complexity doesn't necessarily remove depth

**Binary Clarity**: Yes/no questions faster than arithmetic

**Speed Matters**: Faster resolution improves play experience

**Feel vs. Math**: Advantage feels exciting even if mathematically similar to +5

**Accessibility**: Simpler rules welcome new players

## Adaptations in Other Games

**Various d20 Games**: Adopted Advantage/Disadvantage

**Non-d20 Adaptations**: Roll-twice-take-better/worse in other systems

**Conceptual Influence**: Binary favorable/unfavorable in non-roll-twice contexts
