# Beliefs and Instincts

**First Appeared**: [[Burning Wheel]] (2002)
**Designer**: [[Luke Crane]]
**Category**: Character Motivation / Advancement Driver

## Overview

Beliefs and Instincts, created by Luke Crane for Burning Wheel (2002), revolutionize character depth by making convictions (Beliefs—what character believes and pursues) and automatic behaviors (Instincts—what character always does without thinking) into mechanical elements rewarded through Artha (advancement currency), creating system where playing character's psychology truthfully earns mechanical benefits, influencing how narrative games incentivize character-driven play over tactical optimization and demonstrating how belief-driven advancement could create emergent roleplaying rather than imposed characterization.

**Beliefs** are statements of conviction characters pursue:
- "I will protect the village from bandits at any cost"
- "The corrupt noble must be exposed and brought to justice"
- "I must find my lost sister before winter"

**Pursuing Beliefs** earns Artha (Fate, Persona, Deeds points), especially when costly or creating complications. This mechanically rewards playing character's convictions over optimal tactics.

**Instincts** are automatic behaviors:
- "Always keep my sword within reach"
- "Never trust authority figures"
- "Investigate any mystery encountered"

**Playing Instincts** earns Artha, and they trigger automatically without requiring conscious decision—even when disadvantageous. This creates consistent characterization mechanically enforced.

**Revolutionary Impact**: Demonstrated advancement could come from character psychology rather than combat/quest completion, influencing Keys (The Shadow of Yesterday), Drives (various games), and broader movement toward character-goal-driven XP. Showed mechanical incentives could generate authentic roleplaying rather than requiring it through social pressure.

## Mechanical Framework

### Creating Beliefs

**Each character has 3 Beliefs** expressing:

**Structure**: Statement of conviction character will pursue

**Requirements**:
- **Actionable**: Must drive action, not passive observation
- **Challenging**: Should create complications or difficult choices
- **Character-Defining**: Reflects who character is
- **Specific**: Concrete enough to pursue, broad enough to sustain

**Examples**:
- **Good**: "I will unite the warring clans even if it costs me my birthright"
- **Too Vague**: "I want to be heroic"
- **Too Specific**: "I will talk to the innkeeper tomorrow"
- **Passive**: "I believe in justice" (doesn't drive action)

**Changing Beliefs**: Can rewrite between sessions as character evolves

### Earning Artha from Beliefs

**Pursuing Beliefs**:
- **Fate**: Working toward Belief when convenient
- **Persona**: Pursuing Belief despite costs/complications
- **Deeds**: Resolving Belief or pursuing it in grand memorable way

**Playing Against Self-Interest**: Beliefs that create problems earn more than those making life easier

**GM Awards**: Game Master grants Artha based on Belief pursuit quality

**Example**: 
- Belief: "I must expose the corrupt noble"
- Pursue it (Fate): Investigate noble's dealings
- Costly pursuit (Persona): Investigate despite danger to self
- Grand action (Deeds): Publicly accuse noble at royal court risking execution

**Mechanical Benefit**: Artha spent for:
- Bonus dice on rolls
- Rerolls
- Introducing facts into fiction
- Surviving death

**Cycle**: Pursue Beliefs → Earn Artha → Spend Artha on challenges → Enables pursuing Beliefs more effectively

### Creating Instincts

**Each character has 3 Instincts** expressing automatic behaviors:

**Structure**: "Always/Never [action]" or "When [condition], [action]"

**Requirements**:
- **Automatic**: Triggers without conscious choice
- **Consistent**: Happens every applicable time
- **Characterizing**: Defines character through repeated behavior
- **Sometimes Problematic**: Best Instincts create occasional complications

**Examples**:
- "Always draw weapon when threatened"
- "Never leave companion behind"
- "When entering room, identify exits immediately"
- "Investigate any locked door encountered"

**Triggering Automatically**: When fictional circumstance matches Instinct, it happens—even if disadvantageous

### Earning Artha from Instincts

**Following Instincts**:
- **Fate**: Instinct triggers normally
- **Persona**: Instinct triggers creating interesting complications

**Problematic Instincts Rewarded**: Instincts causing problems earn more Artha than convenient ones

**Example**:
- Instinct: "Always draw weapon when threatened"
- Normal (Fate): Draw weapon in combat
- Problematic (Persona): Draw weapon at diplomatic dinner when insulted, creating incident

**Mechanical Reinforcement**: Instincts mechanically rewarded, encouraging consistent characterization

### Traits: Related System

Burning Wheel also uses **Traits** (personality characteristics):
- "Fearless," "Suspicious," "Ambitious," "Stubborn"
- Earn Artha when playing Traits
- Can invoke for mechanical bonuses

Traits, Beliefs, and Instincts together create comprehensive psychological character definition.

## Design Philosophy

Beliefs and Instincts embody:

**Character-Driven Advancement**: XP from psychology not murder

**Mechanical Psychology**: Convictions and behaviors as game elements

**Costly Beliefs Rewarded**: Complications earn more than convenience

**Authentic Roleplaying**: Incentivizing truthful character play

**Emergent Characterization**: Mechanics generate rather than impose characterization

**Player Agency**: You choose Beliefs/Instincts defining character

**Evolution**: Can change Beliefs as character develops

## Cultural Impact

**Advancement Revolution**: Showed XP could come from character goals

**Keys Lineage**: Directly influenced The Shadow of Yesterday's Keys

**Drives/Aspirations**: Various games adopted goal-based advancement

**PbtA Drives**: Some PbtA games included Drive mechanics

**Character Depth**: Raised standards for psychological complexity

**Design Vocabulary**: "Beliefs" became shorthand for character-goal systems

**Burning Wheel Philosophy**: Emblematic of Luke Crane's design approach

## Games Using Similar Systems

**The Shadow of Yesterday** (2004): **Keys**—advancement from pursuing specific character goals (directly inspired by Beliefs)

**Lady Blackbird** (2009): **Keys**—refined version

**Burning Empires** (2006): Beliefs/Instincts carried forward

**Mouse Guard** (2008): Goals (simplified Beliefs)

**Torchbearer** (2013): Adapted for dungeon-crawling

**Various Narrative Games**: Drive, Ambition, Goal mechanics inspired by Beliefs

## Comparison to Traditional XP

**Traditional (D&D-style)**:
- Kill monsters: Gain XP
- Complete quests: Gain XP
- Accumulate: Level up
- Incentive: Combat and completion

**Beliefs/Instincts (Burning Wheel)**:
- Pursue Beliefs: Earn Artha
- Play Instincts: Earn Artha
- Accumulate: Spend on immediate needs or save
- Incentive: Character-driven action and consistent portrayal

**Behavioral Difference**: 
- Traditional: Encouraged combat optimization
- Beliefs: Encourages pursuing character goals even when suboptimal tactically

## Reception and Criticism

**Praise**:
- Brilliant character-driven advancement
- Mechanically rewards authentic roleplaying
- Creates emergent characterization
- Artha system elegant
- Beliefs/Instincts define characters psychologically
- Costly Beliefs earning more prevents abuse

**Criticism**:
- Requires thoughtful Belief writing
- Some players struggle creating good Beliefs
- GM must remember to award Artha consistently
- Can feel like optimizing Beliefs for Artha rather than authentic character expression
- Instincts can create frustrating automatic behaviors
- Complexity alongside Burning Wheel's other systems

## Design Lessons

Beliefs/Instincts demonstrate:

**Incentivize What You Want**: Mechanically reward desired play

**Psychology as Mechanics**: Internal states can be game elements

**Costs Create Interest**: Complications more rewarding than convenience

**Player Authorship**: Letting players define character goals

**Advancement Alternatives**: XP needn't come from combat

**Emergent Roleplay**: Systems generating authentic character portrayal
