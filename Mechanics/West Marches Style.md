# West Marches Style

**First Appeared**: Ben Robbins blog posts (2007-2008)
**Designer**: Ben Robbins (crystallized existing practice)
**Category**: Campaign Structure / Open Table Framework

## Overview

West Marches Style, documented by Ben Robbins in 2007-2008 blog posts, provides campaign structure for large open-table groups (10-20+ players) where players (not GM) initiate sessions by organizing expeditions into dangerous wilderness, rotating participants each session based on availability, with shared campaign wiki tracking discoveries so any player combination can adventure while maintaining continuity, solving scheduling problems for large groups while creating player-driven exploration where initiative and planning determine what gets explored.

**Core Principles**:

**Player-Initiated Sessions**: Players organize expeditions and recruit party

**Rotating Participants**: Different player combinations each session

**Shared Wiki**: Public documentation of discoveries

**West = Civilization**: Town/fort is safe but boring

**Marches = Wilderness**: Dangerous unexplored lands to east

**No Backtracking**: Once returned to town, can't revisit same session

**Player-Driven**: Players decide where to explore, what to investigate

**GM Prepares Responsively**: Prep areas players indicate interest in, not everything

**Large Player Pool**: 10-40 players with 3-6 attending each session

## Mechanical Framework

### Campaign Setup

**The Town** (West):
- Safe starting location
- No adventures in town
- Downtime and resupply between expeditions
- Information gathering and planning

**The Marches** (East):
- Dangerous wilderness
- Unknown threats and treasures
- Hex-crawl or pointcrawl
- Player exploration reveals content

**Large Player Pool**:
- Many players registered
- 3-6 attend each session
- Scheduling flexible around availability

### Session Structure

**Pre-Session**:
1. Players organize expedition ("Let's explore those ruins we heard about")
2. Recruit party from available players
3. Declare destination/goal
4. GM preps relevant area

**During Session**:
1. Depart town heading east
2. Explore/adventure in Marches
3. Face dangers, discover locations
4. Return to town (mandatory)

**Post-Session**:
- Update shared wiki with discoveries
- Share information with all players
- Plan future expeditions

**No Cliffhangers**: Each session must conclude with town return

### Information Sharing

**Shared Wiki/Map**: 
- All players can access
- Discoveries documented
- Rumors and hooks visible
- Maps updated collaboratively

**In-Character Communication**: Players share information in-character (letters, gatherings)

**Transparency**: All players know what any character knows

**Collaborative Mapping**: Players contribute to shared understanding

### GM Preparation

**Prep What Players Target**: Not everything pre-prepared

**Rumors and Hooks**: Seed multiple adventure possibilities

**Respond to Interest**: Develop areas players indicate wanting to explore

**Consistent World**: Locations remain consistent even with different parties

**No Railroad**: Players choose destinations; GM creates content there

### Character Continuity

**Characters Persist**: Same character across sessions they attend

**Level Disparity Acceptable**: Different experience levels in same party okay (danger scales, players aware of risks)

**Shared Risk**: Everyone knows Marches are dangerous

**Death Happens**: Character death accepted as possibility

**New Characters**: Easy to introduce joining next expedition

## Design Philosophy

West Marches embodies:

**Player-Driven**: Players initiate and direct

**Open Table**: Flexible attendance without breaking campaign

**Exploration Focus**: Discovery primary gameplay

**Player Communication**: In-character coordination between sessions

**Shared World**: Collective ownership through documentation

**No Plot**: Emergent stories from exploration

**Responsive Prep**: GM creates content players pursue

## Cultural Impact

**Open Table Revival**: Enabled large-group gaming

**Player-Initiative**: Shifted agency to players for session organization

**Shared Documentation**: Wiki/map as collective resource

**Scheduling Solution**: Flexible attendance maintaining campaign

**OSR Adoption**: Became common in Old School Renaissance

**Hex-Crawl Renaissance**: Revitalized exploration gaming

**Blog Documentation**: Robbins's posts became definitive reference

## Variations and Adaptations

**Urban Marches**: City exploration instead of wilderness

**Dungeon Marches**: Megadungeon as the Marches

**Seas/Space Marches**: Nautical or space exploration

**Digital Tools**: Discord, wikis, Virtual Tabletops enabling coordination

**Published Adventures**: Some products designed explicitly for West Marches

## Reception and Criticism

**Praise**:
- Solves large-group scheduling brilliantly
- Player-driven creates investment
- Exploration emphasis refreshing
- Open table flexibility excellent
- Shared documentation creates community
- Emergent stories from discovery

**Criticism**:
- Requires disciplined player organization
- GM prep still substantial
- Some players want more narrative structure
- Doesn't suit all game types
- Information management overhead
- Can feel aimless without clear goals

## Design Lessons

West Marches demonstrates:

**Player Initiative**: Empowering players to organize creates engagement

**Open Tables**: Flexible attendance possible with right structure

**Shared Documentation**: Transparency creates community

**Exploration Gaming**: Discovery as sufficient gameplay

**Responsive GMing**: Prep based on player interest

**Large Groups**: Rotating participants can work

## Modern Applications

**Discord West Marches**: Digital tools enabling coordination

**Living Campaigns**: Similar open-table approach

**Adventure League**: Organized play shares some principles

**Streaming**: Some actual-plays use rotating casts

**Megadungeon Campaigns**: Similar exploration focus
