# Heat and Wanted Level

**First Appeared**: [[Blades in the Dark]] (2017)
**Designer**: [[John Harper]]
**Category**: Attention Tracking / Pressure System

## Overview

Heat and Wanted Level in Blades in the Dark track law enforcement attention on criminal crew—accumulating Heat from scores (especially violent/public ones) until reaching threshold triggering Entanglement (raid, arrest, witnesses flipping), with Wanted Level (0-4) determining Heat threshold and representing how notorious crew is, creating pressure system where success breeds attention breeds consequences, forcing crews to occasionally lay low or face escalating law enforcement response, demonstrating how mechanical attention-tracking creates campaign pressure and consequences for criminal activity.

**Heat**: Accumulates from scores based on visibility/violence

**Wanted Level**: Notoriety rating (0-4) determining Heat capacity

**Entanglements**: Complications when Heat fills, rolled randomly from table

**Pressure System**: Success creates attention creates problems

**Campaign Dynamic**: Balancing activity with lying low

**Impact**: Influenced attention/heat mechanics in criminal/heist games

## Mechanics

**Gaining Heat**: After each score, gain Heat based on:
- Mission visibility (public vs. quiet)
- Violence level
- Target prominence
- Witnesses/evidence

**Wanted Level**: Crew's notoriety (0-4)
- Determines Heat capacity before Entanglement
- Higher Wanted = lower threshold

**Entanglement**: When Heat fills:
- Roll on Entanglement table
- Face complication (arrest, interrogation, witnesses, reprisals)
- Clear some Heat
- Crew must deal with consequences

**Reducing Heat**: Downtime action to lower Heat strategically

## Design Philosophy

**Consequences of Success**: Being good at crime draws attention

**Pressure Cycles**: Active periods alternating with laying low

**Heat Management**: Strategic resource (acceptable risk vs. too hot)

**Entanglements**: Keeping crew in trouble, driving stories

## Cultural Impact

Demonstrated attention-tracking creates criminal campaign pressure. Influenced heist/criminal game design. Showed success could create problems maintaining drama.

## Reception

**Praise**: Creates authentic criminal pressure, drives drama, balances success with consequences

**Criticism**: Can feel punitive, some Entanglements frustrating, math tracking
