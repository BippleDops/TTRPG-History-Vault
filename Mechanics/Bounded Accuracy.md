# Bounded Accuracy

**First Appeared**: [[D&D 5th Edition]] (2014)
**Designers**: Mike Mearls, Jeremy Crawford, WotC team
**Category**: Design Principle / Power Curve Philosophy

## Overview

Bounded Accuracy, core D&D 5E design principle, restricts how high bonuses can grow—keeping attack bonuses, skill modifiers, AC, and DCs within relatively narrow ranges (+0 to +11 typical) throughout all 20 levels—ensuring low-level threats (goblins) remain at least theoretically dangerous to high-level characters while maintaining bounded target numbers, contrasting sharply with 3.5E/Pathfinder's escalating bonuses (+30+ possible) creating separate numerical tiers, demonstrating how constraining modifier growth could improve gameplay by maintaining threat relevance, reducing arithmetic, and preventing rocket-tag high-level combat.

**Principle**: Bonuses grow slowly, caps exist

**Proficiency Bonus**: Primary growth, scales +2 to +6 across 20 levels (not +1 per level)

**Ability Scores**: Cap at 20 (rare exceptions)—+5 modifier maximum

**AC**: Typically 12-20, rarely exceeding

**DCs**: 10-30 range, most 10-20

**Attack Bonuses**: Rarely exceed +11 even at level 20

**Impact**: Changed D&D fundamentally. Influenced how d20 games think about power curves. Debated intensely (supporters love maintained relevance; critics miss epic power).

## Philosophy

**Threat Relevance**: Goblins can (theoretically) hit ancient dragons

**Bounded Numbers**: Preventing runaway modifier inflation

**Math Accessibility**: Smaller numbers easier to handle

**Unified Play**: All levels use similar DCs

**Horizontal > Vertical**: More abilities not just bigger bonuses

## Comparison to 3.5E/Pathfinder

**3.5E/PF**: Bonuses grow massively (+30+ possible)—creates numerical tiers where high-level characters mathematically immune to low-level threats

**5E**: Bonuses capped—ancient red dragon AC 22 is threatening but not impossible for level 1 with natural 20

## Reception

**Praise**: Maintains threat relevance, reduces arithmetic, prevents rocket-tag

**Criticism**: Reduces epic feeling, flattens power curve excessively, goblins threatening ancient dragons breaks verisimilitude

## Legacy

Became defining 5E characteristic. Influenced bounded-modifier thinking. Demonstrated alternative to escalation-focused design.
