# Montage Resolution

**First Appeared**: [[Blades in the Dark]] (2017)
**Designer**: [[John Harper]]
**Category**: Time Compression / Downtime Mechanic

## Overview

Montage Resolution in Blades in the Dark compresses downtime activities (recovery, projects, vice indulgence) into structured montage sequence where each player takes one downtime action (recover, work on project, reduce heat, train) rolling dice to determine outcomes, then play moves immediately to next score—creating film-style time compression where days/weeks of between-heist activities resolve in 10-15 minutes of table time, demonstrating how structured downtime with clear action menus could provide character development and progression without session-consuming minutiae.

**One Action Per Character**: Each player chooses one downtime activity

**Roll and Resolve**: Quick mechanical resolution

**Move Forward**: Immediately to next score after all resolved

**Film Montage**: Like training montages or time-passage sequences in movies

**Impact**: Influenced downtime systems in FitD and beyond. Showed how to give downtime mechanical teeth without consuming sessions.

## Downtime Actions

**Recover**: Healing harm and clearing stress

**Work on Long-Term Project**: Advance project clock

**Reduce Heat**: Lower attention on crew

**Train**: Improve abilities

**Indulge Vice**: Clear stress through vice activities (with risks)

**Acquire Asset**: Gain temporary item/contact

**Crafting**: Create special items (some playbooks)

## Design Philosophy

**Compression**: Handle necessary activities quickly

**Meaningful Choices**: Each action matters

**Advancement Opportunities**: Growth through downtime not just scores

**Resource Management**: Spending downtime strategically

**Time Jumps**: Skip boring detailed recovery/prep

## Cultural Impact

Structured downtime became FitD standard. Influenced how narrative games handle between-adventure activities. Demonstrated compression could maintain importance while saving time.
