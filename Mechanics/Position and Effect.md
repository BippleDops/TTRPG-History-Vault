# Position and Effect

**First Appeared**: [[Blades in the Dark]] (2017)
**Designer**: [[John Harper]]
**Category**: Resolution Framework

## Overview

Position and Effect, created by John Harper for Blades in the Dark (2017), revolutionizes RPG resolution by replacing static difficulty ratings with two-axis evaluation framework: Position (controlled/risky/desperate—how dangerous is this action?) and Effect (limited/standard/great—how much can you accomplish?), enabling players to make informed risk-reward tactical decisions based on fictional positioning while maintaining narrative coherence, creating dynamic gameplay where players assess danger versus payoff contextually rather than abstractly.

**Position** answers: "How much danger are you in?"
- **Controlled**: Low risk, probably safe
- **Risky**: Significant danger, might go badly
- **Desperate**: Extreme danger, terrible if it fails

**Effect** answers: "How much can you accomplish?"
- **Limited**: Partial progress toward goal
- **Standard**: Achieve your intent
- **Great**: Exceed expectations, superior outcome

The genius: Position and Effect replace "difficulty number" thinking with contextual evaluation. Instead of "this lock is DC 15," you assess: "Picking this lock is Risky position (guards patrol nearby) with Standard effect (one lock opened)." Then players can trade position for effect ("I'll accept Desperate position for Great effect—breaking through fast but loudly") or seek ways to improve either through preparation, abilities, or fictional changes.

**Massive Influence**: Position and Effect became one of contemporary design's most adopted frameworks, appearing across Forged in the Dark games and adapted/referenced in numerous non-FitD titles. The two-axis evaluation replaced traditional difficulty in many narrative games, demonstrating how fictional assessment could create tactical depth without simulation complexity.

## Mechanical Framework

### Setting Position

GM evaluates danger level based on:

**Fiction**: What's the situation? Guards nearby? Alarm systems? Time pressure?

**Factors Improving Position** (toward Controlled):
- Superior preparation
- Advantageous circumstances
- Relevant abilities/equipment
- Teamwork and support
- Surprising/avoiding opposition

**Factors Worsening Position** (toward Desperate):
- Poor preparation
- Disadvantageous circumstances
- Lacking appropriate tools
- Working alone against opposition
- Enemy awareness and readiness

**Position Isn't Fixed**: Can improve/worsen through fictional changes before rolling

**Example**: Picking lock on mansion door:
- Controlled: At night, guards on break, you have master tools
- Risky: Normal conditions, guards patrol occasionally
- Desperate: Daylight, guards nearby, improvised lockpicks

### Setting Effect

GM evaluates potential accomplishment:

**Scale**: How significant is obstacle relative to your capability?

**Factors Improving Effect** (toward Great):
- Superior quality/potency of tools/abilities
- Overwhelming force/preparation
- Exploiting weaknesses
- Exceptional circumstances favoring you

**Factors Reducing Effect** (toward Limited):
- Inadequate tools
- Tough opposition
- Protected/prepared target
- Poor circumstances

**Effect Isn't Fixed**: Can improve through fictional changes, teamwork (Setup actions), or accepting worse Position

**Example**: Attacking armored knight with knife:
- Limited: Knife barely threatens armor
- Standard: Knife finds gaps in armor
- Great: Knife penetrates critically (maybe targeting weak point, or knight is already wounded)

### Trading Position for Effect (and vice versa)

Players can negotiate:

**Push Yourself**: Accept worse Position for better Effect
- "I'll charge recklessly (Desperate) to hit hard (Great effect)"

**Play It Safe**: Accept worse Effect for better Position
- "I'll stay defensive (Controlled) even though I barely damage them (Limited effect)"

**This Creates Player Choice**: No optimal solution—choose preferred risk-reward profile

### Resolution with Position and Effect

Once Position and Effect are established, roll Action dice:

**Outcomes Modified by Position**:
- **6 (Full Success)**: Achieve effect without consequences
- **4-5 (Partial)**: Achieve effect BUT suffer consequences (severity based on Position—Controlled = minor, Risky = moderate, Desperate = severe)
- **1-3 (Failure)**: Don't achieve effect AND suffer consequences (based on Position)

**Position Determines Consequence Severity**, NOT success probability (dice handle that).

**Example** (Risky position, Standard effect):
- 6: Pick lock successfully, guards don't notice
- 4-5: Pick lock BUT guards heard something (Risky consequence)
- 1-3: Don't pick lock AND guards investigate noise (Risky consequence)

### Modifying Before Rolling

Players can improve Position/Effect before rolling through:

**Setup Actions**: Allies create advantages (Controlled position to create opening)

**Flashbacks**: Establishing earlier preparation

**Using Abilities**: Special moves/equipment improving situation

**Fictional Changes**: Changing approach/circumstances

**Devil's Bargains**: GM offers better Position/Effect for accepting complication

## Comparison to Traditional Difficulty

### Traditional Difficulty (D&D-style)

**Static**: Lock has DC 15 regardless of circumstances

**Binary Success**: Beat DC or don't

**Modifiers**: Circumstantial bonuses/penalties to roll (+2 for tools, -5 for darkness)

**GM Sets Number**: Difficulty predetermined

**Skill-Based**: Your skill modifier primary determinant

### Position and Effect

**Contextual**: Same lock might be Controlled or Desperate based on circumstances

**Two-Axis**: Danger AND accomplishment evaluated separately

**Fictional**: Based on established situation not abstract difficulty

**Player Input**: Can change circumstances affecting assessment

**Dynamic**: Position/Effect change as fiction changes

**No Single Number**: Framework not difficulty class

## Design Philosophy

Position and Effect embodies several principles:

**Fiction-First**: Mechanics follow from established fictional situation

**Contextual Evaluation**: Same action has different risk/reward based on circumstances

**Player Agency**: Understanding risk-reward enables informed decisions

**Dynamic Tactics**: Improving situation through fictional changes creates gameplay

**No Optimal Solution**: Different position/effect combinations suit different goals

**Transparent Assessment**: Players know danger and potential before rolling

**Emergent Complexity**: Simple framework creates complex tactical considerations

## Cultural Impact

**FitD Standard**: Became defining characteristic of Forged in the Dark games

**Design Vocabulary**: "Position and effect" became standard terminology even outside FitD

**Difficulty Replacement**: Many narrative games adopted similar frameworks instead of difficulty numbers

**Tactical Narrative Gaming**: Demonstrated fiction-first didn't mean non-tactical

**Educational**: Used in teaching game design as example of elegant problem-solving

**Influential Framework**: One of 2010s' most adopted mechanical innovations

## Reception and Criticism

**Praise**:
- Elegant replacement for static difficulty
- Creates meaningful tactical decisions
- Maintains fictional coherence
- Player agency through informed choices
- Scales across challenge types
- Generates dynamic gameplay

**Criticism**:
- Requires GM system mastery for consistent assessment
- Can feel subjective/arbitrary if GM inconsistent
- Some players prefer numerical objectivity
- Learning curve for traditional gamers
- Position/Effect interaction complexity for newcomers

## Comparison to Similar Systems

**PbtA Fictional Triggers**: Position/Effect adds risk-reward layer to fictional positioning

**FATE Aspects**: Both use fictional elements mechanically but different frameworks

**Traditional Advantages**: Position functions like advantage/disadvantage but with gradations

**Success Levels**: Effect similar to degree-of-success but evaluated before rolling

## Legacy and Influence

Position and Effect established as fundamental innovation comparable to PbtA Moves or FATE Aspects—specific mechanical framework so successful it became genre-defining and broadly influential beyond origin game.

**FitD Games**: Mandatory element defining Forged in the Dark

**Narrative Gaming**: Standard reference for fictional positioning creating tactical play

**Design Education**: Taught as elegant solution to difficulty/risk assessment

**Ongoing Evolution**: Designers continue refining and adapting framework
