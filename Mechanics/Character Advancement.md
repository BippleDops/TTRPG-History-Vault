# Character Advancement

**First Appeared**: Original D&D (1974) with levels/XP, evolved extensively across gaming
**Category**: Progression System / Character Development

## Overview

Character Advancement encompasses methods for improving characters through play—traditional Experience Points and Levels (D&D's kill-monsters-gain-XP-level-up), Skill Use systems (improve what you use—RuneQuest, Call of Cthulhu), Milestone advancement (story beats not XP—D&D 5E option), goal-based advancement (Keys, Beliefs, Drives), and various hybrids—creating gameplay loops where challenge-overcome-reward drives continued play, with evolution from combat-XP to diverse advancement methods reflecting shifting design priorities from tactical challenge to character-driven narratives.

**Major Advancement Types**:

**1. XP and Levels** (D&D tradition):
- Kill monsters, complete quests → gain XP
- Accumulate XP → level up
- Levels grant new abilities, better stats
- Power curve: exponential growth

**2. Skill Use** (RuneQuest, CoC):
- Use skill → mark checkbox
- Between sessions, roll to improve marked skills
- Skills improve through practice
- Organic: What you use improves

**3. Milestones** (Modern D&D option):
- Level up at story beats
- GM determines when, not XP math
- Narrative pacing controls advancement

**4. Goal-Based** (Indie games):
- Keys (pursue character goals → XP)
- Beliefs (follow convictions → Artha)
- Drives (advance personal agendas)
- Character-driven not combat-driven

**5. Playbook Advancement** (PbtA):
- Mark XP from various triggers (failures, bonds, drives)
- At thresholds, choose improvements from playbook
- Horizontal more than vertical growth

**Impact**: Advancement systems fundamentally shape what games reward and thus how they're played.

## Historical Evolution

**1974-1989: XP for Combat**
- Original D&D: 1 XP per gold piece value, XP for monster HD
- AD&D: XP from defeating monsters primarily
- Incentive: Combat optimization, treasure acquisition

**1990s: Diversification**
- Story XP appears
- Skill-use systems (RuneQuest model)
- World of Darkness: XP from GM award, story participation
- Shadowrun: Karma for runs and roleplay

**2000s: Narrativist Revolution**
- Keys (TSOY): XP from character goals
- Beliefs (Burning Wheel): Advancement from convictions
- PbtA: XP from failures, bonds, drives
- Shift from combat to character-driven

**2010s-Present: Variety**
- Milestones common (5E option)
- No-advancement games (some one-shots)
- Horizontal advancement (new abilities not power increases)
- Hybrid systems combining methods

## Advancement Method Comparison

### XP for Combat (D&D tradition)

**Incentivizes**: Fighting monsters, completing quests, gaining treasure

**Advantages**: Clear, quantifiable, reward for overcoming challenges

**Disadvantages**: Encourages murder-hobo behavior, doesn't reward roleplay/exploration directly, grindy

**Power Curve**: Exponential vertical growth

### Skill Use (RuneQuest/CoC)

**Incentivizes**: Using varied skills, attempting challenges

**Advantages**: Organic (practice improves), encourages experimentation, realistic feel

**Disadvantages**: Successful characters don't improve challenged skills, incentivizes failure-seeking, bookkeeping

**Power Curve**: Gradual horizontal growth

### Milestones (Narrative Pacing)

**Incentivizes**: Story progression, whatever GM deems important

**Advantages**: Simple (no XP tracking), pacing control, story-appropriate

**Disadvantages**: GM-dependent, lacks mechanical clarity, some miss XP accumulation satisfaction

**Power Curve**: GM-controlled

### Goal-Based (Keys/Beliefs)

**Incentivizes**: Playing character authentically, pursuing personal goals

**Advantages**: Character-driven, rewards roleplay, creates emergent characterization

**Disadvantages**: Requires thoughtful goal-creation, can be optimized, subjective GM awards

**Power Curve**: Variable based on goal pursuit

### PbtA XP Triggers

**Incentivizes**: Failures (learning through mistakes), relationship development, playbook-specific triggers

**Advantages**: Failure becomes advancement opportunity, supports character focus, varied triggers

**Disadvantages**: Can encourage failure-seeking, trigger hunting, math tracking

**Power Curve**: Moderate horizontal growth

## Design Philosophy

Advancement systems reveal design priorities:

**What's Rewarded Defines Play**: XP from combat → combat-focused play. XP from character goals → character-focused play.

**Power Curve Philosophy**: 
- Vertical (getting stronger) vs. Horizontal (getting more options)
- Bounded (caps exist) vs. Unbounded (infinite growth)

**Pacing Control**: How fast characters improve affects campaign length

**Player vs. GM**: Who controls advancement (XP math vs. GM milestone)?

**Incentive Structures**: What behaviors does advancement encourage?

## Cultural Impact

**Evolution from Combat**: Shift from murder-XP to diverse advancement

**Narrativist Innovations**: Goal-based advancement widely adopted in indie gaming

**Milestone Adoption**: Even D&D offering non-XP options

**Advancement Debates**: Ongoing discussions about optimal systems

**Design Vocabulary**: XP/milestones/Keys as standard terms

## Reception and Debates

**Combat XP Defenders**: Clear rewards for overcoming challenges, satisfying accumulation, quantifiable

**Combat XP Critics**: Encourages murder-hobo, doesn't reward roleplay, grindy

**Milestone Supporters**: Story-appropriate pacing, simple tracking

**Milestone Critics**: GM-dependent, lacks player agency, misses XP satisfaction

**Goal-Based Advocates**: Character-driven, authentic roleplay rewards

**Goal-Based Skeptics**: Optimization concerns, subjective, requires investment

**Skill-Use Fans**: Organic realistic improvement

**Skill-Use Critics**: Encourages failure-seeking, successful don't improve

## Design Lessons

**Advancement Shapes Play**: What's rewarded determines behavior

**Match Creative Agenda**: Gamist games reward challenge-overcoming; narrativist games reward character-goals

**Consider Incentives**: What does your advancement encourage?

**Pacing**: How fast should characters improve?

**Player Agency**: Who controls advancement rate?

## Modern Trends

**Hybrid Systems**: Combining methods (XP + milestones, combat + roleplay)

**Horizontal Growth**: New options over bigger numbers

**Varied Triggers**: Multiple XP sources

**Transparent**: Clear advancement methods

**Optional**: Some games offering multiple advancement options

**No Advancement**: One-shots sometimes skip advancement entirely
