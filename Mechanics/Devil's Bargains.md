# Devil's Bargains

**First Appeared**: [[Blades in the Dark]] (2017)
**Designer**: [[John Harper]]
**Category**: Complication Trading / Risk-Reward Mechanic

## Overview

Devil's Bargains, created by John Harper for Blades in the Dark (2017), provide simple elegant mechanic where GM offers bonus die for accepting complication before rolling—"You can add +1d to your pool, BUT witnesses will see you" or "Take bonus die, BUT you owe the contact a favor"—creating Faustian bargain moment where players trade future problems for immediate advantage, influencing risk-reward mechanics across narrative gaming and demonstrating how offering player-agency complications creates more engagement than GM-imposed consequences.

**Structure**: Before rolling, GM offers:
- **Benefit**: +1d to your dice pool (significant boost)
- **Cost**: Complication that will occur regardless of roll outcome

**Player Choice**: Accept (get bonus die, accept complication) or decline (normal roll, no complication)

**Brilliant Simplicity**: One sentence mechanic creating tactical and narrative depth

**Influence**: Appeared across FitD games and adapted in various narrative systems as template for player-chosen complications

## Mechanical Framework

### Offering Devil's Bargains

GM can offer before any Action roll:

**Bonus**: Always +1d (flat bonus regardless of circumstance)

**Complication Examples**:
- **Attention**: "Witnesses see you," "Guards notice commotion," "Rival learns of your involvement"
- **Obligations**: "You owe contact a favor," "Ally expects repayment," "Debt to dangerous person"
- **Consequences**: "Equipment damaged," "Relationship strained," "Reputation affected"
- **Exposure**: "Secret revealed," "Identity discovered," "True motives suspected"
- **Costs**: "Expensive resources consumed," "Valuable item lost," "Payment required"
- **Enemies**: "Make new enemy," "Rival takes interest," "Danger follows you"

**Complication Occurs Regardless**: Whether you succeed or fail, accepted Bargain's complication happens

**Player Decision**: Weigh immediate advantage (bonus die) vs. future cost (complication)

### Multiple Bargains

**Up to 2 Per Roll**: GM and other players can each offer one Devil's Bargain

**Different Complications**: Each Bargain has unique complication

**Stacking Bonuses**: Accepting multiple Bargains = +2d (significant)

**Example**:
- GM offers: "+1d but guards hear you"
- Another player offers: "+1d but I want credit for this score"
- Accept both: +2d but face both complications

### When to Offer

**Desperate Situations**: When players need help

**Interesting Complications**: When cool complication fits moment

**Low Rolls**: If player rolled poorly, offering retroactive Bargain

**Thematic Moments**: When Faustian choice enhances drama

**Not Every Roll**: Overuse dilutes impact

### Accepting Bargains

**Pure Player Choice**: No mechanical forcing—always optional

**Tactical Consideration**: More dice improve success chances significantly

**Narrative Consideration**: Is complication worth it?

**Stress Alternative**: Better than spending Stress for bonus die (costs 2 Stress, Bargain is free upfront)

**Risk Assessment**: Weighing known complication vs. unknown consequences from failure

## Design Philosophy

Devil's Bargains embody:

**Player Agency**: You choose complications rather than having them imposed

**Visible Trade-Offs**: Clear cost-benefit evaluation

**Faustian Bargains**: Short-term gain for long-term cost

**Collaborative**: Other players can offer Bargains, distributing GM role

**Fiction Generation**: Complications create story hooks

**Tactical Depth**: Simple mechanic with strategic implications

**Temptation**: Mechanically attractive but narratively costly

## Thematic Purpose

Beyond mechanics, Devil's Bargains serve themes:

**Criminal Compromises**: Scoundrels make deals they'll regret

**Desperation**: Accepting bad bargains when desperate

**Faustian Tales**: Trading future for present

**Mounting Problems**: Accumulated Bargains create web of complications

**Choice and Consequence**: Active decisions creating problems

**Player Complicity**: You chose this, can't blame GM

## Variations Across FitD

Different FitD games adapt Devil's Bargains:

**Scum and Villainy**: Largely identical to Blades

**Band of Blades**: "Risky Tactics" similar concept

**Beam Saber**: Bargains appropriate to mecha combat

**Heart**: "Delve Deeper" similar risk-reward

**Wicked Ones**: "Wicked Bargains" for monster appropriate complications

**Most FitD**: Keep core framework with thematic adjustments

**Player-Agency Complications**: Shifted from GM-imposed to player-chosen

**Faustian Gaming**: Mechanized deal-making with future costs

**Bonus Die Economy**: +1d as standard complication price

**Collaborative Complications**: Other players offering Bargains distributed GM role

**Design Vocabulary**: "Devil's Bargain" became recognized term

**Fiction Generation**: Template for creating narrative hooks mechanically

## Comparison to Similar Mechanics

**FATE Compels**: GM offers Fate Point for accepting complication
- Similar: Player-chosen, immediate benefit, narrative complication
- Different: Currency (Fate Point) vs dice (+1d), timing (anytime vs. before roll)

**Advantage/Disadvantage**: D&D 5E's roll-twice
- Similar: Modifying rolls
- Different: No complication trade-off, circumstantial not chosen

**Hero Points**: Various games' heroic bennies
- Similar: Bonus resources
- Different: Spent by player when wanted, not offered with complications

**Bennies/Chips**: Savage Worlds and others
- Similar: Resource for bonuses
- Different: Spent not offered-with-conditions

## Reception and Criticism

**Praise**:
- Simple elegant mechanic
- Creates player agency over complications
- Generates story hooks naturally
- Faustian bargain theme perfect
- Tactical depth from one sentence
- Other players offering creates collaboration
- Temptation mechanically attractive

**Criticism**:
- Some players always/never accept (removes choice through predictability)
- Can feel like GM bribing players
- Complications sometimes arbitrary
- Over-offering dilutes impact
- Some prefer complications arise organically not mechanically

## Design Lessons

Devil's Bargains teach:

**Player-Chosen Complications > GM-Imposed**: Agency over consequences increases engagement

**Visible Trade-Offs**: Clear cost-benefit enables interesting decisions

**Mechanical Temptation**: Attractive bonus makes refusal difficult

**Fiction from Mechanics**: Complications generate narrative hooks

**Collaborative Tools**: Distributing GM responsibilities to all players

**Simple Profundity**: One-sentence mechanic with deep implications

## Common Bargain Types

**Information Leaks**: Someone learns something

**Attention**: Increased exposure/heat

**Obligations**: Owing favors/debts

**Collateral Damage**: Unintended consequences

**Relationship Strain**: Connections damaged

**Resource Costs**: Equipment/money lost

**Future Threats**: Creating later problems

**Moral Compromises**: Doing questionable things
