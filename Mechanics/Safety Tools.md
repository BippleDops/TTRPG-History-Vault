# Safety Tools

**First Appeared**: Multiple tools developed 2000s-2010s
**Designers**: Ron Edwards, John Stavropoulos, Brie Sheldon, others
**Category**: Consent and Safety Frameworks

## Overview

Safety Tools encompass various mechanics enabling players to establish boundaries, pause uncomfortable content, and ensure consensual play—including X-Card (tap card to remove content, no explanation needed), Lines and Veils (pre-established hard boundaries and fade-to-black topics), Script Change (rewind/fast-forward/pause controls), and Session Zero (pre-game discussion)—revolutionizing how gaming handles potentially sensitive content, becoming standard practice in indie gaming and increasingly mainstream, demonstrating how explicit consent mechanisms improve experiences for everyone.

**X-Card** (John Stavropoulos, 2012): Physical card anyone can tap to immediately remove content without explanation

**Lines and Veils** (Ron Edwards, 2000s): Pre-establish what's absolutely forbidden (Lines) and what happens off-screen (Veils)

**Script Change** (Brie Sheldon, 2016): Rewind (undo), Fast-Forward (skip), Pause (discuss) controls

**Session Zero**: Pre-game discussion establishing expectations, boundaries, themes

**Massive Impact**: Safety tools became expected in indie gaming, increasingly common in mainstream. Changed gaming culture around consent and boundaries.

## X-Card (John Stavropoulos, 2012)

**Mechanism**: Physical card (or virtual equivalent) on table

**Usage**: Anyone can tap card anytime

**Effect**: Immediately stop and remove content that was happening

**No Explanation Required**: Don't have to justify or discuss why

**Continue Play**: Move forward without problematic content

**Universal**: Works in any game, any scenario

**Adoption**: Became most widespread safety tool, appearing in countless games and sessions

## Lines and Veils (Ron Edwards, ~2004)

**Lines**: Absolute boundaries—content that will NOT appear
- "No sexual violence"
- "No harm to children"
- "No spider descriptions"

**Veils**: Fade-to-black topics—can happen but off-screen
- "Torture happens but we don't describe details"
- "Sex occurs but we fade to black"

**Pre-Established**: Discussed before play begins

**Respected**: Once established, honored throughout

**Evolution**: Can adjust during campaign as comfort changes

## Script Change (Brie Sheldon, 2016)

**Three Controls**:

**Rewind**: "Let's undo that and try differently"
**Fast-Forward**: "Let's skip this scene"
**Pause**: "Let's stop and discuss this"

**Film Metaphor**: Using familiar media controls for gaming

**Immediate**: Can invoke anytime

**Collaborative**: Opens discussion when paused

## Session Zero

**Pre-Game Discussion** covering:
- Themes and tone
- Content boundaries (Lines and Veils)
- Player expectations
- Safety tools in use
- Character/world creation
- Social contract establishment

**Standard Practice**: Became expected for campaigns

**Prevents Problems**: Addressing mismatches before they cause issues

## Cultural Impact

**Consent Culture**: Brought explicit consent to gaming

**Mainstream Adoption**: Increasingly common even in traditional games

**Indie Standard**: Expected in story games

**Convention Practice**: Many conventions require safety tools

**Publishing**: Indie publishers include safety tool sections

**Culture Shift**: Changed expectations around boundaries

## Reception

Praised for making gaming safer and more inclusive. Some initially resistant ("weren't needed before") but widespread adoption demonstrated value. Academic attention for consent frameworks in gaming.

## Legacy

Safety tools became gaming standard. Influenced mainstream design. Created safer more inclusive gaming culture. Demonstrated explicit consent mechanisms improve experiences.
