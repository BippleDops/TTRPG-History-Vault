# Aspects and Tags

**First Appeared**: [[FATE]] (2003, refined in FATE Core 2013)
**Designers**: Fred Hicks, Rob Donoghue
**Category**: Descriptive Mechanics / Trait System

## Overview

Aspects (FATE's term) or Tags (similar concept elsewhere) provide descriptive phrases defining characters, situations, and scenes that can be invoked for mechanical bonuses or compelled for complications—"Quick-Witted Reporter" Aspect can be invoked spending Fate Point for bonus when cleverness helps OR compelled by GM offering Fate Point for complication when wit creates problems—creating elegant system where fiction and mechanics integrate through natural-language descriptions rather than numerical stats, revolutionizing how narrative games handle traits and demonstrating descriptive phrases could be mechanically robust.

**Structure**: Short descriptive phrase (3-6 words) capturing something true about character/situation

**Two-Way**: Can help (invoke) OR hinder (compel)

**Economy**: Fate Points spent to invoke, earned through compels

**Examples**:
- Character Aspect: "Stubborn as a Mule" (invoke for determination, compel to refuse help)
- Situation Aspect: "Building is On Fire" (invoke for destruction, compel for danger)
- Scene Aspect: "Crowded Market" (invoke for concealment, compel for witnesses)

**Revolutionary**: Replaced numerical traits with descriptive phrases, demonstrating natural language could be mechanically precise.

**Massive Influence**: Aspects became FATE's signature, influenced Cortex (Distinctions), PbtA (Tags), and narrative gaming broadly.

## Mechanics

### Invoking Aspects

**Spend Fate Point**: Pay 1 FP

**Choose Benefit**:
- +2 to roll
- Reroll dice
- +2 to opposition's difficulty
- Other effects as appropriate

**Fictional Justification**: Explain how Aspect applies

**Example**:
- Aspect: "Master of Disguise"
- Invoke: Spend 1 FP for +2 to Deceive roll impersonating guard
- Justification: Your mastery helps create convincing disguise

### Compelling Aspects

**GM Offers**: "Your Aspect creates complication. Accept for Fate Point?"

**Player Choice**: Accept (earn FP, face complication) or refuse (pay FP to avoid)

**Usually Accept**: Earning FP incentivizes accepting complications

**Examples**:
- Aspect: "Sucker for a Pretty Face"
- Compel: "The duchess flatters you—you trust her despite warnings. Gain 1 FP."
- Aspect: "Wanted by the Law"
- Compel: "Guards recognize you—they alert authorities. Gain 1 FP."

### Aspect Types

**High Concept**: Core character concept ("Cynical Private Eye," "Rebellious Teenage Wizard")

**Trouble**: Built-in complication ("Debts to the Mob," "My Magic is Unstable")

**Other Aspects**: Relationships, beliefs, backgrounds (total ~5 per character)

**Situation Aspects**: Temporary scene elements ("Darkness," "Slippery Floor," "Angry Mob")

### Creating Advantages

**Generate Aspects**: Actions can create new situation Aspects
- "I set the building on fire" → "Burning Building" Aspect
- "I intimidate them" → "Afraid of Me" Aspect on target

**Free Invoke**: Creator gets one free invoke of created Aspect

## Design Philosophy

Aspects embody:

**Descriptive Over Numerical**: Phrases not numbers

**Fiction-Mechanic Integration**: Descriptions are mechanics

**Two-Way Traits**: Helping and hindering

**Natural Language**: Readable English not game jargon

**Flexible Application**: Aspects work in any context

**Economic Loop**: Compels earn FP; invokes spend FP

**Player Agency**: Choose when to invoke/accept compels

## Cultural Impact

**FATE Signature**: Defining FATE characteristic

**Descriptive Mechanics**: Influenced natural-language trait systems

**Compel Economy**: FP-for-complications widely adopted

**Cortex Distinctions**: Direct inspiration

**PbtA Tags**: Influenced descriptive trait thinking

**Design Vocabulary**: "Aspects" became recognized term

## Reception

**Praise**: Elegant fiction-mechanic integration, flexible, narrative richness, compel economy creates drama

**Criticism**: Can feel arbitrary, requires system mastery for good Aspects, some miss numerical precision, compel negotiations slow play

## Legacy

Became FATE's identity. Influenced descriptive trait systems broadly. Demonstrated natural language mechanics could be robust. Compel economy appeared in various forms.
