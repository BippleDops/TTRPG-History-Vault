# Bonds

**First Appeared**: [[Dungeon World]] (2012)
**Designers**: Sage LaTorra, Adam Koebel
**Category**: Party Relationships / Character Connections

## Overview

Bonds, introduced in Dungeon World (2012), mechanize party relationships through statements connecting characters ("I have sworn to protect [character]," "[Character] knows a secret of mine")—earning XP when Bonds resolve or change, creating mechanical incentive for developing party dynamics and ensuring characters have relationships beyond "we met in tavern," demonstrating how PbtA could encourage party cohesion in traditionally individualistic fantasy gaming through simple relationship tracking rewarding connection development.

**Structure**: Fill-in-the-blank statements about other PCs

**Examples**:
- "______ has my back when things go wrong"
- "I worry that ______ is too reckless"
- "______ owes me for saving their life"

**Resolution**: When Bond's situation resolves (protected them, revealed secret, collected debt), mark XP and rewrite Bond

**Mechanical Benefit**: Advancing character while developing relationships

**Impact**: Demonstrated party-cohesion mechanics in PbtA. Influenced relationship systems in various fantasy games.

## Mechanics

**Starting Bonds**: Each playbook has 3-4 Bond templates

**Assigning**: During character creation, write other PCs' names in blanks

**Changing Circumstances**: When Bond's situation resolves, rewrite reflecting new relationship

**XP Award**: Gain XP when Bond resolves/changes

**Aid Bonus**: Some games let resolved Bonds provide mechanical benefits when helping that character

## Design Philosophy

**Party Cohesion**: Ensuring characters connect

**Relationship Development**: Mechanical reward for interpersonal growth

**XP from Connections**: Advancement through relationships not just combat

**Evolution**: Bonds change as relationships develop

## Reception

**Praise**: Encourages party dynamics, mechanical relationship development

**Criticism**: Can feel forced, some struggle writing good Bonds, XP rate varies unpredictably

## Legacy

Appeared in various PbtA games. Influenced party relationship mechanics. Demonstrated connecting PCs mechanically improved play.
