# Troupe Play

**First Appeared**: [[Ars Magica]] (1987)
**Designer**: Jonathan Tweet, Mark Rein-Hagen
**Category**: Character Rotation / Ensemble Cast Mechanics

## Overview

Troupe Play, introduced in Ars Magica (1987), enables players to maintain multiple characters of different types/power-levels, rotating which characters appear in each session based on story needs—magi (powerful wizards) for magical stories, companions (skilled associates) for adventuring, grogs (servants/soldiers) for dangerous missions—creating ensemble cast where everyone plays different roles contextually, solving power-disparity problems while enabling rich covenant (magical community) development through playing multiple perspectives.

**Revolutionary Approach**: Instead of single permanent character, each player has:
- **Magus**: Powerful wizard (main character for magical scenarios)
- **Companion**: Skilled associate (for adventuring)
- **Grogs**: Multiple servants/soldiers (disposable for dangerous missions)

**Rotating Focus**: Different sessions feature different character types

**Shared World**: All characters exist in same covenant (magical community)

**Power-Level Flexibility**: Enables stories at different scales without party balance issues

## Mechanical Framework

### Character Types in Ars Magica

**Magi** (1 per player):
- Powerful wizards
- Focus of magical/political stories
- Rarely adventure (too valuable to risk)
- Long-term projects and research

**Companions** (1 per player):
- Skilled mundanes or minor supernatural
- Adventuring and covenant management
- More expendable than magi but valued

**Grogs** (Shared/multiple):
- Servants, soldiers, workers
- Anyone can play them
- Expendable for dangerous missions
- Comic relief or tragic redshirts

### Rotating Sessions

**Magus-Focused**: Political intrigue, magical research, covenant development (play magi)

**Adventure**: Dungeon delving, travel, combat (play companions and grogs)

**Community**: Covenant management, relationships (play any)

**Different Perspectives**: Same events seen from different character viewpoints

### Benefits

**Power-Disparity Solution**: Different power levels work because they're not in same scenes typically

**Expendability**: Grogs can die without losing primary character

**Rich World**: Multiple perspectives create depth

**Player Investment**: Owning multiple characters creates investment in community not just individual

**Varied Play**: Different session types prevent monotony

## Cultural Impact

**Ensemble Gaming**: Validated multiple-character approach

**West Marches**: Inspired rotating-character open table play

**Ars Magica Legacy**: Emblematic of game's innovative design

**Power-Level Solution**: Template for handling disparate capabilities

## Reception

Praised for enabling rich ensemble play. Some groups struggle managing multiple characters. Ars Magica community embraced fully.

## Legacy

Influenced West Marches and rotating-character campaigns. Demonstrated viable alternative to single-character paradigm. Remained Ars Magica signature.
