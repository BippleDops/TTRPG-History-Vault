# Blackjack Mechanic

**First Appeared**: [[RuneQuest]] (1978), [[Call of Cthulhu]] (1981)
**Designer**: Greg Stafford, Sandy Petersen, et al.
**Category**: Resolution Mechanic / Percentage System

## Overview

Blackjack Mechanic (also called "roll-under-but-high") uses percentile dice (d100) where you want to roll under your skill percentage BUT as high as possible within that range—rolling 87 when you have 90% skill succeeds better than rolling 23 (both succeed, but 87 is closer to limit showing exceptional performance), creating graduated success within roll-under framework where critical hits occur on doubles (11, 22, 33, etc.) and fumbles on 00/100, providing intuitive "higher is better but not over skill" resolution used across Basic Roleplaying family games.

**Roll d100**: Percentile roll (00-99)

**Success**: Result ≤ skill percentage

**Quality**: Higher successful rolls are better (closer to skill ceiling)

**Criticals**: Rolling doubles under skill (11, 22, 33, etc.)

**Fumbles**: Rolling 00 or 100, or sometimes 96-100

**Impact**: Became signature BRP system mechanic. Influenced percentage-based gaming broadly.

## Mechanics

**Skill Ratings**: Percentage (0-100+, though 100+ rare)

**Resolution**:
1. Roll d100
2. Compare to skill percentage
3. Under = success, over = failure
4. Doubles under = critical success
5. 00/100 (sometimes 96-100) = fumble

**Degree of Success**: Some games use how close to skill ceiling

**Opposed Rolls**: Both roll, highest successful roll wins

## Design Philosophy

**Intuitive Percentages**: "65% skill" immediately understandable

**High-Within-Range**: Encourages rolling high without exceeding

**Graduated Success**: Better rolls create better outcomes

**Critical Simplicity**: Doubles are crits (easy to spot)

## Cultural Impact

Defined BRP family resolution. Became CoC signature. Influenced percentage-based gaming. Demonstrated roll-under could have graduated success.

## Reception

**Praise**: Intuitive percentages, elegant criticals (doubles), graduated success

**Criticism**: Math overhead (percentile calculations), less exciting than rolling high being good, d100 clunky

## Legacy

Remains BRP standard. CoC's massive success spread mechanic widely. Influenced percentage-based design thinking.
