# Anchors and Touchstones

**First Appeared**: [[Urban Shadows]] (2015)
**Designers**: [[Andrew Medeiros]], [[Mark Diaz Truman]]
**Category**: Relationship Mechanic / Grounding System

## Overview

Anchors and Touchstones in Urban Shadows represent important people grounding supernatural characters to humanity/sanity—Mortals who anchor you to human world despite supernatural nature, preventing total loss to inhuman impulses, with mechanics where losing Touchstones advances corruption while maintaining them preserves humanity, demonstrating how specific NPCs could mechanically represent character grounding and psychological stability, influencing relationship-as-resource mechanics in urban fantasy and horror games.

**Touchstones/Anchors**: Specific NPCs grounding character

**Mechanical Importance**: Losing them advances corruption/transformation

**Relationship Focus**: Not just allies but people who matter personally

**Grounding**: Representing connection to humanity/normalcy/sanity

**Impact**: Demonstrated how specific relationships could mechanically matter for character stability

## Mechanics

**Establishing**: Create 1-3 important NPC relationships

**Benefits**: Maintaining connections provides mechanical benefits

**Loss**: When Touchstone dies/leaves, face corruption advance

**Protection**: Incentivizes keeping them safe

**Story Hooks**: Touchstones create personal stakes

## Design Philosophy

**Grounding Relationships**: Specific people preventing total corruption

**Mechanical Stakes**: NPCs mechanically important not just narrative

**Loss Consequences**: Deaths/departures have mechanical weight

**Humanity Preservation**: Connections keeping you human/sane

## Cultural Impact

Influenced how urban fantasy games handle humanity/grounding. Demonstrated specific NPCs could have mechanical significance. Appeared in various relationship-focused games.

## Reception

**Praise**: Creates personal stakes, makes NPCs mechanically important, drives stories

**Criticism**: Can feel like hostages for corruption system, some uncomfortable with NPC leverage
