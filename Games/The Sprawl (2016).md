# The Sprawl

**Publisher**: Self-published (Hamish Cameron)
**Designer**: Hamish Cameron
**Year**: 2016
**System**: Powered by the Apocalypse
**Genre**: cyberpunk, heist, corporate-shadowrun
**Status**: in-print

## Overview

The Sprawl, designed by Hamish Cameron in 2016, brings cyberpunk to Powered by the Apocalypse by creating mission-based gameplay where shadowrunners (edgerunners, street samurai, netrunners) execute corporate espionage missions for megacorporations while managing heat (corporate attention), debt (financial/social obligations), and trauma (psychological scarring), using PbtA's fiction-first approach with structured mission framework (Legwork → Action → Fallout) creating tense corporate heists in dystopian near-future where corporations rule and you're mercenary caught between predatory employers.

**Mission Structure**: Each job follows framework—
- Get Legwork: Research target, gather intel
- Execute Action: The actual run
- Face Fallout: Deal with consequences, get paid

**Corporate Attention**: Missions generate Heat; too much Heat brings corporate retaliation

**Cyberpunk Playbooks**: Killer (street samurai), Hacker (netrunner), Driver, Fixer, Hunter, Infiltrator, Pusher, Reporter, Tech—classic cyberpunk archetypes

**Trauma and Debt**: Long-term consequences modeling cyberpunk's brutal economics and psychological toll

## Core Mechanics

**PbtA 2d6+stat**: Standard framework with cyberpunk-specific moves

**Mission Phases**: Legwork (investigation), Action (execution), Fallout (consequences)

**Heat**: Corporate attention tracking—too much triggers Corp retaliation

**Debt**: Financial and social obligations creating complications

**Trauma**: Psychological scarring from violence

**Corp Clocks**: Tracking corporate countermeasures and responses

**Links**: Connections to fixers, contacts, resources

**Cyberware**: Implants and augmentations with mechanical benefits

## Setting

**The Sprawl**: Massive urban megacity controlled by megacorporations

**Megacorps**: All-powerful corporations employing shadowrunners for espionage

**Street Level**: Cyberpunk from ground perspective—you're not executives but mercenaries

**Dystopian**: Capitalism's worst excesses realized, corporations above law

**Technology**: Cyberware, netrunning, advanced weapons, surveillance everywhere

**Themes**: Corporate oppression, mercenary morality, transhumanism, urban decay, desperate survival

## Cultural Impact

Established cyberpunk PbtA as viable genre. Demonstrated mission structure worked for PbtA. Influenced corporate warfare gaming. Compared favorably to Shadowrun as more accessible cyberpunk option.

## Reception

Praised for clean cyberpunk execution, mission framework, accessibility compared to Shadowrun. Some wanted more crunch. Commercial success showed cyberpunk PbtA demand.

## Legacy

Validated cyberpunk as PbtA genre. Mission structure influenced heist-based PbtA games. Provided accessible alternative to Shadowrun's complexity.
