# Annalise

**Publisher**: Nathan D. Paoletta
**Designer**: Nathan Paoletta
**Year**: 2010
**System**: Annalise System
**Genre**: horror, vampire, psychological
**Status**: in-print

## Overview

Annalise, designed by Nathan Paoletta in 2010, creates collaborative vampire horror where group collectively defines what "vampire" means (literal monster, addiction metaphor, toxic relationship, disease), then creates characters affected by vampire and plays through structured Acts building toward confrontation—using Claims (established facts) and Secrets (hidden truths) mechanics where revealing secrets at dramatically appropriate moments earns mechanical benefits, creating horror through collaborative revelation rather than GM-controlled plot.

**Key Innovation**: Collaborative vampire definition and Claims/Secrets mechanics enabling dramatic timing of revelations.

**Flexible Horror**: Vampire can be literal supernatural monster or metaphor for addiction, abuse, corruption, disease—group decides.

## Core Mechanics

**Collaborative Vampire Creation**: Group defines vampire's nature, powers, weaknesses, and what it represents

**Claims and Secrets**: Players establish facts (Claims) and hide truths (Secrets) about characters

**Revelation Mechanics**: Revealing Secrets at appropriate moments provides mechanical benefits

**Three Act Structure**: Rising action toward inevitable confrontation

**Scene Framework**: Structured scene types guiding horror pacing

## Setting and Themes

**Variable Setting**: Any time/place where vampire could manifest

**Vampire as Whatever**: Literal monster, addiction, abuse, corruption—group chooses

**Psychological Horror**: Fear from revelations and corrupted relationships

**Collaborative Creation**: No single GM; group shapes horror collectively

## Cultural Impact

Demonstrated collaborative horror creation could work. Showed vampire as flexible metaphor for various social ills. Influenced Claims/Secrets mechanics in other games.
