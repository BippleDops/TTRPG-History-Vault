# My Life with Master

**Publisher**: Half Meme Press
**Designer**: Paul Czege
**Year**: 2003
**System**: My Life with Master System
**Genre**: horror, gothic, psychological-drama, dark-comedy
**Status**: in-print

## Overview

My Life with Master, designed by Paul Czege and published in 2003, stands as one of the most emotionally powerful and mechanically innovative indie RPGs ever created—a game about playing the miserable, wretched minions of gothic horror Masters (mad scientists, evil sorcerers, vampire lords), caught between serving cruel masters and yearning for human connection, where the endgame is mechanically predetermined: the Master will fall, the only question is whether you'll destroy them through revolution, be destroyed by them, or escape to find redemption. Players portray minions commanded to commit terrible acts while secretly developing Connections to townspeople, creating agonizing tension as Love (attachment to humans) battles Self-Loathing (internalized monstrousness) and accumulating Weariness eventually triggers inevitable endgame.

The game's revolutionary contribution lies in its mechanical guarantee: this story WILL end, and it will end badly for the Master. Unlike traditional RPGs where outcomes remain uncertain, My Life with Master uses accumulating stats (Love, Weariness) that mathematically ensure the endgame triggers within 8-12 scenes. This creates dramatic inevitability—you know the Master falls eventually; play explores HOW it happens and WHO you become in the process.

The Master-minion relationship mechanics prove brilliantly oppressive. The Master can command you to perform terrible deeds, and mechanically you CANNOT refuse unless your Love exceeds specific thresholds. You must obey orders to kidnap innocents, terrorize townspeople, commit murders—the game forces complicity in atrocities while tracking how these actions corrode your soul through Self-Loathing accumulation. This mechanical compulsion mirrors psychological abuse dynamics, creating uncomfortable but powerful gameplay examining autonomy, complicity, and trauma.

Yet alongside horror, the game tracks hope through Connection mechanics. Between Master's commands, minions can seek human connection with townspeople—attempting to court, befriend, or help individuals. These Connections provide Love (mechanically tracked stat) that eventually enables refusing the Master and potentially achieving redemption. The most memorable moments arise when Love for Connection conflicts with Master's commands: will you betray someone you love to serve the Master, or risk the Master's wrath by disobeying?

Critical reception was ecstatic within indie gaming. The game won the 2004 Diana Jones Award for Excellence in Gaming and became instant classic. Reviewers praised how mechanics created guaranteed dramatic arc, how the stats tracked psychological states perfectly, and how 3-4 hour sessions generated complete tragic narratives. However, the game's emotional intensity and mechanical forcing of atrocities made it challenging—many groups played once, experienced profound catharsis, and never returned because the experience was too heavy.

## Historical Context

My Life with Master emerged from Paul Czege's engagement with The Forge's narrativist community and his interest in using mechanics to explore psychological and emotional territory typically handled only through roleplaying. Czege wanted game that:
- Guaranteed specific narrative arc (Gothic horror tragedy)
- Mechanically forced morally questionable actions
- Tracked psychological degradation systematically
- Created inevitable endgame while leaving journey uncertain

Development drew inspiration from multiple sources:
- **Gothic Horror Literature**: Frankenstein, Dracula, The Island of Dr. Moreau
- **Film**: Classic Universal and Hammer horror films
- **Sorcerer**: Humanity tracking and moral degradation mechanics
- **The Forge Theory**: Using mechanics to constrain and enable specific narratives

Czege extensively playtested, refining the mathematical relationships ensuring endgame triggered reliably. The Love/Weariness/Self-Loathing formulas required careful balancing so games neither ended too quickly (before emotional investment) nor dragged (losing dramatic momentum).

The 2003 publication through Half Meme Press (Czege's small operation) meant limited distribution, but the game spread rapidly through PDF sales and rapturous word-of-mouth. Convention demonstrations created immediate converts—watching/playing single session generated complete emotional narrative impossible to achieve in traditional campaign structures.

The Forge community lionized My Life with Master as proof narrativist theory could produce mechanically sophisticated, emotionally resonant games. The game demonstrated how constraining player choice (mechanical compulsion to obey Master) paradoxically created deeper engagement than unlimited freedom—the struggle against constraints generated drama.

Critical acclaim was universal among indie gamers. The 2004 Diana Jones Award validated the design's excellence. Academic attention followed, with game studies scholars analyzing how mechanics modeled abusive relationships and psychological trauma. The game became required reading for designers interested in emotional gameplay and guaranteed narrative arcs.

However, the intense emotional experience limited widespread adoption. Groups typically played once or twice before needing breaks. The game's power came from its emotional heaviness, but that same heaviness prevented casual repeated play. Some players found the forced atrocities too uncomfortable regardless of mechanical justification.

The game's influence extended far beyond direct play. Numerous designers adopted endgame trigger mechanics, psychological stat tracking, and guaranteed narrative arc structures. The idea that games could have predetermined endpoints while leaving the journey uncertain influenced how designers thought about campaign structures and dramatic pacing.

## Core Mechanics

My Life with Master uses elegant mathematical system ensuring tragic arc:

### Master Creation

Group collaboratively creates the Master using these questions:

**Type of Master**: Mad Scientist, Evil Sorcerer, Vampire Lord, Demon Summoner, etc.

**Aspect Deciding Fear**: What does the Master fear? (Townspeople with pitchforks = Fear Outsiders 3)

**Aspect Deciding Reason**: Why doesn't Master act directly? (Sunlight burns them = Reason Outside the Master's Presence 4)

**What Master Wants**: Ultimate goal driving Master's commands

These numbers (Fear and Reason) mechanically define when Master commands must be obeyed and when minions can potentially resist.

### Minion Creation

Each player creates minion with:

**Name**: Often ironic or pathetic

**Type**: Physical descriptor of monstrousness (Shambling Corpse, Deformed Hunchback, Cursed Werewolf)

**More Than Human**: Supernatural ability (Unnatural Strength, Hypnotic Gaze, Wings)

**Less Than Human**: Debilitating weakness (Can't Feel Pain, Speaks Only in Screams, No Free Will)

**Initial Stats**:
- **Self-Loathing**: Starts at 1
- **Weariness**: Starts at 0  
- **Love**: Starts at 0

### Master Commands

The Master regularly commands minions to perform terrible deeds:
- Kidnap innocent victim
- Terrorize townsperson
- Steal holy artifact
- Murder witness
- Grave-rob cemetery

**Obeying/Refusing**: When commanded, roll:
- **To Obey Violent Command**: Self-Loathing vs. (Weariness + Love)
- **To Obey Non-Violent Command**: Self-Loathing vs. Love
- **To Refuse**: Love vs. (Self-Loathing + relevant Master stat)

Early game: Low Love means you CANNOT refuse—mechanically impossible.
Mid game: Rising Love makes refusal possible but dangerous
Late game: High Love enables refusing and eventually confronting Master

This creates arc where you're helpless initially, gradually gain agency, and eventually can rebel.

### Violence Towards Outsiders

When commanded to harm townspeople, roll:
- Self-Loathing + More Than Human vs. resistance
- Success: You commit violence
- Failure: You cannot complete command (gain Weariness)

**Intimacy vs. Desperation**: 
- Can't use More Than Human against those intimate with
- Can't use More Than Human when desperate (high Weariness)

These restrictions create dramatic moments where abilities fail precisely when most needed/unwanted.

### Overture: Seeking Connection

Between Master's commands, minions attempt Overtures—seeking connection with townspeople:
- Courting potential romantic interest
- Befriending kind individual
- Helping someone in need
- Seeking acceptance

**Overture Roll**: Weariness + (d4 if using More Than Human) vs. (Self-Loathing + d4)
- Success: Connection deepens, gain Love
- Failure: Connection weakens or ends, gain Self-Loathing

Connections mechanically tracked—successful Overtures advance track, failures reverse progress.

### Gaining Stats

Stats accumulate through play:

**Self-Loathing increases when**:
- Successfully committing violence against outsiders
- Failed Overtures
- Betraying Connections

**Weariness increases when**:
- Failing to complete Master's command
- Commanded to harm Connection
- Attempting and failing to resist Master

**Love increases when**:
- Successful Overtures
- Protecting Connections from Master
- Receiving kindness despite monstrousness

### The Endgame: Inevitable Conclusion

The game MUST end when:
- Master's Fear ≤ (Highest minion Love - Self-Loathing), OR
- Master's Reason ≤ Total Weariness of all minions

This mathematical certainty means games last 8-12 scenes before triggering endgame.

**Three Possible Endings**:

1. **Master Destroyed by Minions**: If triggering through Love, minions can attempt killing Master. Success means revolution and potential redemption.

2. **Master Destroys Minions**: If Master survives minion attack, Master kills all minions. Tragic end.

3. **Epilogue for Survivors**: Surviving minions determine fates based on final stats—redemption, continued monstrousness, tragic death, or escape.

The endgame mechanics guarantee resolution while leaving specifics uncertain.

### Connection Tracking

Each minion has 1-2 Connections (specific townspeople):
- Named NPCs minion cares about
- Track relationship progress
- Provide Love when Overtures succeed
- Create dramatic tension when Master commands harm

Connections aren't abstractions—they're specific individuals with names, personalities, and stakes.

## Setting and Themes

### Generic Gothic Horror Village

Setting is deliberately generic:
- Small isolated village
- Fearful townspeople
- Master's lair overlooking settlement
- Gothic horror tropes (graveyards, inns, churches, forests)

This generic framing allows customization to any gothic horror context (Transylvania, New England, German forests, etc.)

### The Master: Archetypal Tyrant

The Master embodies archetypal horror villain:
- Commands minions to fulfill twisted goals
- Cannot act freely due to limitations (Reason stat)
- Fears something specific (Fear stat)
- Represents abusive authority and psychological control

Masters aren't complex characters—they're forces of oppression to overcome.

### The Minions: Tragic Monsters

Minions embody pathos:
- Created/cursed/transformed into monstrousness
- More Than Human but Less Than Human
- Capable of both terrible violence and tender connection
- Yearn for humanity while committing atrocities

The central tragedy: you're both victim (of Master) and victimizer (harming innocents).

### The Townspeople: Humanity and Fear

Townspeople represent normal humanity:
- Fear the minions (rightfully)
- Some capable of seeing past monstrousness
- Connections provide hope for redemption
- Also victims of Master's schemes

### Core Themes

**Abuse and Control**: Mechanical compulsion mirrors psychological abuse—you "can't" refuse even when wanting to

**Complicity and Guilt**: Forced to commit atrocities, but does compulsion absolve guilt?

**Hope and Redemption**: Can monsters find redemption through love and connection?

**Monstrousness vs. Humanity**: Are you defined by monstrous form or human feelings?

**Inevitable Rebellion**: Oppression creates conditions for its own overthrow

**Tragic Agency**: Gaining agency to resist comes too late to prevent crimes

## Reception and Criticism

**Praise**:
- Brilliant mechanical modeling of psychological abuse
- Guaranteed dramatic arc creating complete narratives
- Perfect 3-4 hour single-session length
- Profound emotional impact
- Stats perfectly track psychological states
- Love/Self-Loathing/Weariness elegantly interconnected
- Endgame mechanics mathematically elegant
- Gothic horror tone perfectly captured

**Criticisms**:
- **Emotionally Draining**: Too heavy for repeated play
- **Forced Atrocities**: Some uncomfortable with mechanical compulsion to commit evil
- **Limited Replayability**: Core experience exhausted in 1-2 plays
- **Narrow Setting**: Gothic horror specificity limits appeal
- **Mathematical Transparency**: Seeing endgame math can feel mechanical
- **Tragic Inevitability**: Some want possibility of happy endings

**Play Experience Reports**:
- Groups consistently report profound emotional experiences
- Many play once and never return (too intense)
- Cathartic but exhausting
- Memorable scenes of Love vs. Obedience creating genuine anguish
- Endgame often involves tears and/or triumphant rebellion

## Legacy and Influence

**Endgame Trigger Mechanics**: Numerous games adopted mathematical endgame conditions

**Psychological Stat Innovation**: Tracking internal states mechanically became standard narrativist tool

**Guaranteed Arc Design**: Influenced how designers think about predetermined narrative structures

**Abuse Dynamics**: Provided template for mechanically modeling oppressive relationships

**Single-Session Complete Narratives**: Demonstrated games designed for one session could rival campaigns

**Indie Canon**: Achieved classic status, studied by designers learning narrativist techniques

**Emotional Gaming**: Expanded understanding of what emotions games could explore mechanically

**Designer Education**: Required study for learning psychological mechanics and forced choices

**Spiritual Successors**: Inspired games exploring similar themes through different mechanics
