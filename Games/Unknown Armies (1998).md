# Unknown Armies

**Publisher**: [[Atlas Games]]
**Designers**: [[John Tynes]], [[Greg Stolze]]
**Year**: 1998
**System**: Custom (Percentile with Madness Meters)
**Genre**: Postmodern Occult, Contemporary Horror
**Status**: in-print

## Historical Context

Unknown Armies, designed by John Tynes and Greg Stolze and published by Atlas Games in 1998, revolutionized how tabletop RPGs approached magic, madness, and meaning in contemporary horror gaming. Subtitled "A Roleplaying Game of Transcendental Horror and Furious Action," it proposed radically different occult cosmology: magic comes from obsession, reality is what humanity believes it is, and the desperate, damaged people in the "Occult Underground" fight secret wars to remake existence itself. The game asked disturbing questions: What would you sacrifice for power? What does it mean when reality is consensus? How do you maintain sanity when everything you believed is wrong?

The late 1990s saw occult gaming dominated by two paradigms. Call of Cthulhu portrayed cosmic horror where investigators confronted sanity-destroying alien truths. World of Darkness presented gothic-punk supernatural societies with metaphysical absolutes. Both featured established cosmologies with clear supernatural hierarchies. Unknown Armies rejected these approaches entirely, presenting postmodern occultism where meaning was constructed rather than discovered, where broken people's obsessions became magical power, and where humanity's collective unconscious determined reality.

John Tynes brought dark sensibility and conspiracy gaming experience from work on Delta Green and Pagan Publishing. Greg Stolze contributed narrative innovation and psychological depth from various indie projects. Together they created something unique: contemporary occult RPG that was simultaneously darkly comic and genuinely disturbing, mechanically innovative and thematically sophisticated, accessible and deeply weird.

The core concept—"You Did It. You Broke Through. Now They're Coming For You."—captured Unknown Armies' essential tension. Magic exists but gaining it requires breaking yourself, becoming so obsessed with something that reality bends to accommodate your madness. Every adept (self-taught mage) follows obsession-fueled school of magic. Plutomancers gain power through money but become consumed by greed. Dipsomancers channel magic through alcohol but spiral into alcoholism. Pornomancers derive power from sex but destroy intimate relationships. Magic always costs psychological integrity.

The madness meters proved equally innovative. Rather than Call of Cthulhu's descending sanity score, Unknown Armies tracked five madness meters (Violence, Unnatural, Helplessness, Isolation, Self) measuring characters' psychological hardening to different traumas. Each failed madness check created specific failed notches—permanent scars making you simultaneously more resistant to future trauma but also more damaged. A character might be hardened to violence (can kill without hesitation) but broken by isolation (terrified of being alone). This nuanced approach to psychological damage created mechanically distinct trauma responses.

The cosmology centered on ascension and the Invisible Clergy. Reality's infrastructure—concepts like "The Mother," "The Messenger," "The Warrior"—was maintained by 333 Invisible Clergy members, archetypal humans who ascended to become living embodiments of concepts. But whenever Clergy member dies or vacates their position, someone new can ascend through street-level ritual called the Invisible Wars. This meant desperate occultists fought brutal conflicts for chance at god-like power, making cosmic stakes intensely personal.

Atlas Games published Unknown Armies in 1998 to strong critical reception. The game won widespread praise for innovative madness mechanics, disturbing-yet-compelling magic system, and how it modernized occult horror for contemporary sensibilities. It won Origins Award for Best Roleplaying Game of 1999, validating Tynes and Stolze's vision.

The setting material proved equally compelling. Rather than comprehensive setting gazetteer, Unknown Armies provided framework and examples. The Occult Underground consisted of various cabals (groups), schools of magic, and powerful individuals pursuing their agendas. GMs built their own Underground using provided tools, creating personalized conspiracy networks.

Second edition (2002) expanded and refined mechanics while deepening setting material. Third edition (2016) radically reorganized the game into "street-level," "global," and "cosmic" play modes, each with different mechanical frameworks and scope. This evolution demonstrated Unknown Armies' sustained relevance and adaptability.

The game's influence extended significantly beyond its player base. Madness meters influenced how subsequent games approached psychological trauma—nuanced mechanical frameworks replacing simple sanity tracks. The obsession-as-power concept appeared in various occult and modern games. The postmodern approach to magic and reality influenced games exploring consensus reality, constructed meaning, and metaphysical uncertainty.

Unknown Armies represented crucial evolution in contemporary occult gaming. It demonstrated that horror could be psychological and visceral simultaneously, that magic systems could be thematically integrated with mechanics, and that contemporary settings could host profound weirdness without requiring vampires or aliens. For designers, it showed how mechanical innovation could serve thematic goals, how character damage could be nuanced rather than binary, and how setting could be framework rather than encyclopedia.

## Mechanical Innovations

Unknown Armies introduced revolutionary mechanics supporting its postmodern occult vision:

**Madness Meters**: The game's most famous innovation replaced traditional sanity with five separate trauma tracks:
- **Violence**: Trauma from physical harm to yourself or others
- **Unnatural**: Encountering supernatural impossibilities
- **Helplessness**: Situations where you're powerless
- **Isolation**: Loneliness and social disconnection
- **Self**: Threats to identity and self-concept

Each meter has hardened and failed notches. Hardened notches represent psychological scarring making you resistant to that trauma type but marking permanent damage. Failed notches are breakdowns creating lasting negative traits. This creates nuanced psychological profiles—characters might be violence-hardened (can kill easily) but isolation-vulnerable (terrified of being alone).

**Obsession Skills**: Every character has an obsession—central fixation defining them. The obsession skill represents how this fixation helps them. When the obsession applies, use it instead of normal skills. This mechanizes how monomania can make people extraordinarily effective within their fixation while dysfunctional elsewhere.

**Percentile System with Cherries**: Core resolution uses percentile dice (d100). Matching digits (11, 22, 33, etc.) are "cherries"—critical successes. This simple addition to percentile rolling creates dramatic moments and lucky breaks.

**Adept Magic Schools**: Magic comes from obsession channeled through symbolic schools:
- **Plutomancy**: Power through money and transactions
- **Dipsomancy**: Magic via alcoholic intoxication
- **Pornomancy**: Sexual arousal as magical fuel
- **Mechanomancy**: Obsession with machines and systems
- **Narco-Alchemy**: Drugs and altered consciousness
- **Urbanomancy**: Cities as living magical entities
- Many others, each reflecting specific obsession

Each school has taboos that, if broken, cause power loss. This creates mechanical tension between maintaining obsession and functioning socially.

**Random Charges**: Many magic schools use charges (stored magical energy) generated through obsession-appropriate actions. Plutomancers gain charges by spending money or making profitable deals. Dipsomancers need to get drunk. This makes magic accumulation integral to character behavior.

**Avatar Paths**: Alternative to adept magic, avatars channel archetypal identities from collective unconscious. By embodying archetype (The Flying Woman, The Pilgrim, The Merchant, etc.), avatars gain supernatural abilities. This requires living the archetype, creating different obsession-mechanic than adepts.

**Wound System**: Damage divides into minor, significant, and major wounds. Multiple minor wounds can combine to create significant wounds. This creates grounded yet simple combat resolution appropriate to street-level conflicts.

**Flip-Flop Results**: When you roll percentile dice, you can reverse the result (45 becomes 54) by spending points from relevant madness meter. This creates strategic choice—succeed now at cost of psychological damage, or fail but preserve mental health.

**Soul Tracking**: Characters track what they've sacrificed for power via Soul stat. Dehumanizing actions lower Soul. Low Soul makes ascending to Clergy easier but indicates how much humanity you've lost. This mechanizes corruption.

**Initiative Based on Action Type**: Rather than fixed initiative, action order depends on what you're attempting. Hand-to-hand is fastest, firearms slower, magic slower still. This creates tactical choices about speed versus effectiveness.

**Matched Triggers**: Skills can have matched triggers—specific situations where they function better. This creates mechanical differentiation between characters with same skills.

**Experience Through Challenges**: Characters improve by checking boxes when they succeed at meaningful challenges or fail at critical moments. This ties advancement to actual play rather than arbitrary point accumulation.

## Cultural Impact

Unknown Armies' cultural impact demonstrated how sophisticated thematic design could create distinctive gaming experiences:

**Postmodern Occultism Pioneer**: Unknown Armies pioneered postmodern approaches to magic in gaming. Reality as consensus, meaning as constructed, magic as obsession—these concepts influenced subsequent occult games significantly. The game demonstrated that contemporary magic didn't require established mythologies or cosmic horror frameworks.

**Madness Mechanics Evolution**: The madness meters influenced how subsequent games approached psychological trauma:
- More nuanced than simple sanity tracks
- Specific trauma types creating distinct responses
- Hardening and breaking as separate processes
- Mechanical representation of psychological complexity

Games from Trail of Cthulhu to various indie horror games built on Unknown Armies' innovations.

**Contemporary Horror Redefinition**: The game showed contemporary horror could be street-level and cosmic simultaneously. You're fighting for reality itself but doing so in dive bars, strip clubs, and suburban homes. This influenced urban fantasy and contemporary occult gaming broadly.

**Obsession as Mechanic**: The concept that character obsessions should be central mechanical elements appeared in numerous subsequent games. Unknown Armies demonstrated how making psychological traits mechanically relevant created character depth.

**Critical Recognition**:
- Origins Award for Best Roleplaying Game (1999)
- Extensive critical praise for innovation
- Featured in discussions of psychological mechanics
- Studied for thematic-mechanical integration
- Recognized for distinctive voice and vision
- Academic attention for postmodern themes

**Three Editions Demonstrate Longevity**:
- **First Edition** (1998): Revolutionary original
- **Second Edition** (2002): Expanded and refined
- **Third Edition** (2016): Reorganized for different play scales

This sustained publication demonstrates enduring relevance.

**Influence on Delta Green**: While Delta Green predated Unknown Armies slightly, the games influenced each other. Delta Green's subsequent development incorporated psychological mechanics showing Unknown Armies' influence.

**Indie Gaming Philosophy**: Unknown Armies emerged from same Atlas Games indie mindset as Over the Edge and Feng Shui. The game demonstrated that small publishers could tackle ambitious, sophisticated designs mainstream companies avoided.

**Community Development**:
- Devoted fanbase sustained through online communities
- Extensive fan materials and scenarios
- Convention play introduced new audiences
- Actual play reports demonstrated distinctive atmosphere
- Community discussions explored philosophical implications

**Thematic Sophistication**: The game showed RPGs could explore complex themes—reality as consensus, meaning-making, sacrifice for power, corruption and transcendence—without becoming pretentious or unplayable.

**Magic System Influence**: The obsession-based magic schools influenced how subsequent games approached contemporary magic. The concept that magical power requires psychological cost appeared in various occult RPGs.

**Designer Reputation**: Established Tynes and Stolze as significant designers. Stolze particularly built on Unknown Armies' innovations in subsequent work including Godlike, Reign, and other projects.

**Kickstarter Success**: Third edition's successful Kickstarter demonstrated sustained market demand nearly two decades after original publication. The campaign raised substantial funds for ambitious three-book reorganization.

**Academic Interest**: Game studies scholars examined Unknown Armies for postmodern themes, psychological mechanics, and how it reflected late-90s conspiracy culture and reality anxiety.

**Cultural Reflection**: The game captured millennial turn anxieties—reality as unstable, meaning as constructed, truth as contested. This cultural resonance made it feel distinctively "late 1990s" while remaining relevant.

## Design Philosophy

Unknown Armies embodied several core design principles:

**Obsession Drives Everything**: The fundamental philosophy was that obsession makes people extraordinary. Whether adepts channeling magic through fixations, avatars embodying archetypes, or mundanes pursuing their goals monomaniacally, obsession is central. This created thematic coherence across all character types.

**Trauma Is Nuanced**: Rather than treating psychological damage as simple deterioration, Unknown Armies recognized that trauma hardens and breaks people in specific ways. The madness meters reflected this nuanced understanding, creating mechanically distinct trauma responses.

**Power Costs Humanity**: Every path to power in Unknown Armies requires sacrifice. Adepts break themselves to gain magic. Avatars subsume identity to archetypes. Ascending to Invisible Clergy means leaving humanity behind. This created moral weight around power-seeking.

**Reality Is Consensus**: The cosmology positioned reality as collective human belief rather than objective truth. This postmodern premise justified why desperate individuals could reshape existence—if enough people believe something, it becomes true. This philosophy integrated setting and theme.

**Street-Level Cosmic Stakes**: Unknown Armies insisted that cosmic conflicts happened in ordinary places among broken people. You're fighting for reality's nature, but the battlefield is a parking lot behind a strip mall. This juxtaposition created distinctive tonal identity.

**Meaningful Choices Have Costs**: The game consistently created difficult choices with real costs. Use magic but feed your obsession. Succeed now but damage your psyche. Pursue transcendence but lose your humanity. These cost-benefit decisions created ongoing moral complexity.

**Setting as Framework**: Rather than comprehensive setting, Unknown Armies provided framework and examples. GMs built their Occult Underground using provided tools. This empowered customization while maintaining thematic consistency.

**Dark Comedy and Horror Coexist**: The game balanced genuinely disturbing horror with dark absurdist comedy. Pornomancers are simultaneously ridiculous and unsettling. This tonal balance kept the game from becoming oppressively grim while maintaining stakes.

**Character Damage Is Progress**: Madness meters create permanent changes to characters. Each failed check and hardened notch marks character evolution through trauma. This made psychological damage narratively and mechanically meaningful rather than just mechanical penalty.

**Accessible Complexity**: While thematically sophisticated, the game remained playable. Percentile core resolution was straightforward. Complexity came from meaningful choices and thematic depth rather than baroque subsystems.

## Setting and Themes

Unknown Armies' Occult Underground created distinctive contemporary occult setting:

**The Occult Underground**: Hidden world of magically aware people existing beneath mundane society's surface. Adepts, avatars, cabals, and various weirdos fighting secret wars for cosmic stakes while maintaining cover from Sleepers (non-magical people).

**The Invisible Clergy**: 333 archetypal figures embodying fundamental human concepts (The Mother, The Warrior, The Messenger, etc.) who maintain reality's infrastructure. These positions form the Statosphere, reality's operating system. When Clergy member dies or vacates, someone can ascend to replace them through the Invisible Wars.

**The Invisible Wars**: Secret conflicts where cabals, adepts, and avatars compete to ascend to Invisible Clergy. Victory requires either killing current Clergy member or proving you better embody the archetype. These wars happen on streets, in businesses, through social manipulation—invisible to mundanes.

**Ascension**: Ultimate goal for many Underground inhabitants. Becoming Clergy means transcending humanity to become living concept, achieving power and immortality at cost of individual identity. Ascension represents both ultimate achievement and ultimate sacrifice.

**Cabals**: Small groups pursuing shared agendas in the Underground. Unlike traditional adventuring parties, cabals have goals, philosophies, and organizational structures. The game assumes players form or join cabal with specific objectives.

**Magic Schools**: Various obsession-fueled approaches to magic:
- **Plutomancy**: Wealth and transactions
- **Dipsomancy**: Alcohol and intoxication
- **Pornomancy**: Sexual arousal
- **Mechanomancy**: Machines and technology
- **Urbanomancy**: Cities as living entities
- **Entropomancy**: Chaos and destruction
- Many others, each reflecting specific fixation

**Artifacts and Unnatural Phenomena**: Strange objects and events exist throughout the Underground—cursed items, haunted locations, reality glitches, etc. These serve as resources, threats, and evidence that consensus reality isn't stable.

**The Global Occult Network**: While focused on North American Underground, the game acknowledged worldwide occult communities with different traditions, conflicts, and approaches to magic and ascension.

**Contemporary Setting**: Unlike fantasy or historical games, Unknown Armies happens now, in recognizable cities and situations. This groundedness makes the occult elements more unsettling—magic exists in the Denny's parking lot, not distant fantasy realm.

**Themes**:
- **Obsession and Identity**: How fixations define and destroy us
- **Power and Sacrifice**: What you'll give up for transcendence
- **Reality as Construct**: Meaning made rather than found
- **Broken People Seeking Wholeness**: Everyone in Underground is damaged seeking repair through power
- **Cosmic Stakes, Human Scale**: Fighting for reality's nature in dive bars and storage units
- **Meaning-Making**: How we construct significance in universe indifferent to us
- **Corruption and Transcendence**: Whether becoming more means becoming less human
- **Truth and Madness**: Whether understanding reality means losing sanity

**Influences**: The game drew from postmodern occult fiction (Grant Morrison's The Invisibles, Alan Moore's Promethea), conspiracy theory culture, contemporary urban fantasy, and late-90s reality anxiety.

**Tonal Identity**: Unknown Armies balanced horror, dark comedy, action, and philosophical depth. This distinctive mix created recognizable aesthetic—simultaneously disturbing and absurd, violent and thoughtful, cosmic and intimate.

## Reception and Legacy

Unknown Armies achieved significant critical acclaim and lasting influence:

**Critical Acclaim**:
- Origins Award for Best Roleplaying Game (1999)
- Extensive praise for innovation across gaming media
- Recognition for psychological mechanics
- Appreciation for thematic sophistication
- Featured in game design discussions extensively
- Academic attention for postmodern approaches
- Studied as example of thematic-mechanical integration

**Commercial Success**: While never mainstream hit, Unknown Armies sustained publication through three editions and extensive supplements, demonstrating viable niche market.

**Three Editions Across Two Decades**:
- **First Edition** (1998): Revolutionary original establishing core concepts
- **Second Edition** (2002): Expanded setting, refined mechanics, deeper Underground
- **Third Edition** (2016): Reorganized into street/global/cosmic play scales with different mechanical frameworks for each

This longevity demonstrates sustained relevance and adaptability.

**Extensive Supplements**: Atlas Games published numerous Unknown Armies supplements:
- **Postmodern Magick**: Expanded magic schools and avatars
- **Weep**: Horror scenarios and NPCs
- **Hush Hush**: Secrets and conspiracies
- **One Shots**: Anthology adventures
- **Break Today**: Explosive scenarios
- Various others exploring different aspects

**Third Edition Kickstarter**: Successful 2015 crowdfunding campaign demonstrated sustained market demand, enabling ambitious three-book reorganization for different play scales.

**Design Influence**: Unknown Armies influenced numerous subsequent games:
- Psychological trauma mechanics in various horror games
- Obsession-as-power concepts in occult RPGs
- Postmodern approaches to reality and magic
- Nuanced madness systems replacing simple sanity tracks
- Contemporary occult gaming broadly

**Greg Stolze's Career**: Established Stolze as major designer. His subsequent work (Godlike, Reign, various other projects) built on Unknown Armies' innovations in matching mechanics to themes.

**Community Development**:
- Devoted fanbase sustained game across decades
- Active online communities shared materials
- Convention play introduced new audiences
- Fan scenarios and setting materials expanded content
- Community discussions explored philosophical depths

**Actual Play**: The game's psychological complexity and dark themes made it challenging but rewarding for actual play. Sessions exploring character obsessions and moral compromises demonstrated the system's distinctive appeal.

**Academic Interest**: Game studies scholars examined Unknown Armies as:
- Postmodern narrative in gaming
- Psychological mechanics and trauma representation
- Reality as social construct in game design
- Late-90s cultural anxieties reflected in gaming
- Thematic-mechanical integration

**Cultural Moment**: The game captured millennial turn anxieties about reality, truth, and meaning. This cultural resonance made it feel distinctively of its moment while themes remained relevant.

**Criticisms and Limitations**:
- **Dark Content**: The game's disturbing themes (addiction, sexual obsession, self-destruction) weren't for everyone
- **GM Burden**: Creating personalized Occult Underground required creative, committed GMs
- **Tonal Balance**: Balancing dark comedy and genuine horror proved challenging
- **Power Imbalance**: Adepts were mechanically stronger than mundanes, creating balance issues
- **Accessibility**: The psychological complexity and postmodern themes had steep conceptual learning curve

**Influence on Delta Green**: While the games developed somewhat separately, they influenced each other's approaches to contemporary occult horror and psychological mechanics.

**Modern Relevance**: In era of increased interest in psychological gaming, trauma-informed design, and contemporary occult themes, Unknown Armies' approaches remain relevant and influential.

**Designer Commentary**: Tynes and Stolze's openness about design thinking, through essays, interviews, and online discussions, made Unknown Armies important case study for aspiring designers.

**Enduring Community**: Decades after publication, communities still play, discuss, and create Unknown Armies content. This longevity demonstrates genuine lasting appeal.

**Final Assessment**: Unknown Armies stands as one of most innovative and thematically sophisticated occult RPGs ever created. Its madness meters influenced psychological mechanics across gaming. Its obsession-based magic demonstrated how supernatural powers could be thematically integrated with character psychology. Its postmodern cosmology showed contemporary magic gaming didn't require established mythologies or cosmic horror frameworks.

More broadly, the game demonstrated that tabletop RPGs could explore complex themes—reality as consensus, meaning-making, sacrifice for transcendence—while remaining playable and engaging. It proved sophisticated thematic design and mechanical innovation could coexist, that contemporary settings could host profound weirdness, and that psychological damage could be nuanced rather than binary.

For designers, Unknown Armies remains essential study in thematic-mechanical integration, how to create distinctive voice and vision, and how psychological systems can enhance rather than complicate play. For players, it offers experiences impossible in most RPGs—exploring obsession's power and cost, fighting for reality's nature on street level, and confronting what you'd sacrifice for transcendence.

The game's influence extends beyond its direct play to broader understanding of how mechanics can serve themes, how psychological damage can be mechanically sophisticated, and how contemporary horror can be cosmic and intimate simultaneously. In this respect, Unknown Armies achieved lasting importance as genuinely revolutionary design that changed what we believed contemporary occult gaming could be.
