# Steal Away Jordan

**Publisher**: Self-published
**Designer**: Julia Ellingboe
**Year**: 2009
**System**: Steal Away Jordan System
**Genre**: historical, slavery, resistance
**Status**: in-print

## Overview

Steal Away Jordan, designed by Julia Ellingboe in 2009, tackles perhaps the most difficult subject in American history—chattel slavery—creating game where players portray enslaved people seeking freedom through escape, resistance, and survival, using mechanics that honor historical reality's brutality without exploitation, centering enslaved people's agency and humanity rather than depicting them as helpless victims, providing framework for examining historical trauma, systemic violence, and resistance with gravity and respect these subjects demand.

**Historical Grounding**: Extensively researched using slave narratives, historical documentation, and African-American history scholarship.

**Centering Enslaved Perspectives**: Protagonists are enslaved people with agency, goals, relationships—not defined solely by oppression but by how they resist and survive.

**Mechanics of Resistance**: Systems track efforts toward freedom—escape planning, community building, cultural preservation, coded communication.

**Safety and Respect**: Designed with awareness of trauma, historical violence, and need for careful facilitation.

**Educational Potential**: Some use it for historical education about slavery's realities and resistance.

## Core Mechanics

**Character Creation**: Enslaved people with skills, relationships, dreams, resistance plans

**Freedom Track**: Progress toward escape/resistance goals

**Community Bonds**: Relationships providing support and mechanical benefits

**Overseer Mechanics**: GM controls oppressive systems without glorifying violence

**Resistance Actions**: Planning escapes, preserving culture, supporting community

**Historical Accuracy**: Mechanics reflect actual historical conditions and resistance methods

## Setting and Themes

**American Slavery Era**: Historical period of chattel slavery in United States

**Plantation Setting**: Where most gameplay occurs

**Underground Railroad**: Potential escape route

**Themes**:
- Resistance despite oppression
- Humanity in dehumanizing system
- Community survival
- Cultural preservation
- Hope under hopelessness
- Agency within constraint

## Cultural Impact

Demonstrated extremely difficult historical subjects could be addressed through gaming if done with research, respect, and care. Influenced how designers approach historical trauma. Sparked necessary discussions about who tells what stories and how.

## Reception

Praised for brave, thoughtful approach to essential but difficult history. Some debated whether gaming was appropriate medium for this subject. Recognized as important educational tool by some educators.
