# Good Society

**Publisher**: Storybrewers Roleplaying
**Designer**: Hayley Gordon
**Year**: 2017
**System**: Story Game (Jane Austen RPG)
**Genre**: Historical Romance, Regency England
**Status**: in-print

## Overview

Good Society creates collaborative Jane Austen-style narratives through innovative card-based mechanics, structured social scenes, and ensemble storytelling focused on courtship, scandal, reputation, and social maneuvering in Regency England. Designed by Hayley Gordon and published by Storybrewers Roleplaying in 2017, the game synthesizes Jane Austen's literary techniques with modern story game design, creating system where players collaboratively build interconnected stories about eligible bachelors and ladies, family expectations, social obligations, and the quest for advantageous marriages and personal happiness.

The game's brilliance lies in capturing Austen's narrative techniques mechanically. Like Austen novels, Good Society emphasizes misunderstandings, social pressure, gradual relationship development, and ensemble casts where every character has complete story arc. The card-based mechanics include Connections (relationships creating obligations), Secrets (hidden information complicating situations), Desires (character goals), and Resolve (emotional stakes). Letters between characters create dramatic irony and miscommunication. The Facilitator role (similar to GM but with distributed authority) helps orchestrate ensemble narrative without dominating it.

The game structures play through episodes mimicking novel chapters—Invitation (framing next story focus), Novel (main action with multiple scenes), and Monologue (introspective character moments). The Best Part mechanism lets players decide which Desire resolution would create most satisfying story, making satisfying narrative paramount over competitive "winning." This collaborative story-optimization creates genuinely Austen-like ensemble narratives where multiple romantic and social plots interweave toward satisfying conclusions.

Good Society succeeded commercially and critically by demonstrating that literary fiction could be adapted into satisfying game mechanics. The game appeals to Austen fans who might never try traditional RPGs, creating gateway for literary audiences. It proved period piece and romance storytelling deserved serious mechanical design rather than being mere flavor. The game showed that source material's narrative techniques could inform mechanics directly—Austen's use of letters, misunderstandings, and social pressure becoming game systems rather than just flavor text.

The game's influence extends through inspiring period piece games, validating romance as central mechanic, and demonstrating that adapting specific authors' techniques creates distinctive play experiences. It showed story games could successfully target specific literary audiences while maintaining broad appeal.

## Historical Context

Good Society emerged from Hayley Gordon's dual love of Jane Austen novels and story games, combined with recognition that Regency romance's dramatic structures naturally suited collaborative narrative gaming.

The mid-2010s had established various story game patterns but lacked strong period piece or romance-focused designs (excepting romance subplots). Gordon recognized Austen's novels provided perfect template—ensemble casts, interconnected plots, social maneuvering, romantic tension, misunderstandings, and satisfying conclusions where multiple storylines resolve. These elements naturally suited collaborative emergent narrative systems.

The key insight was adapting Austen's specific narrative techniques into mechanics rather than generic "historical romance" rules. Austen uses letters for dramatic irony and miscommunication—make letters mechanic. Austen emphasizes secrets and reputation—mechanize these. Austen balances multiple plots—create ensemble structure. This source-specific adaptation creates authenticity generic systems couldn't achieve.

Development involved extensive playtesting ensuring mechanics generated Austen-like narratives. The card systems, letter mechanics, and Facilitator role went through many iterations. The challenge was balancing structure (needed for Austen authenticity) with emergence (needed for replayability and player creativity).

The Kickstarter campaign in 2016-2017 succeeded dramatically, demonstrating appetite for literary-focused story games. The final production quality matched the game's literary aspirations—beautiful books, elegant card design, period-appropriate aesthetic.

Reception came from multiple audiences: story gamers appreciating innovation, Austen fans discovering RPGs through familiar content, romance readers enjoying collaborative storytelling. This crossover appeal expanded Good Society's impact beyond typical story game reach.

The game won numerous awards including 2018 ENNIE for Best Rules and Product of the Year. Critical acclaim praised faithful Austen adaptation, elegant mechanics, and opening story games to new audiences.

## Core Structure and Mechanics

Good Society creates ensemble Austen narratives through card mechanics, structured social scenes, and collaborative plot development.

**Character Creation**: Players create Regency-era characters including:

**Role Cards**: Define social position—The Clergyman, The Debutante, The Dowager, The Gentleman, The Lady, The Rake, etc. Each role provides starting Connections, Secrets, and guidance.

**Connections**: Relationships to other characters—familial, romantic, financial, social. Connections create obligations and complications.

**Secrets**: Hidden information—scandalous pasts, secret engagements, financial ruin, etc. Secrets drive plot when revealed.

**Desires**: Character goals—find love, restore family honor, escape unwanted marriage, gain independence, etc.

**The Facilitator**: One player serves as Facilitator (like GM but with less authority). Facilitator:
- Frames scenes and introduces complications
- Portrays supporting characters
- Helps orchestrate ensemble narrative
- Ensures spotlight sharing
- Guides pacing and resolution

Unlike traditional GM, Facilitator doesn't control plot—players drive story through Desire pursuit.

**Episode Structure**: Play proceeds through episodes (sessions) structured in three parts:

**The Invitation** (15-30 minutes): Players collaboratively establish next episode's focus—upcoming ball, country house visit, scandal emerging, etc. This framing creates shared direction.

**The Novel** (2-3 hours): Main play consisting of scenes exploring characters' pursuits of Desires, navigating Connections, managing Secrets. Scenes can be:
- **Public Scenes**: Balls, dinners, social gatherings where all characters may participate
- **Private Scenes**: Intimate conversations, secret meetings, personal moments
- **Letters**: Written communication creating dramatic irony

**Monologues** (15-30 minutes): Each player delivers introspective monologue revealing character's thoughts and feelings (like Austen's narrative voice). Monologues provide character development and emotional honesty impossible in social scenes.

**Card Mechanics**:

**Secret Cards**: Hidden information. You can:
- **Reveal Secret**: Dramatically expose secret for narrative impact
- **Hint at Secret**: Allude without revealing, creating suspense
- **Protect Secret**: Work to prevent discovery

Revealed Secrets create scandal, complications, and plot developments.

**Desire Cards**: Goals your character pursues. Pursuing Desires drives scenes and plot. Resolution comes when group determines story is ready.

**Connection Cards**: Represent relationships. Can be:
- **Invoked**: Use relationship to influence scene
- **Strained**: Relationship damaged by actions
- **Strengthened**: Relationship deepened through support

Connections create web of obligations and care.

**Letters**: Written communication between characters. Letters:
- Create dramatic irony (readers know, recipients don't)
- Generate misunderstandings
- Reveal emotions characters can't express face-to-face
- Advance plot through information conveyance
- Can be intercepted, creating scandal

Letters are actual written props enhancing immersion.

**Resolve**: Emotional/social currency. You spend Resolve to:
- Influence scenes favorably
- Resist social pressure
- Protect Secrets
- Pursue Desires against opposition

Limited Resolve creates meaningful choices about when to push.

**The Best Part**: When Desire could resolve, player holding Desire can declare "The Best Part"—describing how they'd like resolution to go. Other players can suggest alternatives. Group discusses which resolution creates most satisfying story. This collaborative story-optimization ensures narratively satisfying conclusions.

**Scandal and Reputation**: Social standing matters mechanically. Scandalous behavior risks reputation, creating social consequences. Characters must balance personal desires against social expectations.

**Multi-Session Play**: Good Society supports campaign play with interconnected episodes building toward season finale where plots resolve.

These mechanics create collaborative Austen-style narratives through systematic support for ensemble storytelling, social maneuvering, and romantic development.

## Narrative Philosophy and Design Goals

Good Society's narrative philosophy centers on ensemble storytelling, Austen-specific techniques, and collaborative narrative satisfaction.

**Ensemble Over Individual**: Unlike traditional RPGs focusing on individual PC arcs, Good Society emphasizes ensemble where every character matters equally. Facilitator ensures spotlight sharing. The Best Part mechanism prioritizes collective satisfaction. This mirrors Austen's novels where multiple characters have complete arcs.

**Source-Specific Adaptation**: Rather than generic historical romance, Good Society adapts Austen's specific techniques:
- Letters for dramatic irony and miscommunication
- Secrets for plot complications
- Social pressure creating meaningful stakes
- Gradual relationship development
- Ensemble balancing multiple plots
- Satisfying conclusions resolving storylines

This specificity creates authenticity generic approaches couldn't achieve.

**Collaborative Story Optimization**: The Best Part mechanism demonstrates revolutionary approach—players prioritize narratively satisfying outcomes over competitive "winning." When Desire could resolve, group decides what resolution creates best story. This collaborative optimization generates genuinely Austen-like satisfying conclusions.

**Letters as Mechanic**: Physical letter-writing enhances immersion while serving mechanical purpose. Letters create dramatic irony (you know things characters don't), generate misunderstandings (misinterpreted correspondence), and reveal emotions (things characters can't say aloud). This demonstrates physical props can be mechanics.

**Social Pressure as Stakes**: Instead of combat or physical danger, Good Society uses social pressure, reputation, and obligation for stakes. Will scandal ruin your family? Can you resist unwanted marriage? Can you pursue love despite social barriers? These social stakes create genuine tension.

**Gradual Romance**: The game rewards gradual relationship development over instant attraction. Connections strengthen through scenes. Desires clarify through play. Romantic resolutions feel earned because players built to them collaboratively across multiple episodes.

**Facilitator as Orchestrator**: The Facilitator helps ensemble narrative flow without dictating plot. They frame scenes, introduce complications, portray supporting characters—but players drive story through Desire pursuit. This distributed authority creates collaborative storytelling.

**Monologues for Interiority**: The monologue phase provides space for revealing inner thoughts impossible in social scenes. This mirrors Austen's narrative voice showing characters' private reflections. Mechanizing interiority creates character depth.

**Campaign Structure**: Multi-episode play allows Austen-novel-length storytelling. Plots develop across multiple sessions, relationships evolve gradually, complications accumulate before resolution. This long-form structure suits source material better than one-shots.

These philosophical commitments create game authentically capturing Austen's narrative techniques through thoughtful mechanical design.

## Cultural Impact and Legacy

Good Society's cultural impact manifested through reaching Austen fans, inspiring period games, and demonstrating literary adaptation's potential.

**Literary Gateway**: Good Society introduced Austen fans to RPGs:
- People who'd never played RPGs trying because "Jane Austen game"
- Book clubs and literary groups playing
- Libraries and bookstores hosting sessions
- Gateway for literary audiences to story games

This expanded RPG demographics beyond typical players.

**Period Piece Validation**: The game demonstrated historical period pieces deserved serious design:

**Subsequent Period Games**:
- **Brindlewood Bay** (Gauntlet, 2020): Cozy mystery with Agatha Christie inspiration
- **The Between** (Gauntlet, 2021): Gothic Victorian mystery
- Various historical romance games

Good Society proved period pieces could be mechanically sophisticated and commercially successful.

**Romance Mechanics**: Validated romance as central rather than peripheral:
- Systematic approach to relationship development
- Romance getting mechanical weight equal to other activities
- Demonstrating romance creates meaningful stakes

**Critical Recognition**:
- **2018 ENNIE Awards**: Best Rules, Product of the Year
- Extensive critical acclaim
- Featured in mainstream press about literary gaming
- Academic interest in adaptation studies

**Community and Play Culture**:
- Active Good Society community
- Player-created "packs" (expansions) for different settings
- Regency fashion at convention games
- Actual-play recordings introducing new audiences

**Storybrewers Roleplaying**: Good Society established Storybrewers as significant publisher:
- Subsequent releases maintaining quality
- Focus on literary and period games
- Demonstrating niche sophistication succeeds

**Long-term Influence**:
- Period piece games becoming more common
- Romance mechanics more sophisticated
- Literary adaptation recognized design approach
- Gateway for new audiences continuing

Good Society's greatest legacy may be proving literary fiction provides rich mechanical inspiration, expanding who plays story games and demonstrating that source-specific adaptation creates distinctive authentic experiences.
