# Masks: A New Generation

**Publisher**: Magpie Games
**Designer**: Brendan Conway
**Year**: 2016
**System**: Powered by the Apocalypse
**Genre**: superhero, teen-heroes, coming-of-age
**Status**: in-print

## Overview

Masks: A New Generation, designed by Brendan Conway in 2016, revolutionizes superhero gaming by focusing on teenage heroes whose powers are established (you HAVE superpowers—that's given) but whose identities are forming, using Labels (Danger, Freak, Savior, Superior, Mundane—how you see yourself) instead of traditional stats, Influence mechanics (others' opinions shifting your self-image), and playbooks representing teen archetypes (The Beacon, The Bull, The Delinquent, The Doomed) rather than power-sets, creating coming-of-age stories where emotional/identity struggles matter more than punching villains.

**Labels Not Stats**: Revolutionary approach—your capabilities defined by self-perception rather than objective abilities

**Influence Mechanics**: Adults and peers can shift your Labels, mechanically representing how others' opinions affect teen self-image

**Teen Focus**: Unlike most superhero games focusing on adult heroes, Masks centers teenage experience—identity formation, adult authority, peer pressure, finding yourself

**Playbooks**: Define emotional/narrative role (The Bull is strong and direct, The Janus has secret identity, The Outsider doesn't fit) rather than powers

**Halcyon City**: Default setting with multi-generational superhero history

## Core Mechanics

**Labels Instead of Stats**: 
- **Danger**: "Am I dangerous/threatening?"
- **Freak**: "Am I weird/different/outcast?"
- **Savior**: "Am I heroic/protective?"
- **Superior**: "Am I better/smarter than others?"
- **Mundane**: "Am I just normal person?"

Roll +Label most relevant to how you're approaching situation. Labels shift up/down based on Influence.

**Influence**: NPCs (especially adults) have Influence over you initially. They can shift your Labels, mechanically representing how authority figures affect teen self-perception. You can reject Influence but it costs.

**Playbooks**: Ten archetypes—
- The Beacon (unpowered inspiration)
- The Bull (strong and straightforward)
- The Delinquent (rebel troublemaker)
- The Doomed (cursed destiny)
- The Janus (secret identity struggle)
- The Legacy (inheriting heroic legacy)
- The Nova (overwhelming cosmic power)
- The Outsider (alien/other)
- The Protégé (mentor relationship)
- The Transformed (body horror)

Each defines emotional/narrative space rather than power-set.

**Conditions**: Instead of hit points, emotional conditions (Angry, Afraid, Guilty, Hopeless, Insecure) that complicate actions and relationships

**Team**: Group creates team identity, relationships, team pool

**Adult NPCs**: Mentors, authority figures with mechanical Influence creating generational tension

**Grow Up/Give In**: Advancement tracks choosing either growing up (rejecting labels) or giving in (accepting external definitions)

## Setting

**Halcyon City**: Multi-generational superhero city—
- Golden Generation (1940s heroes, now elderly)
- Silver Generation (parents' generation)
- Bronze Generation (current adults)
- You (New Generation—teenagers finding your place)

**Generational Conflict**: Adults' expectations vs. your self-definition

**Superhero Saturation**: Halcyon has so many heroes that being super is almost normal

**Teen Life**: School, relationships, family alongside superheroics

**Themes**: Identity formation, adult authority, finding yourself, generational conflict, coming of age, what kind of hero will you become?

## Cultural Impact

**Labels Innovation**: Emotional self-perception as stats influenced psychological mechanics widely

**Influence Mechanics**: Authority figures mechanically shifting character identity became design tool

**Teen Superhero Gaming**: Established as distinct genre from adult superhero play

**ENnie Gold 2017**: Won Best Game

**Commercial Success**: Best-selling Magpie title

**Actual Play**: Regular appearances on streams demonstrating emotional gameplay

**Mainstream Coverage**: Gaming press and superhero media covered release

**Second Edition Development**: Ongoing refinement and expansion

## Reception

Praised for brilliant Labels system, perfect teen superhero tone, emotional depth, how mechanics serve theme. Some traditional superhero fans wanted more tactical combat. Widely acclaimed as best teen superhero game and top-tier PbtA.

## Legacy

Labels instead of stats influenced emotional/psychological stat designs. Influence mechanics appeared in various games. Established teen superhero as recognized genre. Confirmed Magpie as major publisher. Demonstrated PbtA superhero viability.
