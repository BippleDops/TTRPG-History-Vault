# Dog Eat Dog

**Publisher**: Self-published
**Designer**: Liam Liwanag Burke
**Year**: 2011
**System**: Dog Eat Dog System
**Genre**: historical, colonialism, political
**Status**: in-print

## Overview

Dog Eat Dog, designed by Liam Liwanag Burke in 2011, uses game mechanics as critical tool examining colonialism's dynamics: one player portrays Occupation force colonizing Pacific island while others play Native inhabitants, with mechanically asymmetric power ensuring Occupation wins individual conflicts BUT accumulates Strings (representing native resistance) until revolution triggers—creating gameplay where colonial power appears unstoppable scene-by-scene while systematically creating conditions for its own overthrow, demonstrating how oppressive systems generate resistance through their own violence.

**Critical Design**: Explicitly designed to critique colonialism by making players experience power imbalance and examine how occupation functions and ultimately fails.

**Based on History**: Inspired by Philippine-American War and Pacific island colonization experiences.

**Occupation as Occupation**: The Occupation player literally occupies game master role, demonstrating how colonial power monopolizes authority, narrative, and rules themselves.

**Inevitable Revolution**: Strings accumulate until uprising triggers—mechanical guarantee that oppression generates resistance.

## Core Mechanics

**Asymmetric Roles**: One Occupation player, 2-4 Native players

**Occupation Authority**: Occupation player controls conflicts, has mechanical advantages, can impose rules

**Strings**: Each Occupation victory adds String to pool; when Strings equal threshold, revolution triggers

**Conflict Resolution**: Occupation player usually wins individual conflicts but at cost of building resistance

**Custom Rules**: Occupation player creates rules colonizing the game itself

**Endgame Revolution**: Predetermined uprising when Strings accumulate sufficiently

## Setting and Themes

**Pacific Island**: Generic island being colonized

**Historical Parallels**: Philippines, Polynesia, Melanesia, various colonized territories

**Themes**:

## Cultural Impact

Groundbreaking critical design using mechanics to examine colonialism. Influenced political gaming and asymmetric power mechanics. Sparked discussions about games addressing difficult historical subjects.

## Reception

Praised for brilliant critique and mechanical innovation. Some found it too uncomfortable. Academic attention for post-colonial game design. Influenced politically-engaged gaming.
