# Dialect

**Publisher**: Thorny Games
**Designers**: [[Kathryn Hymes]], [[Hakan Seyalioglu]]
**Year**: 2018
**System**: Story Game (Language Creation)
**Genre**: Literary, Language Creation, Community
**Status**: in-print

## Overview

Dialect stands as one of the most intellectually ambitious and emotionally devastating story games ever created, asking players to collaboratively build a language unique to an isolated community, then witness that language—and the community that speaks it—inevitably fade away. Designed by Kathryn Hymes and Hakan Seyalioglu and published by Thorny Games in 2018, Dialect combines linguistics, anthropology, and narrative gaming into profound meditation on language, community, identity, and loss. The game generates complete vocabularies of 15-20 words over 3-4 hours of play, each word carrying layers of cultural meaning, while simultaneously creating the community's history from formation through dissolution.

The game's revolutionary insight recognizes that language and community are inseparable—language shapes how communities think, and communities shape how language develops. As players create words for concepts unique to their isolated group (arctic explorers, space station crew, religious commune, corporate office), they're simultaneously worldbuilding, character-building, and creating linguistic-cultural artifacts. Each word encodes community values, experiences, and relationships. The Aspect cards provide prompts triggering word creation: "We cannot go back" might inspire a word meaning "exile-sweetness" or "home-that-was." These words become more than vocabulary—they're cultural memory encoded linguistically.

The game's tragic inevitability—all communities depicted eventually lose their isolation and their language—creates mounting emotional weight. As play progresses through three ages (Foundation, Emergence of Crisis, Isolation Breaks Down), players witness their community fragmenting and their beautiful constructed language becoming obsolete. In the final Epilogue, each player speaks one last sentence in their dialect before it disappears forever. This formal structure creates genuine grief—you've built something linguistically and culturally meaningful, then watched it die. The language's death represents the community's dissolution, making abstract loss concrete through linguistic erosion.

Dialect succeeded as remarkable synthesis of academic rigor and emotional storytelling. Hymes, a linguist, and Seyalioglu, a game designer, combined expertise creating game that's simultaneously intellectually sophisticated and emotionally accessible. The game requires no linguistics knowledge—prompts guide word creation naturally—but rewards linguistic thinking. It works equally well as literary exercise, anthropological simulation, worldbuilding tool, or pure storytelling experience. This multivalent nature expanded Dialect's audience beyond typical story gamers into academic, literary, and creative communities.

The game demonstrated that story games could engage seriously with academic disciplines while remaining emotionally affecting and mechanically elegant. It proved that formal constraints (must create language, language must die) could generate emergence and catharsis. It showed that collaborative creation could extend to linguistics itself, democratizing language-building previously domain of conlangers and linguists. Most importantly, it created unique play experience impossible in other media—only through collaborative linguistic-narrative creation over hours could this specific emotional resonance emerge.

## Historical Context

Dialect emerged from Kathryn Hymes's dual expertise in linguistics and game design, combined with Hakan Seyalioglu's experience creating emotionally resonant collaborative experiences. Hymes, pursuing PhD in linguistics while active in story game communities, recognized potential for game exploring language creation's emotional and cultural dimensions.

The mid-to-late 2010s story game scene had established collaborative worldbuilding games like Microscope and The Quiet Year, demonstrating that building worlds could be engaging primary activity. Dialect extended this principle to linguistics—what if building language was the game? Existing conlanging (constructed language creation) existed as hobby, but typically involved individuals methodically creating complete grammars and lexicons over years. What if collaborative, emergent, emotionally-driven language creation happened through play?

The key insight came from sociolinguistics research on endangered languages and isolated speech communities. Real-world isolated communities develop unique vocabularies, idioms, and linguistic features reflecting their specific experiences and environments. When isolation breaks down through contact, migration, or cultural change, these unique linguistic features erode, often disappearing within generations. This linguistic death represents irretrievable cultural loss—concepts and ways of thinking vanishing when languages die.

Hymes recognized this process—isolation, linguistic development, contact, erosion, death—created natural narrative arc. If players created language for isolated community, that creation would build investment. The inevitable loss would create emotional payoff mirroring real-world linguistic tragedy. The game could make abstract concepts (linguistic relativity, language death, cultural memory) tangibly felt through play.

Development involved extensive testing across diverse groups and Backdrops (setting templates). Early versions had more explicit linguistic rules; final version trusts players' linguistic intuitions, providing guidance without requiring technical knowledge. The Aspect cards went through many iterations ensuring prompts reliably generated interesting words and community dynamics. The three-age structure emerged from recognizing communities follow predictable patterns—foundation, development, crisis, dissolution.

The Backdrops (eight included in base game, more in expansions) demonstrate the system's flexibility: The Raft (shipwreck survivors), The Dig (archaeological expedition), The Voyage (space station), The Enclave (bunker after apocalypse), The Exodus (religious migration), The Conference (corporate retreat), The Corner (neighborhood community), The Base (arctic research). Each Backdrop provides specific context while leaving creative freedom. This diversity shows language-creation mechanics working across genres and tones.

Thorny Games, co-founded by Hymes and Seyalioglu, self-published Dialect in 2018 as beautifully produced book with elegant design. The production quality matched the game's literary ambitions—this wasn't just game but art object. The visual design emphasized the created language, with space for recording words and their evolving meanings.

Initial reception came from story game communities recognizing Dialect's innovation. Early players reported profound emotional experiences—genuine grief at language's death, attachment to created words, appreciation for emergent cultural depth. The game's intellectual rigor attracted academics and linguists, while emotional core appealed to story gamers. This crossover positioned Dialect uniquely.

Critical acclaim followed. The game won multiple awards including 2019 ENNIE for Best Writing. Reviews praised its innovation, emotional depth, and elegant mechanics. Academic linguists and anthropologists examined it as tool for teaching language and culture concepts. Creative writers adopted it for worldbuilding and character development. Game designers studied it as example of academic concepts transformed into affecting play.

By 2019-2020, Dialect had achieved recognition as essential story game for players interested in literary and linguistic play. It demonstrated that games could engage seriously with academic disciplines, that language creation could be collaborative and emergent rather than solitary and methodical, and that formal constraints (language must die) could create catharsis rather than frustration.

The game's legacy extends through inspiring language-focused games (Sign, Xenolanguage), demonstrating viability of academic-influenced design, and proving that intellectually sophisticated games could achieve emotional resonance and commercial success.

## Core Structure and Mechanics

Dialect creates emergent language and community narratives through structured three-age progression, card-based prompts, and collaborative linguistic creation.

**Setup**:
Players select a Backdrop—setting template providing context and framing for isolated community. Each Backdrop includes:
- **Isolation**: What isolates this community (geography, circumstance, choice)
- **Setting Details**: Time period, location, community type
- **Backdrop-Specific Aspects**: Cards unique to this scenario
- **Questions**: Prompts establishing community details

**Character Creation**:
Each player creates a community member defining:
- **Name and Role**: Who are you in this community?
- **Relationships**: Connections to other characters
- **Personal Details**: Background bringing you here
- **Stake in Language**: Why community's unique language matters to you

No stats or mechanics—pure narrative character definition.

**The Language Sheet**:
Central artifact tracking created language. Contains:
- **Words**: Vocabulary created during play
- **Definitions**: What each word means
- **Etymology**: How/why word was created
- **Usage Examples**: Sentences demonstrating word in context

This sheet documents the language's development, becoming increasingly dense record of community's linguistic culture.

**Three Ages Structure**:

**Age One - Foundation** (60-90 minutes):
- Community is forming or newly isolated
- Creating 5-7 foundational words
- Establishing community dynamics
- Building optimism and unity
- Focus on what makes community unique

**Age Two - Emergence of Crisis** (45-60 minutes):
- Community faces challenges and changes
- Creating 4-6 additional words
- Internal conflicts emerging
- Cracks in unity appearing
- Language evolving to address new realities

**Age Three - Isolation Breaks Down** (30-45 minutes):
- Isolation ending (contact, departure, dissolution)
- Creating final 3-5 words
- Community fragmenting
- Language becoming obsolete
- Approaching inevitable end

This structure creates rising and falling action—hope, development, crisis, dissolution—mirroring real community life cycles.

**Aspect Cards**:
Players draw Aspect cards providing prompts triggering word creation and story moments:

**Card Types**:
- **Backdrop-Specific**: Unique to chosen setting ("The ice shelf is moving")
- **Universal**: Work across all Backdrops ("We cannot go back," "There is an outsider among us")
- **Positive**: Community-building moments
- **Challenging**: Conflicts and difficulties

**Card Structure**:
Each card contains:
- **Aspect**: The situation or concept
- **Prompt**: Question or situation to address
- **Word Creation Trigger**: Opportunity to create word reflecting this Aspect

**Turn Structure**:
On your turn:

1. **Draw and Read Aspect Card**: Read card aloud to group
2. **Discuss Aspect**: Collaboratively discuss what this means for community
3. **Create Scene** (Optional): Roleplay scene exploring Aspect
4. **Create Word**: Collaboratively create word in your dialect relating to Aspect
5. **Record Word**: Add to Language Sheet with definition and etymology

The turn passes to next player. Over multiple rounds across three ages, the language and community both develop.

**Word Creation Process**:
Creating words is collaborative:

**Discussion**: What concept needs naming? What does this mean to community?

**Brainstorming**: Propose sounds, syllable combinations, linguistic features. No "correct" approach—phonetic, poetic, pragmatic all work.

**Refinement**: Adjust and polish until group satisfied.

**Definition**: Explain what word means, including connotations and cultural significance.

**Etymology**: Explain how/why word emerged from this Aspect.

**Usage Example**: Demonstrate word in sentence or phrase.

**Example Word Creation**:
- **Aspect**: "We share everything we have"
- **Discussion**: The commune practices radical resource sharing
- **Word Created**: "Shurra" (SHOO-rah)
- **Definition**: The joy felt when giving something you need but sharing makes you happier
- **Etymology**: Comes from two members sharing their last food, discovering shared meal tasted better
- **Usage**: "I felt shurra watching them eat my portion"

Words encode cultural values, experiences, and community identity.

**Language Evolution**:
As ages progress, language evolves:
- **Age One**: Basic vocabulary for core concepts
- **Age Two**: Words for conflict, change, complexity
- **Age Three**: Words for loss, departure, endings

This linguistic evolution mirrors community trajectory.

**The Isolation Breaks**:
In Age Three, players determine how isolation ends:
- External contact (discovered, rescued, invaded)
- Internal dissolution (community fragments, members leave)
- Environmental change (resource depletion, disaster)
- Planned ending (purpose fulfilled, time to leave)

This ending mechanism is predetermined—isolation WILL break—but how it happens emerges from play.

**The Epilogue**:
After final age, each player speaks one final statement:

**In Common Language**: Describe where your character is now, after community ended

**In Your Dialect**: One last sentence using created words, honoring language before it disappears forever

These final dialect sentences carry profound weight—last utterances in language about to die. Players often struggle with emotion delivering them.

**Post-Game**:
The Language Sheet remains as artifact documenting what existed. Some groups keep their dialect documentation. Others ceremonially destroy it, making the loss literal.

**Variants and Modifications**:

**Reduced Playtime**: Fewer words per age shortens game to 2-3 hours

**Expanded Play**: More words creates richer languages over 5-6 hours

**Solo Play**: Can be played solo as worldbuilding/language creation exercise, though loses collaborative emergence

**Interconnected Dialects**: Multiple groups creating dialects for connected communities, then playing contact scenarios

These mechanics create framework generating emotionally resonant language-building experiences through structured collaborative creation, inevitable loss, and linguistic-cultural documentation.

## Narrative Philosophy and Design Goals

Dialect's narrative philosophy centers on language as cultural memory, communities as linguistic shapers, and loss as generator of meaning.

**Language is Community Identity**: The game's core insight recognizes language and community as inseparable. Language isn't neutral communication tool—it shapes thought, encodes values, expresses cultural priorities. Creating unique vocabulary collaboratively builds community identity tangibly. Each word represents shared experience, value, or concept unique to this group. The language IS the community's soul made audible.

**Linguistic Relativity Through Play**: Dialect makes Sapir-Whorf hypothesis (language shapes thought) experientially real. As players create words for concepts lacking common language equivalents, they experience how language enables thinking. A word for "exile-sweetness" or "shared-scarcity-joy" makes that concept thinkable, shareable, real. This demonstrates linguistic relativity without requiring theoretical understanding.

**Emergent Sociolinguistics**: The game procedurally generates sociolinguistic processes:
- **Community Formation**: Shared language building group identity
- **Linguistic Innovation**: New words for new experiences
- **Semantic Shift**: Word meanings evolving with community
- **Language Death**: Erosion when isolation ends

Players experience these abstract concepts concretely through their created language's trajectory.

**Loss as Meaning Generator**: The inevitable ending creates emotional weight. Knowing the language will die makes its creation precious. Each word carries bittersweetness—beautiful but temporary. This impermanence transforms collaborative creation from mere exercise into meaningful experience. We value what we lose. The game makes linguistic death personally felt.

**Cultural Memory Encoded**: Words become repositories of cultural memory. "Shurra" isn't just sound—it's memory of that first shared meal, the commune's founding principle, every instance of joyful sharing. Words accumulate cultural sediment, becoming rich with associations. When language dies, those memories become inaccessible except through translation losing nuance.

**Isolation as Creative Force**: The game recognizes isolation enables linguistic innovation. Cut off from mainstream language evolution, communities develop unique features. Isolation isn't just setting—it's requirement for linguistic distinctiveness. When isolation breaks, linguistic uniqueness erodes through contact and assimilation.

**Collaborative Linguistic Creation**: Dialect democratizes conlanging. Traditional constructed languages (Klingon, Dothraki, Elvish) are expert-created over years. Dialect makes language creation accessible to anyone through:
- Prompts guiding without requiring expertise
- Collaborative brainstorming reducing individual pressure
- Accepting any linguistic choices (no "wrong" words)
- Focus on meaning over technical correctness

This accessibility enables linguistic creativity for non-linguists.

**Three-Age Narrative Arc**: The predetermined structure creates satisfying narrative:
- **Foundation**: Hope, unity, building
- **Crisis**: Challenges, conflicts, fracturing
- **Dissolution**: Loss, endings, grief

This arc mirrors real community life cycles and creates emotional progression from optimism through struggle to loss.

**Academic Rigor Meeting Emotional Play**: Dialect bridges academic linguistics and emotional storytelling:
- Linguistically sound (words function like real language)
- Anthropologically grounded (communities work like real isolated groups)
- Emotionally affecting (creates genuine feelings about fictional language)
- Intellectually engaging (prompts linguistic thinking)

This synthesis creates unique experience—both learning and feeling.

**The Epilogue's Power**: Final dialect sentences create cathartic moment. Speaking language one last time before it dies forever makes abstract loss concrete. Players often report struggling with emotion delivering final statements. This demonstrates emotional attachment to collaboratively created linguistic-cultural artifact—remarkable achievement for 3-4 hours of play.

**Language as Play Artifact**: The Language Sheet documents what existed. Unlike many game experiences evaporating after play, Dialect creates persistent artifact. The recorded language can be revisited, shared, preserved. Some groups use their created dialects in subsequent storytelling or worldbuilding. The language outlives the play session, though the game's fiction presents it as dying.

**Respectful Engagement with Language Death**: The game treats language death seriously, reflecting real-world tragedies of endangered languages. This respectful engagement educates players about real linguistic-cultural losses while maintaining appropriate distance through fictional framing.

These philosophical commitments create game that's simultaneously intellectual exploration of linguistics/anthropology, emotional story about community and loss, collaborative creative exercise, and meditation on impermanence and meaning. It demonstrates that academic rigor and emotional depth can coexist productively in game design.

## Cultural Impact and Recognition

Dialect's cultural impact manifested through academic adoption, linguistic community interest, and influence on language-focused game design.

**Academic and Educational Adoption**: Dialect found significant use beyond entertainment:

**Linguistics Courses**: Professors used Dialect to teach:
- Sociolinguistics and language variation
- Language death and endangerment
- Linguistic relativity (Sapir-Whorf hypothesis)
- Language and identity
- Constructed languages

The game made abstract concepts experientially real for students.

**Anthropology Classes**: Teaching about:
- Cultural relativism
- Community formation and dissolution
- Isolation and contact
- Cultural memory and transmission
- Ethnographic thinking

**Creative Writing Programs**: For worldbuilding, character development, and cultural creation exercises.

**Language Preservation Advocacy**: Some language activists used Dialect in workshops raising awareness about endangered languages.

**Critical Recognition**:
- **2019 ENNIE Award for Best Writing**
- **2019 Golden Geek Award nominee**
- Extensive positive reviews across gaming media
- Featured in academic conferences on games and learning
- Covered in linguistics publications and blogs

**Influence on Language-Focused Games**: Dialect inspired subsequent language-focused designs:

**Direct Influences**:
- **Sign** (Thorny Games, 2019): Gestural language creation for isolated community
- **Xenolanguage** (Thorny Games, 2019): First contact linguistic challenges
- Various community language-creation games

**Design Patterns Established**:
- Language creation as primary play activity
- Collaborative emergent linguistics
- Cultural concepts encoded in vocabulary
- Language death as narrative device
- Academic concepts through play

**Story Game Community Impact**: Within story games, Dialect demonstrated:

**Intellectual Rigor Works**: Games can engage seriously with academic disciplines while remaining accessible

**Niche Audiences Matter**: Targeting linguistic/literary audiences created dedicated community

**Crossover Potential**: Story games can appeal beyond typical gaming demographics

**Emotional Sophistication**: Players engage with complex emotions (grief for fictional language) through thoughtful design

**Thorny Games**: Dialect established Hymes and Seyalioglu's publishing house:

**Product Line**:
- Dialect core game
- Expanded Backdrops (additional setting templates)
- Sign (gestural language variant)
- Xenolanguage (first contact expansion)

**Design Philosophy**: Thorny Games became known for linguistically sophisticated emotionally resonant designs.

**Conlanging Community**: Dialect reached constructed language enthusiasts:

**Collaborative Conlanging**: Demonstrated language creation could be collaborative quick process, not just solitary methodical work

**Emotional Conlanging**: Showed language creation could prioritize emotional resonance over technical completeness

**Accessibility**: Made conlanging approachable for non-experts

**Play Culture and Community**:

**Language Documentation**: Players shared created languages online:
- Full vocabularies with etymologies
- Cultural context explanations
- Stories using created dialects
- Appreciation for emergent linguistic beauty

**Backdrop Creations**: Community created additional Backdrops:
- Various time periods and settings
- Diverse cultural contexts
- Experimental scenarios

**Actual Play**: Podcast and stream features, though challenging format (watching language creation less dynamic than action-heavy play)

**Long-term Viability**: Years after release:
- Continued academic adoption
- Regular play in story game circles
- Ongoing Backdrop development
- Thorny Games product line expansion
- Cultural relevance maintained

**Design Lessons**: Dialect taught important principles:

1. **Academic Concepts Can Be Playable**: Linguistic theory accessible through play
2. **Constraints Generate Investment**: Knowing language dies creates emotional weight
3. **Collaboration Enables Complexity**: Group creation achieves sophisticated results
4. **Documentation Matters**: Language Sheet as artifact enhances experience
5. **Crossover Audiences Exist**: Literary/academic players expand market
6. **Emotional Sophistication Works**: Players engage seriously with complex feelings
7. **Expertise Can Inform Without Requiring**: Linguistic knowledge enriches design without creating barriers

**Publishing Impact**: Dialect demonstrated:
- Niche sophisticated designs can succeed commercially
- Academic expertise enhances game design
- Production quality matters for literary games
- Direct publishing (Thorny Games) viable for focused catalogs

**Legacy Contributions**:

**Language-Focused Game Category**: Established language creation as viable primary game mechanic

**Academic-Game Bridge**: Demonstrated games as effective teaching tools for complex concepts

**Emotional Design**: Showed games can create genuine grief for fictional creations

**Collaborative Linguistics**: Democratized language creation through accessible collaborative processes

Dialect's greatest achievement may be making linguistics emotionally affecting and accessible—demonstrating that language creation can be collaborative quick meaningful process generating genuine attachment and grief for fictional languages. It proved academic rigor and emotional depth create synergy rather than conflict in thoughtful game design.
