# Cartel

**Publisher**: Magpie Games
**Designer**: Mark Diaz Truman
**Year**: 2018
**System**: Powered by the Apocalypse
**Genre**: crime-drama, narco-thriller, political
**Status**: in-print

## Overview

Cartel, designed by Mark Diaz Truman in 2018, creates narco-thriller stories where players portray people entangled in drug war—cartel members, law enforcement, journalists, civilians—navigating violence, corruption, and impossible moral choices in fictionalized Mexican drug conflict, using PbtA framework with playbooks spanning multiple sides (not all playing "good guys"), creating morally complex political crime drama examining how drug war destroys communities while acknowledging difficult subject requires thoughtful handling.

**Controversial Subject**: Drug war gaming requires sensitivity—game includes safety tools and acknowledges real human cost

**Multi-Sided Play**: Not all PCs on same "side"—some cartel, some law enforcement, some civilian, creating tension

**Playbooks**: Diverse roles—Cartel Boss, Hitman, Dirty Cop, Journalist, Survivor, etc.

**Moral Complexity**: No heroes, just people making difficult choices in terrible situation

**Political**: Examines drug war as political/economic phenomenon, not just crime adventure

## Core Mechanics

**PbtA 2d6+stat**: Standard framework

**Multi-Factional**: PCs may oppose each other mechanically and narratively

**Violence and Corruption**: Tracking how drug war corrupts everyone involved

**Harm and Trauma**: Physical and psychological consequences

**Political Moves**: Navigating power structures

**Safety Tools**: X-card, lines/veils explicitly included for difficult content

## Setting

**Fictionalized Mexico**: Drug war inspired by real conflicts but fictionalized for play

**Multiple Perspectives**: Cartel members, law enforcement, civilians all protagonists

**Systemic Violence**: Drug war as institutional rather than individual problem

**Themes**: Corruption, violence, impossible choices, systemic problems, human cost

## Cultural Impact

Demonstrated difficult political subjects could be gaming material if handled thoughtfully. Sparked discussions about appropriate subjects for games. Showed PbtA flexibility for crime drama.

## Reception

Praised for thoughtful handling of difficult subject, moral complexity. Some debated appropriateness of drug war gaming. Critical acclaim for mechanics but broader debate about subject matter.
