# Thirsty Sword Lesbians

**Publisher**: Evil Hat Productions
**Designer**: April Kit Walsh, et al.
**Year**: 2021
**System**: Powered by the Apocalypse
**Genre**: fantasy, queer, swashbuckling, romance
**Status**: in-print

## Overview

Thirsty Sword Lesbians, designed by April Kit Walsh and published by Evil Hat in 2021, creates unapologetically queer swashbuckling fantasy where lesbian/sapphic protagonists fight patriarchy, oppression, and evil through swordplay and flirting, using PbtA with playbooks like The Beast (cursed monster-form), The Chosen (prophesied hero), The Infamous (charming scoundrel), combining sword-fighting moves with flirting/romance mechanics where emotional connections are as important as combat prowess, creating joyful queer power fantasy that became surprise mainstream success.

**Queer Centering**: Explicitly lesbian/sapphic protagonists—not "representation" but actual focus

**Sword and Flirt**: Combat and romance both mechanically supported

**Playbooks**: Ten archetypes blending fighting styles with emotional roles

**Fight the Patriarchy**: Literally—many enemies represent oppressive systems

**Joyful Tone**: Despite serious themes, emphasizes queer joy and community

**Mainstream Success**: Evil Hat backing brought major publisher support to explicitly queer game

## Core Mechanics

**PbtA 2d6+stat**: Standard Apocalypse World framework

**Playbooks**: The Beast, The Chosen, The Devoted, The Infamous, The Kinetic, The Nature Witch, The Scoundrel, The Seeker, The Spooky Witch, The Trickster

**Sword Moves**: Combat mechanics balancing lethality with swashbuckling fun

**Flirting Moves**: Romance and attraction mechanically supported

**Emotional Bonds**: Tracking relationships between characters

**Strings**: Social/romantic leverage similar to Monsterhearts

**Defying Toxic Masculinity**: Mechanics for confronting and defeating patriarchal forces

## Setting

**Variable Fantasy**: Works for any swashbuckling fantasy setting

**Six Settings Provided**: Complete mini-settings demonstrating range

**Queer-Normative**: LGBTQ+ identities default, not exceptional

**Themes**: Fighting oppression, queer community, found family, romance, identity, joy despite struggle

## Cultural Impact

**Mainstream Queer Gaming**: Evil Hat's support brought explicitly queer game to mainstream visibility

**Viral Title**: Name became meme, driving awareness

**Commercial Success**: Strong sales demonstrated market for queer-centered games

**Representation Milestone**: Showed major publishers would back explicitly LGBTQ+ content

**Critical Acclaim**: ENnie Award, mainstream gaming press coverage

**Cultural Moment**: Released during She-Ra animated series popularity surge

## Reception

Praised for joyful queer celebration, fun swashbuckling, emotional depth, beautiful production. Title controversy (some found "Thirsty" inappropriate) faded as gameplay quality won over critics. Strong commercial and critical success.

## Legacy

Demonstrated major publishers would support explicitly queer games. Showed queer centering (not just representation) was commercially viable. Influenced queer game design. Established sword-sapphic as recognized subgenre.
