# Monsterhearts

**Publisher**: [[Buried Without Ceremony]]
**Designer**: [[Avery Alder]]
**Year**: 2012
**System**: Powered by the Apocalypse
**Genre**: Teenage Monster Drama, Queer Horror
**Status**: in-print

## Historical Context

Monsterhearts, designed by Avery Alder and published by Buried Without Ceremony in 2012, revolutionized how tabletop RPGs approached sexuality, queerness, adolescent identity, and the horror of becoming. Subtitled "A Story Game about the Lives of Teenage Monsters," it used supernatural horror as metaphor for adolescent transformation, sexual awakening, queer identity exploration, and the terrifying experience of bodies and desires changing beyond your control. The game stands as landmark in queer representation, explicitly centering LGBTQIA+ experiences while creating mechanically sophisticated framework for exploring desire, power, identity, and the monstrous feminine/queer.

The early 2010s indie RPG scene increasingly explored mature themes and marginalized perspectives, but few games centered queerness as explicitly or thoughtfully as Monsterhearts. Avery Alder, a queer non-binary designer, brought lived experience and sophisticated understanding of queer theory to create game where queerness wasn't exceptional but assumed, where fluid sexuality and gender were mechanically integrated, and where the horror came not from being queer but from navigating desire and power in world that makes both dangerous.

The game drew inspiration from supernatural teenage drama media—Buffy the Vampire Slayer, True Blood, Ginger Snaps, Twilight—but queered them explicitly. Where these sources often coded queerness subtextually (vampirism as AIDS metaphor, werewolf transformation as puberty/queerness), Monsterhearts made the subtext text. Characters were explicitly queer or questioning, sexuality was fluid and complicated, and the horror came from how desire makes us vulnerable, how our bodies betray us, how power and attraction intertwine dangerously.

The Skins (character playbooks) embodied different aspects of teenage monstrous experience:
- **The Ghoul**: Died and returned wrong, embodying dissociation and alienation
- **The Vampire**: Feeding on others emotionally and literally, about parasitic relationships
- **The Werewolf**: Uncontrollable transformation, bodies changing frighteningly
- **The Witch**: Power through suffering, reclaiming agency from victimhood
- **The Mortal**: Being ordinary in extraordinary circumstances
- **The Infernal**: Bargaining for power, selling pieces of self
- **The Fae**: Existing between worlds, not fully belonging anywhere
- **The Queen**: Social power's dark seduction
- And others, each exploring different monstrous teenage experiences

Alder adapted Apocalypse World's Powered by the Apocalypse (PbtA) framework brilliantly. The Moves became emotionally and sexually charged: "Turn Someone On" wasn't euphemism but literal move about creating sexual/romantic tension; "Lash Out Physically" channeled violence's relationship to desire; "Gaze Into the Abyss" represented confronting your monstrous nature. These Moves mechanized how teenage sexuality, identity, and monstrousness intertwined.

The Strings mechanic proved revolutionary. When you gained leverage over someone—manipulating them emotionally, seeing them vulnerable, having dirt on them, sharing intimacy—you gained a String on them. Strings could be spent to influence that person or resist their influence. This mechanized the complex power dynamics in relationships, how intimacy creates vulnerability, how desire and control interweave, how teenage relationships become battlegrounds for identity and power.

The Darkest Self mechanic embodied losing control to your monstrous nature. When overwhelmed, characters entered their Darkest Self—the Ghoul's dissociation, the Werewolf's feral rage, the Vampire's predatory hunger, the Witch's vengeful fury. Escaping required specific conditions, often involving vulnerability or connection. This mechanized how adolescence feels—being hijacked by forces beyond your control, needing others to bring you back from edges.

Crucially, Monsterhearts included robust safety tools and explicit discussion of consent, boundaries, and sensitive content. The game dealt with sexuality, power dynamics, abuse potential, and identity trauma—requiring thoughtful, consensual engagement. Alder provided frameworks for negotiating boundaries while enabling exploration of difficult themes safely.

The 2012 release received immediate critical acclaim and excited response from queer gaming communities. Here was game that didn't treat queerness as exotic exception or tragic storyline but as default assumption, where fluid sexuality and gender exploration were mechanically integrated and thematically central. The game won numerous awards and influenced how subsequent RPGs approached sexuality, queerness, and identity.

Monsterhearts became cultural touchstone for queer gaming, demonstrating that games could center LGBTQIA+ experiences while being mechanically sophisticated and commercially successful. It proved that exploring difficult themes around sexuality, power, and identity could create compelling gameplay when handled thoughtfully and designed by people from those communities.

Second edition (2017) refined mechanics, expanded Skins, and updated safety tools, demonstrating sustained relevance and community commitment. The game's influence extended far beyond its direct play—it changed conversations about representation, queerness in gaming, and how mechanics can explore identity and desire.

## Mechanical Innovations

Monsterhearts introduced powerful mechanics exploring teenage monstrosity, sexuality, and power:

**The Skins (Playbooks)**: Instead of generic character classes, Skins embody specific monstrous teenage experiences. Each has unique Moves, Darkest Self, and relationship to desire/power:
- **The Ghoul**: Death and return, dissociation, alienation from life
- **The Vampire**: Feeding on others, parasitic intimacy, predatory desire
- **The Werewolf**: Uncontrollable transformation, body horror, animal nature
- **The Witch**: Power through suffering, hexes and vengeance, reclaimed agency
- **The Mortal**: Being ordinary, humanity as strength and limitation
- **The Infernal**: Demonic bargains, selling self for power, Faustian deals
- **The Fae**: Between worlds, fae promises, belonging nowhere fully
- **The Queen**: Social power, manipulation, dark charisma
- **The Ghost**: Unfinished business, haunting, incorporeal existence
- **The Serpentine**: Hypnotic, cold-blooded, alien psychology
- **The Chosen**: Destiny's burden, prophesied hero, duty versus desire

Each Skin explores different aspect of teenage monstrous experience.

**Strings**: The signature mechanic. Gain Strings on someone when:
- You see them vulnerable
- You manipulate them successfully
- They share intimacy with you
- You have dirt/leverage on them
- You Turn Them On successfully

Spend Strings to:
- Add +1 to rolls against them
- Offer them experience to do what you want
- Inflict conditions on them
- Interfere with their actions

This mechanizes power dynamics in relationships—how intimacy creates vulnerability, how desire and control intertwine.

**Turn Someone On**: Explicit move about creating sexual/romantic tension. When you Turn Someone On (and succeed), give them Condition "turned on" and gain String on them. They choose: give themselves to you, promise something, or lash out. This makes attraction mechanically relevant and dangerously powerful.

**The Darkest Self**: Each Skin has unique Darkest Self—monstrous nature taking full control:
- **Ghoul's Darkest Self**: Dissociation, becoming terrifyingly empty
- **Vampire's Darkest Self**: Predatory feeding, hurting those you care about
- **Werewolf's Darkest Self**: Feral rage, uncontrollable violence
- **Witch's Darkest Self**: Vengeful fury, hexing everyone

Triggering Darkest Self when you take fourth Harm or other specific triggers. Escaping requires conditions unique to each Skin, often involving vulnerability or connection. This mechanizes losing control to monstrous nature.

**Harm and Healing**: Damage isn't just physical but emotional and social. Conditions like "turned on," "terrified," "broken," or "shamed" mechanically affect characters. Healing requires vulnerability and connection, not just rest.

**Sex Moves**: When characters have sex, trigger their Skin's Sex Move with unique effects. This makes sexual intimacy mechanically and narratively significant, creating consequences beyond the moment.

**Season Advancement**: Characters advance between "episodes," improving abilities, adding Moves, or changing fundamentally. This creates television-like season structure.

**Moves**: PbtA-style Moves include:
- **Gaze Into the Abyss**: Confront your monstrous nature
- **Lash Out Physically**: Violence entwined with desire and power
- **Manipulate an NPC**: Social power over others
- **Run Away**: Fleeing danger or emotion
- **Hold Steady**: Resisting emotional/physical threats

These Moves mechanize how teenage monstrosity manifests.

**The MC Moves**: MC (Master of Ceremonies/GM) has Moves enabling them to:
- Put them in a spot
- Reveal uncomfortable truths
- Separate them
- Make them feel something intensely
- Announce trouble elsewhere

These create dramatic teenage horror scenarios.

**Safety Tools**: Explicit content negotiation, Lines & Veils, X-Card integration, and boundary-setting tools. The game acknowledges dealing with sexuality, power, and identity requires consensual, safe engagement.

## Cultural Impact

Monsterhearts' cultural impact on queer representation and gaming was profound:

**Queer Gaming Landmark**: Monsterhearts stands as watershed moment in LGBTQIA+ representation in tabletop gaming. It was first major RPG explicitly centering queerness throughout design—not as exception but assumption, not as tragic storyline but lived reality. This representation mattered enormously to queer gamers.

**Fluid Sexuality and Gender**: The game mechanically assumed fluid sexuality and gender exploration. Characters weren't locked into orientations; attraction happened across genders; identity was questioning and evolving. This fluidity reflected queer experiences more accurately than binary assumptions.

**Queer Horror Exploration**: Monsterhearts demonstrated how horror and queerness intertwined powerfully—not because queerness is monstrous but because both involve becoming, transformation, society's fear of difference, and bodies/desires beyond control. This sophisticated queer horror theory influenced subsequent games and media.

**Power and Desire Mechanics**: The Strings system mechanized how power and desire intertwine in relationships, how intimacy creates vulnerability, how attraction becomes leverage. This sophisticated understanding of relationship power dynamics influenced how games approached social interactions.

**Safety Tools Advancement**: Monsterhearts pushed safety tools forward, demonstrating that games exploring difficult themes required robust consent and boundary negotiation. This influenced broader adoption of safety tools across indie gaming.

**Critical Recognition**:
- 2013 Golden Geek Best RPG Nominee
- 2013 Indie RPG Award Winner for Innovation
- Extensive critical praise across gaming media
- Featured in queer media and academic discussions
- Studied for representation and mechanical innovation
- Referenced constantly in queer gaming conversations

**Avery Alder's Impact**: Established Alder as crucial voice in queer gaming and design innovation. Their subsequent work (Dream Askew, The Quiet Year) continued exploring marginalized experiences and innovative mechanics.

**Representation Conversation**: The game's success sparked important conversations:
- How do we center marginalized experiences?
- What does good LGBTQIA+ representation look like?
- How can mechanics explore identity and sexuality thoughtfully?
- Who should design games about marginalized experiences?

These discussions shaped indie gaming's approach to representation.

**Influence on PbtA Games**: Demonstrated how PbtA framework could explore sexuality, identity, and horror. Influenced subsequent PbtA games' approaches to:
- Playbooks embodying specific experiences
- Moves mechanizing social/emotional dynamics
- Safety tools integration
- Relationship mechanics

**Second Edition (2017)**: Updated version refined mechanics, expanded Skins (adding The Chosen and The Serpentine), improved safety tools, and demonstrated sustained relevance and community commitment.

**Academic Attention**: Gender studies, queer theory, and game studies scholars examined Monsterhearts for:
- Queer representation in gaming
- How mechanics explore identity
- Sexuality in game design
- Horror as queer metaphor
- Community-specific design

**Community Formation**: Created devoted community of queer gamers and allies who:
- Shared actual play experiences
- Created additional Skins
- Discussed queer themes and mechanics
- Supported each other through difficult play experiences
- Championed queer-inclusive gaming

**Actual Play Impact**: Recorded sessions demonstrated how the game created intensely emotional, queer-positive stories, introducing broader audiences to queer gaming while showcasing mechanical sophistication.

**Educational Use**: Used in educational contexts for:
- Gender and sexuality studies
- Queer theory application
- Game design education
- Understanding power dynamics
- Exploring identity safely

**Mainstream Influence**: While remaining indie, Monsterhearts influenced mainstream gaming's approach to sexuality, queerness, and identity, demonstrating that centering these experiences was commercially and critically viable.

## Design Philosophy

Monsterhearts embodied sophisticated design philosophy about queerness, horror, and adolescence:

**Queerness as Default**: Rather than treating straight/cis as default with queer as exception, Monsterhearts assumed fluidity and queerness as baseline. This fundamental shift created space where LGBTQIA+ experiences were centered rather than marginalized.

**Horror as Metaphor for Becoming**: The game used monster horror to explore adolescent transformation—bodies changing beyond control, desires emerging frighteningly, identities shifting, becoming something you don't fully understand or control. This resonated powerfully with queer experiences of coming out, transition, and identity exploration.

**Power and Desire Intertwined**: The Strings system embodied understanding that desire and power can't be separated in relationships. Attraction creates vulnerability; intimacy becomes leverage; love and manipulation interweave. This sophisticated relationship model created mature, complex social play.

**Vulnerability as Requirement**: Escaping Darkest Self, healing, forming genuine connections—all required vulnerability. The game mechanically rewarded opening up despite danger, reflecting how genuine connection requires risk.

**Bodies as Sites of Horror**: The physical transformations, feeding needs, and uncontrollable changes mechanized body horror reflecting puberty, transition, chronic illness, and how bodies betray or become strange. This resonated with queer, trans, and disabled experiences.

**Sexuality as Central**: Rather than avoiding sexuality or treating it as "adult content," Monsterhearts made sexuality mechanically and narratively central. Turn Someone On, Sex Moves, and desire-driven actions positioned sexuality as fundamental to teenage experience.

**Consent and Safety Priority**: Acknowledging the game dealt with sensitive content, Alder prioritized consent tools, boundary negotiation, and safety mechanisms. This modeled how to explore difficult themes responsibly.

**Monsters as Marginalized**: The game explored how society treats difference, how being "other" feels, how monstrousness and queerness parallel—not because queer people are monsters but because society's fear of difference manifests similarly.

**Emotional Truth Over Literal**: Focused on emotional reality of teenage experience rather than literal accuracy. The heightened drama, impossible situations, and supernatural elements created metaphorical truth about adolescence.

**Community-Specific Design**: Alder's queer, non-binary perspective informed every design choice. This exemplified how marginalized designers bring authenticity and nuance impossible from outside perspectives.

## Setting and Themes

Monsterhearts' contemporary high school setting with supernatural elements created distinctive atmosphere:

**Anywhere High School**: The setting is deliberately generic contemporary high school—could be anywhere, any town. This universality makes it accessible while allowing customization. The ordinariness makes the monstrousness more striking.

**The Mundane and Monstrous**: Everyday high school experiences (classes, parties, social hierarchies, romance, friendship drama) coexist with supernatural horror (feeding on others, transforming violently, hexing enemies, making demon bargains). This juxtaposition creates distinctive tone.

**Social Hierarchies**: High school social structures—popular kids, outcasts, cliques, status games—become literally monstrous through supernatural power. The Queen's social manipulation gains magical force; the Ghost haunts social spaces; the Fae exists between social worlds.

**Contemporary Setting**: Unlike period horror or fantasy worlds, Monsterhearts happens now with smartphones, social media, contemporary references. This groundedness makes themes more immediate and relatable.

**The Secret Monster World**: Like Buffy, True Blood, etc., monsters exist secretly among mundane population. But rather than exploring how monsters hide from humanity, Monsterhearts focuses on how teenage monsters navigate each other and their own monstrousness.

**No Monster Hunters**: Unlike media where monsters fight slayers, Monsterhearts rarely features external threats. The horror and conflict come from within—the characters themselves, their desires, their transformations, their relationships with each other.

**Themes**:

**Sexual Awakening**: The game explores how sexuality emerges frighteningly and powerfully during adolescence, how desire makes us vulnerable, how attraction can feel like losing control.

**Queer Identity**: Questioning sexuality and gender, coming out, finding community, facing discrimination, navigating fluid identities—all central to Monsterhearts' thematic focus.

**Body Horror**: Bodies changing beyond control, unwanted transformations, physical needs overriding will, dysphoria and alienation from your own flesh—resonating with puberty, transition, chronic illness, disability.

**Power Dynamics**: How relationships involve power struggles, how intimacy creates leverage, how desire and manipulation intertwine, how social hierarchies oppress and corrupt.

**Becoming**: The constant transformation—into monsters, into adults, into your authentic self, into something you fear—capturing adolescence's terrifying uncertainty.

**Desire and Danger**: How wanting things/people makes you vulnerable, how attraction can be dangerous, how pursuing desire risks everything, how love and horror intertwine.

**Otherness**: Being different, feeling monstrous, not fitting in, existing outside norms—paralleling queer, trans, neurodivergent, and other marginalized experiences.

**Loss of Control**: To bodies, to desires, to monstrous natures, to darkest selves—reflecting adolescent powerlessness and terror of transformation.

**Community and Isolation**: Finding people who understand versus navigating alone, chosen family versus biological family, solidarity among monsters versus competition.

**Surviving Yourself**: The real horror isn't external but internal—your own nature, your desires, your capacity to hurt others, becoming something you fear.

## Reception and Legacy

Monsterhearts achieved significant critical acclaim and lasting cultural impact:

**Critical Acclaim**:
- 2013 Golden Geek Best RPG Nominee
- 2013 Indie RPG Award Winner for Innovation
- Extensive praise across gaming media
- Featured in queer publications and media
- Academic attention from gender/queer studies
- Recognition as queer gaming landmark
- Studied extensively in game design contexts

**Queer Community Impact**: The game resonated profoundly with LGBTQIA+ gamers who finally had game centering their experiences. Community response was enthusiastically positive, with queer gamers championing it widely.

**Representation Advancement**: Monsterhearts advanced LGBTQIA+ representation in gaming by:
- Centering queer experiences rather than marginalizing them
- Assuming fluid sexuality and gender
- Exploring queer themes mechanically, not just narratively
- Being designed by queer creator with lived experience
- Demonstrating commercial viability of queer-focused games

**Design Influence**: The game influenced numerous subsequent designs:
- **The Veil**: Exploring identity through cyberpunk
- **Masks**: Teenage superhero identity struggles
- Various PbtA games exploring sexuality and identity
- Relationship mechanics in indie games
- Safety tools adoption across gaming

**Safety Tools Impact**: Monsterhearts' integration of robust safety tools influenced their broader adoption across indie and mainstream gaming.

**Second Edition Reception**: The 2017 refined edition received warm reception, demonstrating sustained relevance and community commitment to continual improvement.

**Community Development**:
- Devoted fanbase created additional Skins
- Extensive actual play recordings
- Online communities supporting players
- Fan materials expanding content
- Discussions exploring queer themes deeply

**Academic Recognition**: Scholars examined Monsterhearts for:
- Queer representation in games
- How mechanics explore identity/sexuality
- Horror as queer metaphor
- Community-specific design
- Power dynamics in relationships

**Educational Use**: Used in academic contexts for:
- Gender and sexuality studies
- Queer theory
- Game design education
- Understanding power and consent
- Safe identity exploration

**Actual Play Impact**: Recorded sessions showcased emotionally intense, queer-positive stories, introducing broader audiences while demonstrating sophisticated mechanics.

**Cultural Conversations**: The game sparked important discussions:
- Representation in gaming
- Who should design games about marginalized experiences
- How mechanics can explore identity
- Safety and consent in gaming
- Queerness in horror

**Mainstream Influence**: While remaining indie, Monsterhearts influenced how mainstream gaming approached sexuality, queerness, and identity representation.

**Criticisms and Challenges**:
- **Intensity**: Emotionally demanding content wasn't for everyone
- **Safety Concerns**: Exploring sexuality/power required careful, consensual engagement
- **Specific Experiences**: Not all queer experiences represented equally
- **Potential for Harm**: Misuse of sexuality/power mechanics could create uncomfortable situations
- **Learning Curve**: The emotional sophistication required maturity and communication

**Modern Relevance**: As gaming increasingly prioritizes diverse representation and inclusive design, Monsterhearts' approaches remain relevant and influential.

**Avery Alder's Legacy**: Established Alder as crucial voice in queer gaming and innovative design, leading to subsequent influential work.

**Lasting Impact on Queer Gaming**: Created template for how to center LGBTQIA+ experiences thoughtfully, mechanically, and commercially successfully.

**Final Assessment**: Monsterhearts stands as landmark in queer representation and mechanical innovation. It proved that centering LGBTQIA+ experiences could create commercially successful, critically acclaimed games. Its sophisticated exploration of sexuality, power, and identity through mechanics influenced how designers approached these themes. Its integration of safety tools advanced responsible design around difficult content.

More than specific mechanics, Monsterhearts demonstrated that games designed by and for marginalized communities could achieve excellence while remaining authentic to those experiences. It showed that queerness needn't be exceptional or tragic but could be default assumption, that fluid sexuality and gender could be mechanically integrated, and that horror and queerness intertwined powerfully when explored thoughtfully.

For queer gamers, Monsterhearts provided experience impossible in most RPGs—seeing themselves centered, exploring identity safely, playing through metaphors for coming out and transformation. For gaming broadly, it advanced conversations about representation, mechanics, and whose stories deserve telling.

The game's influence extends beyond direct play to how we think about identity in gaming, how mechanics can explore marginalized experiences, and how thoughtful design can create space for authentic, transformative stories. For these contributions, Monsterhearts deserves recognition as genuinely revolutionary design that changed what we believed possible in queer representation and tabletop gaming.
