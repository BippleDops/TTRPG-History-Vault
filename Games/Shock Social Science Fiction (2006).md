# Shock: Social Science Fiction

**Publisher**: Glyphpress
**Designer**: Joshua AC Newman
**Year**: 2006
**System**: Shock System
**Genre**: science-fiction, social-commentary
**Status**: in-print

## Overview

Shock: Social Science Fiction, designed by Joshua AC Newman in 2006, enables groups to collaboratively create social science fiction stories examining how technological/social changes (Shocks) interact with societal problems (Issues) through matrix-based worldbuilding where players cross Issues with Shocks generating story space, create protagonists positioned at intersections, and explore consequences through play—perfectly capturing literary sci-fi's focus on social commentary over space opera action.

**Key Innovation**: Issue-Shock Matrix worldbuilding creates focused speculative fiction exploring specific social questions through extrapolation.

**Literary Sci-Fi**: Explicitly inspired by Ursula K. Le Guin, Samuel Delany, Kim Stanley Robinson—writers using sci-fi for social examination rather than adventure.

## Core Mechanics

**Matrix Creation**: Grid crossing Issues (social problems: poverty, gender, power) with Shocks (changes: AI, climate catastrophe, immortality)

**Protagonist Creation**: Characters positioned at matrix intersections embodying Issue-Shock interaction

**Antagonist Assignment**: Each player creates antagonist for another player's protagonist

**Minutiae**: Players create setting details collaboratively

**Resolution**: Dice-based with winner narrating outcomes

## Setting and Themes

**Variable Settings**: Every group creates unique speculative world

**Social Commentary**: Focus on examining societal issues through sci-fi lens

**Collaborative Creation**: World emerges from group discussion and matrix design

**Literary Inspiration**: Le Guin's thoughtful extrapolation over space battles

## Cultural Impact

Demonstrated collaborative worldbuilding matrix design. Influenced social commentary gaming. Showed how structure could guide speculative fiction creation.
