# InSpectres

**Publisher**: Memento Mori Theatricks
**Designer**: Jared Sorensen
**Year**: 2002
**System**: InSpectres System
**Genre**: comedy, horror, supernatural, franchise-simulation
**Status**: in-print

## Overview

InSpectres, designed by Jared Sorensen and published in 2002, delivers one of the most purely fun indie RPGs ever created—a game where players run supernatural investigation franchise (à la Ghostbusters), balancing catching ghosts/monsters with managing business operations, told through mockumentary format with confessional interviews and player-driven narrative control based on dice roll success. Rather than playing heroic paranormal investigators, you're struggling small business owners dealing with demanding clients, equipment malfunctions, interpersonal drama, and occasionally actual ghosts—all while cameras film your exploits for potential reality TV show.

The game's genius lies in its documentary framing device. Play alternates between "In-Game" scenes (actually investigating paranormal events) and "Confessionals" (character-to-camera interviews commenting on events). Confessionals let players introduce complications, foreshadow problems, and provide character insight, breaking the fourth wall in ways that enhance rather than disrupt immersion. This structure mimics reality TV mockumentary format (The Office, Parks & Recreation) making supernatural comedy feel grounded through documentary realism.

The franchise mechanics transform typical RPG resource management into compelling business simulation. The InSpectres franchise has stats like Bank (operating capital), Credit (business reputation), and Franchise Dice (available resources). Successful jobs earn franchise dice for future adventures while failures deplete resources, creating natural campaign arc where struggling startup either succeeds and expands or fails spectacularly.

The resolution system uses dice pools with brilliant twist: your roll doesn't just determine success/failure but narrative control. Rolling well lets you narrate what happens, determining truth about the supernatural threat and how events unfold. This distributes traditionally GM-exclusive authority to players based on character competence—the expert in occult lore who rolls well gets to define what the ghost actually wants; the tech specialist rolling high determines how equipment functions.

The game proved commercially successful for indie release, achieving multiple printings and inspiring franchise supplement (Vacation and Ghostbusters Companion). Critical reception praised the accessibility, the comedy-horror balance, and how mechanics supported the mockumentary premise perfectly. InSpectres demonstrated indie games could be funny, light, and accessible while still mechanically innovative—not everything needed grimdark seriousness or tragic inevitability.

## Historical Context

InSpectres emerged from Jared Sorensen's playful approach to indie game design. Unlike many Forge-affiliated designers pursuing serious narrativist themes, Sorensen wanted to prove narrative mechanics could serve comedy and fun as effectively as drama and tragedy.

The design drew inspiration from:
- **Ghostbusters**: Obviously, the core franchise
- **Reality TV**: The confessional format from Real World, Survivor
- **Mockumentaries**: The Office (UK version aired 2001), Christopher Guest films
- **Comedy Improv**: "Yes, and" principles and ensemble comedy
- **Indie Narrativism**: Forge-style player narrative authority

Development occurred during indie RPG's experimental phase when designers explored what narrative mechanics could achieve beyond traditional structures. Sorensen wanted accessible game that:
- Could be explained and played same session
- Generated naturally funny situations through mechanics
- Distributed narrative authority without complexity
- Created campaign arc through franchise growth/decline

The 2002 publication through Memento Mori Theatricks (Sorensen's publishing operation) reached audiences through PDF and small print runs. The Ghostbusters inspiration made concept immediately understandable—"you run paranormal investigation business like Ghostbusters" communicated everything needed.

Convention play drove awareness. InSpectres sessions reliably generated laughter and entertaining stories, making it perfect demo game. Groups discovering the game through cons would purchase PDF, spreading grassroots adoption.

Critical reception emphasized accessibility and fun. Reviewers noted how quickly groups could start playing (character creation takes 10 minutes) and how reliably sessions generated comedy. The confessional mechanic received particular praise for elegantly handling dramatic irony and foreshadowing.

The game found audiences beyond typical indie gamers. Traditional roleplayers who bounced off heavier narrative games enjoyed InSpectres's straightforward fun. Comedy-focused groups embraced it. Even non-gamers could grasp the mockumentary premise quickly.

Subsequent supplements expanded content: *Vacation* (paranormal tourism adventures) and unofficial *Ghostbusters Companion* material showed system's flexibility. The game proved sustainable through multiple editions, continuing availability via PDF/POD into 2020s.

The influence was quieter than design-revolutionary games but notable. Numerous comedy RPGs cited InSpectres as inspiration. The confessional device appeared in various forms. The idea that success-level could determine narrative control beyond simple success/failure influenced resolution design. And the game demonstrated comedy was legitimate genre for mechanically interesting RPGs.

## Core Mechanics

InSpectres uses simple dice pool system with narrative control twist:

### Franchise Creation

Group creates their InSpectres franchise business:

**Company Name**: Usually pun-based (Ghoul Intentions, Poltergeist Patrol, Spectral Solutions)

**Franchise Stats**:
- **Bank**: Operating capital (starts at 10)
- **Credit**: Business reputation (starts at 0)
- **Franchise Dice**: Available for jobs (starts at 10)

**Headquarters Location**: Where franchise operates

**Equipment**: What gear the company owns (often inadequate/malfunctioning)

This collaborative creation establishes shared investment in business success.

### Character Creation: The Team

Players create InSpectres team members:

**Concept**: Role in company (Researcher, Tech, Skeptic, Believer, Salesperson, Face)

**Skills**: Rate 4 skills (Academics, Athletics, Technology, Contact) from 1-4 dice

**Talent**: Special ability unique to character

**Stress Markers**: 5 boxes (when filled, character freaks out)

Quick creation enables starting play within 15 minutes.

### Job Structure: The Case

Each job follows structure:
1. **Client Call**: Potential customer reports problem
2. **Interview**: Meet client, assess situation
3. **Investigation**: Research and prepare
4. **Confrontation**: Face supernatural threat
5. **Resolution**: Resolve haunting/infestation
6. **Wrap-Up**: Get paid (or not), earn reputation

This episodic structure creates self-contained adventures.

### Resolution: Dice Pool with Narrative Control

When facing challenges, roll dice pool (skill rating):

**Results**:
- **0**: Failure—GM narrates outcome and introduces complications
- **1-2**: Partial success—GM narrates but player suggests direction
- **3-4**: Success—Player narrates outcome
- **5-6**: Exceptional success—Player narrates and earns permanent upgrade (Franchise Dice to Bank conversion, Credit increase)

The higher your roll, the more narrative control you gain.

**Example**: Researcher investigating library ghost rolls 4 dice, gets 3 successes. Player narrates: "I discover the ghost is former librarian murdered in 1923 over overdue book dispute. She's seeking her killer's descendant—who happens to be current head librarian."

This system distributes traditionally-GM authority to players based on character competence.

### Franchise Dice: The Resource

Franchise Dice represent company resources available for job:
- Spend before rolling to add dice to pool
- Limited pool requires strategic spending
- Earning new Franchise Dice happens through exceptional success

Managing Franchise Dice creates strategic layer—spend aggressively to succeed now or conserve for future jobs?

### Stress: The Freak-Out Mechanic

When scary/stressful events occur, mark Stress. At 5 Stress, character freaks out:
- Player narrates character's breakdown
- Temporarily removed from action
- Can recover through support from teammates

Stress adds comedy through breakdowns and creates mechanical reason for team support.

### Confessionals: Documentary Framing

At any time, players can call for Confessional:
- Character speaks directly to camera/audience
- Provides commentary on events
- Can foreshadow complications ("I knew that ghost jar wouldn't hold...")
- Reveals character thoughts
- Adds dramatic irony

Confessionals break fourth wall productively, enhancing mockumentary feel.

### Gym Bag: Equipment Rules

Equipment in InSpectres is notoriously unreliable:
- Players describe what they "probably" have in gym bag
- Roll to see if equipment actually works
- Malfunctions create comedy

This parodies Ghostbusters' often-failing proton packs while creating mechanical humor.

### Job Outcomes

After each job:
- **Success**: Earn Franchise Dice, possibly Credit, get paid
- **Partial Success**: Get paid but no other benefits
- **Failure**: Lose Bank, possibly Credit, might not get paid

Outcomes affect franchise's long-term viability.

### Campaign Arc: Building the Business

Over multiple jobs:
- Franchise Dice accumulation enables bigger cases
- Credit determines reputation and client quality
- Bank enables upgrades and expansion
- Eventually franchise succeeds spectacularly or fails comedically

This creates natural campaign structure through business simulation.

## Setting and Themes

### Contemporary Urban America

Setting is present-day ordinary cities with supernatural undertones:
- Coffee shops and office buildings
- Skeptical public mostly unaware of real supernatural
- InSpectres franchises operate semi-legitimately
- Reality TV culture documents everything

### The Paranormal: Real But Mundane

Ghosts, monsters, and supernatural events exist but are:
- Often mundane problems (ghost wants apology, demon needs directions home)
- Subject to bureaucracy and procedures
- Treated professionally rather than with awe
- Sometimes silly rather than genuinely frightening

This demystification creates comedy through incongruity.

### The Franchise: Small Business Struggles

InSpectres franchises face real business problems:
- Paying rent on headquarters
- Dealing with difficult clients
- Managing employee drama
- Maintaining equipment on budget
- Building reputation
- Competing with rival franchises

The supernatural is secondary to business operations.

### Core Themes

**Comedy from Mundanity**: Treating supernatural as mundane bureaucratic problem

**Documentary Realism**: Mockumentary format makes absurdity feel grounded

**Workplace Comedy**: Interpersonal dynamics and office politics in supernatural context

**Professionalism vs. Reality**: Maintaining professional facade while chaos erupts

**Small Business Struggles**: Entrepreneurial challenges with paranormal twist

**Found Family**: Team bonding through shared supernatural experiences

### Tone: Comedy-Horror Balance

The game leans comedy but includes genuine scares:
- Supernatural threats are real and sometimes dangerous
- Stress mechanics create tension
- But overall tone stays light through documentary framing

## Cultural Impact

**Mockumentary Gaming**: Pioneered documentary-style framing in RPGs

**Confessional Mechanics**: Introduced character-to-camera devices in tabletop gaming

**Narrative Control Through Success**: Influenced how designers distributed authority based on dice results

**Comedy Horror**: Validated comedy as legitimate genre for mechanically interesting RPGs

**Franchise Simulation**: Business management in RPG context

**Accessible Indie Design**: Demonstrated indie games could be quick, fun, and easy to learn

**Reality TV Influence**: Showed contemporary TV formats could inspire RPG structures

**Success**: Multiple printings and sustained availability proved commercial viability

## Reception and Criticism

**Praise**:
- Hilariously fun gameplay
- Accessible rules, quick start
- Confessionals enhance rather than disrupt play
- Perfect Ghostbusters homage without licensing
- Business simulation adds strategic layer
- Reliably generates comedy through mechanics
- Mockumentary format works brilliantly

**Criticisms**:
- **Limited Depth**: Simplicity great for one-shots, less for campaigns
- **Comedy Requirement**: Requires players willing to be funny
- **Narrow Concept**: Ghostbusters-inspired premise has limited variations
- **Mechanical Lightness**: Some want more tactical/strategic complexity
- **Tonal Consistency**: Maintaining comedy-horror balance difficult for some groups

## Legacy and Influence

**Confessional Device**: Appeared in various RPGs inspired by InSpectres

**Success-Driven Narration**: Influenced resolution systems distributing authority

**Comedy Gaming**: Validated comedy RPGs as mechanically worthwhile

**Franchise Mechanics**: Business simulation in RPGs became more common

**Mockumentary Format**: Inspired documentary-style gaming

**Indie Accessibility**: Showed indie games could be mainstream-friendly

**Sustained Publication**: Continued availability demonstrates enduring appeal
