# HeroQuest

**Publisher**: Issaries Inc (2000-2003), Moon Design Publications (2009-present)
**Designers**: [[Robin D. Laws]], [[Greg Stafford]]
**Year**: 2000 (as HeroWars), 2003 (as HeroQuest)
**System**: Custom (Contest Resolution)
**Genre**: Mythic Fantasy, Bronze Age
**Status**: in-print

## Historical Context

HeroQuest (originally published as HeroWars in 2000, renamed HeroQuest in 2003) represents one of the most radical innovations in fantasy RPG design, pioneering contest resolution, keyword-based character definition, and relationship mechanics that influenced narrative gaming profoundly. Designer Robin D. Laws, working with Glorantha creator Greg Stafford, created system that prioritized narrative outcomes over tactical positioning, character concepts over numerical optimization, and thematic coherence over simulation. The game asked fundamental question: what if RPG mechanics resolved conflicts holistically rather than action-by-action?

Glorantha, created by Greg Stafford in 1966 and first published via RuneQuest (1978), stood as one of fantasy gaming's most sophisticated settings—Bronze Age mythic world where gods were real, mythology shaped reality, and cultural identity determined magical ability. RuneQuest's percentile-skill system served Glorantha well but was complex, tactically focused, and often at odds with mythic storytelling the setting demanded. By the late 1990s, Stafford sought new system better matching Glorantha's mythic nature.

Robin D. Laws brought perfect sensibility. His work on Over the Edge, Feng Shui, and other games demonstrated understanding of how mechanical design creates genre feel. He recognized that Glorantha's mythic Bronze Age required different approach than D&D-style tactical combat or RuneQuest's percentile simulation. Myths don't proceed blow-by-blow; they resolve through contests where heroes demonstrate worthiness, cleverness, or divine favor. Laws set out to create system reflecting this narrative structure.

The breakthrough was contest resolution: rather than resolving individual actions ("I swing my sword," "I cast spell"), resolve entire conflicts ("I want to defeat the enemy," "We need to escape the burning temple"). Players declare what they're trying to achieve and how they're attempting it, opposition is established, single extended contest (or series of simple contests) determines outcome. This shift from task to conflict transformed play fundamentally.

Keywords provided equally radical innovation. Instead of listing dozens of skills, characters had keywords—broad descriptors like "Vingan Warrior" or "Praxian Nomad" or "Gloranthan Farmer"—encompassing all competencies reasonable for that concept. Want to fight? Your "Vingan Warrior" keyword covers it. Track animals? "Praxian Nomad" includes that. This abstraction trusted players and GMs to determine what made sense rather than requiring exhaustive skill lists.

The relationship mechanics proved equally innovative. Characters defined relationships with communities, gods, spirits, and individuals. These relationships weren't just background but mechanical resources providing augments (bonuses) in relevant contests. Your relationship with "Orlanth the Thunderer" might augment contests where his strength applies. Your relationship with "Storm Bull clan" helps in situations where clan loyalty matters. This mechanized social connections and divine relationships central to Glorantha.

Issaries Inc., Stafford's company, published HeroWars in 2000 as massive 360-page rulebook. The initial reception was mixed. Glorantha fans appreciated the mythic feel but struggled with radically different resolution system. The keyword abstraction confused players accustomed to detailed skill lists. The contest mechanics required different mindset than tactical gaming.

The 2003 revision as HeroQuest streamlined presentation and clarified procedures while maintaining core innovations. This helped accessibility significantly. The renamed game (avoiding confusion with board game of same name) gained traction among narrative-focused players.

Second edition (HeroQuest 2, 2009) refined the contest mechanics further, creating the "resistance" framework where opposition was rated on same scale as abilities. This elegant unification simplified GM preparation and play. The system was later genericized as QuestWorlds, demonstrating its versatility beyond Glorantha.

HeroQuest's influence proved profound despite modest commercial success. The contest resolution approach influenced 13th Age's conflict resolution, various narrative games' approach to resolving situations rather than actions, and broader conversation about when to roll dice and what outcomes mean. The keyword system influenced games using broad descriptors rather than skill lists. The relationship mechanics appeared in numerous subsequent games mechanizing social connections.

For Glorantha specifically, HeroQuest proved transformative. The system finally matched the setting's mythic nature. Heroquesting—journeying into mythic time to relive and potentially alter myths—worked elegantly in system designed for narrative flexibility. Cultural magic and divine relationships became central rather than peripheral.

The game demonstrated that fantasy RPGs didn't need to follow D&D paradigms. Combat needn't be tactical miniatures. Character definition needn't be exhaustive lists. Resolution needn't be action-by-action. These lessons influenced indie and mainstream design, showing alternative approaches were viable and could create distinctly different play experiences.

## Mechanical Innovations

HeroQuest introduced revolutionary mechanics that influenced narrative gaming significantly:

**Contest Resolution**: The game's signature innovation. Rather than resolving individual actions, contests determine whole conflict outcomes:
- **Simple Contests**: Single roll determines entire conflict. "I want to persuade the chieftain" vs. "He doesn't trust outsiders." Roll, compare, determine degree of success/failure. Done.
- **Extended Contests**: Multi-round contests for important conflicts where back-and-forth matters. Each round, both sides roll; results create advantage/disadvantage leading to eventual resolution.
- **Chained Contests**: Series of simple contests creating narrative sequence.

This resolves what matters—whether you achieve goals—rather than how specifically you accomplish them.

**The Resistance Principle**: Opposition is rated on same scale as abilities (ratings like 15, 18, 5W, 10W2, etc.). Everything uses unified framework—enemies, obstacles, social resistance, environmental hazards. This elegant unification means GMs rate opposition using same system as player abilities.

**Keywords**: Characters have keywords—broad descriptors encompassing related competencies:
- **Occupational Keywords**: "Vingan Warrior," "Lhankor Mhy Sage," "Praxian Nomad"—covering all skills reasonable for that occupation
- **Cultural Keywords**: "Heortling," "Lunar," "Praxian"—providing cultural knowledge and competencies
- **Magic Keywords**: Religious or mystical affiliations providing magical abilities

Keywords replace skill lists entirely, trusting players and GMs to determine what makes sense.

**Augments**: Other abilities can augment (provide bonuses to) primary ability in contests. Your "Heortling Culture" keyword might augment your "Vingan Warrior" when fighting in Heortling lands. Your relationship with "Orlanth" might augment contests where his strength applies. This creates rich tactical choices about which abilities apply and how.

**Relationship Mechanics**: Characters define relationships with communities, deities, spirits, allies, and enemies. These relationships are rated abilities that can augment contests. A "Close to Storm Bull Clan 17" relationship provides +3 augment in relevant situations. This mechanizes social connections and divine relationships.

**Degrees of Success**: Contest outcomes create degrees of success/failure:
- **Complete Victory/Defeat**: Total success/failure
- **Major Victory/Defeat**: Significant success/failure
- **Minor Victory/Defeat**: Marginal success/failure
- **Marginal Victory/Defeat**: Barely achieved goal/failed

These gradations create nuanced outcomes rather than binary success/failure.

**Hero Points**: Narrative currency characters spend to:
- Bump results (improve degree of success)
- Augment abilities temporarily
- Activate special abilities
- Survive otherwise fatal situations

This gives players narrative control while creating resource management.

**Flaws**: Characters have flaws—personality traits, physical limitations, social disadvantages. Flaws can be used against characters (GM uses them to create complications) but earn Hero Points when they complicate situations. This mechanizes three-dimensional characters while rewarding accepting complications.

**The Mastery System**: Abilities rated beyond 20 add "masteries" (marked as W, W2, W3, etc.). Each mastery adds automatic success and shifts the rating. So "15W" is mastery-level ability. This handles improvement without number inflation while maintaining meaningful distinctions.

**No Initiative**: Contests don't use initiative. Narrative flow determines who acts when. This eliminates bookkeeping while maintaining dramatic pacing.

**Setting-Integrated Magic**: Three magic systems (Theist, Animist, Wizardry) reflect Gloranthan cultural approaches. Magic is cultural practice integrated with keywords rather than separate subsystem.

**Community Resources**: Communities have ratings characters can draw upon—wealth, military strength, magical power. This mechanizes community support and makes group affiliations mechanically relevant.

**Narrator Emulation**: The rules explicitly position GM as narrator shaping story rather than referee adjudicating rules. This philosophical approach permeates the design.

## Cultural Impact

HeroQuest's cultural impact demonstrated how radical mechanical innovation could create distinctive gaming experiences:

**Contest Resolution Pioneer**: HeroQuest proved that resolving entire conflicts rather than individual actions could work beautifully. This influenced:
- **13th Age**: Conflict-focused resolution
- **Fate**: Contests and conflicts
- **Various narrative games**: Situation resolution over task resolution
- **Design conversations**: When to roll, what outcomes mean

The demonstration that this approach worked influenced how designers thought about resolution fundamentally.

**Keyword Innovation**: The keyword system showed character definition could be abstract and flexible rather than exhaustive. This influenced:
- Games using broad descriptors over skill lists
- Aspect-based character definition
- Trusting players to determine competencies
- Narrative-forward character creation

**Relationship Mechanics**: Mechanizing relationships as usable abilities appeared in numerous subsequent games. HeroQuest demonstrated that social connections, divine relationships, and community ties could be mechanical resources rather than just background.

**Glorantha Renaissance**: HeroQuest revitalized Glorantha, creating system finally matching the setting's mythic nature. The game enabled:
- Successful heroquesting mechanics
- Cultural magic integration
- Mythic storytelling support
- Narrative flexibility for Gloranthan campaigns

**Simple vs. Complex Dialectic**: HeroQuest embodied interesting tension—mechanically simple (everything uses same resolution) yet conceptually complex (requires different mindset than tactical gaming). This demonstrated that accessibility and sophistication could coexist.

**Critical Recognition**:
- Diana Jones Award nomination
- Widespread praise for innovation
- Recognition as radical design
- Studied extensively in design contexts
- Featured in discussions of narrative mechanics

**Three Editions Demonstrate Evolution**:
- **HeroWars** (2000): Revolutionary but challenging original
- **HeroQuest** (2003): Renamed, refined presentation
- **HeroQuest 2** (2009): Streamlined resistance framework
- **QuestWorlds**: Genericized system-neutral version

**Robin D. Laws' Reputation**: Solidified Laws as major innovative designer. His genre-focused design philosophy visible across his work found sophisticated expression in HeroQuest.

**Narrative Gaming Influence**: The game influenced narrative gaming broadly by demonstrating that:
- Mechanics could resolve situations rather than actions
- Character definition could be abstract yet precise
- Relationships and connections could be mechanical
- Simple resolution could generate complex play
- Genre feel came from mechanical choices

**Community Development**:
- Devoted Glorantha community embraced system
- Active online discussions and support
- Extensive fan materials and scenarios
- Convention play demonstrated system
- Community helped refine through feedback

**Academic Interest**: Game studies scholars examined HeroQuest for:
- Contest resolution mechanics
- Keyword abstraction
- Relationship mechanics
- How mechanics create genre feel
- Narrative authority distribution

**QuestWorlds Legacy**: The genericized QuestWorlds system demonstrated HeroQuest's mechanics worked beyond Glorantha, influencing various settings and genres.

**Design Conversations**: HeroQuest sparked important conversations about:
- When should games resolve actions vs. conflicts?
- How much abstraction is appropriate?
- What role should relationships play mechanically?
- How do mechanics create genre experiences?

These discussions influenced design thinking broadly.

**Criticisms Drive Innovation**: The initial reception's criticisms (too abstract, too different, requires different mindset) led to refinements that influenced subsequent narrative game design—how to present radical innovations accessibly.

## Design Philosophy

HeroQuest embodied several core design principles:

**Resolve What Matters**: The fundamental philosophy was resolving conflicts and situations rather than individual actions. This kept focus on outcomes—whether you achieve goals—rather than blow-by-blow action accounting.

**Trust Players and GMs**: The keyword system and contest adjudication required trusting players to determine what made sense and GMs to judge fairly. This trust enabled flexibility impossible in more restrictive systems.

**Genre Over Simulation**: Every design choice served mythic storytelling over tactical simulation. Gloranthan myths don't proceed action-by-action; heroes demonstrate worth through contests. The mechanics matched this narrative structure.

**Unified Resolution**: Everything uses same basic framework—combat, social interaction, magic, environmental challenges. This unification simplified play while maintaining mechanical consistency.

**Relationships Are Resources**: Social connections, divine relationships, and community ties aren't just background but mechanical resources. This reflected Gloranthan reality where who you know and what you worship determines capabilities.

**Degrees Matter**: Nuanced success/failure (complete/major/minor/marginal victories and defeats) created richer outcomes than binary pass/fail. This supported storytelling by providing gradations of achievement.

**Narrative Authority**: The system distributed narrative authority between players (declaring intentions, describing approaches) and GM (determining difficulty, describing outcomes). This collaborative storytelling differed from traditional GM-centric authority.

**Keywords Enable Concepts**: Broad keywords let players create character concepts without forcing translation into predetermined categories. "Vingan Warrior" communicates concept immediately without listing individual skills.

**Accessibility Through Simplicity**: Core resolution was simple (compare abilities, determine degree), making system accessible despite conceptual sophistication. Complexity came through choices and options, not baroque core mechanics.

**Magic as Culture**: Integrating magic with cultural keywords made it central to identity rather than separate subsystem. This reflected Gloranthan reality where religion, culture, and magic were inseparable.

**Community Matters**: Mechanizing community resources and relationships emphasized that characters existed within social contexts. Lone wolves were possible but mechanically disadvantaged.

## Setting and Themes

HeroQuest's Glorantha setting shaped and was shaped by the mechanics:

**Glorantha**: Greg Stafford's Bronze Age mythic fantasy world where:
- Myths are literally true
- Gods walk among mortals
- Magic comes from religious/cultural practice
- Runes define fundamental reality
- Multiple cultures with distinct worldviews coexist
- History is cyclic, not linear

**Dragon Pass**: The primary setting region where:
- Heortling (Orlanthi) tribes practice storm god worship
- Lunar Empire expands, bringing imperial order
- Praxian nomads ride their beasts
- Trolls, elves, dwarves, and other non-human races coexist
- Dragonewt mysteries puzzle everyone

**The Runes**: Fundamental forces (Air, Earth, Fire, Water, Darkness, Light, Movement, Stasis, Truth, Illusion, etc.) that define reality. Characters have runic affiliations determining magical approach and personality.

**Heroquesting**: Journeying into mythic time to relive, experience, or alter myths. HeroQuest's mechanics finally made heroquesting playable—contest resolution handled mythic conflicts elegantly, keywords let characters take mythic roles, outcomes could reshape reality.

**Cultural Diversity**: Multiple cultures with distinct:
- **Heortlings**: Storm god worshippers, tribal, egalitarian
- **Lunars**: Moon goddess followers, imperial, syncretist
- **Praxians**: Beast-riding nomads, survival-focused
- **Trolls**: Darkness dwellers with alien psychology
- **Elves**: Plant-beings fundamentally different from humans

Each culture has distinct magical approach and worldview.

**Three Magic Systems**:
- **Theism** (Heortlings, etc.): Worship gods, gain divine magic
- **Animism** (Praxians, Hsunchen): Bargain with spirits
- **Wizardry** (Westerners): Study and manipulate runes intellectually

These reflect cultural approaches to the sacred.

**Mythic Time**: Past, present, and mythic time coexist. Heroes can enter myths, affecting both mythic and mundane reality. This non-linear temporality distinguishes Glorantha from standard fantasy.

**Themes**:
- **Identity Through Community**: Who you are comes from community, culture, religion
- **Myth Made Real**: Stories have power; myths shape reality
- **Cyclical History**: Events recur; heroes relive mythic patterns
- **Cultural Conflict**: Different worldviews clash, each valid within own framework
- **Divine Relationships**: Gods are real, relationships with them matter
- **Heroism's Cost**: Great deeds require sacrifice
- **Community vs. Individual**: Tension between personal goals and communal obligations

**Bronze Age Feel**: Unlike medieval European fantasy, Glorantha is Bronze Age:
- Tribal rather than feudal
- Clan loyalty over individual achievement
- Oral culture, myths over texts
- Ritual and tradition central
- Different gender roles and social structures

The setting's uniqueness demanded unique system, which HeroQuest provided.

## Reception and Legacy

HeroQuest achieved critical recognition while finding devoted niche audience:

**Critical Acclaim**:
- Diana Jones Award nomination
- Extensive praise for innovation
- Recognition as radical design
- Featured in design discussions constantly
- Studied as example of narrative mechanics
- Academic attention for contest resolution

**Commercial Modest Success**: Never mainstream hit, but sustained publication through multiple editions and extensive supplements. The devoted Glorantha community kept it commercially viable.

**Three Major Versions**:
- **HeroWars** (2000): Revolutionary original
- **HeroQuest** (2003): Refined presentation
- **HeroQuest 2** (2009): Streamlined resistance framework
- **HeroQuest Glorantha** (2015): Updated for modern Glorantha
- **QuestWorlds**: Generic system version

**Extensive Supplements**: Numerous books explored:
- Different Gloranthan regions and cultures
- Detailed heroquesting rules
- Specific cults and religions
- Campaign frameworks
- Rules expansions and clarifications

**Design Influence**: HeroQuest influenced:
- **13th Age**: Conflict resolution, relationship mechanics (icon relationships)
- **Fate**: Contest mechanics, aspect-like keywords
- **Various narrative games**: Situation resolution, relationship mechanics
- **Design thinking**: When to roll, what outcomes mean, abstraction levels

**Robin D. Laws' Legacy**: Established Laws as innovative designer willing to challenge fundamental assumptions. His work demonstrated genre-focused mechanics could be radically different from D&D paradigms.

**Glorantha Revitalization**: HeroQuest enabled Gloranthan renaissance. The system:
- Made heroquesting playable
- Supported mythic storytelling
- Integrated cultural magic elegantly
- Attracted new Glorantha fans
- Sustained setting across decades

**QuestWorlds Impact**: The genericized system demonstrated HeroQuest's mechanics worked universally, not just for Glorantha. This validated the design's fundamental soundness.

**Community Devotion**: Passionate Glorantha fans sustained the game through:
- Active online communities
- Extensive fan materials
- Convention games
- Scenario sharing
- Rules discussions and refinements

**Criticisms and Challenges**:
- **Steep Learning Curve**: Conceptual shift from tactical gaming confused many
- **Abstraction Issues**: Keyword flexibility created interpretation challenges
- **GM Burden**: Contest adjudication required skilled, fair GMs
- **Limited Appeal**: Mythic Bronze Age setting and abstract mechanics had niche appeal
- **Initial Presentation**: HeroWars' 360-page tome was overwhelming

**Refinement Over Time**: Each edition addressed criticisms:
- Clearer presentation
- Better examples
- Streamlined procedures
- Improved accessibility
- Maintained innovation

**Academic Recognition**: Game studies examined HeroQuest for:
- Contest resolution mechanics
- Narrative authority distribution
- How abstraction affects play
- Cultural representation in gaming
- Relationship mechanics

**Modern Relevance**: As narrative gaming gained popularity, HeroQuest's innovations became more appreciated. Mechanics that seemed radical in 2000 feel prescient in retrospect.

**13th Age Connection**: Rob Heinsoo and Jonathan Tweet's 13th Age incorporated HeroQuest-influenced mechanics (icon relationships resembling HeroQuest relationship mechanics), demonstrating mainstream adoption of its innovations.

**Design Lessons**: HeroQuest taught:
- Contest resolution can work beautifully
- Keywords enable flexibility and character concepts
- Relationships can be mechanical resources
- Simple cores can generate complex play
- Genre feel comes from mechanical choices
- Abstraction requires trust and communication

**Final Assessment**: HeroQuest stands as one of most radical and influential innovations in fantasy RPG design. Its contest resolution approach challenged fundamental assumptions about what RPG mechanics should do. Its keyword system demonstrated character definition could be abstract and flexible. Its relationship mechanics showed social connections could be mechanically meaningful.

While never achieving mainstream success, HeroQuest profoundly influenced narrative gaming. Its innovations appeared in numerous subsequent games, from 13th Age's icon relationships to Fate's contests to various games resolving situations rather than actions. Its demonstration that fantasy gaming could work radically differently from D&D paradigms encouraged designers to experiment fearlessly.

For Glorantha specifically, HeroQuest was transformative—finally providing system matching the setting's mythic nature. The game enabled heroquesting, cultural magic, and mythic storytelling in ways RuneQuest's percentile simulation couldn't.

More broadly, HeroQuest proved that sophisticated narrative mechanics and mechanical simplicity could coexist, that abstraction could enhance rather than diminish play, and that challenging fundamental assumptions could create genuinely new gaming experiences. For these contributions, it deserves recognition as genuinely revolutionary design that changed what we believed fantasy RPGs could be.
