# Mobile Frame Zero: Firebrands

**Publisher**: Independent (Vincent Baker)
**Designers**: [[D. Vincent Baker]], [[Meguey Baker]]
**Year**: 2017
**System**: Story Game (Minigame Collection)
**Genre**: Mecha, Romance, Sci-Fi
**Status**: out-of-print

## Overview

Mobile Frame Zero: Firebrands revolutionized story game structure by creating modular collection of "minigames"—structured scenes with specific mechanical procedures—that players invoke as needed to resolve mecha combat, navigate tense confrontations, and explore romantic connections. Designed by D. Vincent Baker and Meguey Baker and released in 2017, Firebrands belongs to Mobile Frame Zero universe (tactical mecha wargame) but functions as completely different game type—GM-less narrative system where players portray resistance fighters piloting mechs while managing interpersonal relationships, political tensions, and romantic entanglements against backdrop of guerrilla warfare.

The game's radical innovation comes from its minigame structure. Instead of universal resolution system applied to all situations, Firebrands provides approximately ten different minigames, each with specific procedures for specific scene types. "Do You Know Who I Am?" structures confrontations where authority and reputation matter. "How'd You Get In Here?" handles infiltration and social engineering. "Setting Someone Straight" mechanizes arguments and persuasion. "Battle" provides tactical-narrative combat system. "The Cliffhanger" creates action-movie escape sequences. Most innovatively, several minigames handle romance and connection: "Catching Feelings," "Pillow Talk," and "Gazing Into the Abyss" create structured space for exploring attraction, intimacy, and vulnerability.

This minigame approach creates remarkable flexibility. Players identify what kind of scene they want, select appropriate minigame, execute its procedures, then return to freeform play until next structured scene. The minigames provide just enough mechanical engagement to create stakes and uncertainty while avoiding complex universal systems requiring constant reference. Each minigame typically runs 5-15 minutes, creating focused intense scenes before returning to collaborative narration.

The game's synthesis of mecha warfare and romance proved unexpectedly powerful. Giant robot combat and tender relationship moments seem incongruous until you recognize they're both high-stakes emotionally intense experiences. Firebrands treats them with equal mechanical weight—romance gets as much procedural attention as battles. This demonstrates story games can honor multiple play modes simultaneously, validating that relationship drama matters as much as tactical combat in creating compelling narratives.

Firebrands influenced subsequent mecha games (Beam Saber, Armour Astir) and romance-focused designs (Thirsty Sword Lesbians). Its minigame structure inspired designers to consider modular scene-specific systems rather than comprehensive universal mechanics. The game demonstrated that explicit romance mechanics could enhance rather than constrain intimate scenes, and that games could successfully synthesize seemingly disparate genres into coherent wholes.

## Historical Context

Mobile Frame Zero: Firebrands emerged from Vincent Baker's decade-plus experience designing innovative RPGs and from collaboration with his wife Meguey Baker, accomplished designer in her own right. The Bakers wanted to create game in Mobile Frame Zero universe (tactical mecha wargame designed by Vincent and others) but approaching mecha storytelling completely differently from tactical combat focus.

The mid-to-late 2010s story game scene had established various design patterns—PbtA moves, GM-less frameworks, card-based systems. The Bakers synthesized these while adding distinctive innovation: minigames as modular scene-resolution tools. This concept drew from board game design (games within games) and freeform LARP techniques (structured scenes with specific procedures).

The mecha-and-romance synthesis came from recognizing both operate on high emotional intensity. Giant robot shows (Gundam, Macross, Evangelion) prominently feature interpersonal drama and romantic subplots alongside mech combat. Firebrands honors this genre truth mechanically—romance gets structured procedures like combat does, validating its narrative importance.

Development involved creating, testing, and refining each minigame until procedures reliably generated intended scene types. Early versions had more minigames; final selection represents most successful and versatile. The challenge was ensuring minigames felt cohesive despite mechanical diversity—different procedures but unified aesthetic and purpose.

The Bakers self-published Firebrands in 2017, continuing their independent publishing approach. The game's unusual structure—part anthology, part toolkit, part complete game—made it challenging to pitch concisely, but players who engaged discovered its unique value.

Initial reception came from story gamers familiar with Baker designs. The minigame structure intrigued some while confusing others—was this complete game or just scene toolkit? Players who embraced it discovered profound flexibility and emotional range. The game worked equally well for mecha combat heavy play, romance focused sessions, or balanced mixture.

Critical recognition followed, with designers examining the minigame structure as innovative approach. The game demonstrated that modular scene-specific mechanics could work, that romance deserved mechanical weight equal to combat, and that genre synthesis (mecha + romance) could enhance both elements.

## Core Structure and Mechanics

Firebrands creates modular narrative through minigame collection providing structured procedures for specific scene types.

**Setup**: Players create resistance cell fighting against oppressive regime. Each player creates character including:
- **Name and Pronouns**
- **Role**: Pilot, commander, support personnel, etc.
- **Mech**: Personal mobile frame with name and description
- **Relationships**: Connections to other characters

No stats or systematic character creation—pure narrative definition.

**Play Structure**: Freeform collaborative narration until someone wants structured scene, then:
1. Identify what kind of scene (confrontation, infiltration, romance, etc.)
2. Select appropriate minigame
3. Follow minigame procedures
4. Return to freeform play after resolution

**The Minigames**:

**Combat and Action Minigames**:

**"Battle"**: Tactical mecha combat using abstract positioning, dice, and narrative description. Players describe attacks and defenses, rolling dice to determine outcomes. Defeated mechs are damaged but pilots typically survive to fight again.

**"The Cliffhanger"**: Action-movie escapes. One player describes dangerous situation, others describe how they escape. Escalating stakes create mounting tension before final dramatic escape or capture.

**"Get Away From Her"**: Protecting someone from danger. Structured escalation where protector must choose how far they'll go to save someone.

**Social and Political Minigames**:

**"Do You Know Who I Am?"**: Confrontations where reputation and authority matter. Players invoke their status, threaten consequences, or humble themselves to achieve goals. Power dynamics shift through procedural exchanges.

**"How'd You Get In Here?"**: Social infiltration and lying. Players justify presence in restricted spaces through creative fast-talking and social engineering.

**"Setting Someone Straight"**: Arguments and persuasion. Structured debate where both sides present cases and someone shifts position or conflict escalates.

**Romance and Connection Minigames**:

**"Catching Feelings"**: Discovering attraction. Players play out scene where characters realize mutual interest through exchanges alternating vulnerability and deflection until feelings are acknowledged or denied.

**"Pillow Talk"**: Post-intimate conversations. Players portray tender or awkward moments after physical intimacy, sharing vulnerabilities, hopes, or fears.

**"Gazing Into the Abyss"**: Witnessing someone at their worst and choosing how to respond. Creates space for showing support or judgment during vulnerable moments.

**"Do You Love Me?"**: Direct relationship confrontation. One character demands answer to title question. Structured exchange forces honest response.

**Minigame Procedures**: Each minigame has specific structure, often including:
- **Roles**: Who plays which part (initiator, responder, etc.)
- **Escalation**: Procedures for raising stakes
- **Resolution**: How scene concludes
- **Outcome**: What changes as result

**Example Minigame - "Catching Feelings"**:
1. Two players agree to portray developing attraction
2. Players alternate describing moments revealing interest
3. Each moment can be met with reciprocation or deflection
4. Escalation continues until both acknowledge feelings or one definitively rebuffs
5. Scene ends with relationship status changed

**Between Minigames**: Players engage in freeform collaborative narration, setting up situations, portraying daily life, establishing world details. Minigames get invoked when structured resolution needed.

**No GM**: All players share authority. Anyone can frame scenes or invoke minigames. Collaborative truth-creation through consensus.

**Mech Combat**: Unlike traditional tactical games, Firebrands makes mech combat fast and narratively driven. Battles take 15-30 minutes and emphasize dramatic descriptions over detailed positioning.

**Romance Mechanics**: The game treats romantic scenes with same procedural seriousness as combat, demonstrating relationships matter mechanically. Romance isn't awkward freeform—it has structures providing safety and direction.

**Session Structure**: Typical session includes:
- Several freeform scenes establishing situation
- 2-4 minigames resolving key moments
- Mix of action and relationship scenes
- Resistance operations and personal connections
- Ongoing development of characters and relationships

The modular structure allows players to emphasize preferred content—more combat, more romance, or balanced mixture.

## Narrative Philosophy and Design Goals

Firebrands' narrative philosophy centers on modular scene tools, romance as mechanically valid, and genre synthesis enhancing both elements.

**Minigames as Scene Frameworks**: Instead of universal system forcing all scenes into same mechanical structure, different scenes get different procedures. Combat plays differently from romance, which plays differently from infiltration. This acknowledges that different dramatic moments benefit from different mechanical approaches.

**Romance Deserves Mechanics**: The game makes bold statement: romantic scenes deserve structured procedures like combat does. This validates relationship drama as mechanically important rather than relegating it to pure freeform roleplay. Romance minigames provide:
- Safety through structure
- Direction without scripting
- Stakes and uncertainty
- Permission to explore intimacy

**Genre Synthesis**: Mecha and romance seem incongruous until you recognize both operate on high emotion. Firebrands honors this, giving both equal mechanical weight. Giant robot battles and tender confessions both matter. This synthesis creates richer experience than either alone.

**Player Authority**: GM-less structure distributes narrative control. Anyone can frame scenes, invoke minigames, or introduce complications. This democratic authority prevents single-player domination while ensuring everyone contributes.

**Structured Improvisation**: Minigames structure improvisation without scripting it. Procedures provide framework—what happens in this type of scene—while players provide content through creative responses. This balance makes improvisation accessible.

**Emotional Intensity**: The game embraces high emotion—desperate battles, passionate romance, intense confrontations. Minigames channeling this intensity create cathartic experiences.

**Resistance Context**: The setting—guerrilla fighters against oppression—creates meaningful stakes for both combat and romance. Battles matter because you're fighting for freedom. Romance matters because time is limited and death is possible. The context elevates both.

**Modularity as Strength**: The minigame collection lets groups customize experience. Want combat-heavy play? Use battle and action minigames. Want relationship drama? Emphasize romance minigames. Want balance? Mix freely. This flexibility serves diverse play preferences.

These philosophical commitments create game validating both tactical excitement and emotional intimacy through thoughtful procedural design.

## Cultural Impact and Design Influence

Firebrands' cultural impact manifested through inspiring minigame structures, validating romance mechanics, and influencing mecha game design.

**Minigame Structure Innovation**: Firebrands demonstrated modular scene-specific systems work:

**Design Influence**:
- Games adopting minigame approaches for specific scenes
- Recognition that universal systems aren't always optimal
- Scene-specific procedures as design tool
- Modular toolkit games vs. comprehensive systems

**Romance Mechanics Validation**: The game normalized romance having structured procedures:

**Subsequent Romance Games**:
- **Thirsty Sword Lesbians** (Evil Hat, 2021): Action and romance with equal weight
- Various romance-focused story games
- Increased comfort with explicit romance mechanics
- Recognition that structure can enhance intimate scenes

**Mecha Game Influence**: Inspired narrative-focused mecha games:

**Direct Influences**:
- **Beam Saber** (Austin Ramsay, 2020): Forged in the Dark mecha game
- **Armour Astir: Advent** (Briar Sovereign, 2021): Mecha with strong relationships
- Various PbtA mecha hacks emphasizing interpersonal drama

**Community Reception**:
- Embraced by players wanting both action and romance
- LGBTQ+ community appreciating explicit queer romance space
- Designers studying minigame structure
- Play reports documenting both mecha battles and relationship development

**Critical Recognition**:
- Examined in design discussions
- Praised for innovation
- Studied for modular approach
- Cited as romance mechanics example

**Long-term Influence**:
- Minigame concept inspiring subsequent designs
- Romance mechanics becoming more common
- Genre synthesis approach validated
- Baker design legacy continuing

Firebrands' greatest legacy may be proving romance and action deserve equal mechanical attention, influencing subsequent games to take relationship mechanics seriously.
