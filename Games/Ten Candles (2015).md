# Ten Candles

**Publisher**: Cavalry Games
**Designer**: Stephen Dewey
**Year**: 2015
**System**: d6 Pool with Burning Candles
**Genre**: Tragic Horror / Apocalyptic
**Status**: in-print

## Historical Context

Ten Candles, designed by Stephen Dewey and released in 2015, represents one of horror gaming's most emotionally powerful and mechanically innovative achievements. The game uses ten burning candles as both timer and dice pool: as candles burn down and extinguish through play, the dice pool shrinks and darkness consumes the table. Players portray survivors in an apocalyptic scenario where "They" have brought darkness to the world. The game's core promise is stark and unchangeable: your characters will die. The only question is how they face that inevitable end.

By 2015, indie horror gaming had established physical props as powerful mechanical tools. Dread's Jenga tower (2005) demonstrated how physical components could create genuine tension. Fiasco (2009) showed tragic narratives could be deeply satisfying. Ten Candles synthesized these lessons while adding profound innovations: the candles burning in real-time created actual countdown, the shrinking light pool created escalating despair, and the guaranteed tragic ending reframed play from "can we survive?" to "how will we be remembered?"

Dewey conceived Ten Candles as meditation on mortality, meaning, and hope in hopeless circumstances. The design emerged from personal experiences with loss and questions about how people face inevitable endings. Rather than power fantasy or heroic victory, the game explored dignity, sacrifice, connection, and meaning when facing certain death. This philosophical depth distinguished Ten Candles from typical horror gaming focused on survival or triumph.

The game's structure enforced tragic arc through mechanical inevitability. Play begins with ten lit candles representing hope, light, and capability. As scenes progress, candles are blown out—removing dice from the pool and plunging the play space into deeper darkness. By game's end, all candles are extinguished, all characters are dead, and darkness is total. This mechanical certainty meant players couldn't avoid tragedy through clever play or lucky rolls. They could only choose how characters faced the end.

Ten Candles launched through Kickstarter in 2015, seeking modest funding. The campaign's success exceeded expectations, raising over $100,000—demonstrating strong appetite for innovative horror gaming. The Kickstarter presentation emphasized the game's unique mechanics and emotional experience, attracting backers interested in meaningful tragedy rather than typical gaming power fantasy.

The game's release generated significant indie gaming attention. The burning candles created visual spectacle perfect for actual-play videos and convention demos. Watching the table slowly descend into darkness while narratives spiraled toward inevitable doom created compelling viewing. This visual drama amplified awareness beyond typical indie game reach.

Ten Candles won critical acclaim for emotional depth, mechanical innovation, and commitment to tragic narrative. Reviews praised the candle mechanics' elegance, the game's ability to create genuine emotional catharsis, and the beautiful production values. The game appeared on numerous "essential indie games" lists and won several awards including multiple ENnie nominations.

The design influenced subsequent horror games toward physical components, guaranteed tragic endings, and emotional resonance over mechanical challenge. It demonstrated that games promising failure could be deeply satisfying through meaning and dignity rather than victory.

## Mechanical Innovations

Ten Candles' innovations centered on the burning candles creating literal and mechanical countdown to darkness:

**Ten Candle Timer/Dice Pool**: Game begins with ten lit candles. These candles serve dual purpose—atmospheric lighting and dice pool. When characters attempt dangerous actions, they roll d6s equal to remaining lit candles. As candles extinguish through play, dice pool shrinks, making success increasingly difficult. This creates mechanical inevitability: characters grow weaker as hope fades.

**Hope Dice**: Players can add "hope dice" to rolls by narrating how their character draws strength from connections, memories, or purpose. This creates mechanical incentive for emotional character moments and meaningful relationships. Hope becomes tangible resource.

**Brink Dice**: The GM adds "brink dice" representing "Them"—the darkness, monsters, or apocalyptic force. Brink dice oppose hope dice, creating direct mechanical conflict between light and dark.

**Candle Burning/Extinguishing**: Candles burn in real-time during play. Additionally, specific triggers extinguish candles:
- Scene endings
- Failed rolls (players choose which candle to blow out)
- Dramatic moments
- GM triggers based on narrative

Each extinguished candle removes one die from pool and increases darkness. This creates actual time pressure and escalating mechanical difficulty.

**Guaranteed Death**: The game explicitly states your characters will die. This removes survival pressure and shifts focus to how characters face death—with dignity, sacrifice, courage, fear, rage, or acceptance. This reframing makes tragedy the point rather than failure.

**Moments and Truths**: Character creation involves establishing "Moments" (hopes, fears, memories) and "Truths" (facts about the apocalypse and characters). These narrative elements become mechanical resources—players can invoke them for hope dice or narrative authority.

**Traits**: Characters have ten traits (five virtues, five flaws). Traits provide narrative guidance but aren't numerical bonuses. They exist to create character depth quickly.

**Module System**: The game provides modular scenarios ("modules") describing different apocalyptic situations:
- "They" are shadowy entities killing in darkness
- "They" are the dead returning
- "They" are reality-breaking phenomena
- Custom apocalypses

Modules provide evocative prompts while leaving details deliberately vague for player interpretation.

**Four Acts Structure**: Play follows four-act structure:
1. **Establishment**: Characters meet, situation revealed, hope exists
2. **Conflict**: Challenges emerge, candles begin extinguishing
3. **Reflection**: Characters face mortality, relationships deepen
4. **Resolution**: Final confrontation, death, darkness

This structure creates natural tragic arc from hope to doom.

**No GM Prep Beyond Module**: GMs choose module but don't prepare specific plot. The game unfolds through player choices and collaborative narrative within apocalyptic framework. This makes Ten Candles accessible for one-shots without extensive preparation.

**Recording Final Moments**: Game encourages recording final scenes—audio or video. This captures characters' last words and actions, creating haunting artifacts of fictional tragedy. Some groups make this recording their "memorial" to characters.

**Darkness Is Real**: Players literally experience darkness as candles extinguish. This environmental change creates physical immersion—by game's end, players sit in near-darkness, mirroring characters' situation.

**Failure Is Success**: The game reframes failure. Characters dying isn't "losing"—it's fulfilling the game's promise. Successful Ten Candles session ends with everyone dead and darkness consuming everything. This inverts typical RPG goals.

**Last Words**: When character dies, player narrates final moments and speaks character's last words. This ritualistic element provides closure and meaning to death.

**Epilogue Options**: After all characters die, groups can share epilogue—did anything survive? Did characters' actions matter? Were they remembered? This provides narrative closure while maintaining tragic tone.

## Cultural Impact

Ten Candles' cultural impact operated through emotional design validation, physical component innovation, and tragic horror normalization:

**Tragedy As Satisfying Play**: Demonstrated that guaranteed character death and tragic endings could create deeply satisfying play experiences. This challenged gaming's typical power fantasy orientation, validating tragedy, sacrifice, and meaningful death as legitimate design goals.

**Emotional Catharsis Gaming**: Created space for genuine emotional catharsis through play. Players reported crying, experiencing genuine grief for characters, and finding the experience emotionally meaningful. This demonstrated games could facilitate emotional experiences beyond entertainment.

**Physical Components Evolution**: Advanced physical prop use beyond Dread's tower. The burning candles created visual spectacle, environmental immersion, mechanical function, and symbolic weight simultaneously. This multilayered component use influenced subsequent designs.

**Actual-Play Appeal**: The visual drama of candles slowly extinguishing while darkness consumed the table created compelling actual-play content. Videos of Ten Candles sessions spread awareness significantly. The aesthetic was perfect for visual media.

**Convention Signature Game**: Became sought-after convention experience. The darkened room, burning candles, and guaranteed tragic ending created memorable one-shot sessions. Convention organizers featured Ten Candles as showcase game.

**Safety and Boundaries**: The game's intense emotional content helped normalize safety discussions in gaming. Groups playing Ten Candles discussed boundaries, triggers, and comfort levels before play. This contributed to broader safety tool adoption.

**Philosophical Horror**: Demonstrated horror gaming could explore philosophical questions—mortality, meaning, sacrifice, legacy—rather than just scares or survival. This intellectual depth attracted players seeking meaningful experiences.

**Production Value Influence**: The beautiful physical production—quality components, thoughtful presentation, professional aesthetics—influenced indie game presentation standards. Ten Candles looked and felt premium, demonstrating indie games could match or exceed mainstream production values.

**Tragic Horror Sub-Genre**: Helped establish tragic horror as distinct sub-genre separate from survival horror or cosmic horror. Games exploring inevitable doom, meaningful death, and sacrifice found validation.

**Memorial Gaming**: The recording of final moments created new gaming practice—making memorials to fictional characters. This ritualistic element added weight and significance to play.

**Kickstarter Success Story**: Over $100,000 funding demonstrated market appetite for innovative horror design. This success encouraged other designers to pursue ambitious indie horror projects.

**Award Recognition**: Multiple ENnie nominations and indie game awards provided visibility and legitimacy. This recognition helped Ten Candles reach audiences beyond typical indie circles.

**Accessibility Through Simplicity**: Despite emotional intensity, mechanical simplicity made the game accessible. Teaching new players took minutes. This accessibility brought non-gamers to meaningful gaming experiences.

**Darkness Aesthetics**: The literal darkness, candlelight, and visual atmosphere influenced how groups approached horror gaming atmosphere. Physical environment became deliberate design element.

## Design Philosophy

Ten Candles embodies several philosophical principles about tragedy, mortality, and meaning:

**Tragedy Is Not Failure**: The game fundamentally reframes failure. Your characters dying isn't losing—it's fulfilling the narrative promise. This challenges gaming's typical win/lose framework, making tragedy itself the goal and meaning.

**Mortality Gives Meaning**: By guaranteeing death, the game makes every moment precious. Knowing time is limited forces characters to prioritize what matters—connection, legacy, sacrifice, courage. Mortality creates urgency for meaning.

**Hope Despite Inevitability**: Even knowing death is certain, characters can maintain hope—not for survival, but for dignity, sacrifice, protecting others, or making death meaningful. This explores realistic hope versus false hope.

**Physical Immersion**: The burning candles, shrinking light, and encroaching darkness create physical environment mirroring fiction. This environmental design enhances immersion and emotional engagement.

**Simplicity Enables Emotion**: Minimal rules mean players focus on narrative and emotion rather than mechanics. The system gets out of the way, facilitating emotional engagement.

**Collaborative Tragedy**: The game requires group collaboration in creating tragedy. Everyone contributes to the doomed narrative, making tragedy shared creation rather than imposed defeat.

**Dignity in Death**: How characters face death matters more than preventing it. The game explores courage, fear, sacrifice, rage, acceptance, and meaning when facing mortality.

**No False Hope**: The game doesn't pretend survival is possible. This honesty allows genuine exploration of facing certain doom rather than maintaining survival illusion.

**Memory and Legacy**: What characters leave behind—actions, sacrifice, final words—matters even if they die. Legacy and memory provide meaning beyond survival.

**Time Is Literal**: The candles burn in real-time, creating actual countdown. This makes time pressure tangible rather than abstract.

## Setting and Themes

Ten Candles is setting-agnostic apocalyptic horror with deliberate vagueness:

**The Premise**: "They" came ten days ago. "They" brought darkness—unnatural, thick, absolute. Sunlight fails to return. Technology fails. Communities collapse. "They" kill any who venture into darkness. Now, your characters huddle in temporary sanctuary with ten candles, trying to survive one more night. They won't.

**Deliberate Vagueness**: The game intentionally leaves "They" undefined. Modules provide prompts, but groups define specifics:
- Shadow entities hunting in darkness?
- The dead returning?
- Alien invasion?
- Reality itself collapsing?
- Supernatural apocalypse?
- Psychological horror where "They" might not exist?

This vagueness lets groups create horror matching their preferences.

**Module Framework**: Modules provide evocative scenario skeletons:

**Default Module**: "They" are entities killing in darkness. Survivors shelter with dwindling candles.

**Other Modules** (examples):
- **We Are Going Home**: Apocalypse survivors trying to reach home before darkness consumes everything
- **Living Ghosts**: "They" are the dead returning, and living are becoming like "Them"
- **Our Last Night**: Final night in doomed sanctuary before darkness claims all

Each module provides different emotional focus—terror, loss, sacrifice, memory.

**Setting Flexibility**: While defaulting to modern apocalypse, the system supports:
- Historical settings (candlelight always appropriate)
- Science fiction (space station losing power)
- Fantasy (magical darkness consuming world)
- Any scenario where light fades and hope dies

**Common Elements**:
- Sanctuary (temporary safe space)
- Dwindling light (candles representing hope)
- "They" (undefined threat in darkness)
- Survivors (characters facing doom together)
- Ten candles worth of time (one night, one journey, etc.)
- Inevitable darkness

**Themes**:
- **Mortality**: Everyone will die; how you face it matters
- **Hope and Despair**: Maintaining hope despite hopeless circumstances
- **Sacrifice**: Dying for others, protecting the vulnerable
- **Connection**: Relationships matter when time is limited
- **Meaning**: Finding purpose facing certain death
- **Memory**: What we leave behind, how we're remembered
- **Dignity**: Facing fear with courage or accepting fear honestly
- **Light and Dark**: Not just physical but spiritual, emotional
- **Time**: Precious, limited, burning away
- **Legacy**: What survives after we're gone

**Emotional Arc**: Games move from hope (all candles lit, maybe we'll survive) through growing despair (candles extinguish, death approaches) to acceptance or defiance (final candles, facing the end) to darkness and death (all candles out, characters gone).

## Reception and Legacy

Ten Candles achieved significant success and influence within indie gaming:

**Critical Reception**:
- Widespread critical acclaim from gaming media
- Multiple ENnie Award nominations
- Featured in "best indie games" lists consistently
- Praised for emotional depth and mechanical innovation
- Recognized for unique horror approach
- Design community studied mechanics extensively

**Commercial Success**:
- Kickstarter raised over $100,000 (modest goal: $3,500)
- Strong sustained sales
- Multiple print runs
- International distribution
- Active in-print status
- Successful supplements and expansions

**Community Response**:
- Passionate devoted following
- Extensive actual-play content (audio and video)
- Emotional player testimonials
- Convention organized play
- Strong word-of-mouth recommendations
- Active online communities

**Actual-Play Impact**:
- Numerous podcast and video actual-plays
- Visual medium perfect for candle atmosphere
- Compelling viewing due to escalating darkness
- Emotional moments resonated with audiences
- This visibility brought new players

**Design Influence**:
Inspired various games and mechanics:
- Physical component innovation
- Guaranteed tragic endings
- Countdown mechanics using real-time elements
- Emotional safety tools
- Tragic horror sub-genre
- Memorial/legacy mechanics

**Convention Presence**: Became convention staple for dramatic one-shots. The darkened room experience created memorable sessions and strong word-of-mouth.

**Awards and Recognition**:
- 2017 ENnie Award nominations (multiple categories)
- 2016 Golden Geek Award nominations
- Featured at major conventions (Gen Con, PAX, etc.)
- Academic and design community study

**Emotional Impact**: Players reported profound emotional experiences—crying, genuine grief, catharsis. This demonstrated games could create meaningful emotional engagement.

**Safety Tool Adoption**: The game's intensity encouraged safety discussion, contributing to broader safety tool adoption in gaming.

**Criticisms**:
- Guaranteed death felt restrictive to some
- Emotional intensity too much for some players
- Required specific candles (accessibility/availability)
- Fire safety concerns in some venues
- Not suitable for campaign play
- Some found predetermined tragedy limiting

**Accessibility Considerations**:
- Fire safety concerns addressed through LED alternatives
- Emotional intensity required content warnings and safety tools
- Some venues prohibited open flames
- Community developed flame-free alternatives

**Long-term Impact**: Remained relevant and in-print years after release. New players continued discovering it. Sustained sales validated design approach.

**Production Evolution**: Later editions included safety guidance, alternative lighting options, and expanded modules based on community feedback.

**Legacy**: Ten Candles demonstrated that:
- Guaranteed tragic endings could be deeply satisfying
- Physical components could serve multiple simultaneous functions
- Horror could explore philosophical depth and emotional catharsis
- Simplicity enabled rather than restricted meaningful play
- Indie games could achieve production values rivaling mainstream
- Tragedy itself could be legitimate play goal

The game's greatest achievement was proving that knowing characters will die doesn't diminish experience—it reframes it toward meaning, dignity, sacrifice, and legacy. The burning candles became iconic symbol of tragic horror gaming, instantly recognizable even to those who never played.

Ten Candles secured place as one of indie horror gaming's most important achievements, demonstrating horror RPGs could facilitate genuine emotional experiences and philosophical exploration while remaining mechanically elegant and accessible.
