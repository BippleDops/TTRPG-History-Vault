# Misspent Youth

**Publisher**: Self-published
**Designer**: Robert Bohl
**Year**: 2010
**System**: Misspent Youth System
**Genre**: young-adult, dystopia, rebellion
**Status**: in-print

## Overview

Misspent Youth, designed by Robert Bohl in 2010, creates Hunger Games-style YA dystopian rebellion stories where players portray teenage revolutionaries fighting oppressive Authority, using rock-paper-scissors resolution and structured campaign deterioration ensuring rebellion eventually fails—but the question becomes how much damage you inflict on Authority and whether your defiance inspires future resistance before inevitable tragic ending.

**Key Innovation**: The Authority (GM) mechanically grows stronger each episode while YFs (Young Fugitives—the teen protagonists) deteriorate, ensuring predetermined tragic arc while individual scene outcomes remain uncertain.

**YA Aesthetics**: Embraces young adult fiction tropes—teenagers as protagonists, adult authority as oppressive, rebellion as coming-of-age, tragic sacrifice as heroism.

## Core Mechanics

**Rock-Paper-Scissors Resolution**: Contests resolved through RPS rather than dice

**Authority Growth**: GM gains power each episode

**Deterioration**: Teen protagonists gradually lose battles and options

**Episode Structure**: Each session is self-contained episode building toward tragic series finale

**Friendship Convictions**: Relationships between YFs mechanically significant

## Setting and Themes

**Customizable Dystopia**: Group creates specific oppressive Authority (corporate, governmental, religious, technological)

**YA Protagonists**: Playing teenagers, not adults—perspective and agency matter

**Doomed Rebellion**: Predetermined failure creates tragic hero narratives

**Coming of Age Through Resistance**: Growing up means fighting injustice despite futility

## Cultural Impact

Demonstrated YA dystopia as viable RPG genre. Showed predetermined failure could enhance rather than diminish drama. Influenced games with structured campaign deterioration.
