# Carolina Death Crawl

**Publisher**: Bully Pulpit Games
**Designer**: Jason Morningstar
**Year**: 2014
**System**: Carolina Death Crawl System
**Genre**: historical, war, Southern-Gothic
**Status**: in-print

## Overview

Carolina Death Crawl, designed by Jason Morningstar in 2014, creates highly structured historical game about Sherman's March through South Carolina in 1865, with one player controlling Sherman's Army (following historically accurate route destroying everything) while others play Southern civilians witnessing destruction of their world, using asymmetric mechanics where Sherman player has unstoppable force while civilian players experience loss, creating uncomfortable but historically grounded examination of war's brutality and devastation without romanticizing any perspective.

**Historical Basis**: Extensively researched using actual records, documented locations, real events from Sherman's March

**Asymmetric Play**: Sherman player follows historical march route with mechanical advantages; civilian players react to destruction they cannot prevent

**Structured Progression**: March proceeds through specific South Carolina locations in historical order

**Difficult Subject**: Tackles complex historical moment—Union Army destroying South during Civil War—without simplifying or glorifying

## Core Mechanics

**The March Track**: Sherman advances through predetermined locations each turn

**Destruction Mechanics**: Sherman player destroys property, resources, communities systematically

**Civilian Response**: Other players narrate civilians' experiences, losses, survival attempts

**Asymmetric Power**: Sherman has mechanical advantages; civilians have narrative authority over their experiences

**Historical Outcomes**: March reaches sea; South is devastated; question is what happens to civilians

## Setting and Themes

**South Carolina 1865**: Final year of Civil War, Sherman cutting through South

**Total War**: Sherman's scorched earth policy destroying infrastructure

**Civilian Suffering**: Non-combatants experiencing war's horror

**Historical Complexity**: No simple heroes/villains, just people in terrible situation

**Southern Gothic**: Tone blending horror, tragedy, historical brutality

## Cultural Impact

Demonstrated games could tackle difficult historical subjects thoughtfully. Showed asymmetric mechanics could model historical power imbalances. Influenced historical gaming approaching complex moments without oversimplification.

## Reception

Praised for historical rigor and thoughtful handling of difficult subject. Some found it too uncomfortable. Sparked discussions about representing historical trauma in games.
