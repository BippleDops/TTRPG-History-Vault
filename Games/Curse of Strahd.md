# Curse of Strahd

**Curse of Strahd** stands as one of the most celebrated and influential adventure modules released for [[Dungeons & Dragons Fifth Edition (2014)]], representing a masterful reimagining of the classic gothic horror adventure that first introduced the demiplane of Ravenloft to the [[Dungeons & Dragons]] multiverse. Published by [[Wizards of the Coast]] in March 2016, this 256-page hardcover adventure has achieved enduring popularity among players and Dungeon Masters, consistently ranking as one of the best-received fifth edition adventures and cementing gothic horror as a viable tone for modern D&D campaigns.

## Overview

Curse of Strahd transports adventurers to the mist-shrouded land of Barovia, a demiplane trapped within the [[Demiplane of Dread]]. At the heart of this tragic realm stands Castle Ravenloft, the imposing fortress of Count Strahd von Zarovich, a vampire lord cursed to eternally pursue his lost love, Tatyana, whose soul reincarnates throughout the generations. The adventure presents a sandbox-style exploration of Barovia, where player characters must navigate political intrigue, supernatural threats, and moral ambiguity while ultimately confronting Strahd himself.

The module is designed for characters levels 1-10, though it recommends starting at level 3. Unlike many contemporary fifth edition adventures that follow a more linear structure, Curse of Strahd embraces an open-world approach that gives players significant agency in how they explore Barovia and ultimately confront its master. This design philosophy, combined with its mature themes and complex antagonist, has made it a favorite among experienced players seeking a darker, more sophisticated D&D experience.

Lead designer [[Chris Perkins]], along with contributors including [[Jeremy Crawford]], [[Adam Lee]], and others, crafted an adventure that honors the legacy of the original Ravenloft while incorporating modern game design sensibilities and fifth edition's mechanical framework. The result is an adventure that feels simultaneously classic and contemporary, offering both nostalgia for veteran players and a compelling introduction to gothic horror for newcomers.

## Connection to Original Ravenloft (1983)

The roots of Curse of Strahd extend back to 1983, when [[Tracy Hickman]] and [[Laura Hickman]] created the original Ravenloft module (I6) for [[Advanced Dungeons & Dragons (1977)]]. That groundbreaking adventure introduced Count Strahd von Zarovich and established many of the gothic horror elements that would define the setting for decades. The original module was revolutionary for its time, presenting D&D not as a straightforward dungeon crawl but as a atmospheric horror experience with a sympathetic yet irredeemable villain.

Curse of Strahd pays extensive homage to its predecessor while expanding the scope dramatically. Where the original I6 module focused primarily on Castle Ravenloft itself with limited exploration of the surrounding land, the fifth edition version transforms Barovia into a fully-realized setting with multiple villages, wilderness locations, and narrative threads. The village of Barovia, Vallaki, and Krezk each present distinct communities with their own problems, politics, and potential allies or enemies.

Key elements preserved from the original include the Tarokka card reading that determines the locations of crucial items and allies, the tragic love story between Strahd and Tatyana (now reincarnated as Ireena Kolyana), and the general layout of Castle Ravenloft itself. However, the fifth edition version deepens these elements considerably. Strahd receives more extensive characterization, with detailed motivations, tactics, and personality traits that make him one of the most three-dimensional villains in D&D history. The card reading mechanic has been refined and integrated more thoroughly into the narrative structure.

The adventure also draws inspiration from the broader Ravenloft campaign setting that evolved from the original module, incorporating elements from the extensive line of Ravenloft products published during the 1990s for [[Advanced Dungeons & Dragons Second Edition (1989)]]. References to the Dark Powers, the nature of the Demiplane of Dread, and the cosmology of domains of dread all reflect this expanded lore, though the adventure remains accessible to those unfamiliar with previous Ravenloft materials.

## Adventure Structure and Design

One of Curse of Strahd's greatest strengths lies in its sophisticated sandbox structure. After an initial hook that draws characters into the mists surrounding Barovia, the adventure opens into a largely non-linear experience. The Tarokka card reading performed by the Vistani seer Madam Eva randomizes the locations of three crucial items (the Tome of Strahd, the Sunsword, and the Holy Symbol of Ravenkind) as well as the identity of a potential ally and the location of the final confrontation with Strahd. This randomization ensures that each playthrough feels unique and prevents players who have experienced the adventure from knowing exactly what to expect.

The adventure is organized around several key locations:

**The Village of Barovia** serves as the initial entry point, a depressing settlement where the populace lives in constant fear. Here adventurers meet Ireena Kolyana, Strahd's latest obsession and a key NPC whose safety becomes a major concern throughout the campaign. The village also houses Death House, an optional introductory dungeon that establishes the horror tone and provides lower-level parties with their first serious challenges.

**Vallaki** presents a larger community ruled by Baron Vargas Vallakovich, whose obsession with forced festivals and mandatory happiness creates a powder keg of tension ready to explode. Multiple factions vie for control of the town, and player choices can determine which faction ultimately seizes power. This section excels at political intrigue and demonstrates how different types of horror—not just supernatural, but also human cruelty and delusion—can coexist within the setting.

**Krezk** offers a more isolated community built around the Abbey of Saint Markovia, now occupied by the mad Abbot and his flesh golem experiments. This location highlights body horror and questions of morality, as the Abbot believes he can end Strahd's curse by creating a perfect bride for the vampire lord.

**Castle Ravenloft** itself serves as the ultimate dungeon, a massive castle with over 80 keyed locations that can be approached through multiple strategies. The castle is not designed for a single clear-and-conquer approach; instead, it rewards clever planning, stealth, and careful resource management. Strahd himself actively pursues characters within his castle, creating a cat-and-mouse dynamic that builds tension.

Beyond these major locations, the adventure includes numerous smaller sites: the wizard's tower of [[Khazan]], the ruins of Berez, the werewolf den, the Amber Temple (a late-game location offering dark pacts and power), and many more. Each location connects to the broader narrative while maintaining its own self-contained story and challenges.

The adventure structure encourages Dungeon Masters to view Strahd as an active participant rather than a stationary boss monster. Throughout the adventure, Strahd observes the party, tests them, toys with them, and occasionally aids them if it serves his purposes. This creates a psychological horror element where players never feel truly safe, knowing that Strahd could appear at any moment. The adventure provides extensive guidance on roleplaying Strahd, including his goals, tactics, and how to use him to maximum dramatic effect.

## Gothic Horror Elements

Curse of Strahd commits fully to gothic horror aesthetics and themes, creating an atmosphere distinct from typical high-fantasy D&D adventures. The design team drew inspiration from classic gothic literature and horror films, particularly the works of Bram Stoker, Edgar Allan Poe, and Universal horror classics. This influence permeates every aspect of the adventure, from its narrative themes to its visual presentation.

The land of Barovia itself embodies gothic horror's essential quality: a beautiful but corrupted landscape that reflects its master's cursed nature. Dense forests shroud the land in perpetual gloom, mists prevent escape, and even the weather seems to respond to Strahd's moods. The descriptions emphasize decay, fog, and oppressive atmosphere. Everything in Barovia exists in a state of decline—villages crumble, hope fades, and even the sun rarely breaks through the clouds.

The adventure's horror operates on multiple levels. Supernatural horror appears in the forms of vampires, werewolves, ghosts, and other undead. Body horror emerges in locations like the Abbot's flesh golem workshop. Psychological horror pervades the interactions with Strahd and the realization that escape from Barovia may be impossible. Social horror manifests in communities like Vallaki, where normal people become monsters through fear, desperation, or madness.

Themes of doomed romance, generational curses, and tragic inevitability run throughout the narrative. Strahd's obsession with Tatyana/Ireena provides the emotional core of the adventure. He is not simply an evil overlord but a romantic figure twisted by immortality and obsession. The tragedy lies in the fact that he genuinely believes he loves Ireena, even as his very nature ensures he can never truly possess her or win her love. This complexity elevates him beyond a simple villain into a genuinely tragic antagonist.

The adventure also explores themes of moral compromise and corruption. Characters face difficult choices with no clear right answers. Should they deliver Ireena to Strahd to save a village? Can they trust the Abbot's mad plan? Should they accept dark powers offered at the Amber Temple to gain the strength to defeat Strahd? These decisions force players to engage with the horror on a personal level, not simply as external threats to overcome.

The visual presentation reinforces the gothic aesthetic. The adventure book features moody artwork by various artists that captures the gloom and grandeur of Ravenloft. Maps show castles perched on mountain spires, fog-shrouded villages, and foreboding forests. The graphic design uses aged parchment textures and decorative borders that evoke old grimoires and Victorian Gothic novels.

## Tarokka Card Mechanic

One of Curse of Strahd's most celebrated innovations is its integration of the Tarokka card reading as a central randomization mechanic. This fortune-telling scene occurs relatively early in the adventure when characters meet Madam Eva, a Vistani seer who performs a reading that determines several crucial elements of the campaign.

The Tarokka deck is a specialized fortune-telling deck similar to Tarot cards, consisting of 54 cards divided into suits representing various aspects of fate and fortune. During the reading, Madam Eva draws five cards that determine:

1. **The location of the Tome of Strahd** - A diary revealing Strahd's history and psychology
2. **The location of the Holy Symbol of Ravenkind** - A powerful magical amulet
3. **The location of the Sunsword** - A legendary weapon effective against vampires
4. **The identity of an ally** who will aid the party against Strahd
5. **The location within Castle Ravenloft** where the final confrontation occurs

Each card corresponds to a specific location or NPC detailed in the adventure. For example, the Artifact card might indicate that the Sunsword can be found in the crypt of a particular person in Castle Ravenloft, while the Wizard card might reveal Ezmerelda d'Avenir as the prophesied ally. The adventure includes a complete appendix mapping each possible card to its corresponding location or ally.

This randomization serves multiple purposes. First, it ensures replayability—groups running the adventure multiple times will have different experiences as items appear in different locations. Second, it prevents metagaming, as players who have read the adventure or played it previously cannot rely on prior knowledge. Third, it creates narrative momentum, giving characters specific goals and locations to pursue rather than wandering aimlessly through Barovia.

The card reading also serves important atmospheric and theatrical purposes. Madam Eva's reading provides an in-character prophecy that feels mystical and significant, adding to the gothic atmosphere. Dungeon Masters can enhance this scene with dramatic presentation, perhaps using actual Tarot cards or a custom Tarokka deck (available as an official accessory). The cryptic nature of the card interpretations creates mystery and gives players clues to puzzle over.

[[Wizards of the Coast]] produced an official Tarokka deck as a separate product, though the adventure includes instructions for using a standard deck of playing cards as a substitute. The deck features custom artwork for each card reflecting Ravenloft's themes and aesthetics. Many Dungeon Masters consider the physical deck a worthwhile investment for the enhanced theatrical experience it provides.

The adventure also includes an alternative option for DMs who prefer narrative control over randomization: predetermined locations for items and allies that the designers consider optimal for campaign flow and challenge balance. However, most groups embrace the randomization as part of the Curse of Strahd experience.

## Reception and Impact

Curse of Strahd received overwhelmingly positive reviews upon release and has maintained its reputation as one of fifth edition's finest adventures. Critics and players praised its atmospheric writing, complex villain, open-ended structure, and commitment to horror themes. The adventure demonstrated that D&D could successfully support darker, more mature narratives without losing its essential character.

Professional reviews highlighted several strengths. The mature, sophisticated storytelling stood in contrast to some earlier fifth edition adventures that had been criticized for railroad plots or inconsistent tone. The sandbox structure empowered Dungeon Masters to customize the experience while providing enough guidance to prevent analysis paralysis. Strahd himself received particular praise as a multidimensional antagonist far more interesting than typical villain-of-the-week monsters.

The adventure's success influenced subsequent fifth edition design. Later adventures like Tomb of Annihilation and Waterdeep: Dragon Heist incorporated more open-ended structures and complex NPCs. The willingness to embrace darker themes paved the way for adventures like Descent into Avernus, set in the Nine Hells, and Rime of the Frostmaiden, featuring body horror and isolation.

Curse of Strahd achieved remarkable longevity within the D&D community. Years after publication, it remains one of the most-run fifth edition adventures, frequently appearing on "best D&D adventures" lists. Online communities dedicated to running the adventure share resources, modifications, and advice. The adventure has spawned extensive third-party support, including supplemental adventures, additional maps, alternative endings, and expanded NPC backstories.

The adventure revitalized interest in gothic horror within D&D and the broader Ravenloft setting. While the classic Ravenloft campaign setting from second edition AD&D had been dormant for years, Curse of Strahd's success demonstrated continued appetite for dark fantasy and horror themes. This eventually led to the publication of Van Richten's Guide to Ravenloft in 2021, which expanded the Domains of Dread and provided tools for creating horror adventures across multiple gothic subgenres.

Actual play shows contributed significantly to the adventure's cultural impact. Several high-profile actual play series ran Curse of Strahd campaigns, exposing millions of viewers to the adventure. Dice, Camera, Action, hosted by [[Chris Perkins]] himself, ran an extended Curse of Strahd campaign that showcased the adventure's potential. Numerous other actual play groups on YouTube, Twitch, and podcasts chose Curse of Strahd, drawn by its strong narrative and memorable villain.

The adventure's influence extends beyond D&D. Its success demonstrated the commercial viability of darker, more thematically coherent adventures, influencing adventure design across the broader TTRPG industry. The emphasis on complex villains with understandable motivations became more common across multiple game systems.

Some criticism emerged regarding the adventure's difficulty level and potential for character death. Curse of Strahd does not pull punches—characters can die, often in brutal ways, and the horror atmosphere requires player buy-in to function effectively. The adventure includes several notoriously deadly encounters, including Death House's sacrificial chamber and various ambush scenarios. Some groups found this mismatch with fifth edition's generally more forgiving design philosophy jarring, though others appreciated the higher stakes.

The adventure's treatment of the Vistani—Ravenloft's equivalent of Romani people—also drew criticism for perpetuating stereotypes about Romani culture. [[Wizards of the Coast]] acknowledged these concerns and released revised text addressing problematic elements in subsequent printings and digital versions. This represented part of a broader reckoning within D&D regarding cultural sensitivity and representation.

## Related Supplements

The success of Curse of Strahd led to several supplemental releases expanding the adventure and the Ravenloft setting:

**Revamped (2021)** - A deluxe reprint of Curse of Strahd featuring revised text addressing cultural sensitivity concerns, new artwork, and premium production values. This edition included corrections to errors, updated NPC statistics, and refinements based on years of play feedback.

**Van Richten's Guide to Ravenloft (2021)** - A full campaign setting book exploring the Domains of Dread beyond Barovia. This supplement provides tools for creating horror adventures, details dozens of different domains ruled by various Darklords, and updates the Ravenloft cosmology for fifth edition. While not specifically a Curse of Strahd sequel, it builds directly on the adventure's foundation and provides context for expanding campaigns beyond Strahd's defeat.

Numerous third-party publishers produced unofficial supplements enhancing the Curse of Strahd experience. These include expanded encounter locations, additional NPCs, alternative adventure hooks, and extended epilogues. The DMs Guild marketplace features hundreds of Curse of Strahd-related products, from simple random encounter tables to elaborate expansions adding dozens of hours of content.

The adventure also inspired several board games and other media. The Curse of Strahd board game, part of the D&D Adventure System series, adapts the adventure into a cooperative dungeon-crawl format. Strahd himself has appeared in multiple D&D video games, novels, and comic books, cementing his status as one of D&D's most iconic villains.

Online resources have become essential companions to running the adventure. The r/CurseofStrahd subreddit maintains extensive guides, including the popular "Curse of Strahd: Reloaded" fan revision that reorganizes certain sections for improved pacing and provides additional content for underdeveloped areas. These community resources demonstrate the adventure's remarkable staying power and the dedication of its fan base.

## Conclusion

Curse of Strahd represents a high-water mark for fifth edition adventure design and gothic horror in Dungeons & Dragons. Its successful modernization of the classic Ravenloft module demonstrates how thoughtful design can honor legacy material while adapting it for contemporary audiences. The adventure's enduring popularity, years after publication, testifies to the strength of its core concepts: a compelling villain, atmospheric setting, open-ended structure, and commitment to horror themes.

For players and Dungeon Masters seeking a D&D experience that emphasizes atmosphere, roleplay, and moral complexity over straightforward heroics, Curse of Strahd delivers an unforgettable campaign. It stands as proof that D&D can support sophisticated narratives and mature themes while remaining mechanically accessible and immensely entertaining. Count Strahd von Zarovich's castle may be shrouded in mist, but the adventure's legacy shines bright in the annals of tabletop roleplaying history.
