# Mythender

**Publisher**: Self-published (free PDF)
**Designer**: Ryan Macklin
**Year**: 2012
**System**: Mythender System
**Genre**: fantasy, mythology, god-slaying, tragedy
**Status**: free-download

## Overview

Mythender, designed by Ryan Macklin and released free in 2012, delivers bombastic god-slaying action where players portray Mythenders—mortals empowered to kill gods—fighting Norse deities and monsters at apocalyptic scale while mechanically tracking corruption: every divine power you wield pushes you closer to becoming god yourself, creating tragic arc where ultimate victory (killing all gods) requires becoming the very thing you sought to destroy. The game embraces absurd escalation—ripping Fenrir's jaw off, punching Thor through mountains, wrestling concepts into submission—while corruption mechanics ensure even victorious Mythenders face choice: stop before apotheosis or continue until you've become new god requiring future Mythenders to kill you.

**Key Innovation**: Mythic Power fuels abilities but accumulates Corruption. At maximum Corruption, you become god (NPC). This creates agonizing resource management: use power to win fights but risk losing humanity, or conserve power and possibly die to gods you're fighting.

**Free Release**: Macklin released Mythender as Creative Commons, enabling free distribution and hacking, spreading the game widely while demonstrating alternative to traditional commercial models.

## Core Mechanics

**Mythic Dice Pools**: Roll massive pools (10-30+ dice) representing godlike power

**Corruption Track**: Using powers advances Corruption toward inevitable apotheosis

**Weapons**: Conceptual armaments (Sword of Courage, Fists of Vengeance) defining approach

**Escalation**: Conflicts escalate from verbal to physical to mythic to titanic scales

**Fate Track**: Death's proximity tracked; reaching end means mortality or godhood

## Setting and Themes

**Mythic Norden**: Norse mythology-inspired world ruled by cruel gods

**Mythenders**: Mortals gaining divine power to kill gods

**Tragedy**: Victory requires becoming what you hate

**Escalation**: Every conflict grows more absurd and destructive

## Cultural Impact

Demonstrated free/Creative Commons models could spread games widely. Showed mythic-scale combat could work mechanically. Influenced corruption-through-power-use mechanics.
