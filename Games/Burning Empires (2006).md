# Burning Empires

**Publisher**: Self-published by Luke Crane
**Designer**: [[Luke Crane]], Thor Olavsrud
**Year**: 2006
**System**: Modified Burning Wheel
**Genre**: science-fiction, space-opera, political-drama
**Status**: in-print

## Overview

Burning Empires, released in 2006 by Luke Crane and Thor Olavsrud, represents one of the most ambitious and mechanically sophisticated sci-fi RPGs ever created—a game that asks "what if we combined Burning Wheel's rich mechanical depth with space opera political drama and made the GM and players explicitly oppose each other in structured strategic campaign?" Set in Christopher Moeller's Iron Empires universe where humanity fights desperate war against parasitic alien infection, the game creates sprawling planetary campaigns where player characters lead resistance against insidious invasion while the GM systematically works to subvert civilization from within.

The game's central innovation, the Infection mechanic, transforms traditional RPG campaign structures. Rather than open-ended adventuring, Burning Empires campaigns follow predetermined arc: twelve scenes (Maneuvers) across three phases (each representing months of fictional time) where both players and GM pursue specific victory conditions. Players fight to preserve planetary independence; GM works to infect, infiltrate, and ultimately conquer the world. This creates genuine strategic opposition—the GM isn't neutral facilitator but active antagonist with mechanical tools for pursuing victory.

The Burning Wheel system foundation provides remarkable character depth. Skills, beliefs, instincts, and traits create psychologically complex protagonists. The Duel of Wits, Range and Cover combat, and Fight! systems enable intricate social, firefight, and melee conflicts with tactical richness rivaling tactical miniatures games. Characters improve through Belief-driven advancement—pursuing philosophical commitments rather than killing monsters for experience points.

However, this mechanical sophistication comes at cost: Burning Empires is notoriously complex, demanding significant system mastery from all participants. The rulebook exceeds 650 pages. The strategic campaign framework requires commitment to seeing multi-month campaigns through completion. The learning curve is steep, even for experienced roleplayers. This complexity limited Burning Empires's audience despite its design excellence and thematic power.

Yet for groups willing to invest, Burning Empires delivers experiences impossible in other RPGs. The political maneuvering, the creeping infection, the desperate resistance, the strategic resource management, the psychological depth—all combine to create campaigns of operatic scope and intimate character drama simultaneously. The game proved Luke Crane's Burning Wheel system could power space opera as compellingly as fantasy while demonstrating how structured campaign frameworks could transform RPG experiences.

## Historical Context

Burning Empires emerged from intersection of multiple design trajectories. Luke Crane's Burning Wheel (2002) had established him as innovative designer pursuing sophisticated character-driven gameplay. The game's psychological depth, belief-driven play, and mechanical richness attracted devoted following willing to engage with complex systems for payoffs in emergent drama.

Christopher Moeller's Iron Empires comics created rich sci-fi setting: humanity spread across stars faces invasion by Vaylen—parasitic worms that infect human hosts, subverting civilizations from within. The comics depicted desperate political struggles, military campaigns, and psychological horror of invasion where anyone might be infected. This setting provided perfect foundation for political science-fiction gaming.

Crane had long admired Iron Empires, seeing potential for role-playing game adaptation. Moeller's comics emphasized political intrigue, factional conflicts, and characters driven by convictions—perfectly aligned with Burning Wheel's strengths. The challenge was adapting fantasy-focused Burning Wheel system to hard science fiction while capturing comics' strategic scope.

Development involved close collaboration with Thor Olavsrud and extensive consultation with Moeller. Crane wanted mechanical fidelity to source material—the game needed to feel like Iron Empires comics, where infection spreads methodically, politics matter more than laser battles, and conviction drives action.

The Infection mechanic emerged as central innovation. Rather than open sandbox, campaigns would follow structured arc mirroring comic narratives: planetary invasion progressing through phases (Infiltration, Usurpation, Invasion) with specific mechanical victory conditions. This created genuine strategic opposition—players and GM pursuing incompatible goals within mechanical framework.

The 2006 release coincided with renewed interest in hard sci-fi gaming. Battlestar Galactica TV series (2004-2009) demonstrated audience appetite for morally complex space opera with political drama and paranoia. The New Space Opera literary movement (Banks, Reynolds, Stross) showed science fiction could balance grand scale with intimate character focus. Burning Empires positioned itself at this intersection—operatic scope with character psychology.

The rulebook's production was ambitious—full-color hardcover exceeding 650 pages, extensively illustrated with Moeller's artwork. This was expensive indie RPG by 2006 standards ($35 retail), but the production values matched the mechanical depth and thematic richness.

Reception was polarized. Design-focused gamers praised the structured campaign framework, the psychological character depth, and the strategic gameplay. The game won ENnie Gold for Best Game 2007. Reviewers called it sophisticated, ambitious, and uniquely compelling. However, accessibility concerns dominated criticism: the complexity, the page count, the system mastery requirements all created barriers. The game attracted devoted following but never achieved mainstream success.

The game influenced subsequent designs thinking about structured campaigns, GM-versus-players mechanics, infection/corruption systems, and how to adapt Burning Wheel's framework to different genres. While never as widely played as simpler sci-fi games, Burning Empires demonstrated what sophisticated systemic design could achieve in role-playing.

## Core Mechanics

Burning Empires uses modified Burning Wheel system, adding space opera-specific subsystems:

### Core Resolution: Burning Wheel Foundation

Roll d6 dice pool equal to skill + attribute, count successes (4+ on each die). More successes means better outcome. Difficulty varies by obstacle rating (number of successes needed).

**Example**: Piloting a damaged fighter through debris field might require 4 successes. Roll 6 dice (Pilot skill 4 + Perception 2), count dice showing 4+.

### Character Burning: Deep Creation

Characters are "Burned" (created) through lifepath system:
- Choose series of lifepaths representing character history (Noble Born → Academy → Military Service → Veteran Officer)
- Each lifepath provides skill points, trait points, resource points
- Sum total determines character capabilities

This creates psychologically rich backgrounds. A character's lifepaths aren't just flavor—they mechanically define skills, relationships, resources, and beliefs.

### Beliefs, Instincts, Traits

**Beliefs**: Character's core convictions driving action. "I will protect the colony from Vaylen infection at any cost." Players must pursue beliefs to earn advancement.

**Instincts**: Automatic behaviors. "Always wear sidearm." These trigger without conscious decision, creating consistent characterization.

**Traits**: Personality quirks and character defining features. "Paranoid," "Gifted Orator," "Battle-Scarred." Traits provide mechanical bonuses/penalties contextually.

These systems ensure mechanical benefits flow from character psychology, not optimization. You advance by playing character truthfully, not maximizing numerical efficiency.

### Artha: The Advancement Currency

Characters earn Artha (fate, persona, deeds) through:
- Pursuing Beliefs (especially when costly)
- Playing Instincts and Traits
- Working against self-interest for Beliefs
- Suffering for convictions

Artha can be spent to:
- Add dice to pools
- Call for tests in your favor
- Introduce facts into fiction
- Survive

death-touches

This creates virtuous cycle: play character depth → earn mechanical benefits → enable greater challenges → create more dramatic situations.

### The Infection Mechanic: Structured Campaign

Burning Empires campaigns follow rigid structure:
- **Three Phases**: Infiltration, Usurpation, Invasion
- **Four Maneuvers per Phase**: Twelve total scenes
- **Scene Types**: Building (players advance their agendas), Color (development/exploration), Interstitial (GM advances Vaylen agenda)

Each Maneuver, one player gets Building scene (pursuing personal goal), one gets Color scene (character development), and GM gets Interstitial scene (advancing infection).

**Disposition Tracks**: Both sides have Disposition representing planetary integrity and infection spread. Scenes advance or reduce these tracks. At campaign end, remaining Disposition determines victor:
- Players high = World saved
- GM high = World infected/conquered
- Mixed = Pyrrhic outcomes

This creates genuine strategic framework where every scene matters mechanically.

### Duel of Wits: Social Combat

Social conflicts use structured exchange system:
1. Set stakes for conflict
2. Determine Disposition (social stamina)
3. Choose tactics (Point, Rebut, Obfuscate, Dismiss, Incite)
4. Roll opposed tests
5. Compare successes, reduce opponent's Disposition
6. First to zero Disposition loses, stakes resolve as predetermined

This makes social conflicts as mechanically engaging as combat, perfect for political intrigue focus.

### Fight! and Range & Cover: Combat Systems

**Fight!**: Melee combat uses exchange-based system with volley of actions (Strike, Block, Avoid, Lock, Throw). Players script actions simultaneously, reveal, resolve. This creates tactical depth as you predict opponent actions.

**Range & Cover**: Firefight system modeling positioning, cover, suppression, and tactical movement. Actions cost hesitation (initiative resource), creating strategic choices about aggression versus caution.

Both systems are complex but reward tactical thinking and fictional positioning.

### Technology and Resources

The game includes systems for:
- **Resources**: Abstract wealth and influence
- **Reputation**: Social standing and fame
- **Affiliations**: Relationships with organizations
- **Technology**: Equipment, ships, weapons, armor all mechanically defined

These aren't secondary—they're integral to character effectiveness and campaign goals.

### Character Types

Multiple character types available:
- **Human**: Standard characters with full psychological depth
- **Vaylen**: Infected humans serving alien agenda
- **Mukhadish**: Alien species
- **Various Lifepaths**: Noble, Soldier, Bureaucrat, Criminal, Religious, etc.

This variety enables diverse cast appropriate for political space opera.

### Circles: Relationship System

Characters use Circles tests to introduce NPCs they know. This distributes narrative authority—players help populate world rather than GM creating all characters. "I call on an old academy friend now serving in planetary defense."

### Advancement Through Belief

Characters improve skills by:
- Using them under pressure (tests)
- Succeeding AND failing (both teach lessons)
- Pursuing Beliefs that challenge them

This means advancement comes from dramatic engagement, not grinding or optimization.

## Setting and Themes

Burning Empires assumes the Iron Empires setting:

### The Iron Empires: Humanity's Remnant

Millennia-old human civilization spread across stars has fractured into autonomous worlds following collapse of central authority. Each world is independent empire/republic/theocracy/etc. connected by infrequent trade and occasional military alliances.

This fragmentation means no unified response to Vaylen threat—each world faces invasion alone, often unaware others are similarly besieged.

### The Vaylen: Parasitic Infection

The Vaylen are parasitic worms that infect human hosts, taking control while preserving host's memories and capabilities. Infected individuals ("Naiven") appear normal, making detection nearly impossible without specialized scanning.

The invasion follows pattern:
1. **Infiltration**: Small numbers of Vaylen secretly infect key individuals
2. **Usurpation**: Infected individuals subvert institutions, preparing for main invasion
3. **Invasion**: Vaylen fleets arrive, open warfare begins, infected reveal themselves

This creates paranoia-inducing gameplay where anyone might be infected, trust is liability, and political victory matters as much as military success.

### Technology: Hard Sci-Fi Aesthetic

Technology is advanced but grounded:
- Faster-than-light travel exists but is slow (months between stars)
- Artificial gravity, energy weapons, powered armor all standard
- No transporters, replicators, or magic-like technology
- Warfare combines near-future tactics with sci-fi elements
- Communications lag means worlds operate independently

This creates logistically realistic space opera rather than soft sci-fi wish fulfillment.

### Political Focus: Intrigue Over Combat

The game emphasizes political maneuvering. Battles happen, but campaigns are won/lost through:
- Identifying infected individuals before they consolidate power
- Securing resources and alliances
- Maintaining planetary unity against divisive infiltration
- Balancing security measures against civil liberties
- Preventing panic while mobilizing defense

This makes Burning Empires fundamentally political game wearing space opera clothing.

### Conviction and Belief: Character Drivers

Characters are defined by Beliefs (convictions) pursued regardless of cost. A noble might believe "The monarchy must be preserved to prevent chaos," while revolutionary believes "The corrupt nobility enables Vaylen infiltration." These incompatible Beliefs create intra-party tension even as characters cooperate against invasion.

The game mechanically rewards pursuing Beliefs, making conviction-driven action optimal strategy.

### Factions and Organizations

Multiple organizations provide affiliation options:
- Military forces (Anvil, Hammer, Coregum)
- Religious orders (Mundus Humanitas)
- Noble houses
- Criminal syndicates
- Merchant guilds

These affiliations provide mechanical benefits while creating political complexity.

## Cultural Impact

**Structured Campaign Design**: Burning Empires pioneered mechanically-structured campaign arcs with explicit victory conditions, influencing how designers think about campaign frameworks.

**GM-Versus-Players Framework**: Demonstrated adversarial GM role could work within collaborative framework when transparent and mechanically bounded.

**Infection Mechanics**: The systematic infection spread influenced corruption/infiltration mechanics in subsequent games.

**Belief-Driven Play**: Showed mechanical systems could incentivize character-driven decision-making over tactical optimization.

**Political Focus**: Proved political intrigue could drive space opera campaigns as compellingly as combat.

**ENnie Gold 2007**: Winning Best Game validated complex, demanding designs could achieve critical acclaim.

**Burning Wheel Adaptation**: Demonstrated Burning Wheel system's flexibility across genres, validating its engine status.

**Character Psychology**: Influenced how designers thought about psychological depth through mechanical systems.

**Hard Sci-Fi Gaming**: Positioned sophisticated hard science fiction as viable RPG genre beyond Star Wars-style space fantasy.

## Reception and Criticism

**Praise**:
- Sophisticated mechanical framework supporting strategic gameplay
- Deep character psychology through Beliefs/Instincts/Traits
- Structured campaign creating focused narratives
- Political intrigue emphasis perfect for setting
- Paranoia and infection mechanics thematically compelling
- Beautiful production with Moeller's artwork
- Strategic depth rivaling board games
- Genuine innovation in campaign structure

**Criticisms**:
- **Overwhelming Complexity**: 650+ page rulebook, dense systems, steep learning curve
- **System Mastery Requirements**: Demands significant investment before play becomes smooth
- **Campaign Commitment**: Twelve-scene structure inflexible, difficult to adjust mid-campaign
- **GM Burden**: Running Burning Empires well requires extensive preparation and system knowledge
- **Limited Accessibility**: Complexity excludes casual players and beginners
- **Slow Play**: Combat/social conflict systems can bog down pacing
- **Narrow Appeal**: Hard sci-fi political focus doesn't suit all space opera preferences

**Community Response**:
- Devoted following among design-focused gamers
- Extensive online discussion and strategy guides
- Actual-play recordings demonstrating complexity
- Community-created resources helping accessibility
- Considered "dream game" by some, "nightmare game" by others

**Comparison to Other Sci-Fi**:
- More complex than Star Wars d6/FFG
- More politically focused than Traveller
- More mechanically sophisticated than Stars Without Number
- Comparable complexity to Rolemaster
- Different tone from space opera like Scum and Villainy

## Legacy and Influence

**Campaign Structure Innovation**: The Infection mechanics influenced structured campaign designs in games like Band of Blades, Legacy: Life Among the Ruins, and other framework-driven games.

**Adversarial GM Mechanics**: Demonstrated transparent GM opposition could create compelling play, influencing games with explicit GM-versus-players elements.

**Burning Wheel Validation**: Proved Burning Wheel system could power diverse genres, encouraging further adaptations and hacks.

**Political Intrigue Systems**: Influenced how designers approach faction play, infiltration mechanics, and political campaigns.

**Belief Systems**: The mechanical incentives for pursuing character convictions influenced advancement systems in narrative games.

**Design Ambition**: Encouraged designers to pursue mechanically sophisticated projects despite accessibility concerns, validating complexity as viable design choice.

**Cult Classic Status**: Remains reference point for "complex but rewarding" game design, studied by designers interested in strategic campaigns.
