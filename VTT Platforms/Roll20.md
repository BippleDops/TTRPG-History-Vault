# Roll20

**Platform Type**: Virtual Tabletop (VTT)
**Launched**: 2012
**Business Model**: Freemium (free tier + subscriptions)
**User Base**: 10+ million registered users
**Website**: https://roll20.net
**Status**: Active and growing
**Impact on Industry**: 5/5

## Overview

Roll20 is a web-based virtual tabletop platform that enables players to play tabletop roleplaying games online. Launched in 2012 via Kickstarter, Roll20 became the most widely-used VTT platform, democratizing online play by requiring no software downloads and offering a robust free tier. The platform played a crucial role in making TTRPGs accessible to geographically dispersed players and later became essential infrastructure during the COVID-19 pandemic when in-person gaming became impossible.

Roll20 provides tools for game masters to create maps, manage characters, handle dice rolls, and run campaigns for dozens of different TTRPG systems. Its marketplace connects content creators with players, allowing purchase of official modules, third-party adventures, and community-created assets. The platform's ease of use, browser-based accessibility, and freemium model made it the default choice for many groups transitioning to online play.

The success of Roll20 fundamentally changed the TTRPG landscape, enabling remote play to become standard rather than exceptional, supporting the growth of actual play streaming, and helping facilitate D&D's mainstream renaissance in the 2010s and 2020s.

## History and Development

### Origins and Kickstarter (2012)

Roll20 was founded by Riley Dutton, Nolan T. Jones, and Richard Zayas, three tabletop gaming enthusiasts who wanted to continue playing RPGs after moving to different cities. Frustrated by existing virtual tabletop solutions that required software downloads, had poor interfaces, or lacked critical features, they decided to build their own web-based platform.

In April 2012, they launched a Kickstarter campaign seeking $5,000 to fund development. The campaign resonated with the TTRPG community, ultimately raising over $39,000 from nearly 1,000 backers. This early support demonstrated significant demand for accessible online play tools.

Key selling points from the original Kickstarter:
- **Browser-based**: No downloads, play from any computer
- **Free tier**: Core functionality available to everyone
- **System agnostic**: Support for multiple RPG systems
- **Video/voice chat**: Integrated communication tools
- **Character sheets**: Digital management of PC stats
- **Dynamic lighting**: Fog of war and vision blocking

### Early Growth (2012-2014)

Roll20 launched its beta in August 2012 and quickly gained users:

**2012-2013**: Initial user base of thousands of players discovering the platform through word-of-mouth, gaming forums, and Reddit

**Key Early Features**:
- Drag-and-drop map creation
- Token management for characters and monsters
- Dice roller with visible results
- Character sheet templates for popular systems
- Layer system for maps (background, tokens, dynamic lighting, etc.)

**Community Development**:
- Users creating custom character sheets for various systems
- API scripts extending functionality
- Community forums forming around the platform
- Early actual play shows using Roll20 (pre-dating the streaming boom)

### Marketplace Launch (2014)

In 2014, Roll20 launched its marketplace, fundamentally changing its business model and the VTT landscape:

**Content Categories**:
- **Official modules**: Partnerships with publishers for licensed adventures
- **Third-party creators**: Independent creators selling maps, tokens, and modules
- **Art assets**: Tokens, map tiles, and visual resources

**Impact**:
- Created revenue stream beyond subscriptions
- Enabled content creators to monetize TTRPG assets
- Provided easy access to professional-quality modules
- Established Roll20 as platform for official content distribution

**Early Partnerships**:
- Wizards of the Coast (D&D 5E modules)
- Paizo (Pathfinder adventures)
- Chaosium (Call of Cthulhu content)

### Rapid Expansion (2015-2019)

Roll20's user base exploded during the mid-to-late 2010s, coinciding with D&D 5E's success and the actual play streaming boom:

**User Growth**:
- 2015: 1 million registered users
- 2017: 2 million registered users
- 2019: 5 million registered users

**Feature Additions**:
- **Character Vault** (2015): Store characters across campaigns
- **Compendium** (2016): Searchable rule references (D&D SRD initially)
- **Dynamic Lighting Overhaul** (2018-2020): Improved vision and lighting system
- **Mobile app** (2017): iOS and Android support
- **Jukebox improvements**: Audio and ambience tools

**Partnerships Expanded**:
- D&D Beyond integration announced
- More publishers added to marketplace
- Official character sheet support for major systems
- Sponsored actual play shows on Twitch/YouTube

### COVID-19 Pandemic (2020-2021)

The COVID-19 pandemic transformed Roll20 from useful tool to essential infrastructure:

**User Surge**:
- March 2020: Massive spike in new users as in-person gaming became impossible
- June 2020: 7 million registered users
- By end of 2021: 10+ million registered users

**Server Strain and Response**:
- Initial server issues from unprecedented traffic
- Rapid infrastructure scaling to meet demand
- Free tier maintained despite costs
- Premium subscriptions increased to support growth

**Community Impact**:
- Existing groups forced online
- New players discovering TTRPGs through remote play
- Normalization of hybrid (some remote, some in-person) games
- Long-term shift in play patterns even after pandemic restrictions eased

**Industry Impact**:
- Publishers accelerated digital content strategies
- VTTs became standard consideration for game design
- Increased legitimacy for online play

### Post-Pandemic Evolution (2022-Present)

Following the pandemic, Roll20 has focused on retention and feature improvements:

**User Retention**:
- Many pandemic users continued playing online
- Hybrid play (some players remote, others in-person) became common
- Geographic barriers to playing permanently reduced

**Feature Development**:
- Charactermancer improvements (guided character creation)
- Enhanced mobile experience
- Better D&D Beyond integration
- Performance optimizations
- Accessibility improvements

**Competition**:
- Foundry VTT gaining market share among experienced users
- D&D Beyond's integrated VTT announced (2023)
- Other platforms like Fantasy Grounds, Astral, Owlbear Rodeo emerging

## Features and Functionality

### Core Features

**Map and Token System**:
- Drag-and-drop map uploading
- Grid and hex map support
- Multi-layer system (background, objects, tokens, lighting, GM layer)
- Token manipulation (health bars, status markers, vision settings)
- Measuring tools and drawing tools

**Dynamic Lighting**:
- Fog of war revealing as characters explore
- Line-of-sight calculations
- Light source simulation (torches, spells, darkvision)
- Colored lighting effects
- Vision blocking for walls and obstacles

**Character Sheets**:
- System-specific character sheets for 100+ RPGs
- Automated calculations
- Integrated dice rolling from sheet values
- Custom macros for complex actions
- Character Vault for cross-campaign character storage

**Dice Rolling**:
- Full dice notation support (d20, 3d6+5, etc.)
- Advantage/disadvantage for D&D 5E
- Exploding dice, keep highest/lowest, etc.
- Macro system for complex rolls
- Roll templates for formatted results
- Whisper rolls to GM

**Communication**:
- Integrated video chat (powered by Tokbox/Vonage)
- Voice chat
- Text chat with in-character and out-of-character modes
- Emotes and character portraits in chat
- Dice roll results visible in chat log

**Jukebox**:
- Background music playback
- Sound effect triggers
- Volume controls
- Playlist creation
- Integration with online music sources

### Advanced Features

**API Scripts** (Pro subscribers):
- JavaScript API for custom functionality
- Community library of thousands of scripts
- Automation of complex mechanics
- Custom commands and macros
- Integration with external tools

**Compendium**:
- Searchable rules reference
- D&D 5E SRD included
- Purchasable expanded content (Player's Handbook, etc.)
- Drag-and-drop from compendium to game
- Monster stat blocks and spell descriptions

**Marketplace Content**:
- Official publisher modules
- Third-party adventures
- Map packs and tokens
- Art and asset libraries
- Pre-built encounters

**Transmogrifier** (Pro/Plus):
- Copy content between games
- Share handouts, characters, maps across campaigns
- Template creation for recurring elements

## Business Model

### Subscription Tiers

**Free Tier**:
- Core functionality fully accessible
- Up to 100 MB storage
- Limited dynamic lighting features
- Basic character sheet access
- No API access

**Plus Subscription** ($4.99/month):
- 3 GB storage
- Full dynamic lighting
- Priority support
- Transmogrifier access
- Character vault expansion

**Pro Subscription** ($9.99/month):
- 5 GB storage (expandable)
- API script access
- Enhanced dynamic lighting tools
- Content linking and sharing
- Priority server access

### Marketplace Revenue

**Revenue Sharing**:
- Publishers and creators receive percentage of sales
- Roll20 takes platform fee
- Incentivizes high-quality content creation

**Content Pricing**:
- Official modules: $20-$50 typically
- Third-party content: $5-$30 commonly
- Individual assets: $1-$10
- Bundles and sales periodically offered

## Controversies and Challenges

### The Nolan T. Jones Incident (2018)

Co-founder Nolan T. Jones engaged in heated argument with community member on Reddit, leading to:

**Incident**:
- User criticized Roll20 customer service
- Jones responded defensively and banned user
- Community backlash over perceived censorship
- Jones initially doubled down before apologizing

**Consequences**:
- Temporary user exodus
- Competitors gained attention
- Roll20 implemented community guidelines
- Jones stepped back from direct community engagement
- Lasting trust issues for some users

**Lessons Learned**:
- Importance of professional community management
- Separation of personal and company accounts
- Value of community trust
- How quickly online controversies escalate

### Performance and Technical Issues

**Common Complaints**:
- Dynamic lighting performance problems
- Large maps causing browser crashes
- Video chat quality inconsistent
- Mobile app limitations
- Load times for complex games

**Response**:
- Ongoing optimization efforts
- Dynamic lighting system rebuilt (2020)
- Performance improvement updates
- User guidance on map optimization
- Investment in infrastructure

### Competition and Feature Lag

**Challengers**:
- **Foundry VTT** (2020): One-time purchase, self-hosted, highly customizable
- **Fantasy Grounds** (longtime competitor): Deep system integration
- **Astral Tabletop**: Modern interface, free tier
- **Owlbear Rodeo**: Simple, focused tool
- **D&D Beyond's VTT** (announced 2023): Wizards of the Coast's integrated solution

**User Concerns**:
- Perception that Roll20 not innovating fast enough
- Features added slowly compared to competitors
- Interface feeling dated
- Some users migrating to alternatives

**Roll20's Response**:
- Continued feature development
- Leveraging large user base and network effects
- Marketplace content library as moat
- Free tier keeping barrier to entry low
