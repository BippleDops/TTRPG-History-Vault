# Not Another D&D Podcast

**Format**: Audio podcast actual play
**System**: D&D 5th Edition
**First Episode**: March 29, 2018
**Cast**: Brian Murphy (DM), Emily Axford, Caldwell Tanner, Jake Hurwitz
**Network**: HeadGum
**Status**: Ongoing (Multiple campaigns completed)

## Overview

Not Another D&D Podcast (NADDPOD) is an audio-first actual play podcast that masterfully balances comedy, genuine emotional storytelling, and strategic D&D gameplay. Created by four comedians and former CollegeHumor staff members, the show launched in 2018 and quickly distinguished itself in the increasingly crowded actual play landscape through its unique combination of absurdist humor, deeply emotional character moments, and sophisticated mechanical gameplay.

The show is hosted by Dungeon Master Brian Murphy (often called "Murph"), with players Emily Axford (his wife and frequent collaborator), Caldwell Tanner, and Jake Hurwitz. All four hosts come from comedy backgrounds, having worked together at CollegeHumor, and their natural chemistry and comedic timing infuse every episode with genuine laughter while never undermining the serious stakes of their storytelling.

What sets NADDPOD apart from other comedy-focused actual play shows is its commitment to both mechanical gameplay and narrative depth. Unlike some comedy actual plays that treat rules loosely, NADDPOD demonstrates that rigorous adherence to D&D mechanics can enhance rather than limit storytelling. The players are tactically sophisticated, regularly employing creative combat strategies and building mechanically optimized characters without sacrificing character development or roleplay.

The show's first campaign, set in the homebrew world of Bahumia, ran for 100 episodes and established NADDPOD as a major force in actual play podcasting. The campaign followed the Band of Boobs (later rebranded as the Band of Merry Mutineers) through an epic story that began with comedic hijinks and evolved into a genuinely moving narrative about family, destiny, and sacrifice. Subsequent campaigns have explored different tones and settings while maintaining the show's signature balance of humor and heart.

NADDPOD has built a devoted fanbase known as the "Two Crew" (named after the podcast's signature "2-Crew" chant), who engage with the show through fan art, live show attendance, Patreon support, and active community participation. The show's influence extends beyond its listener base, inspiring other podcasters to embrace longer-form campaigns and demonstrating that actual play content can succeed without video production.

## Production History

### Origins: CollegeHumor Alumni and the Birth of NADDPOD (2017-2018)

The origins of Not Another D&D Podcast trace back to the collaborative relationships formed at CollegeHumor, the comedy website and video production company where all four hosts worked. Brian Murphy, Emily Axford, Caldwell Tanner, and Jake Hurwitz had collaborated on various comedy projects and sketches throughout their tenure at CollegeHumor, developing strong creative chemistry and comedic timing.

Brian Murphy had been running D&D games for friends and colleagues for years, including games with his future NADDPOD co-hosts. As actual play content began gaining mainstream attention in 2017-2018, particularly with the success of shows like [[Critical Role]] and [[The Adventure Zone]], Murphy and his collaborators saw an opportunity to bring their own comedic sensibilities to the format.

The show's title itself is a playful jab at the proliferation of D&D podcasts during this period. Rather than trying to differentiate themselves through a unique name, the hosts leaned into the oversaturation with deliberate irony, creating a title that simultaneously acknowledged and embraced their entry into an already crowded field.

NADDPOD launched on March 29, 2018, on the HeadGum podcast network, which was founded by former CollegeHumor head writer Jake Hurwitz and his colleague Amir Blumenfeld. This connection gave NADDPOD immediate distribution infrastructure and cross-promotional opportunities with other HeadGum shows, helping establish an initial audience.

### Campaign One: Bahumia and the Band of Boobs (2018-2020)

The first campaign of NADDPOD introduced listeners to Bahumia, a homebrew fantasy world created by Brian Murphy. The campaign followed three unlikely heroes: Hardwon Surefoot (played by Jake Hurwitz), a half-elven fighter with dwarven upbringing; Beverly Toegold V (played by Caldwell Tanner), a young human paladin from a noble family; and Moonshine Cybin (played by Emily Axford), a wood elf druid from the Crick.

The party initially called themselves the Band of Boobs, a deliberately crude name that became iconic among fans. The campaign began with relatively straightforward adventuring but quickly evolved into an epic narrative involving:

- Ancient evils threatening the world
- Complicated family dynamics and legacies
- Divine conflicts between gods and mortals
- Personal character arcs exploring themes of belonging, destiny, and self-worth
- World-ending stakes that felt earned rather than arbitrary

The campaign's success lay in its ability to pivot from absurdist comedy to genuine pathos, sometimes within the same scene. Moments like Hardwon's confrontation with his adoptive father, Beverly's grappling with his role as the Chosen One, and Moonshine's connection to her community became genuinely moving despite (or perhaps because of) being interspersed with crude jokes and comedic bits.

Campaign One concluded after 100 episodes in September 2020, providing a narratively satisfying conclusion that brought tears to both the cast and listeners. The finale demonstrated NADDPOD's commitment to long-form storytelling and emotional payoff, with character arcs that had been building since episode one reaching their culmination.

### Expansion and Short Rest (2018-Present)

From the beginning, NADDPOD supplemented its main feed with "Short Rest" episodes available to Patreon supporters. These after-show discussions allowed the cast to discuss the episode's events, answer listener questions, and provide behind-the-scenes insights into their creative process. The Short Rest episodes became nearly as popular as the main show, with fans appreciating the casual conversations and deeper dives into character motivations and mechanical decisions.

The Patreon model proved highly successful for NADDPOD, building a sustainable revenue stream that allowed the show to remain independent while investing in improved production quality. The show's Patreon community became central to its identity, with supporter-exclusive content, live show access, and community interaction creating a deeply engaged fanbase.

### Campaign Two: Eldermourne (2020-2022)

After completing the Bahumia campaign, NADDPOD launched Campaign Two in October 2020, set in the world of Eldermourne. This darker, more gothic fantasy setting represented a tonal shift from Bahumia's more traditional high fantasy. The campaign featured a new party: Fia (Caldwell Tanner), a goblin ranger; Henry Hogfish (Jake Hurwitz), a human barbarian; and Zirk Vervain (Emily Axford), a human artificer.

Eldermourne explored themes of industrialization, class struggle, and moral ambiguity in a world where magic and technology intersected. The campaign ran for 60 episodes and demonstrated the show's versatility, proving they could create compelling narratives in different tones and settings while maintaining their signature style.

### Campaign Three: Return to Bahumia (2022-Present)

In 2022, NADDPOD returned to Bahumia for Campaign Three, titled "The Sprawl Saga." Set years after the events of Campaign One, this campaign features Calder Killion (Jake Hurwitz), a half-elf paladin; Sol Bufo (Caldwell Tanner), a goliath monk; and Calliope Petrichor (Emily Axford), a water genasi sorcerer.

The return to Bahumia allowed the show to explore how the world had changed after the events of Campaign One while introducing new characters and storylines. Fan-favorite NPCs from the original campaign appear in new roles, creating satisfying connections for long-time listeners while remaining accessible to newcomers.

### Mixed Bags and Special Campaigns

Between major campaigns, NADDPOD has produced numerous shorter series called "Mixed Bags," featuring different DMs, guest players, and experimental formats:

- Various one-shots and short arcs with celebrity guests
- "Trinyvale": Emily Axford as DM with the rest of the crew as players
- "Hot Boy Summer": A comedy-focused mini-campaign
- "Dungeon Court": A listener question and advice segment combining D&D discussion with comedy

These Mixed Bags allow the show to experiment with different formats and give the regular cast breaks from their usual roles while maintaining consistent content output for Patreon supporters.

## Cultural Impact

### Bridging Comedy and Sincerity in Actual Play

NADDPOD's most significant cultural contribution to the actual play space is demonstrating that comedy and emotional depth are not mutually exclusive. In the early days of the actual play boom, there was often an implicit divide between "serious" shows focused on dramatic storytelling and "comedy" shows that prioritized laughs over narrative coherence.

NADDPOD rejected this binary, proving that a show could feature crude humor, running gags, and absurdist comedy while simultaneously delivering genuinely moving character arcs and high-stakes dramatic moments. This approach influenced numerous subsequent actual play shows, including [[Dungeons and Daddies]], which similarly balances irreverent humor with emotional storytelling.

The show's ability to transition seamlessly between comedy and drama—sometimes within a single scene or even a single exchange—became a hallmark of its style. Listeners learned to expect that an episode might make them laugh hysterically one moment and cry genuine tears the next. This emotional range created deep investment in characters and story while maintaining the entertainment value of comedy.

### Audio-First Production in a Video-Dominated Space

While many high-profile actual play shows in 2018 were investing heavily in video production, streaming capabilities, and visual presentation, NADDPOD committed to being audio-first. This decision proved both practically and aesthetically significant.

Practically, focusing on audio allowed NADDPOD to maintain a consistent production schedule without the logistical challenges of video production. The four hosts could record around their other commitments, and listeners could consume episodes during commutes, workouts, or other activities where video would be impractical.

Aesthetically, the audio-only format encouraged listeners to engage their imagination, visualizing the world of Bahumia or Eldermourne in their minds. This participatory aspect of audio storytelling created a different kind of engagement than video actual play, with each listener developing their own mental images of characters and settings.

NADDPOD's success demonstrated that actual play content could thrive without video production, encouraging other creators who lacked resources or interest in video content to pursue podcasting. Shows like [[Three Black Halflings]] and others followed NADDPOD's model of high-quality audio production focused on storytelling rather than visual presentation.

### Community Building and the Two Crew

The NADDPOD community, known as the "Two Crew" (named after the show's signature "2-Crew" chant, itself a reference to a joke about the character Balnor), has become one of the most active and creative fanbases in actual play podcasting.

The Two Crew produces extensive fan art, with dedicated artists creating illustrations of characters, scenes, and moments from the show. The cast regularly showcases this fan art on social media and in video compilations, creating a feedback loop that encourages further creative engagement. Some fan artists have gained significant followings within the community, with their interpretations of characters becoming iconic representations.

The community's creativity extends beyond visual art to include:
- Fan fiction exploring character relationships and alternative storylines
- Musical compositions inspired by the show, including the "2-Crew" song
- Cosplay of NADDPOD characters at conventions
- Community Discord servers for discussion and roleplay
- Charity initiatives organized by fans

NADDPOD's live shows have become major community events, with tours selling out theaters across North America. These live performances feature the cast playing special one-shots or campaign episodes in front of audiences, creating shared experiences that strengthen community bonds. The energy of live audiences adds a unique dynamic to episodes, with crowd reactions and participation becoming part of the performance.

### Influence on D&D Play Culture

NADDPOD has influenced how many people approach playing D&D, particularly in balancing mechanical optimization with roleplay:

**Mechanical Sophistication**: Unlike some actual play shows that handwave mechanics in favor of narrative flow, NADDPOD demonstrates sophisticated tactical gameplay. Emily Axford, in particular, is known for building mechanically powerful characters and employing creative combat strategies, showing that optimization and roleplay aren't opposing forces.

**Homebrew Content**: Brian Murphy's extensive homebrewing—creating custom magic items, monsters, and world mechanics—has inspired DMs to personalize their games. The show demonstrates how homebrew content can enhance rather than replace core D&D mechanics.

**Collaborative Storytelling**: The cast's approach to collaborative storytelling, where players actively contribute to world-building and narrative development, has become a model for tables seeking more shared creative control.

**Handling Failure**: The show's embrace of failure as a storytelling opportunity, celebrating terrible rolls and turning setbacks into character moments, has helped destigmatize failure in D&D play.

### Patreon Model and Independent Production

NADDPOD's success with Patreon-based funding demonstrated the viability of fan-supported actual play content. By offering Short Rest episodes, bonus content, live show access, and community features to Patreon supporters, the show built a sustainable business model that didn't rely on corporate sponsorship or network support.

This model has been replicated by numerous other actual play podcasts, proving that creators could maintain creative independence while building profitable ventures through direct fan support. The show's transparency about its business model and gratitude toward supporters helped normalize Patreon funding in the actual play space.

## Notable Moments

### Campaign One: Bahumia

**The Watchman Incident (Episode 1)**: The very first episode established NADDPOD's tone with the infamous "Watchman" incident, where the party's attempted stealth infiltration went catastrophically wrong, leading to absurd escalation and establishing the show's blend of tactical gameplay and chaotic energy.

**Hardwon's Father (Episode 25-26)**: The confrontation between Hardwon and his adoptive father Gemma became one of the campaign's most emotional moments, with Jake Hurwitz delivering a genuinely moving performance that had both cast and listeners in tears. This two-part arc demonstrated the show's capacity for serious emotional storytelling.

**Beverly's Chosen One Arc**: Throughout the campaign, Beverly's journey from an inexperienced young paladin to a true hero provided consistent character development. His relationship with Erlin and eventual acceptance of his role created multiple memorable moments, including his final sacrifice and resurrection.

**Moonshine and Pawpaw**: Emily Axford's relationship with her possum familiar Pawpaw became iconic, with their bond providing both comedic moments and genuine pathos. Pawpaw's various deaths and resurrections became running gags that somehow never lost their emotional impact.

**Thiala's Redemption**: The complex villain Thiala's arc from antagonist to tragic figure to redeemed ally showcased Brian Murphy's sophisticated approach to villain characterization, creating moral complexity that enriched the narrative.

**The Finale (Episode 100)**: The campaign's conclusion brought together threads from across 100 episodes, delivering satisfying resolution to each character's arc while leaving space for the world to continue existing beyond the story. The emotional epilogue had the entire cast crying, with the moment becoming legendary among fans.

### Campaign Two: Eldermourne

**The Autumn Court Heist**: An extended sequence involving infiltrating the Autumn Court demonstrated the cast's tactical creativity and ability to maintain tension across multiple episodes while incorporating comedy.

**Henry's Revelations**: Jake Hurwitz's portrayal of Henry discovering uncomfortable truths about his past and his family created compelling dramatic tension in a campaign that already leaned darker than Bahumia.

**Zirk's Moral Complexity**: Emily Axford's artificer Zirk operated in moral gray areas more than her previous characters, leading to interesting table dynamics as the party navigated ethically complicated situations.

### Campaign Three: The Sprawl Saga

**Returning to Bahumia**: The first moments of returning to the world of Campaign One created genuine excitement among long-time listeners, with references to previous events and appearances by beloved NPCs generating enthusiasm.

**Calder's Paladin Journey**: Jake Hurwitz's third paladin character brought new dimensions to the class, exploring different aspects of divine service and oaths than Beverly had.

**Callbacks and Continuity**: The campaign's integration of Campaign One lore and characters has created numerous satisfying moments for long-time fans while remaining accessible to new listeners.

### Recurring Moments and Running Gags

**The 2-Crew Chant**: Originally a joke about the character Balnor, the "2-Crew" chant became the show's signature, with fans recording themselves performing it and the cast incorporating it into live shows.

**"Happy Dunk Day"**: A holiday celebrating dunking on people became a beloved recurring element, with fans celebrating "Dunk Day" in real life.

**"Mixed Bag"**: The show's title for short campaigns and one-shots became synonymous with experimental actual play content.

**Emily's Character Builds**: Emily Axford's reputation for creating powerful, mechanically sophisticated characters became a running discussion point, with each new character reveal generating speculation about how she would optimize the build.

## Legacy and Influence

### Impact on Comedy Actual Play

NADDPOD's success in balancing comedy with emotional storytelling directly influenced the development of subsequent comedy actual play shows. [[Dungeons and Daddies]], which launched in 2019, explicitly cited NADDPOD as an inspiration for their approach of combining improvised comedy with genuine emotional stakes. The "comedy actual play" genre, once limited to purely humorous shows, evolved to embrace emotional range largely due to NADDPOD's demonstration that audiences wanted both laughs and tears.

The show proved that comedians could create actual play content that appealed beyond their existing fanbases. While the CollegeHumor connection provided initial audience, NADDPOD's listener base grew to include many people with no prior exposure to the hosts' comedy work, demonstrating that the show succeeded on its actual play merits rather than simply as a vehicle for established performers.

### Influence on Podcast Production Values

NADDPOD set high standards for audio production quality in actual play podcasting. From the beginning, the show featured professional audio editing, music selection, and sound design that enhanced rather than distracted from the storytelling. This commitment to production quality influenced other podcasters to invest in better equipment and editing, raising the overall standard for actual play audio content.

The show's use of music—both original compositions and licensed tracks—to enhance emotional moments and establish tone became a model for other podcasts. Brian Murphy's careful selection of musical cues and willingness to let music play during emotional moments demonstrated how audio-only content could create cinematic experiences.

### Long-Form Campaign Structure

NADDPOD's 100-episode first campaign demonstrated that podcast audiences would stay engaged with actual play content across years of storytelling. While shows like [[Critical Role]] had proven this in video format, NADDPOD showed that audio-only podcasts could sustain listener interest across comparable lengths.

The show's approach to campaign structure—with clear narrative arcs, character development, and satisfying conclusions—provided a template for other podcasters planning long-form actual play campaigns. The balance between episodic content and overarching narrative allowed both casual listeners and deeply invested fans to find entry points and maintain engagement.

### Mechanical Gameplay and Optimization

In an actual play landscape where some shows prioritized narrative flow over mechanical accuracy, NADDPOD's commitment to tactical gameplay and character optimization influenced discussions about how to balance rules and story. The show demonstrated that rigorous adherence to D&D mechanics could enhance drama rather than limit it, with mechanical consequences creating genuine stakes and tactical victories feeling earned.

Emily Axford's sophisticated character builds and combat strategies, in particular, inspired players to explore mechanical optimization as a form of creative expression. Her builds became topics of discussion in D&D communities, with players analyzing her choices and learning optimization techniques from her gameplay.

### Community Models

The Two Crew's organization and engagement provided a model for building actual play communities. The combination of Patreon exclusivity, public social media presence, and live events created multiple tiers of engagement that allowed fans to participate at various levels of investment.

NADDPOD's approach to fan content—actively celebrating and promoting fan art, encouraging community creativity, and maintaining boundaries around respect and inclusivity—influenced how other actual play shows managed their communities.
