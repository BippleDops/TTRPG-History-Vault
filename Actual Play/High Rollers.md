# High Rollers

**Format**: Livestream and VOD actual play
**System**: D&D 5th Edition (primary), Call of Cthulhu, various others
**First Episode**: July 1, 2016
**Cast**: Mark Hulmes (DM), Kim Richards, Chris Trott, Katie Morrison, Rhiannon Gower, Tom Hazell (various campaigns)
**Network**: Independent (formerly associated with Yogscast)
**Status**: Ongoing (Multiple completed campaigns, current campaign ongoing)

## Overview

High Rollers is a British actual play series that began in 2016 as one of the earliest D&D 5th Edition livestreams on Twitch. Created by Dungeon Master Mark Hulmes and emerging from the Yogscast gaming content network, High Rollers has evolved into one of the longest-running and most prolific actual play shows, with hundreds of episodes across multiple campaigns, showcasing the potential for sustained, serialized TTRPG storytelling on streaming platforms.

The show features professional content creators and performers playing D&D in homebrew worlds crafted by Mark Hulmes, a DM whose world-building ambition and narrative complexity have created rich settings spanning multiple campaigns. The core cast has included Kim Richards (consistent across campaigns), Chris Trott (Lightfall and Aerois campaigns), Katie Morrison (Aerois campaign), Rhiannon Gower (Aerois campaign), and Tom Hazell (Lightfall campaign), with different configurations for various campaigns and one-shots.

What distinguishes High Rollers in the actual play landscape is its commitment to long-form campaign storytelling within deeply developed homebrew worlds. The show's two major campaigns—Lightfall and Aerois—have run for hundreds of hours each, developing intricate lore, complex character arcs, and interconnected storylines that reward long-term viewer investment. Mark Hulmes' DMing combines tactical combat, political intrigue, cosmic-scale threats, and intimate character moments, creating campaigns that balance multiple storytelling modes.

High Rollers also represents the evolution of British actual play streaming, helping establish the UK's presence in the Twitch actual play space during the format's formative years. The show's success demonstrated that British creators could build substantial audiences for TTRPG content on streaming platforms, encouraging other UK-based actual play productions and contributing to the global actual play community.

The show's production has evolved from relatively simple early streams to professionally produced episodes with high-quality audio and video, dedicated sets, graphic overlays, and polished presentation. This evolution mirrors the broader professionalization of actual play content, with High Rollers demonstrating how long-running shows can continuously improve production quality while maintaining audience engagement across years of content.

## Production History

### Origins: Yogscast and Early Streaming (2016)

High Rollers emerged from the Yogscast network, a British gaming content creator collective that rose to prominence through Minecraft videos and expanded to various gaming content. Mark Hulmes, known as "Sherlock Hulmes" within the Yogscast community, was an experienced Dungeon Master who had been running games for friends and colleagues for years.

In 2016, as D&D 5th Edition was gaining popularity and [[Critical Role]] was demonstrating the appeal of streamed actual play content, Hulmes proposed creating a D&D livestream for the Yogscast network. The timing was opportune—Twitch was becoming a major platform for gaming content beyond just video game streams, and there was growing interest in TTRPG content among gaming communities.

The initial cast consisted of:
- **Mark Hulmes**: DM, experienced tabletop gamer and content creator
- **Kim Richards**: Yogscast member, relatively new to D&D
- **Chris Trott**: Part of Hat Films (Yogscast creators), experienced with improv comedy
- **Tom Hazell**: Yogscast member, new to D&D

The first campaign, set in the world of Lightfall, launched on July 1, 2016. Early episodes were streamed on Twitch and archived on YouTube, establishing the dual-platform approach that would characterize the show throughout its run.

### Lightfall Campaign (2016-2018)

The Lightfall campaign introduced viewers to Mark Hulmes' homebrew world, a high fantasy setting featuring:
- **Cam Buckland** (Kim Richards): A paladin seeking redemption
- **Elora Asilith** (Katie Morrison, who joined partway through): A ranger with mysterious connections
- **Jiǔtóu** (Chris Trott): A monk with complex backstory
- **Reynard Longstride** (Tom Hazell): A rogue with noble connections

The campaign established several elements that would define High Rollers:

**Homebrew World-Building**: Mark's creation of Lightfall demonstrated ambition beyond adapting published settings, with detailed geography, politics, history, and cosmic mythology.

**Tactical Combat**: The show featured mechanically complex combat encounters that challenged players tactically, distinguishing it from more narrative-focused actual plays.

**Character Development**: Despite tactical focus, the campaign prioritized character growth and interpersonal relationships, balancing combat with roleplay.

**Production Evolution**: Early episodes had simpler production, but quality steadily improved as the show gained audience and resources.

**UK Streaming Presence**: As one of the early British actual play streams, High Rollers helped establish UK presence on Twitch's TTRPG directory.

The Lightfall campaign ran for over 100 episodes across two years, developing complex storylines involving ancient evils, political machinations, and cosmic threats. The campaign concluded in 2018, providing narrative resolution while setting up potential future stories in the world.

### Transition and Aerois Campaign Launch (2018)

Following Lightfall's conclusion, High Rollers underwent significant changes. Tom Hazell departed (though he continued appearing in one-shots and special content), and two new players joined: Katie Morrison (returning after Lightfall guest appearances) and Rhiannon Gower, both experienced performers and TTRPG players.

In September 2018, the Aerois campaign launched, set in an entirely new homebrew world. Aerois represented Mark Hulmes' most ambitious world-building project, featuring:

**Sci-Fantasy Setting**: Unlike Lightfall's traditional fantasy, Aerois blended high fantasy with science fiction elements, including crashed spaceship(s), ancient technology, and cosmic entities.

**Nova Aeterna** (Kim Richards): A guardian construct seeking identity and purpose, providing sci-fi character archetype in fantasy setting.

**Quilliver "Quill" Herendost** (Rhiannon Gower): A high elf from a floating city with mysterious powers.

**Aila** (Katie Morrison): A guardian of ancient forests with deep connections to the world's nature.

**Lucius Virion-Elluin Elenasto** (Chris Trott): A high elf sorcerer from noble family, providing political connections and comic relief.

The new campaign demonstrated High Rollers' creative evolution, with more sophisticated production, deeper lore, and bolder genre-blending than Lightfall. The sci-fantasy elements allowed exploration of themes and scenarios impossible in purely medieval fantasy settings.

### Independence and Growth (2019-2021)

In 2019, High Rollers became an independent production, separating from the Yogscast network while maintaining friendly relationships. This independence gave the show greater creative control and direct relationship with its audience, though it also meant taking on all production responsibilities and costs.

The transition to independence was supported by a strong Patreon community that had developed around the show. Patreon subscribers received bonus content, including:
- Extended episodes with additional roleplay
- Behind-the-scenes content and DM prep materials
- One-shots featuring different systems and settings
- Community interaction and input opportunities

During this period, the Aerois campaign deepened considerably:

**Complex Mythology**: The world's backstory involving the crashed ship Bastion and its crew became central to the plot, revealing layers of ancient history.

**Political Intrigue**: The campaign explored political conflicts between nations and factions, adding complexity beyond monster-fighting.

**Cosmic Horror**: Influences from cosmic horror genre appeared, with ancient entities threatening reality itself.

**Character Arcs**: Each character developed extensive personal storylines interweaving with the main plot.

**Guest Appearances**: Various special guests appeared, including other UK TTRPG creators and performers, building community connections.

### Production Evolution and Professionalization (2020-Present)

High Rollers continued professionalizing production:

**Dedicated Studio**: Investment in professional studio space with purpose-built sets, lighting, and audio equipment.

**Graphic Design**: Custom graphics, animated overlays, and visual elements enhanced stream presentation.

**Professional Editing**: YouTube VODs received professional editing with chapters, highlights, and improved audio mixing.

**Merchandise**: Official High Rollers merchandise featuring campaign characters and iconography.

**Live Shows**: In-person live shows at UK conventions and special events, building community and demonstrating popularity.

**Cross-Platform Presence**: Maintained strong presence on Twitch (live streams), YouTube (VODs), and podcast platforms (audio versions).

The COVID-19 pandemic in 2020-2021 temporarily disrupted production, requiring remote streaming solutions. The show adapted successfully, maintaining content output while physically separated, demonstrating actual play's resilience and adaptability.

### Recent Developments (2022-Present)

The Aerois campaign concluded in 2022 after over 160 episodes, providing emotionally satisfying conclusions to character arcs and resolving the cosmic-scale threats threatening the world. The finale demonstrated High Rollers' commitment to narrative payoff, bringing together threads established years earlier.

Following Aerois, the show has continued with new campaigns and experiments:

**Altheya: The Dragon Empire**: A new campaign set in another homebrew world, demonstrating Mark's continued world-building ambition.

**System Diversity**: Increased experimentation with non-D&D systems, including Call of Cthulhu, Blades in the Dark, and others.

**Rotating Formats**: Mix of ongoing campaigns and shorter arcs, providing variety while maintaining serialized storytelling.

**Community Building**: Continued investment in community through Discord, social media, and live events.

## Cultural Impact

### UK Actual Play Streaming Pioneer

High Rollers was among the earliest British actual play streams on Twitch, helping establish the UK's presence in the actual play streaming space. When the show launched in 2016, actual play streaming was still emerging as a content category, with [[Critical Role]] having pioneered the format but few other sustained productions existing.

As a prominent UK show starting early in the streaming era, High Rollers:

- Demonstrated British creators could build audiences for TTRPG streaming
- Helped establish European time zone streaming for actual play content
- Created pathway for other UK actual play creators
- Connected UK TTRPG communities to broader actual play culture
- Showed that actual play could succeed outside American dominance of the space

The show's longevity and sustained audience proved that UK actual play wasn't a novelty but could maintain long-term viability, encouraging investment in British TTRPG content creation.

### Long-Form Campaign Commitment

With over 300 episodes across multiple campaigns, High Rollers demonstrates the potential for truly long-form actual play storytelling. While shows like [[Critical Role]] have proven similar lengths possible, High Rollers' achievement is notable for an independent production without major corporate backing.

The show proved that audiences would remain engaged with campaigns spanning hundreds of hours across multiple years, that character development could sustain interest across lengthy narratives, and that DMs could maintain creative energy and quality across extremely long campaigns.

This commitment to long-form storytelling influenced expectations around campaign length and demonstrated that actual play could compete with long-running television series for sustained narrative engagement.

### Homebrew World-Building Standards

Mark Hulmes' elaborate homebrew worlds—particularly Aerois with its sci-fantasy elements and deep mythology—demonstrated the potential for original world creation in actual play. Rather than using published D&D settings, Hulmes created entirely original worlds with detailed histories, pantheons, geography, and cosmic mythology.

This approach:

- Showed that homebrew worlds could be as compelling as published settings
- Demonstrated DM creativity in world-building
- Inspired other DMs to develop original settings
- Proved that audiences would invest in learning new worlds rather than requiring familiar settings
- Elevated world-building as a valued aspect of actual play DMing

The Aerois setting in particular, with its science fantasy elements, demonstrated that D&D 5E could support genre-blending beyond traditional medieval fantasy, influencing how other shows approached setting design.

### Tactical Combat in Narrative Actual Play

High Rollers balanced tactical combat complexity with narrative storytelling, demonstrating these elements weren't mutually exclusive. While some actual play shows simplified or handwaved combat for narrative flow, High Rollers embraced complex encounters requiring tactical thinking and mechanical optimization.

This approach:

- Appealed to audiences interested in D&D's tactical gameplay
- Demonstrated that combat could advance character development and story
- Showed tactical play and roleplaying could coexist productively
- Influenced other shows to take combat more seriously as storytelling tool
- Validated audiences who enjoyed mechanical complexity alongside narrative

The show proved that actual play could satisfy both optimization-focused players and story-focused audiences simultaneously.

### Independent Production Model

High Rollers' transition to independence and sustainable operation demonstrated the viability of independent actual play production supported by community funding. The show's success with Patreon-based funding while maintaining free content showed that actual play creators could build sustainable careers without corporate backing.

This model influenced other actual play shows to pursue independence, contributing to the diversification of actual play content beyond network-backed productions.

## Legacy and Influence

### UK Actual Play Scene Development

High Rollers' success helped establish and grow the UK actual play scene, demonstrating that British creators could compete internationally in TTRPG content creation. The show:

- Inspired other UK actual play productions
- Created networks within UK TTRPG community
- Supported UK gaming conventions and events
- Demonstrated viability of UK-based actual play careers
- Connected UK creators to international actual play community

### Streaming Format Development

As an early actual play stream, High Rollers contributed to developing streaming format conventions:

- Weekly streaming schedules
- Integration of chat interaction
- Balance between live and VOD audiences
- Technical production standards for TTRPG streaming
- Platform-specific content strategies (Twitch vs YouTube)

These developments influenced how subsequent actual play streams approached production and platform utilization.

### Homebrew Campaign Templates

Mark Hulmes' approach to homebrew campaign creation—particularly the sci-fantasy elements of Aerois—provided templates for other DMs creating original settings. The successful integration of science fiction elements into D&D fantasy demonstrated genre-blending possibilities that influenced other campaigns and published content.

### Long-Form Storytelling Techniques

High Rollers' sustained campaigns demonstrated techniques for maintaining narrative engagement across hundreds of episodes:

- Interweaving character arcs with world-level plots
- Gradual revelation of setting mysteries
- Balancing episodic adventures with serialized storytelling
- Managing power scaling across long level progressions
- Maintaining tonal variety while preserving thematic coherence

These techniques influenced how other DMs approached long-form campaign planning and execution.

### Independent Creator Sustainability

The show's successful transition to independence while maintaining production quality and audience demonstrated that actual play creators could build sustainable careers through community support. This success story encouraged other creators to pursue actual play professionally.
