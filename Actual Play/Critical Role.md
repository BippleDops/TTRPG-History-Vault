# Critical Role

**Format**: Livestream actual play
**System**: D&D 5th Edition (primarily)
**First Episode**: March 12, 2015
**Cast**: Matthew Mercer (DM), Laura Bailey, Travis Willingham, Liam O'Brien, Sam Riegel, Taliesin Jaffe, Marisha Ray, Ashley Johnson
**Platform**: Twitch, YouTube, Podcast
**Status**: Ongoing (Campaign 3 as of 2024)

## Overview

Critical Role is a live-streamed actual play web series where professional voice actors play Dungeons & Dragons. Beginning in 2015, the show has become the most influential actual play series in TTRPG history, transforming from a home game into a multimedia entertainment empire and fundamentally changing how the broader public perceives tabletop roleplaying games.

The show features Dungeon Master Matthew Mercer guiding a cast of seven fellow voice actors through epic fantasy campaigns set in his homebrew world of Exandria. Known for its combination of dramatic storytelling, character-driven narratives, professional production values, and genuine emotional moments, Critical Role has attracted millions of viewers and helped spark the "actual play revolution" of the late 2010s and early 2020s.

Over the course of three campaigns (and counting), Critical Role has evolved from a simple stream of friends playing D&D into a major media company, producing animated series, publishing sourcebooks, launching a charitable foundation, and inspiring a generation of new players to try tabletop roleplaying games.

## History and Development

### Origins: The Home Game (2013-2015)

Critical Role began as a private birthday present. In 2013, voice actor Liam O'Brien asked Matthew Mercer to run a one-shot D&D game for his birthday, inviting several of their fellow voice actor friends. The game used Pathfinder initially, and the chemistry was so strong that the group continued meeting regularly to continue the campaign.

For nearly two years, this remained a private home game among friends in the voice acting community. The players developed deep connections with their characters, and Mercer created an expansive homebrew world called Exandria as the campaign setting. The original campaign followed a group called Vox Machina through adventures in the continent of Tal'Dorei.

### Going Public (2015)

In early 2015, Felicia Day's Geek & Sundry network approached the group about streaming their game. The cast agreed, but decided to switch from Pathfinder to D&D 5th Edition (which had been released in 2014) for better accessibility to viewers unfamiliar with RPGs.

On March 12, 2015, Critical Role premiered on Geek & Sundry's Twitch channel. The first episode began in medias res, with the party already established and at level 9, having completed about two years of adventures off-camera. This created an immediate sense of history and depth to the characters and world.

### Early Growth (2015-2017)

The show found its audience quickly. While initial viewership was modest, word-of-mouth spread rapidly through the D&D community and beyond. Viewers were drawn to the combination of:

- **Professional voice acting talent** bringing characters to life
- **Matthew Mercer's DM style** balancing rules, storytelling, and player agency
- **Genuine emotional investment** from players in their characters
- **High production values** (improving over time) including set design and audio quality
- **Accessibility** of four-hour weekly episodes with accompanying podcasts

By mid-2016, Critical Role episodes were regularly pulling tens of thousands of concurrent viewers on Twitch. The show became Geek & Sundry's flagship content and a significant driver of the network's growth.

### Campaign 1 Conclusion and Expansion (2017-2018)

Campaign 1 (Vox Machina) concluded in October 2017 after 115 episodes spanning 373 hours of gameplay. The finale was broadcast live from a theater in Los Angeles to a sold-out crowd. The emotional conclusion brought both cast and audience to tears, demonstrating the deep connection viewers had formed with these characters.

In 2018, Critical Role launched a Kickstarter campaign to fund an animated special based on one of Vox Machina's early adventures. The campaign asked for $750,000. It raised over $11.3 million, becoming the most-funded film/video project in Kickstarter history at the time and demonstrating the show's massive, passionate fanbase.

### Independence and Campaign 2 (2018-2021)

In early 2019, Critical Role announced they were separating from Geek & Sundry to become an independent company. This gave them full creative control and ownership of their content and characters.

Campaign 2 (The Mighty Nein) ran from January 2018 to June 2021, totaling 141 episodes. This campaign explored darker themes and featured characters with more morally complex backgrounds. The Mighty Nein's adventures took them across different regions of Exandria, expanding the world's lore significantly.

Campaign 2 also saw the launch of the Legend of Vox Machina animated series on Amazon Prime Video in 2022, fulfilling the Kickstarter promise and introducing Critical Role to mainstream audiences who had never engaged with actual play content.

### Campaign 3 and Multimedia Expansion (2021-Present)

Campaign 3 began in October 2021 and continues as of 2024. Set in the continent of Marquet, it has further expanded Exandria's lore while telling another epic story.

The Critical Role brand has expanded far beyond the main campaign:
- **The Legend of Vox Machina** animated series (2 seasons, renewed for Season 3)
- **The Mighty Nein** animated series announced
- **Exandria Unlimited** one-shot and limited series with different DMs and players
- **4-Sided Dive** talk show discussing campaigns
- **Critical Role Foundation** charitable organization
- **Darrington Press** tabletop game publishing imprint
- **Beacon** subscription service with exclusive content
- **Numerous published sourcebooks** for D&D 5E through partnership with Wizards of the Coast

## Format and Style

### Show Structure

Each Critical Role episode typically runs 3-4 hours and follows a consistent structure:

- **Pre-show countdown and announcements** (15-30 minutes)
- **Introduction and recap** by Matthew Mercer (5-10 minutes)
- **First half** of gameplay (90-120 minutes)
- **Mid-episode break** with announcements and ads (10-15 minutes)
- **Second half** of gameplay (90-120 minutes)
- **"Is it Thursday yet?"** closing catchphrase

The show streams live on Thursdays at 7pm Pacific Time on Twitch and YouTube, with episodes becoming available as podcasts shortly after.

### DM Style

Matthew Mercer's DM style has become highly influential, sometimes controversially so (see "Cultural Impact" section). His approach includes:

- **Cinematic descriptions** using his voice acting skills
- **Consistent world-building** with detailed lore and recurring NPCs
- **Rule of cool** balanced with mechanical consistency
- **Player agency** emphasized over predetermined story outcomes
- **Emotional investment** in NPC characters and story beats
- **Homebrew content** creating unique magic items, spells, and mechanics for Exandria
- **Musical cues** and ambient sound to enhance atmosphere

### Player Dynamics

The cast's friendship and collaboration creates a unique dynamic:

- **In-character dialogue** dominates over meta-gaming discussion
- **Support for each other's storytelling** with players setting up dramatic moments
- **Comfort with failure** embracing poor rolls as story opportunities
- **Emotional vulnerability** allowing characters to be flawed and make mistakes
- **Collaborative storytelling** players adding to world-building and lore

## Cultural Impact

### The "Mercer Effect"

Critical Role's success has had both positive and potentially negative impacts on the TTRPG community. The "Mercer Effect" refers to unrealistic expectations some players develop about what home games should be like, comparing their DMs to Matthew Mercer's professional-level performance and production values.

While Mercer himself and the Critical Role team have consistently emphasized that "your game is the best game" and encouraged people to play in whatever way brings them joy, some DMs report feeling pressure to match Critical Role's style and production quality. The community has largely worked to counteract this, reminding players that most DMs aren't professional voice actors with extensive prep time and production support.

### Mainstream Breakthrough

Critical Role is widely credited as a major factor in D&D's mainstream breakthrough in the late 2010s and early 2020s. The show:

- **Introduced millions** to tabletop RPGs who had never played
- **Demonstrated TTRPG storytelling** to skeptical non-players
- **Normalized emotional investment** in fictional characters and stories
- **Created a viewing community** around actual play content
- **Provided accessible entry point** for learning D&D rules and gameplay

The show's influence extends beyond existing TTRPG fans into broader entertainment:
- Mainstream media coverage in Variety, Hollywood Reporter, etc.
- Cast appearances on late-night talk shows
- Amazon Prime Video animated series reaching non-TTRPG audiences
- Academic papers analyzing the show's narrative structure and community impact

### Economic Impact

Critical Role demonstrated that actual play content could be economically viable:

- **Direct revenue** through subscriptions, merchandise, and ad revenue
- **Publishing deals** leading to official D&D sourcebooks (Explorer's Guide to Wildemount, Tal'Dorei Campaign Setting Reborn)
- **Media expansion** with animated series, comic books, and novels
- **Industry investment** in other actual play productions trying to replicate success
- **Local business** boost to game stores as new players sought materials to start playing

The show's economic success inspired a wave of actual play productions, from high-production streaming shows to intimate podcasts, creating an entire industry of professional actual play performers.

### Community and Fandom

The Critical Role community, known as "Critters," has become one of the most active and passionate fandoms in gaming:

- **Fan art** shared weekly with thousands of submissions
- **Cosplay** of Exandrian characters at conventions
- **Fan fiction** expanding Exandria's stories
- **Charity work** through the Critical Role Foundation raising millions
- **Community events** including watch parties and conventions
- **Content creation** with recap blogs, podcasts, and analysis videos

The community's positivity and inclusion (albeit not without occasional controversies) has helped shape the broader TTRPG community's culture toward welcoming new players and celebrating diverse storytelling.

## Notable Campaigns and Moments

### Campaign 1: Vox Machina (2015-2017)

The first campaign followed Vox Machina, a group of adventurers who evolved from mercenaries to heroes saving the world. Key arcs included:

- **The Briarwood Arc**: Revenge quest that became foundation for animated series
- **The Chroma Conclave**: Ancient dragons threatening Exandria
- **Vecna Arc**: Final battle against a near-godlike lich

Memorable moments:
- Scanlan's "I have an idea" leading to creative problem-solving
- Vax's bargain with the Raven Queen
- Scanlan's Wish and departure from the party
- The final battle with Vecna
- Keyleth's goldfish incident

### Campaign 2: The Mighty Nein (2018-2021)

A darker, more morally complex campaign following a group of outcasts who found family in each other. Major arcs included:

- **Bowlgate**: Nott's traumatic backstory reveal
- **The Dynasty and Bright Queen**: Political intrigue with the Kryn Dynasty
- **The Somnovem**: Cosmic horror and city-mind entities
- **The Tomb Takers**: Confronting Mollymauk's mysterious past
- **Peace Negotiations**: Attempting to end a continental war

Memorable moments:
- Mollymauk's death and its aftermath
- Fjord breaking his pact with Uk'otoa
- Caleb's confrontation with Trent Ikithon
- Jester's cupcake prank on the Traveler
- "We're the Mighty Nein!"

### Campaign 3: Bells Hells (2021-Present)

The current campaign exploring Exandria's moon Ruidus and its mysterious influence. Ongoing as of 2024, it has featured:

- **Ruidus revelations**: Exploring the red moon's significance
- **Laudna's death and resurrection**: Emotional zenith for the cast
- **Ashton's transformation**: Dangerous power absorption
- **Political conspiracy**: Uncovering plots in Marquet

## Influence on Game Design and Publishing

### Official D&D Content

Critical Role's success led to official D&D collaborations:

- **Explorer's Guide to Wildemount** (2020): Official D&D sourcebook for Campaign 2's setting
- **Tal'Dorei Campaign Setting Reborn** (2022): Revised and expanded Campaign 1 setting guide
- **Blood Hunter class**: Matthew Mercer's homebrew class officially published
- **Weapon and spell options**: Various homebrew mechanics published officially

These books demonstrated that third-party settings could succeed within D&D's official product line, opening doors for other creators.

### Actual Play as Product

Critical Role proved actual play content could be a viable business:

- **Monetization models**: Subscriptions, live shows, merchandise
- **Professional production**: Investment in quality audio, video, and set design pays off
- **Long-form storytelling**: Audiences will invest in hundreds of hours of content
- **Community building**: Engaged fanbase supports multiple revenue streams

This led to an explosion of actual play content across platforms, from high-production shows to intimate podcasts, creating new career paths for TTRPG performers and creators.

## Controversies and Criticisms

### Accessibility and Representation

While praised for inclusivity, Critical Role has faced criticism regarding:

- **Disability representation**: Handling of character disabilities and recovery
- **Cultural sensitivity**: Portrayal of fantasy cultures and real-world parallels
- **Gender dynamics**: Treatment of female characters and player experiences
- **Class and economics**: Wealthy voice actors playing working-class characters

The cast has generally responded to criticism by acknowledging mistakes, learning, and trying to do better. They've added content warnings, consulted sensitivity readers, and publicly addressed controversies.

### The Mercer Effect

As mentioned above, the show's success has created unrealistic expectations for some players and DMs, leading to frustration in home games that can't match Critical Role's production values or performance quality.

### Commercialization Concerns

Some long-time fans have expressed concern about the show's evolution from a home game into a commercial enterprise, worrying that business considerations might affect storytelling decisions or authentic emotional moments.

## Legacy and Ongoing Influence

### Transforming the Actual Play Landscape

Critical Role demonstrated that actual play content could:
- Reach mainstream audiences beyond existing TTRPG fans
- Support professional careers for performers
- Generate substantial revenue through multiple channels
- Create lasting intellectual property through characters and settings
- Build passionate, engaged communities around shared stories

### Educational Impact

The show has become an educational resource:
- **Teaching D&D**: New players learn rules by watching
- **DM techniques**: Aspiring DMs study Mercer's methods
- **Storytelling structure**: Writing and media scholars analyze narrative techniques
- **Community building**: Groups model positive table dynamics on the cast's interactions

### Industry Transformation

Critical Role's success contributed to:
- **D&D's renaissance**: Record sales for D&D 5E
- **Streaming ecosystem**: Proliferation of actual play content
- **Publisher strategies**: Increased focus on digital content and community engagement
- **Voice actor careers**: TTRPG actual play becoming a viable career path
- **Animation opportunities**: Proving adult-animated fantasy can succeed

## Production Evolution

### Technical Quality

The show's production values have improved dramatically:

**2015-2016**: Basic setup with single camera, minimal lighting, simple audio
**2017-2018**: Multi-camera setup, improved lighting, professional soundproofing
**2019-2020**: Custom-built set, animated overlays, dynamic lighting
**2021-Present**: Fully professional studio, multiple sets, advanced graphics and effects

### COVID-19 Adaptation

Campaign 2's conclusion and Campaign 3's early episodes were affected by COVID-19:
- Remote filming with cast in individual studios
- Creative solutions for maintaining energy and interaction
- Eventual return to in-person play with safety protocols
- Demonstrated adaptability of actual play format
