# Relics and Rarities

**Format**: Professionally produced web series actual play
**System**: D&D 5th Edition
**First Episode**: March 22, 2018
**Cast**: Deborah Ann Woll (DM), Xander Jeanneret, Omar Najam, Erika Ishii, with guest appearances including Matthew Mercer
**Network**: Geek & Sundry
**Status**: Completed (12 episodes, 2018)

## Overview

Relics and Rarities was a high-production actual play web series that aired on Geek & Sundry in 2018, notable for featuring actress Deborah Ann Woll (True Blood, Daredevil) as Dungeon Master. The show distinguished itself through its unique premise—adventurers running an antiquities shop who investigate the histories and dangers of magical artifacts—and its emphasis on mystery-solving and investigation over traditional dungeon-crawling combat.

The series followed a party of artifact collectors and investigators: Raes an Welyra (Xander Jeanneret), a knowledge-seeking wizard; Halis Derev (Omar Najam), a half-orc detective; and Taryin Azal-Khürt (Erika Ishii), a noble-born archaeologist. Together they operated "Avalon" an antiquities shop serving as home base while they pursued mysterious magical items, uncovering dangerous histories and confronting threats tied to ancient artifacts.

What set Relics and Rarities apart was Deborah Ann Woll's sophisticated DMing style, which emphasized mystery, investigation, and historical detective work. As a professional actress with extensive experience in dramatic performance, Woll brought particular attention to NPC characterization, atmospheric description, and emotional storytelling. Her approach to DMing demonstrated how skills from professional acting could enhance TTRPG performance and presentation.

The show's production values were exceptionally high, even by Geek & Sundry standards. The custom-built set resembling an antique shop, professional lighting and cinematography, elaborate props including physical representations of magical artifacts, and cinematic editing created a polished viewing experience that emphasized the show's premium production approach. This attention to production detail set standards for what high-budget actual play content could achieve visually and atmospherically.

Despite lasting only twelve episodes—one complete story arc—Relics and Rarities made significant cultural impact. The show demonstrated the appeal of mystery-focused actual play campaigns, highlighted the potential for professional actors to transition into DMing, and provided important representation of women as Dungeon Masters in high-profile actual play content. Woll's success as a DM in such a visible role influenced perceptions of who could DM and what DMing styles could look like, contributing to diversification of actual play content.

## Production History

### Development and Premise (2017-2018)

Relics and Rarities emerged during Geek & Sundry's expansion of actual play content following the success of [[Critical Role]]. The network was actively developing new TTRPG shows with different premises and casts, seeking to diversify their actual play offerings beyond their flagship show.

Deborah Ann Woll had become known in TTRPG communities for her passionate advocacy of D&D and skilled DMing. She had appeared on various actual play shows and spoken publicly about how D&D helped her develop acting skills and emotional expression. Her enthusiasm for the game and obvious competence as a DM made her an attractive candidate for leading an actual play series.

The premise of Relics and Rarities was developed to differentiate the show from other actual plays while suiting Woll's DMing strengths. Rather than traditional adventuring parties seeking treasure, the show would focus on:

**Antiquarian Fantasy**: Characters interested in historical artifacts and their stories rather than pure treasure-seeking
**Mystery and Investigation**: Emphasis on detective work, research, and uncovering secrets over combat encounters
**Urban Setting**: Primarily city-based adventures rather than wilderness exploration
**Episodic Structure**: Self-contained mysteries that could be resolved within single episodes or short arcs
**Artifact Focus**: Each episode centered on a specific magical item with unique history and dangers

This premise allowed for:
- Showcasing different D&D play styles beyond dungeon-crawling
- Highlighting investigation and social interaction mechanics
- Creating opportunities for historical storytelling and lore-building
- Producing episodic content more accessible to new viewers than serialized campaigns
- Demonstrating D&D's versatility as framework for various genres

The cast was carefully selected to complement this vision:

**Xander Jeanneret**: Experienced TTRPG player with strong character work
**Omar Najam**: Actor and improviser bringing strong performance skills
**Erika Ishii**: Multi-talented performer with extensive actual play experience and strong presence
**Guest Stars**: Various episodes featured special guests, including [[Critical Role]]'s Matthew Mercer, creating crossover appeal

### Production and Visual Design (2018)

Relics and Rarities received significant production investment from Geek & Sundry, resulting in one of the most visually distinctive actual play shows:

**Custom Set Design**: The set was designed to resemble an authentic antique shop, with shelves of curiosities, vintage furniture, atmospheric lighting, and detailed decoration creating immersive environment. The set itself became a character in the show, reinforcing the antiquarian theme.

**Physical Props**: Unlike most actual play shows that rely on theater of the mind or battle maps, Relics and Rarities featured elaborate physical props representing the magical artifacts central to each episode. These tangible items enhanced storytelling and provided visual interest for viewers.

**Cinematography**: Professional camera work with multiple angles, dramatic lighting, and cinematic composition created viewing experience closer to television drama than typical actual play streams.

**Editing and Post-Production**: Extensive editing added flashbacks, visual effects, atmospheric sound design, and narrative structure that enhanced storytelling beyond raw gameplay footage.

**Music and Sound**: Original music and carefully designed soundscapes created atmospheric depth supporting the mystery and historical themes.

This production approach positioned Relics and Rarities as premium content, demonstrating what actual play could achieve with television-level production resources. The visual polish made the show accessible to audiences who might be put off by lower-production actual play content, while the production values themselves became part of the show's appeal.

### Broadcast and Reception (2018)

Relics and Rarities premiered on Geek & Sundry's Twitch channel and YouTube in March 2018. The show aired weekly, with episodes running approximately 45-60 minutes—shorter than many actual play shows, making content more digestible for casual viewers.

Initial reception was enthusiastic, with audiences responding positively to:

- Deborah Ann Woll's DMing, particularly her NPC voices and emotional storytelling
- The unique antiquarian premise and mystery focus
- High production values and visual presentation
- Strong chemistry among cast members
- Accessible episode length and episodic structure

The show attracted both existing actual play fans and viewers drawn by Deborah Ann Woll's celebrity or the high production values. Discussions in TTRPG communities highlighted Woll's DMing as evidence that professional actors could bring unique strengths to TTRPG performance beyond player roles.

### Conclusion and Legacy (2018)

Relics and Rarities concluded after twelve episodes, completing one story arc. The finale brought together threads from throughout the season, resolving the overarching mystery while providing character closure.

The show's conclusion was planned—it was conceived as a limited series rather than ongoing campaign—but many fans expressed desire for continuation or future seasons. However, Geek & Sundry's changing business priorities and eventual decline meant no second season was produced.

Despite its limited run, the show achieved its goals:
- Showcasing Deborah Ann Woll's DMing talents
- Demonstrating alternative actual play formats and premises
- Setting production standards for premium actual play content
- Providing representation of female DMs in high-profile actual play

The show's influence extended beyond its viewership, with Woll's subsequent DMing appearances and advocacy continuing to impact TTRPG culture and actual play production.

## Cultural Impact

### Female DM Representation

Relics and Rarities' most significant cultural contribution was providing high-profile representation of a woman as Dungeon Master. In 2018, the actual play landscape was dominated by male DMs, particularly in the most visible shows. While women played prominently in shows like [[Critical Role]], female DMs were less common in high-profile productions.

Deborah Ann Woll's role as DM in a professionally produced, heavily promoted show demonstrated:

- Women could successfully DM in high-pressure, high-visibility contexts
- Female DMs brought unique perspectives and styles to TTRPG storytelling
- Professional actresses could leverage acting skills in DMing
- Audiences would embrace shows led by female DMs

Woll's success helped normalize female DMs in actual play content and inspired more women to pursue DMing, both in home games and actual play productions. The show provided a visible counter-example to lingering assumptions that DMing was predominantly male domain.

The representation was particularly important because Woll was the clear creative lead and authority figure in the show, not a guest or co-DM. Her central role demonstrated women's capacity for narrative control and creative leadership in TTRPG spaces.

### Mystery and Investigation in Actual Play

Relics and Rarities demonstrated the potential for mystery-focused actual play campaigns, showing that D&D could effectively support detective work and investigation rather than primarily combat and exploration.

The show's approach to mystery influenced subsequent actual play shows to:
- Incorporate more investigation-focused storylines
- Develop mystery structure and clue delivery in TTRPG contexts
- Balance combat with social interaction and puzzle-solving
- Use magical items as narrative devices rather than just mechanical bonuses

The antiquarian premise particularly showcased how thematic focus could create distinctive actual play content, inspiring other shows to develop unique conceptual frameworks rather than following traditional adventuring party formulas.

### Professional Actor DMing

Deborah Ann Woll's transition from player to DM in a high-profile show demonstrated how professional actors' skills translated to DMing. Her approach highlighted:

**Character Voice and Performance**: Woll's actor training enabled distinctive, fully-realized NPC voices and performances, creating memorable characters that enhanced emotional engagement.

**Emotional Storytelling**: Professional acting experience informed Woll's attention to emotional beats, character motivation, and dramatic pacing, bringing narrative sophistication to campaign structure.

**Improvisation**: Acting and improv training supported responsive, dynamic DMing that adapted to player choices while maintaining narrative coherence.

**Atmospheric Description**: Performance skills enhanced Woll's descriptive narration, creating vivid imagery and emotional atmosphere through verbal storytelling.

This demonstration influenced perceptions of DMing as performance art and encouraged other actors to explore DMing. The show proved that skills valuable in traditional acting directly enhanced TTRPG performance, creating crossover between acting and TTRPG communities.

### Episodic Actual Play Structure

Relics and Rarities' episodic structure—with mysteries largely resolved within single episodes or short arcs—demonstrated alternatives to serialized campaign formats dominating actual play. This approach:

- Made content more accessible to new viewers who could start at any episode
- Allowed for varied stories and tones across episodes
- Created satisfying narrative closure regularly rather than requiring long-term investment
- Showcased different types of TTRPG gameplay across episodes

The episodic model influenced discussions about actual play structure and accessibility, with some shows incorporating more episodic elements to balance serialization with accessibility.

## Notable Moments

### Key Episodes and Mysteries

**"The Memory Stone" (Episode 1)**: The premiere established the antiquarian premise and introduced the party dynamic, with a mystery involving a magical gem containing someone's memories. Woll's DMing immediately showcased her atmospheric description and emotional storytelling.

**"The Basilisk Brooch"**: An investigation involving a cursed jewelry piece demonstrated the show's approach to magical items as narrative devices with dangerous histories rather than simple treasures.

**"The Singing Blade"**: Featured musical elements and demonstrated how magical items could have personalities and desires, creating moral complexity around ownership and use.

**"Guest Star Episodes"**: Matthew Mercer's guest appearance created crossover appeal with [[Critical Role]] fans while showcasing collaborative storytelling between DMs (Mercer as player under Woll's DMing).

**Season Finale**: The final episodes brought together clues and mysteries from throughout the season, revealing larger conspiracy and providing character closure.

### Character Moments

**Raes's Knowledge Obsession**: Xander Jeanneret's portrayal of a wizard driven by insatiable curiosity created both comedic moments and genuine character depth.

**Halis's Detective Work**: Omar Najam's half-orc detective brought noir sensibility to fantasy setting, with investigation skills driving many mysteries.

**Taryin's Noble Background**: Erika Ishii's character navigation of noble expectations while pursuing archaeological passion added social complexity to adventures.

**Party Chemistry**: The found-family dynamic among the antique shop operators created emotional investment beyond individual mysteries.

### Production Highlights

**Set Atmosphere**: The antique shop set created immediate visual identity and atmospheric immersion that enhanced storytelling.

**Artifact Props**: Physical representations of magical items added tangibility and visual interest, making abstract game mechanics concrete.

**Flashback Sequences**: Edited sequences showing artifacts' histories provided cinematic storytelling that enhanced mysteries.

**NPC Performances**: Woll's various NPC characterizations, from mysterious clients to dangerous antagonists, showcased her acting range and created memorable encounters.

## Legacy and Influence

### Inspiring Female DMs

Relics and Rarities' most lasting impact is its inspiration to women who DM or aspire to DM. Deborah Ann Woll's high-profile DMing demonstrated that women could successfully lead actual play shows, encouraging:

- More women to pursue DMing in home games
- Female DMs to seek visibility through streaming and actual play content
- Production companies to feature female DMs in actual play shows
- Broader recognition of diverse DMing styles and perspectives

Woll's continued DMing in various contexts (including her "Relics and Rarities" style one-shots and appearances on other shows) has maintained this influence, with her becoming one of the most recognized female DMs in actual play.

### Mystery Campaign Templates

The show provided templates for mystery-focused TTRPG campaigns:

- Structuring investigations with clues and revelations
- Balancing player agency with mystery pacing
- Using magical items as mystery catalysts
- Creating recurring investigation structures (the antique shop framework)
- Mixing episodic and serialized mystery elements

These templates influenced DMs creating mystery campaigns and demonstrated D&D's capacity for detective fiction beyond traditional fantasy adventure.

### Production Ambition

While few actual play shows could match Relics and Rarities' production budget, the show demonstrated what was possible with investment in:

- Set design creating thematic environments
- Props enhancing tangibility and visual interest
- Professional cinematography and editing
- Atmospheric sound and music design

This demonstration influenced ambitions for actual play production, even if resource limitations prevented exact replication. The show proved actual play could be premium content worthy of significant production investment.

The show's twelve-episode format demonstrated that actual play didn't require hundreds of episodes to tell satisfying stories. This influenced:

- Limited series actual play productions
- Seasonal structures with defined endpoints
- Episodic actual play with self-contained stories

The success of a limited, focused actual play series provided alternative models to endless campaigns, showing that completed stories had value even without indefinite continuation.

### Actor-to-DM Pipeline

Woll's successful transition from actual play player to DM in a high-profile show demonstrated a career path for actors in actual play:

This influenced how actors engaged with actual play, with more performers exploring DMing as creative outlet and performance opportunity.
