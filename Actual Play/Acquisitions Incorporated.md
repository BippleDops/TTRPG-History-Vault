# Acquisitions Incorporated

**Format**: Podcast, live shows, web series
**System**: D&D 4th Edition (early), D&D 5th Edition (later)
**First Episode**: 2008 (podcast format)
**Cast**: Chris Perkins (DM), Jerry Holkins, Mike Krahulik, Scott Kurtz, with various guests including Patrick Rothfuss, Morgan Webb, Wil Wheaton, and others
**Network**: Penny Arcade (in partnership with Wizards of the Coast)
**Status**: Ongoing (irregular schedule, primarily live shows)

## Overview

Acquisitions Incorporated (often abbreviated "Acq Inc") is a pioneering actual play series that began in 2008 as a podcast and evolved into one of the most influential actual play franchises in TTRPG history. Created by Penny Arcade webcomic creators Jerry Holkins and Mike Krahulik in collaboration with Wizards of the Coast's Chris Perkins, the show follows the misadventures of a fantasy adventuring company that operates as a corporate business, blending traditional D&D dungeon-crawling with satirical corporate culture.

The premise positions adventuring as a business venture, with the player characters working for Acquisitions Incorporated—a company that takes on dangerous quests for profit while navigating corporate hierarchies, franchise opportunities, and business politics alongside traditional fantasy threats. This corporate overlay on D&D adventure created unique comedy opportunities while satirizing both fantasy tropes and modern business culture.

What makes Acquisitions Incorporated historically significant is its pioneering role in establishing actual play as viable entertainment. Beginning in 2008—before the concept of "actual play" had entered common usage and years before the actual play boom of the mid-2010s—Acq Inc demonstrated that people would listen to and watch others play D&D. The show's early podcast episodes and particularly its live shows at PAX (Penny Arcade Expo) conventions helped normalize actual play content and inspired the creators who would later produce shows like [[Critical Role]] and [[The Adventure Zone]].

Chris Perkins' DMing in Acquisitions Incorporated, characterized by theatrical NPC performances, elaborate plot construction, and willingness to embrace player chaos while maintaining narrative coherence, influenced a generation of DMs. His work on Acq Inc, combined with his role as Principal Story Designer at Wizards of the Coast, made him one of the most influential figures in modern D&D culture.

The show's longevity—continuing for over fifteen years across podcasts, live performances, web series, and official D&D sourcebooks—demonstrates the sustainability of actual play content and the enduring appeal of well-executed TTRPG entertainment. Acquisitions Incorporated's influence extends from inspiring other actual play shows to affecting official D&D products, including published adventures and the Acquisitions Incorporated campaign sourcebook.

## Production History

### Origins: Penny Arcade and D&D 4th Edition (2007-2008)

Acquisitions Incorporated's genesis traces to the launch of Dungeons & Dragons Fourth Edition in 2008. Wizards of the Coast, seeking to promote the new edition, partnered with Penny Arcade—the popular webcomic and gaming culture website founded by Jerry Holkins (writer, known as "Tycho") and Mike Krahulik (artist, known as "Gabe").

Penny Arcade had established itself as a major force in gaming culture, and the annual PAX conventions (launched in 2004) had become significant gaming events. Wizards of the Coast recognized the promotional opportunity in having prominent gaming culture figures play and discuss the new D&D edition.

Chris Perkins, a designer and DM at Wizards of the Coast with extensive experience and theatrical DMing style, was selected to run a D&D game for Holkins and Krahulik. Scott Kurtz, creator of the PvP webcomic and friend of the Penny Arcade creators, joined to complete a three-player party.

The initial concept was straightforward: create podcast content featuring popular gaming personalities playing D&D 4th Edition, demonstrating the system's gameplay while providing entertainment. The corporate theme emerged from the players' creative choices and Perkins' improvisational world-building, with the idea of adventuring as business venture creating natural comedy opportunities.

### Early Podcasts and PAX Shows (2008-2010)

The first Acquisitions Incorporated content appeared in 2008 as podcast episodes featuring recorded game sessions. These early episodes introduced:

- **Omin Dran** (Jerry Holkins): A human cleric and the CEO of Acquisitions Incorporated
- **Jim Darkmagic** (Mike Krahulik): A human wizard with more confidence than sense
- **Binwin Bronzebottom** (Scott Kurtz): A dwarf fighter focused on "hitting things"

The podcast format allowed for edited gameplay with comedic pacing, sound effects, and narrative structure. The early episodes demonstrated that recorded D&D sessions could be entertaining to audiences beyond the players, proving the viability of what would later be called "actual play" content.

However, Acquisitions Incorporated's most significant innovation came with live performances at PAX conventions. Beginning in 2010, the Acq Inc crew performed D&D sessions in front of large convention audiences, creating theatrical actual play experiences that were simultaneously:

- Live entertainment for attending audiences
- Recorded content for later distribution
- Promotional events for D&D and PAX
- Community-building experiences for TTRPG fans

These live shows established templates for live actual play performance that influenced countless subsequent shows. The format featured:

- **Theatrical presentation** with the players on stage
- **Chris Perkins' performance** as an entertaining DM who played to the audience
- **Player comedy** and improvisation creating memorable moments
- **Production values** including sound, lighting, and sometimes visual effects
- **Audience participation** through reactions and sometimes voting on outcomes

The PAX live shows became annual traditions, with audiences growing from hundreds to thousands attending in large venues. The shows demonstrated actual play's potential as live entertainment and created shared experiences that built community around TTRPG content.

### Expansion and The "C" Team (2010-2017)

As Acquisitions Incorporated grew in popularity, the franchise expanded:

**Rotating Cast**: Various episodes and live shows featured different players, including:
- **Patrick Rothfuss** (fantasy author): Playing various characters
- **Morgan Webb** (gaming journalist): Adding new perspectives
- **Wil Wheaton** (actor): Bringing celebrity appeal
- Others from gaming and entertainment industries

This rotating cast approach demonstrated actual play's flexibility and introduced different dynamics while maintaining the core concept.

**The "C" Team (2017)**: A spin-off series featuring a different party working for Acquisitions Incorporated's franchise operations. The C Team featured:
- **Jerry Holkins** (DM): Reversing roles from main series
- **Kate Welch, Kris Straub, Amy Falcone, Ryan Hartman** (players)

The C Team ran as a weekly streaming show on Twitch, demonstrating Acq Inc's adaptation to the streaming era and expansion of the franchise beyond the core crew.

**Official D&D Content**: Acquisitions Incorporated became integrated into official D&D products:
- Published adventures featuring Acq Inc characters and locations
- **Acquisitions Incorporated Campaign Sourcebook** (2019): Official D&D supplement providing rules for running corporate-themed campaigns
- Integration into D&D lore and settings

### Transition to Fifth Edition (2014-2015)

When D&D 5th Edition launched in 2014, Acquisitions Incorporated transitioned from 4th Edition to the new system. This transition was handled smoothly, with the characters converting to 5E mechanics while maintaining their personalities and storylines.

The shift to 5E aligned Acquisitions Incorporated with D&D's most successful edition, ensuring the show remained relevant as 5E dominated the TTRPG market. The transition demonstrated how actual play campaigns could adapt to new systems while preserving what made them compelling.

### Recent Development (2018-Present)

In recent years, Acquisitions Incorporated has continued primarily through:

- **Annual PAX live shows**: Maintaining tradition of theatrical performances
- **Occasional special episodes**: Celebrating milestones or special events
- **Official D&D integration**: Continued presence in published materials
- **Franchise expansion**: The C Team and other spin-off content

The show's schedule has become less regular than during peak years, reflecting the cast's various other commitments, but Acq Inc remains an active and beloved actual play franchise.

## Cultural Impact

### Pioneering Actual Play as Entertainment

Acquisitions Incorporated's most significant cultural contribution is its pioneering demonstration that actual play content could be viable entertainment. Beginning in 2008—before "actual play" was a recognized category and years before the actual play boom—Acq Inc proved:

- Audiences would consume content of people playing D&D
- Actual play could be funny and entertaining beyond the game table
- Both audio and live formats could work for TTRPG content
- Production values enhanced actual play entertainment
- TTRPG content could build audiences and communities

This pioneering work created the foundation for the entire actual play genre. When [[Critical Role]], [[The Adventure Zone]], and other shows launched in the mid-2010s, Acquisitions Incorporated had already demonstrated the concept's viability, helping legitimize actual play as content category worth pursuing.

Many actual play creators cite Acquisitions Incorporated as inspiration or validation for their own shows. The existence of successful actual play content before their entry into the space gave creators confidence that audiences existed for TTRPG entertainment.

### Live Actual Play Format

The PAX live shows established templates for live actual play performance that influenced countless subsequent live shows, tours, and events. Acq Inc demonstrated:

- Actual play could work as theatrical performance
- Large audiences would attend live TTRPG events
- Live shows could be both entertaining for attendees and recordable for broader distribution
- Theatrical presentation enhanced rather than diminished genuine gameplay
- DMs and players could perform for audiences while maintaining authentic play

The success of Acq Inc live shows encouraged other actual play productions to pursue live performances, contributing to the growth of TTRPG live entertainment including convention appearances, touring shows, and special events.

### Chris Perkins' DMing Influence

Chris Perkins' work on Acquisitions Incorporated, combined with his role at Wizards of the Coast, made him one of D&D's most influential DMs. His style, characterized by:

- **Theatrical NPC performances** with distinct voices and personalities
- **Narrative flexibility** adapting to player chaos while maintaining story coherence
- **Comedy timing** knowing when to play straight and when to embrace absurdity
- **Production awareness** performing for both players and audience
- **Player support** facilitating player success and memorable moments

These techniques influenced DMs across the TTRPG community. Perkins became a model for entertaining DMing that balanced mechanical gameplay with theatrical performance.

His dual role—DMing Acquisitions Incorporated while designing official D&D content—created unique synergy between actual play and published materials, with each informing the other.

### Corporate-Themed D&D Content

The corporate overlay on traditional D&D adventure created a subgenre that influenced:

- **Official D&D content**: The Acquisitions Incorporated sourcebook provided rules and guidance for corporate-themed campaigns
- **Other actual play shows**: Various shows incorporated business or corporate elements
- **Home campaigns**: Many DMs adopted corporate themes for their own games
- **TTRPG design**: Consideration of how modern concepts could integrate with fantasy settings

The corporate satire demonstrated how modern contexts could enhance rather than diminish fantasy adventure, expanding D&D's thematic possibilities.

### Wizards of the Coast Partnership Model

Acquisitions Incorporated established a model for partnerships between content creators and TTRPG publishers. The show demonstrated that:

- Publisher-creator collaborations could be mutually beneficial
- Official support enhanced actual play reach and legitimacy
- Actual play content could serve promotional purposes while maintaining entertainment value
- Integration of actual play content into official products created new revenue opportunities

This partnership model influenced how Wizards of the Coast and other publishers approached actual play content, including official support for [[Critical Role]], [[Dimension 20]], and other shows.

### Convention Culture Integration

The PAX shows integrated actual play into gaming convention culture, establishing TTRPG content as convention programming staple. This integration:

- Normalized actual play as convention entertainment
- Created communal experiences around TTRPG content
- Demonstrated audience demand for live TTRPG events
- Influenced convention programming to include more actual play content

The success of Acq Inc at PAX encouraged other conventions to feature actual play shows, contributing to TTRPG presence at gaming events.
