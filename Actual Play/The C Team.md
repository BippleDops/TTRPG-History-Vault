# The C Team

**Format**: Weekly livestream actual play
**System**: D&D 5th Edition
**First Episode**: June 1, 2017
**Cast**: Jerry Holkins (DM), Kate Welch, Kris Straub, Amy Falcone, Ryan Hartman
**Network**: Penny Arcade (in partnership with Wizards of the Coast)
**Status**: Ended (126 episodes, concluded 2020)

## Overview

The C Team was a weekly livestream actual play series set in the [[Acquisitions Incorporated]] universe, featuring Jerry Holkins (Tycho from Penny Arcade) as Dungeon Master leading a party of franchise operators for the Acquisitions Incorporated corporation. Running from June 2017 to February 2020 across 126 episodes, The C Team distinguished itself through consistent weekly streaming, sophisticated urban fantasy storytelling, deep character development, and Jerry Holkins' distinctive DMing style that emphasized narrative complexity and emotional depth alongside corporate comedy.

The premise positioned the player characters as operators of an Acquisitions Incorporated franchise in Red Larch, a small town that became the center of increasingly cosmic-scale threats. The party consisted of K'thriss Drow'b (Kris Straub), a drow warlock with connections to dark entities; Walnut Dankgrass (Amy Falcone), a wood elf druid obsessed with documentation; Donaar Blit'zen (Ryan Hartman), a dragonborn paladin seeking redemption; and Rosie Beestinger (Kate Welch), a halfling monk with a mysterious criminal past.

What distinguished The C Team was Jerry Holkins' evolution from player to DM, bringing a writer's sensibility to campaign construction. Holkins, known for his work writing the Penny Arcade webcomic, approached DMing with attention to narrative structure, thematic development, and long-term plotting that created one of actual play's most intricately constructed campaigns. His DMing demonstrated how skills from other creative disciplines could enhance TTRPG storytelling.

The show's weekly streaming schedule and consistent runtime made it appointment viewing for fans, building a devoted community that engaged deeply with the characters and ongoing storylines. The C Team became known for balancing corporate comedy with genuine emotional stakes, cosmic horror elements with character-driven drama, and mechanical D&D gameplay with sophisticated narrative construction.

The C Team's influence extends beyond its viewership to demonstrate the viability of weekly actual play streams, showcase different approaches to DMing from established players, and explore how franchise-focused campaigns could develop unique identities while existing within larger fictional universes. The show's sophisticated storytelling and character development proved that spin-off actual play content could achieve creative independence while honoring source material.

## Production History

### Development and Launch (2017)

The C Team emerged from the success of [[Acquisitions Incorporated]] and the broader actual play boom of the mid-2010s. By 2017, actual play content was experiencing remarkable growth, with shows like [[Critical Role]] demonstrating the viability of weekly streaming and building engaged communities through consistent content.

Penny Arcade, in partnership with Wizards of the Coast, recognized an opportunity to expand the Acquisitions Incorporated brand through a regular streaming show. However, rather than simply continuing the main Acq Inc series, they developed a spin-off concept that would allow:

- **Regular weekly schedule**: Unlike Acq Inc's irregular live shows and podcasts
- **New characters and cast**: Fresh perspectives within established universe
- **Streaming format**: Adapting to Twitch and live streaming culture
- **Franchise focus**: Exploring Acq Inc's franchise operations rather than corporate headquarters

The concept of franchise operators allowed The C Team to exist within Acq Inc continuity while maintaining creative independence. The characters would occasionally interact with main Acq Inc characters and universe but would develop their own storylines, challenges, and identity.

Jerry Holkins' selection as DM represented a role reversal—he had been a player (playing Omin Dran) in main Acquisitions Incorporated content. His transition to DM brought:

- **Writer's perspective**: His webcomic writing experience informing narrative construction
- **Deep Acq Inc knowledge**: Understanding the universe's established lore and tone
- **Creative ambition**: Interest in exploring darker, more complex themes than main Acq Inc
- **Collaborative approach**: Supporting player creativity and character development

The cast was carefully selected to complement Holkins' DMing:

**Kate Welch**: Experienced TTRPG player and designer, bringing mechanical sophistication and strong character work
**Kris Straub**: Webcomic creator and improviser, providing comedy and creativity
**Amy Falcone**: Performer and cosplayer, bringing energy and commitment to character
**Ryan Hartman**: Musician and performer, adding musical elements and earnest character portrayal

This cast combination created chemistry that balanced comedy with genuine character investment.

### Early Campaign: Red Larch and Franchise Operations (2017-2018)

The C Team premiered on June 1, 2017, with weekly Thursday streams on Twitch. Early episodes established:

**The Franchise**: An Acquisitions Incorporated franchise operation in Red Larch, providing structure for adventures while allowing corporate satire
**Character Introduction**: The distinctive personalities of K'thriss, Walnut, Donaar, and Rosie
**Red Larch Setting**: The small town serving as home base while harboring dark secrets
**Corporate Comedy**: Franchise management alongside traditional adventuring
**Tone Balance**: Mixing Acq Inc's corporate humor with increasingly dark undertones

The early campaign followed relatively straightforward franchise operations—investigating local problems, managing business concerns, and building reputation. However, Jerry Holkins gradually introduced darker elements:

**K'thriss' Patron**: The drow warlock's connection to mysterious dark entities hinted at cosmic horror
**Walnut's Documentation**: The druid's obsessive record-keeping revealed world mysteries
**Donaar's Past**: The paladin's history and redemption quest added emotional depth
**Rosie's Secrets**: The monk's criminal background and mysterious connections created intrigue

This gradual darkening demonstrated Holkins' long-term planning, with seemingly minor details in early episodes gaining significance later.

### Development and Escalation (2018-2019)

As the campaign progressed, The C Team evolved beyond simple franchise adventures into complex narrative exploring:

**Cosmic Horror**: K'thriss' patron and connections to dark dimensions introduced Lovecraftian elements
**Town Secrets**: Red Larch's innocent exterior concealed disturbing truths
**Character Depth**: Each character's personal storylines developed extensive complexity
**Moral Ambiguity**: Situations requiring difficult choices without clear right answers
**Existential Threats**: Stakes escalating from local problems to reality-threatening dangers

Jerry Holkins' DMing showcased increasing sophistication:

**Long-Term Plotting**: Details from early episodes paying off dozens of sessions later
**Thematic Coherence**: Consistent themes of identity, memory, reality, and corporate absurdity
**NPC Complexity**: Rich characterization for recurring NPCs creating investment
**Atmospheric Storytelling**: Using description and pacing to create mood and tension
**Player Support**: Facilitating character moments while advancing plot

The cast responded with increasingly invested performances:

**Kris Straub's K'thriss**: Evolved from simple joke character into complex figure grappling with cosmic forces
**Amy Falcone's Walnut**: The obsessive documenter uncovering terrible truths
**Ryan Hartman's Donaar**: The paladin's earnest faith tested by moral complexity
**Kate Welch's Rosie**: The monk's mysterious past and capabilities gradually revealed

The campaign's complexity attracted devoted viewership, with fans creating extensive art, theories, and analysis. The C Team community developed strong identity, engaging deeply with ongoing storylines and character development.

### Peak Storytelling and Community (2019)

By 2019, The C Team had developed into one of actual play's most narratively sophisticated shows. The campaign's interweaving plotlines, cosmic scope, and character depth created compelling weekly viewing.

Significant developments included:

**Reality Threats**: The campaign's stakes involving fundamental reality and existence
**Character Revelations**: Major reveals about characters' true natures and pasts
**Emotional Peaks**: Genuinely moving character moments and dramatic confrontations
**Community Investment**: Devoted fanbase engaged with every detail and development

The show demonstrated that weekly streaming actual play could sustain complex, long-form storytelling rivaling any serialized narrative medium. Jerry Holkins' campaign construction proved that actual play could be both entertaining and artistically ambitious.

### Conclusion (2020)

The C Team concluded in February 2020 after 126 episodes, providing narrative closure to the campaign. The finale brought together threads from throughout the series, resolving character arcs and cosmic threats while honoring the journey.

The conclusion was planned rather than abrupt, allowing Jerry Holkins to craft satisfying endings for character stories and overarching narrative. The finale demonstrated the value of completed campaigns with intentional conclusions rather than indefinite continuation.

Following The C Team's conclusion, the cast and crew moved to other projects, with the show's legacy preserved through archived episodes and community memory.
