# Dimension 20: Fantasy High

**Format**: High-production web series actual play
**System**: D&D 5th Edition
**First Episode**: September 19, 2018 (Dimension 20 launch)
**Cast**: Brennan Lee Mulligan (DM), Emily Axford, Brian Murphy, Siobhan Thompson, Ally Beardsley, Zac Oyama, Lou Wilson
**Network**: CollegeHumor (now Dropout)
**Status**: Completed (Season 1: 17 episodes, Sophomore Year: 20 episodes)

## Overview

Dimension 20: Fantasy High is the inaugural season of Dimension 20, CollegeHumor's (now Dropout's) premium actual play series that revolutionized high-production TTRPG content through theatrical presentation, elaborate practical sets, and innovative genre-blending. Launched in September 2018, Fantasy High combined the high school coming-of-age genre with traditional Dungeons & Dragons fantasy, creating a unique setting where adventurers navigate both dungeon crawls and high school social dynamics.

The campaign is set in Elmville, a fantasy world where heroes go to high school, with traditional D&D classes and races integrated into modern educational and social structures. The story follows "The Bad Kids"—a group of misfit students at Aguefort Adventuring Academy including Fig Faeth (Emily Axford), Gorgug Thistlespring (Zac Oyama), Adaine Abernant (Siobhan Thompson), Fabian Seacaster (Lou Wilson), Kristen Applebees (Ally Beardsley), and Riz Gukgak (Brian Murphy)—as they uncover a conspiracy threatening their school and world while dealing with typical teenage concerns like dating, popularity, and parental relationships.

What distinguishes Fantasy High and Dimension 20 generally is the extraordinary production investment. The show features elaborate practical sets with working props, dynamic lighting, professional cinematography with multiple cameras, theatrical staging that places the DM on an elevated throne overlooking the players, and post-production values matching television drama. This commitment to production quality positioned Dimension 20 as premium content and demonstrated that actual play could achieve production values rivaling any other entertainment medium.

Dungeon Master Brennan Lee Mulligan, whose DMing combines encyclopedic system knowledge, improvisational brilliance, emotional storytelling, and sharp comedy, emerged through Dimension 20 as one of actual play's most acclaimed DMs. His ability to balance mechanical complexity with narrative flow, create vivid NPC characterizations, and respond dynamically to player choices while maintaining campaign coherence has influenced an entire generation of DMs.

Fantasy High achieved remarkable success, becoming one of Dropout's flagship offerings and establishing Dimension 20 as a premier actual play brand. The campaign's popularity led to "Fantasy High: Sophomore Year," continuing the story, and numerous subsequent Dimension 20 seasons in different settings and genres. The show's influence on actual play production standards, genre experimentation, and streaming service TTRPG content cannot be overstated.

## Production History

### CollegeHumor and Actual Play Ambitions (2017-2018)

Dimension 20: Fantasy High emerged from CollegeHumor's strategic expansion into premium subscription content. By 2018, CollegeHumor had operated for years as a comedy website and video production company, creating sketches and comedy content for YouTube and their own platform. However, the company recognized the need for subscription-based revenue to supplement advertising income.

CollegeHumor launched "Dropout," a subscription streaming service featuring exclusive original content. The company needed flagship shows to attract subscribers and justify the subscription cost. Actual play content, which was demonstrating remarkable popularity through shows like [[Critical Role]] and [[The Adventure Zone]], represented an attractive opportunity.

Brennan Lee Mulligan, a CollegeHumor performer and writer with extensive D&D experience, had been running games for colleagues for years. Emily Axford and Brian Murphy (who would go on to create [[Not Another DnD Podcast]]) were among his regular players, along with other CollegeHumor staff including Siobhan Thompson, Ally Beardsley, and Zac Oyama. Lou Wilson, a performer with strong improv and comedy credentials, joined to complete the six-player cast.

CollegeHumor's leadership recognized that with their production resources, comedy talent, and Mulligan's DMing skills, they could create actual play content that differentiated itself through production quality and comedy sophistication. Rather than simple livestreaming, they would produce actual play as premium television-quality content.

The concept for Fantasy High came from Brennan Lee Mulligan's creative vision: what if traditional D&D fantasy elements existed within a high school setting? This genre-blending allowed for:

- Familiar high school archetypes mapped onto D&D classes and races
- Social dynamics and teenage concerns integrated with fantasy adventure
- Comedy arising from contrasting mundane school life with epic questing
- Coming-of-age themes explored through fantasy lens
- Subversion of both high school and fantasy genre conventions

The title "Dimension 20" referenced the twenty-sided die central to D&D while suggesting exploration of different dimensions/settings across seasons.

### Production Design and Innovation (2018)

CollegeHumor invested significantly in Dimension 20's production, creating visual presentation that distinguished the show from all existing actual play content:

**The Throne**: Brennan Lee Mulligan DM'd from an elevated throne overlooking the players' table, creating visual hierarchy that emphasized the DM's role while allowing dramatic camera angles. This theatrical staging became iconic.

**Practical Sets**: Rather than battle maps or digital displays, Dimension 20 featured elaborate miniature sets with three-dimensional terrain, buildings, and environmental features. These handcrafted sets by Dwarven Forge and custom pieces created tangible, beautiful play spaces.

**Miniature Figures**: Custom-painted miniatures representing each player character and important NPCs added visual character and allowed for tactical positioning.

**Lighting and Cinematography**: Professional lighting design and multi-camera cinematography with dynamic angles, close-ups, and establishing shots created cinematic presentation.

**Editing and Post-Production**: Extensive editing added graphics, flashbacks, recaps, and narrative structure that enhanced storytelling.

**Sound Design**: Professional audio mixing, sound effects, and music created atmospheric depth.

This production approach positioned Dimension 20 as premium content, distinguishing it from streaming-focused actual play shows and justifying Dropout's subscription cost.

### Fantasy High Season One (September-December 2018)

Fantasy High premiered on September 19, 2018, as Dropout's flagship content. The first season followed The Bad Kids through their freshman year at Aguefort Adventuring Academy, uncovering a conspiracy involving:

- A missing vice principal
- Corrupted corn that created rage-filled monsters
- A demonic plot threatening the school and world
- Personal challenges for each character navigating teenage life and identity

The campaign structure brilliantly balanced:

**High School Life**: Attending classes, dealing with social hierarchies, navigating romantic interests, and managing parental expectations
**Fantasy Adventure**: Dungeon crawling, fighting monsters, solving mysteries, and confronting evil forces
**Character Development**: Each character grappled with identity questions—sexuality, family, belonging, purpose—through both mundane and magical challenges

Character highlights included:

**Fig Faeth** (Emily Axford): A tiefling bard discovering she's actually half-demon, navigating changing identity while starting a rock band
**Gorgug Thistlespring** (Zac Oyama): A half-orc barbarian raised by gnome parents, dealing with anger issues and finding belonging
**Adaine Abernant** (Siobhan Thompson): A high-elf wizard with crippling anxiety and neglectful parents, learning self-worth
**Fabian Seacaster** (Lou Wilson): A half-elf fighter, son of a legendary pirate, managing impossible parental expectations
**Kristen Applebees** (Ally Beardsley): A cleric questioning her religious faith and discovering her sexuality
**Riz Gukgak** (Brian Murphy): A goblin rogue investigating his father's death while navigating high school as an outsider

The season's seventeen episodes built to an epic climax involving battling demons, saving the world, and each character achieving personal growth. The finale delivered both narrative satisfaction and emotional payoff, demonstrating that actual play could achieve novelistic structure and thematic depth.

### Reception and Impact (2018-2019)

Fantasy High's reception was overwhelmingly positive. Audiences responded enthusiastically to:

- The unique high school fantasy setting
- Brennan Lee Mulligan's DMing brilliance
- The cast's chemistry and comedy
- Exceptional production values
- Emotional character development alongside comedy

The show became Dropout's most popular original content, driving subscriptions and establishing Dimension 20 as a flagship offering. Discussion in TTRPG communities highlighted the show's production quality and creative premise as setting new standards for actual play.

The success validated CollegeHumor's investment in premium actual play content and established the template for future Dimension 20 seasons.

### Fantasy High: Sophomore Year (2019-2020)

The success of Fantasy High led to "Sophomore Year," a twenty-episode second season continuing The Bad Kids' story. Sophomore Year featured even higher production values, deeper character exploration, and more ambitious narrative scope.

The campaign followed the characters into their second year at Aguefort, facing new challenges:

- A mysterious entity called "The Nightmare King" threatening reality
- Time travel and alternate dimensions
- Deeper exploration of each character's personal struggles
- Higher stakes threatening not just the world but existence itself

Sophomore Year demonstrated that Dimension 20 could sustain serialized storytelling across multiple seasons while raising production and narrative ambitions. The season's conclusion provided satisfying resolution to character arcs begun in Season One while leaving possibilities open for future stories.

### Dimension 20 Expansion (2019-Present)

Fantasy High's success enabled Dimension 20 to become an anthology series, with Brennan Lee Mulligan and various guest DMs creating campaigns in different settings and genres:

- **The Unsleeping City**: Urban fantasy set in magical New York City
- **A Crown of Candy**: Game of Thrones-style political intrigue in a world made of candy
- **Pirates of Leviathan**: Swashbuckling adventure (Fantasy High universe side story)
- **Tiny Heist**: Honey I Shrunk the Kids-style adventure
- Numerous other seasons exploring different genres and systems

This anthology approach allowed creative experimentation while maintaining the Dimension 20 brand's production quality standards.
