# Glass Cannon Podcast

**Format**: Actual play podcast with comedy and drama
**Systems**: Pathfinder 1E (original), Pathfinder 2E, Starfinder, Call of Cthulhu, Delta Green, various others
**First Episode**: June 15, 2015
**Cast**: Troy Lavallee (primary GM), Joe O'Brien, Skid Maher, Grant Berger, Matthew Capodicasa
**Platform**: Podcast, Patreon, YouTube, Twitch
**Status**: Ongoing with multiple concurrent shows

## Overview

The Glass Cannon Podcast (GCP) is one of the longest-running and most successful actual play podcasts, launching in June 2015 and growing from a group of friends playing Pathfinder into a full-fledged media network with multiple shows, live tours, and a dedicated community known as "The Naish" (The Glass Cannon Nation). The show has become particularly significant as the flagship actual play for Paizo's game systems (Pathfinder and Starfinder).

The original show features five friends playing through Paizo's "Giantslayer" Adventure Path for Pathfinder 1st Edition, distinguished by:
- **Rules commitment**: Careful attention to Pathfinder's complex mechanics
- **Deadly stakes**: Permanent character death is real and frequent
- **Comedy and heart**: Balancing crude humor with emotional storytelling
- **Production evolution**: Growth from amateur podcast to professional network
- **Community building**: Creating one of actual play's most passionate fanbases
- **Business success**: Demonstrating sustainable actual play career paths

What began as friends recording sessions in Troy Lavallee's apartment has evolved into the Glass Cannon Network (GCN), a multi-show actual play empire employing dozens of people and producing content across multiple systems and formats. The GCP represents the possibility of turning tabletop gaming passion into professional careers while maintaining the authenticity and friendship that made the show appealing.

## History and Development

### Origins: Five Guys in an Apartment (2015)

The Glass Cannon Podcast began in June 2015 when five friends in New York decided to record their Pathfinder campaign. The founding cast:

**Troy Lavallee**: GM, the "harsh but fair" Game Master
**Joe O'Brien**: Player, marketing professional, eventual CEO of GCN
**Skid Maher**: Player, writer and comedy background
**Grant Berger**: Player, audio engineer providing technical expertise
**Matthew Capodicasa**: Player, completing the core five

The group chose to play Paizo's "Giantslayer" Adventure Path, a published campaign taking characters from level 1 to level 17+ fighting progressively larger giants. This choice of a published adventure path rather than homebrew proved significant, providing structure and allowing other groups to play along.

Early production was basic:
- Recorded in Troy's apartment
- Minimal editing
- Free on podcast platforms
- No expectation of commercial success

The show's tone combined:
- **Adult humor**: Crude jokes and R-rated comedy
- **Rules focus**: Commitment to playing Pathfinder correctly
- **Genuine stakes**: Real risk of character death
- **Friend chemistry**: Natural banter and ribbing

### Finding an Audience (2015-2017)

The GCP grew organically through word-of-mouth in the Pathfinder community:

**Early Growth Factors**:
- **Quality production**: Grant's audio engineering created professional sound
- **Consistent release**: Weekly episodes built loyal audience
- **Pathfinder focus**: Filling niche for Pathfinder actual play (most shows used D&D)
- **Authentic gameplay**: Not performing for audience, just playing well
- **Community engagement**: Responsive to listener feedback

**Milestone Moments**:
- First Patreon launch enabling improved production
- Early character deaths establishing stakes
- Live show appearances at PaizoCon
- Community formation around the show
- Creation of "The Naish" (Glass Cannon Nation) fan community

By 2017, the GCP had become one of the most popular actual play podcasts, particularly within the Pathfinder community.

### Network Expansion (2017-2019)

Success of the main show led to expansion:

**Androids & Aliens** (2018): Starfinder actual play using Paizo's sci-fi system, featuring the same core cast playing different characters in a new setting.

**Patreon Growth**: Exclusive content and bonus shows for subscribers:
- **Raiders of the Lost Continent**: Patreon-exclusive Pathfinder campaign
- **The Haunted City**: Call of Cthulhu one-shot that became recurring
- Behind-the-scenes content
- Blooper reels and extended cuts

**Live Shows**: Regular touring with sold-out performances:
- PaizoCon appearances
- Gen Con live shows
- Multi-city tours across North America
- Interactive audience elements (the "Bottle Cap" award)

**Professionalization**:
- Cast members going full-time with GCP
- Forming GCP as official LLC
- Joe O'Brien becoming CEO
- Hiring additional staff
- Studio space for recording

### Glass Cannon Network Formation (2019-2020)

The show evolved from single podcast to media network:

**New Shows Launched**:
- **Get in the Trunk**: Delta Green (modern horror conspiracy)
- **New Game, Who Dis?**: Rotating systems and one-shots
- **Legacy of the Ancients**: Another Pathfinder 2E show
- **Time for Chaos**: Call of Cthulhu actual play

**Video Expansion**:
- Twitch streaming
- YouTube video content
- Virtual tabletop integration
- Visual production for select shows

**Cast Expansion**:
- Hiring additional performers
- Rotating cast across shows
- Guest appearances
- Diverse voices and perspectives

### COVID-19 and Remote Transition (2020-2021)

The pandemic forced major changes:

**Challenges**:
- Transition from in-person to remote recording
- Losing in-person chemistry and energy
- Technical hurdles for multiple remote participants
- Cancellation of live tours

**Adaptations**:
- Investment in remote recording technology
- Virtual live shows
- Increased Patreon content
- Focus on producing more shows remotely
- Community engagement through virtual events

**Silver Linings**:
- Ability to include guests from anywhere
- More frequent releases across network
- Experimentation with formats
- Deeper Patreon engagement

### Return to Form and Current Era (2021-Present)

Post-pandemic growth and evolution:

**Giantslayer Conclusion**: The original campaign that started it all concluded after over 300 episodes, providing emotional closure to multi-year story arc.

**Pathfinder 2E Transition**: Major shift from Pathfinder 1E to 2nd Edition:
- **Blood of the Wild**: Main 2E campaign
- Learning new system with audience
- Demonstrating system evolution

**Studio Expansion**:
- Professional studio space in New York
- Multiple recording capabilities
- Video production facilities
- In-person recording return

**Network Growth**:
- Multiple concurrent shows across systems
- Hiring more full-time staff
- Expanded touring schedule
- Partnership with Paizo

**Current Status** (2024):
- Multiple active shows across GCN
- Regular touring
- Strong Patreon support (thousands of subscribers)
- Professional studio operation
- Estimated dozens of employees

## Format and Style

### Podcast Structure

The flagship Glass Cannon Podcast follows consistent format:

**Episodes**:
- Released weekly on Thursdays
- 90-120 minutes typically
- Edited from longer sessions
- Professional audio production
- Opening/closing music and segments

**Structure**:
- Cold open/recap
- "Cannon Fodder" newsletter mentions
- Gameplay
- Cliffhanger endings frequent
- Post-credits scenes occasionally

### GM Style: Troy Lavallee

Troy's GMing style defines the show:

**"Harsh But Fair"**:
- Rules as written (RAW) emphasis
- No fudging dice or pulling punches
- Character death is real and permanent
- Consequences matter
- But always fair - no arbitrary rulings

**Preparation**:
- Deep knowledge of published adventure
- Modifications to personalize story
- NPC characterizations
- Balancing lore with table fun

**Performance**:
- Distinctive NPC voices
- Dramatic storytelling
- Emphasis on tension and stakes
- Dark humor and horror elements
- Emotional investment in story

**Table Management**:
- Keeping pacing moving
- Managing complex Pathfinder rules
- Adjudicating disputes
- Creating memorable moments
- Balancing players' spotlight time

Troy's "killer GM" reputation (earned through multiple PC deaths) has become part of the show's identity, creating genuine tension when characters are in danger.

### Player Dynamics

The core five have distinctive roles and chemistry:

**Joe O'Brien**:
- Often plays optimized, competent characters
- Straight man to absurdity
- Rules lawyer (affectionate)
- Business brain behind GCN growth

**Skid Maher**:
- Chaotic player choices
- Comedy background brings improvisation
- Memorable character voices
- Often plays unconventional builds

**Grant Berger**:
- Audio engineer ensuring quality
- Often plays tanks/defenders
- Dry wit and dark humor
- Technical backbone of production

**Matthew Capodicasa**:
- Heart of the party characters
- Emotional investment
- Often plays healers/support
- Genuine reactions to events

The friendship among the cast creates authentic moments - they're not performing friendship, they're actually friends playing together, which resonates with listeners.

## Influence on Actual Play and GMing

### Rules-as-Written Philosophy

GCP's commitment to RAW influenced actual play culture:

**Impact**:
- Demonstrating value of rules consistency
- Teaching complex systems accurately
- Creating trust through fairness
- Showing consequences matter

**Debate**:
- Some prefer "rule of cool" flexibility
- Balance between rules and fun
- Different approaches for different groups
- RAW as baseline vs. guidelines

### Deadly Stakes

Permanent character death as feature:

**Philosophy**:
- Death makes victories meaningful
- Investment in character survival
- Emotional weight of loss
- Authentic danger

**Influence**:
- Other shows adopting deadlier approaches
- Reaction against plot armor
- Debates about character death in actual play
- Tension vs. guaranteed survival

### The Bottle Cap System

GCP's implementation of Pathfinder's hero point variant:

**Concept**:
- Players earn bottle caps (hero points) for good roleplay, humor, etc.
- Can spend for mechanical benefits
- Positive reinforcement system
- Community awards caps at live shows

**Adoption**:
- Other groups implementing similar systems
- Demonstrating reward mechanics
- Positive reinforcement culture
