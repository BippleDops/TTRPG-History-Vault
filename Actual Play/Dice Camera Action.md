# Dice, Camera, Action!

**Format**: Web series livestream actual play
**System**: D&D 5th Edition
**First Episode**: March 1, 2016
**Cast**: Chris Perkins (DM), Anna Prosser, Holly Conrad, Jared Knabenbauer, Nate Sharp
**Network**: Wizards of the Coast (official D&D content)
**Status**: Ended (117 episodes, concluded 2019, content later removed)

## Overview

Dice, Camera, Action! (often abbreviated DCA) was an official Dungeons & Dragons actual play web series produced by Wizards of the Coast, featuring renowned DM Chris Perkins leading a party through an extended campaign primarily set in the gothic horror realm of Ravenloft. Launching in March 2016 as D&D 5th Edition was gaining mainstream momentum, the show ran for 117 episodes across three years, becoming one of Wizards of the Coast's flagship actual play productions and introducing audiences to the dark fantasy horror of the Curse of Strahd adventure and broader Ravenloft setting.

The campaign followed the "Waffle Crew"—Diath Woodrow (Jared Knabenbauer), a human rogue; Strix (Holly Conrad), a tiefling wizard; Evelyn Marthain (Anna Prosser), a human paladin; and Paultin Seppa (Nate Sharp), a human bard—as they were drawn into the mist-shrouded land of Barovia and became entangled in the machinations of the vampire lord Strahd von Zarovich. What began as an adaptation of the Curse of Strahd adventure module evolved into an expansive campaign exploring multiple domains of dread and developing intricate character relationships.

Chris Perkins' DMing brought his characteristic theatrical flair and narrative sophistication to gothic horror storytelling, creating an atmosphere of dread and mystery while balancing dark themes with moments of levity and character development. His portrayal of Strahd von Zarovich became one of the most memorable villain performances in actual play, establishing the vampire lord as a terrifying, manipulative presence that haunted the campaign even when not directly present.

What distinguished Dice, Camera, Action! was its position as official Wizards of the Coast content, serving both as entertainment and as promotional material for D&D products, particularly the Curse of Strahd adventure. The show demonstrated how actual play could effectively showcase published adventures while maintaining narrative flexibility and character-driven storytelling. The campaign's extensive exploration of Ravenloft helped popularize the setting among 5th Edition players and influenced broader interest in horror-themed D&D content.

The show's legacy is complicated by its controversial conclusion and subsequent removal from official platforms. Following revelations about cast member behavior leading to their departure, Wizards of the Coast removed DCA content from their official channels, creating complex questions about content preservation, corporate responsibility, and separating art from artist in actual play. Despite this troubled ending, Dice, Camera, Action!'s influence on horror actual play, Ravenloft popularity, and official publisher-produced actual play content remains significant.

## Production History

### Launch and Curse of Strahd (2016)

Dice, Camera, Action! launched on March 1, 2016, as part of Wizards of the Coast's expanded digital content strategy. The company was investing in video content to promote D&D 5th Edition, which was experiencing remarkable commercial success, and actual play shows like [[Critical Role]] and [[Acquisitions Incorporated]] had demonstrated the format's promotional and entertainment value.

Chris Perkins, already well-known for his work on [[Acquisitions Incorporated]] and his role as Principal Story Designer at Wizards of the Coast, was the natural choice to DM. The cast consisted of content creators and performers from gaming and entertainment communities:

- **Anna Prosser**: Host and esports personality, bringing on-camera experience
- **Holly Conrad**: Cosplayer and content creator, known for creativity and character work
- **Jared Knabenbauer**: YouTube gaming content creator (ProJared), bringing established audience
- **Nate Sharp**: Musician and content creator (NateWantsToBattle), adding musical elements

The show's initial concept was to play through Curse of Strahd, the gothic horror adventure module that had been released shortly before the show's launch. This alignment between published adventure and actual play content created promotional synergy while demonstrating how DMs could run the module for their own groups.

The early episodes established the Waffle Crew's dynamic:
- **Diath Woodrow** (Jared): A paranoid rogue with traumatic past
- **Strix** (Holly): A reclusive wizard with trust issues and distinctive personality
- **Evelyn Marthain** (Anna): An optimistic paladin devoted to Lathander, god of dawn
- **Paultin Seppa** (Nate): A laid-back bard with mysterious connections

The characters' contrasting personalities—Evelyn's optimism versus Strix's cynicism, Diath's paranoia versus Paultin's nonchalance—created natural dramatic tension and comedy while their gradual bonding provided emotional through-line.

### Development and Expansion (2016-2017)

As the campaign progressed beyond the initial Curse of Strahd adventure, Dice, Camera, Action! expanded into broader Ravenloft lore and original storylines. Chris Perkins incorporated:

- Multiple domains of dread beyond Barovia
- Original NPCs and storylines expanding published material
- Integration with broader D&D multiverse, including connections to other settings
- Crossover events with other Wizards of the Coast actual play content
- Exploration of character backstories and personal arcs

The show's production evolved to include:

**Higher Production Values**: Improved streaming quality, graphics, and presentation
**Community Engagement**: Active fan community creating art, theories, and discussions
**Cross-Promotional Content**: Integration with D&D products and other official content
**Regular Schedule**: Consistent weekly episodes building audience habits

The campaign developed several memorable extended storylines:

**The Death Curse**: Integration with the Tomb of Annihilation adventure, connecting Ravenloft storylines to other D&D content
**Character Deaths and Resurrections**: Permanent consequences and dramatic resurrections creating genuine stakes
**Strahd's Machinations**: The vampire lord's ongoing manipulation and presence creating persistent threat
**Romance and Relationships**: Character relationships, including Diath and Strix's developing romance, adding emotional investment

### Peak Popularity and Community (2017-2018)

At its height, Dice, Camera, Action! had built a devoted fanbase that engaged deeply with the content:

**Fan Art**: Extensive community art depicting characters, scenes, and moments
**The Waffle Crew**: The party name became beloved brand, with merchandise and community identity
**Live Shows**: Occasional live performances at conventions and events
**Crossover Events**: Appearances by characters in other D&D shows and content
**Community Theories**: Active discussion and speculation about plot developments

The show demonstrated how official publisher-produced actual play could build engaged communities comparable to independent productions. The Wizards of the Coast backing provided resources and promotional support while the creative team maintained authentic gameplay and storytelling.

Chris Perkins' DMing reached new heights during this period, with his portrayal of Strahd becoming iconic and his ability to balance horror atmosphere with character moments creating compelling viewing. The show's exploration of Ravenloft helped establish the setting's appeal for 5th Edition players.

### Conclusion and Controversy (2018-2019)

In 2019, Dice, Camera, Action! faced significant controversy when allegations emerged regarding cast member Jared Knabenbauer's personal behavior. The situation led to:

- Jared's departure from the show
- Disruption of ongoing storylines
- Community division and discussion about separating art from artist
- Wizards of the Coast's difficult position regarding official content

The campaign continued briefly with different cast configuration, but the disruption fundamentally changed the show's dynamic. Eventually, Dice, Camera, Action! concluded, and Wizards of the Coast subsequently removed DCA content from their official channels.

This removal created controversy regarding:

**Content Preservation**: Accessibility of years of actual play content and storytelling
**Corporate Responsibility**: Publisher obligations regarding problematic content creators
**Community Impact**: Fans' relationship with content affected by creator behavior
**Creative Legacy**: How to assess and discuss shows with complicated histories

The controversy and removal complicated Dice, Camera, Action!'s legacy, creating ongoing debates about content preservation, accountability, and the relationship between creators and their work in actual play.

## Cultural Impact

### Ravenloft and Horror D&D Popularity

Dice, Camera, Action!'s most positive and lasting cultural impact is popularizing Ravenloft and gothic horror D&D among 5th Edition players. The show's extensive exploration of the setting:

- Introduced many players to Ravenloft for the first time
- Demonstrated how to run horror-themed D&D campaigns
- Showcased Curse of Strahd's narrative potential
- Established Strahd von Zarovich as iconic 5E villain
- Influenced broader interest in horror fantasy in D&D

The show's success contributed to Ravenloft's revival in 5th Edition, with subsequent products and content exploring the setting. Many DMs reported being inspired by DCA to run Curse of Strahd or create horror campaigns, with Chris Perkins' atmospheric DMing providing templates for gothic horror storytelling.

### Official Publisher Actual Play Model

As official Wizards of the Coast content, Dice, Camera, Action! demonstrated:

- How publishers could produce actual play promoting products
- Integration of actual play with adventure modules and sourcebooks
- Balancing promotional purposes with authentic entertainment
- Resources and support publishers could provide actual play

This model influenced how Wizards of the Coast and other publishers approached actual play content, though the show's controversial ending also highlighted risks in official content production.

### Chris Perkins' Villain Portrayal

Perkins' portrayal of Strahd von Zarovich became one of actual play's most memorable villain performances, demonstrating:

- How to maintain villain presence across long campaigns
- Creating terrifying antagonists through personality rather than just mechanics
- Balancing villain power with player agency
- Using villains to drive narrative tension and character development

His Strahd became the standard against which other actual play villain portrayals were measured, influencing how DMs approached major antagonists.

### Character-Driven Horror Storytelling

The show demonstrated that horror D&D could be deeply character-driven, with personal stakes and relationships enhancing rather than competing with horror atmosphere. The balance of:

- Gothic horror atmosphere and genuine scares
- Character development and emotional moments
- Comedy providing tension relief without undercutting horror
- Personal character arcs within horror framework

This approach influenced how other shows tackled horror content, showing that multiple tones and priorities could coexist productively.

### Community Engagement Model

DCA's community engagement demonstrated how official content could foster fan creativity and participation:

- Encouraging fan art and creative content
- Building community identity around shows
- Creating spaces for discussion and theory-crafting
- Integrating community feedback into storytelling

These practices influenced community management for actual play shows.

### Controversial Legacy and Content Preservation

The show's removal following controversy raised important questions about:

- Preserving actual play content with problematic creators
- Publisher responsibilities regarding official content
- Community relationships with compromised content
- Balancing accountability with creative legacy

These discussions continue affecting how actual play industry handles similar situations.
