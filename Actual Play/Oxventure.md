# Oxventure

**Format**: Video and audio actual play
**System**: D&D 5th Edition (main campaign), Blades in the Dark, various others
**First Episode**: September 10, 2017
**Cast**: Johnny Chiodini (DM), Ellen Rose, Andy Farrant, Jane Douglas, Mike Channell, Luke Westaway
**Network**: Outside Xbox/Outside Xtra (Independent YouTube channels)
**Status**: Ongoing (Multiple ongoing campaigns)

## Overview

Oxventure is a British actual play series that emerged from the video game journalism YouTube channels Outside Xbox and Outside Xtra. Launched in September 2017, the show features video game journalists and presenters playing Dungeons & Dragons and other tabletop RPG systems, combining sharp improvisational comedy, genuine character development, and accessible entertainment that appeals to both TTRPG enthusiasts and casual viewers with no gaming experience.

The core cast consists of Johnny Chiodini (formerly known as Johnny Oxventure) as the primary Dungeon Master, with players Ellen Rose, Andy Farrant, Jane Douglas, Mike Channell, and Luke Westaway (who joined later). All cast members come from video game media backgrounds, bringing their skills in on-camera presentation, comedy timing, and audience engagement to create highly watchable actual play content that feels polished and professional while maintaining genuine spontaneity.

What distinguishes Oxventure in the crowded actual play landscape is its unique position at the intersection of video game culture and tabletop roleplaying. The show emerged from established YouTube channels focused on video game content, giving it an initial audience of gamers who might not have otherwise engaged with actual play content. This crossover appeal helped introduce tabletop roleplaying to audiences primarily interested in digital gaming, contributing to the broader mainstreaming of TTRPGs.

The main D&D campaign follows the "Oxventurers Guild"—a deliberately dysfunctional group of adventurers including Prudence the tiefling warlock, Corazón de Ballena the half-orc pirate, Merilwen the wood elf druid, Dob the half-elf bard, and Egbert the dragonborn paladin. Their adventures balance slapstick comedy, genuine character moments, and creative problem-solving, with the cast's chemistry creating memorable interactions that have garnered millions of views.

Beyond the main D&D campaign, Oxventure has expanded to include "Oxventure Presents," featuring different systems and settings with rotating DMs, demonstrating versatility in TTRPG content creation. The show's "Blades in the Dark" campaign set in the fantasy city of Geth has become particularly popular, showcasing the cast's ability to adapt to different play styles and tones.

Oxventure's success demonstrates the viability of actual play content emerging from adjacent entertainment communities, the appeal of British comedy sensibilities in actual play, and the potential for video-first actual play to maintain consistent quality while releasing content in both video and podcast formats.

## Production History

### Origins: Video Game Journalists Play D&D (2017)

Oxventure emerged from Outside Xbox and Outside Xtra, two YouTube channels focused on video game content. Outside Xbox launched in 2012, featuring video game news, comedy sketches, and list-based content about gaming culture, with hosts Andy Farrant, Jane Douglas, and Mike Channell building a dedicated following through their chemistry and humor.

Outside Xtra launched in 2016 as a sister channel, featuring Ellen Rose and Luke Westaway with similar content focused on different aspects of gaming culture. Both channels had established audiences of hundreds of thousands of subscribers interested in video game content presented with British humor and production polish.

In 2017, Johnny Chiodini (then working with the channels and known for their video series "Show of the Weekend") proposed creating a D&D actual play series. The timing was opportune—actual play content was gaining mainstream attention through shows like [[Critical Role]] and [[The Adventure Zone]], and there was growing interest in tabletop gaming within video game communities.

The concept was straightforward: the presenters from Outside Xbox and Outside Xtra would play D&D together, with Johnny as Dungeon Master, creating content that would appeal to their existing audience while potentially attracting TTRPG enthusiasts. The series would be filmed in video format for YouTube, distinguishing it from the primarily audio-based actual play podcasts dominating the space.

The first episode, "The Oxventure Begins," premiered on September 10, 2017, introducing the Oxventurers Guild and establishing the show's comedic tone. The initial response was enthusiastic, with existing channel subscribers embracing the D&D content while new viewers discovered the show through TTRPG communities.

### Early Campaign: Establishing the Guild (2017-2018)

The first year of Oxventure established the show's identity and core dynamics. The five original player characters were introduced:

- **Prudence** (Ellen Rose): A tiefling warlock with a dark pact and darker humor
- **Corazón de Ballena** (Andy Farrant): A half-orc pirate with questionable intelligence and unquestionable confidence
- **Merilwen** (Jane Douglas): A wood elf druid who can speak to animals and frequently does
- **Dob** (Mike Channell): A half-elf bard who is "definitely not" Corazón's son
- **Egbert** (Luke Westaway, joined later): A dragonborn paladin devoted to a dragon god

The early episodes established several key elements:

**Accessible Comedy**: The show's humor was broad and accessible, avoiding inside jokes or references that would alienate non-TTRPG audiences. Physical comedy, misunderstandings, and character-based humor made the content enjoyable even for viewers unfamiliar with D&D.

**Production Quality**: Filmed in the channels' existing studio space with professional lighting, multiple cameras, and quality audio, Oxventure maintained high production values from the start. This polish distinguished it from many early actual play shows with more amateur production.

**Character-Driven Stories**: Johnny's DMing focused on creating scenarios that highlighted character personalities and relationships rather than complex tactical combat. This approach prioritized entertainment and character development over mechanical complexity.

**Short, Episodic Format**: Early episodes ran 30-45 minutes, shorter than many actual play shows, making content more digestible for casual viewers and easier to produce within the channels' existing content schedules.

**Family-Friendly Content**: Unlike some actual play shows embracing adult humor and themes, Oxventure maintained relatively family-friendly content appropriate for the channels' broad audience demographics.

### Expansion and Growth (2018-2020)

As Oxventure's popularity grew, the show expanded in several directions:

**Longer Episodes**: As the cast and audience became more invested in the ongoing narrative, episodes grew longer, eventually settling around 60-90 minutes to allow more complex storytelling and character development.

**Live Shows**: The cast began performing live Oxventure episodes at gaming conventions and special events, bringing actual play to in-person audiences and demonstrating the show's popularity beyond YouTube views.

**Podcast Format**: Recognizing that many fans preferred audio consumption, Oxventure episodes began releasing in podcast format alongside video, expanding the audience and competing with audio-first actual play shows.

**Musical Episodes**: Leveraging Mike Channell's musical talents (Dob being a bard), the show occasionally featured musical numbers, including original songs performed in character. These musical moments became fan favorites and showcased the cast's versatility.

**Holiday Specials**: The show created special holiday episodes, including Christmas-themed adventures that became annual traditions, building audience anticipation and creating evergreen content.

**Character Development**: As the campaign progressed, the characters evolved beyond their initial comic archetypes. Prudence's relationship with her patron, Corazón's family connections, Merilwen's environmental concerns, Dob's search for identity, and Egbert's faith all received substantial development.

### Johnny's Departure and DM Evolution (2020-2021)

In 2020, Johnny Chiodini announced they would be stepping back from their role as primary DM for Oxventure while continuing involvement with the show. This transition marked a significant moment for the series, as Johnny had been the primary creative force behind the campaign's narrative direction.

The departure was handled gracefully, with Johnny continuing to appear in some episodes while other cast members took turns DMing. This transition led to "Oxventure Presents," a format where different cast members would DM one-shots or short campaigns in various systems, showcasing different DMing styles and game systems.

The main D&D campaign adapted by having different cast members rotate DM duties, demonstrating the ensemble's versatility. Luke Westaway, in particular, took on significant DMing responsibilities, bringing his own creative voice to the campaign while honoring the established world and characters.

### Oxventure Presents and System Diversity (2020-Present)

"Oxventure Presents" became a major part of the show's identity, featuring:

**Blades in the Dark Campaign**: Set in the fantasy city of Geth, this campaign became one of the most popular Oxventure series. The heist-focused gameplay and darker tone demonstrated the cast's range, with Ellen Rose as GM creating a compelling urban fantasy world.

**Deadlands**: A weird west campaign showcasing the cast in a different genre and historical setting.

**Call of Cthulhu**: Horror-focused one-shots demonstrating the cast's ability to handle different tones and systems.

**Honey Heist and Other One-Shots**: Lighter, comedy-focused games providing variety and experimentation.

This diversity demonstrated Oxventure's evolution from a D&D-focused show to a broader actual play brand capable of creating compelling content across multiple systems and genres.

### Recent Development (2021-Present)

Recent years have seen Oxventure continue evolving:

**Increased Production Values**: The show has invested in better sets, lighting, graphics, and editing, competing with higher-budget actual play productions.

**Cross-Platform Growth**: The show maintains strong presence on YouTube, podcast platforms, and Patreon, with exclusive content for subscribers supporting ongoing production.

**Community Engagement**: Active social media presence, fan art showcases, and community interaction have built a dedicated fandom.

**Merchandise**: Official Oxventure merchandise featuring characters and catchphrases has created additional revenue streams while strengthening brand identity.

**Continued Innovation**: The show continues experimenting with formats, including animated recaps, highlight compilations, and special event episodes.

## Cultural Impact

### Video Game to TTRPG Pipeline

Oxventure's most significant cultural contribution is demonstrating the natural connection between video game and tabletop gaming communities. By emerging from established video game content channels, Oxventure introduced TTRPGs to audiences who might not have otherwise encountered actual play content.

Many viewers came to Oxventure through their interest in video games and Outside Xbox/Xtra content, discovering tabletop roleplaying through familiar presenters. This crossover helped normalize TTRPGs among gaming communities that might have perceived tabletop gaming as separate from or inferior to digital gaming.

The show demonstrated that skills valuable in video game content creation—on-camera presence, comedy timing, audience awareness, production quality—translated effectively to actual play content. This realization influenced other content creators from adjacent entertainment fields to explore actual play production.

### British Actual Play Representation

Oxventure became one of the most prominent British actual play shows, helping establish a distinct UK presence in an actual play landscape dominated by American productions. The show's British humor, cultural references, and sensibilities created content that felt distinct from American actual play shows while remaining accessible to international audiences.

This British representation was significant for UK-based TTRPG communities, providing actual play content that reflected their cultural context and humor. The show's success encouraged other UK-based actual play productions and demonstrated that actual play audiences existed globally, not just in North America.

The show also helped connect UK TTRPG communities with broader actual play culture, with cast members appearing at UK gaming conventions and events, supporting local game stores, and engaging with British TTRPG creators and communities.

### Accessibility and Family-Friendly Content

Oxventure's commitment to relatively family-friendly content filled a niche in the actual play space. While many popular actual play shows embraced adult humor and themes, Oxventure demonstrated that actual play could be entertaining and sophisticated while remaining appropriate for younger viewers or family co-viewing.

This accessibility made Oxventure a gateway actual play show for many younger viewers or parents seeking content to share with children. Teachers and educators have reportedly used Oxventure episodes to introduce students to creative storytelling and collaborative problem-solving through TTRPGs.

The show's approachable humor and clear explanations of mechanics also made it accessible to viewers completely unfamiliar with D&D or TTRPGs generally, serving as an educational resource while remaining entertaining.

### Musical Integration in Actual Play

Oxventure's use of original music and musical performances set it apart from most actual play shows. Mike Channell's performances as Dob the bard, including original songs written for the character, demonstrated how musical elements could enhance actual play entertainment.

These musical moments became highlights that were frequently shared on social media, introduced new audiences to the show, and inspired other actual play shows to incorporate more musical elements. The success of Oxventure's musical content showed that actual play could embrace performance elements beyond standard roleplaying.

### Video-First Production in Podcast Era

While many successful actual play shows were audio-first podcasts that added video later (or not at all), Oxventure demonstrated the viability of video-first production. The show's visual comedy, physical performances, and production design added value that pure audio couldn't capture, while still providing audio versions for podcast listeners.

This approach influenced other video game content creators and YouTube-based creators to explore actual play content, recognizing that their existing video production capabilities could translate to actual play. The show proved that actual play could succeed as visual entertainment competing with other YouTube content for attention.
