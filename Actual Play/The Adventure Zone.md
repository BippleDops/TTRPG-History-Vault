# The Adventure Zone

**Format**: Comedy actual play podcast
**Systems**: D&D 5E, Monster of the Week, Fate, various others
**First Episode**: March 27, 2014
**Cast**: Griffin McElroy (DM/GM), Justin McElroy, Travis McElroy, Clint McElroy
**Platform**: Podcast (MaxFun Network), YouTube animatics
**Status**: Ongoing (multiple completed arcs, current arc in progress)

## Overview

The Adventure Zone (TAZ) is a comedy actual play podcast produced by the McElroy family, beginning in 2014 as a one-off bonus episode of their popular comedy advice podcast "My Brother, My Brother and Me" (MBaM). What started as a goofy D&D session evolved into one of the most beloved and influential actual play podcasts in the medium, known for balancing genuine humor with surprisingly emotional storytelling.

The show features three brothers (Griffin, Justin, and Travis) and their father Clint playing various tabletop RPGs, with Griffin serving as the primary Game Master/Dungeon Master for most arcs. While it began with Dungeons & Dragons 5th Edition, the show has since explored numerous different game systems, each supporting different narrative arcs.

The Adventure Zone distinguished itself from other actual play content through its:
- **Tight editing**: Episodes are edited down to 60-90 minutes of the most entertaining content
- **Comedy-first approach**: Prioritizing humor and entertainment over rules accuracy
- **Narrative ambition**: Increasingly complex storytelling with major plot twists and emotional payoffs
- **Accessibility**: Low barrier to entry for non-TTRPG fans through the podcast format
- **Family dynamic**: Unique chemistry of a father playing with his three adult sons

Over multiple story arcs spanning different systems and settings, The Adventure Zone has built a massive, devoted fanbase, spawned a bestselling graphic novel series, and helped prove that actual play content could succeed in pure audio format without video components.

## History and Development

### Origins: Here There Be Gerblins (2014)

The Adventure Zone began on March 27, 2014, as a bonus episode for the McElroy brothers' comedy podcast "My Brother, My Brother and Me." The episode was titled "Here There Be Gerblins" and featured Griffin DMing a D&D adventure for his brothers Justin and Travis, plus their father Clint.

None of the participants were experienced with D&D 5th Edition (which had just been released in 2014), and the first episodes reflect this inexperience with frequent rule misunderstandings and mechanical fumbling. However, the family's natural comedic chemistry shone through immediately.

The episode was so well-received by MBaM listeners that the McElroys decided to continue it as a separate podcast series. Early episodes were released irregularly, fitting around the family's other projects and podcasts.

### Balance Arc: Finding the Voice (2014-2017)

What became known as "The Balance Arc" started as a simple D&D campaign but evolved dramatically over 69 episodes. The arc followed three adventurers:
- **Taako** (Justin) - An elf wizard chef
- **Magnus Burnsides** (Travis) - A human fighter
- **Merle Highchurch** (Clint) - A dwarf cleric

The arc began with straightforward D&D adventures and gradually became more narratively complex. Griffin introduced a metaplot involving the mysterious "Bureau of Balance" and dangerous magical artifacts called the Grand Relics.

Key developments during Balance:
- **Lunar Interludes**: Story episodes between major arcs allowing for character development
- **Increasingly serious themes**: Death, sacrifice, memory, and redemption
- **Major plot twists**: The Stolen Century revelation, character backstory reveals
- **Reduced rules emphasis**: Story began taking priority over mechanical accuracy
- **Musical themes**: Griffin composed leitmotifs for characters and moments

The Balance arc concluded in August 2017 with a finale that brought many listeners to tears, proving that comedy podcasts could deliver genuine emotional catharsis. The finale episodes drew massive audiences and cemented TAZ as a major force in actual play content.

### Experimental Arcs (2017-2018)

After Balance's conclusion, the McElroys experimented with different systems and storytelling approaches:

**Commitment** (March-May 2017): A mini-arc using the Fate Accelerated system, set in a superhero wrestling world. This served as a palate cleanser between Balance and the next major arc.

**Dust** (August 2018): A weird Western setting using the Fate Core system. Featured a murder mystery in a magical frontier town.

**Amnesty Arc** (2018-2020): The next major arc, using the Monster of the Week system (Powered by the Apocalypse). Set in modern-day West Virginia, it followed paranormal investigators protecting a small town. This ran for 36 episodes and represented a significant tonal shift toward horror and mystery, while maintaining the show's humor.

### Subsequent Arcs and Current Status (2020-Present)

**Graduation** (2019-2021): Travis took over as DM for this arc using D&D 5E, set in a school for heroes and villains. This arc proved divisive among fans, with criticism of pacing and railroad storytelling, but also showcased Travis's different DMing style.

**Ethersea** (2021-2022): Griffin returned to DMing for this arc using D&D 5E with significant homebrew. Set in an underwater world, it featured a unique collaborative worldbuilding prologue using "The Quiet Year" game system.

**Steeplechase** (2022-2023): Travis returned as GM using the "Blades in the Dark" system, set in a theme park on an alternate-history moon.

**Abnimals** (2023-Present): The current arc featuring anthropomorphic animals in a fantasy setting, using a custom system based on "Kids on Bikes."

## Format and Style

### Podcast Structure

The Adventure Zone differs from livestream actual play shows in several key ways:

**Edited Content**: Unlike live shows, TAZ episodes are heavily edited:
- Sessions recorded privately, then edited down to 60-90 minutes
- Dead air, rule lookup, and less entertaining tangents removed
- Music, sound effects, and production elements added in post
- Story moments emphasized over mechanical play

**Production Quality**: Professional podcast production including:
- Original music composed by Griffin McElroy
- Sound design and effects
- Professional audio engineering
- Consistent release schedule (bi-weekly typically)

**Accessibility Focus**: Designed for listeners unfamiliar with TTRPGs:
- Rules explained in accessible terms
- Focus on story and character over mechanics
- Comedy provides entry point for non-gamers
- No requirement to understand complex RPG systems

### Narrative Approach

The show's storytelling evolved significantly over time:

**Early Balance**: Relatively traditional D&D adventure structure
**Mid-Balance**: Increasingly complex metaplot and character arcs
**Late Balance**: Highly narrative-focused with planned story beats
**Post-Balance**: Experimentation with different systems and storytelling modes

Griffin's DM style became increasingly narrative-focused, sometimes drawing criticism from traditional RPG fans for prioritizing story over player agency. However, this approach resonated strongly with the podcast's audience who valued emotional storytelling.

### Comedy Style

The McElroy family's humor defines the show:
- **Absurdist comedy**: Ridiculous character decisions and situations
- **Pop culture references**: Heavy use of memes and cultural touchstones
- **Character voices**: Distinct character personalities and running gags
- **Genuine affection**: Jokes balanced with sincere emotional moments
- **Family dynamics**: Inside jokes and brotherly ribbing
- **Improv skills**: Quick wit and building on each other's ideas

## Cultural Impact

### Proving Audio-Only Actual Play

The Adventure Zone demonstrated that actual play content could succeed without video:
- **Podcast success**: Millions of downloads per episode at peak
- **Theater of the mind**: Listeners engaged without visual aids
- **Accessibility**: Audio format allowed listening during commutes, chores, etc.
- **Production focus**: Editing and sound design enhanced rather than captured live play
- **Broader audience**: Reached listeners who wouldn't watch 4-hour video streams

This inspired numerous other actual play podcasts and proved the format's commercial viability.

### Gateway to TTRPGs

TAZ introduced countless people to tabletop gaming:
- **Non-gamer audience**: Many listeners had never played TTRPGs
- **Comedy entry point**: Humor made the concept accessible to skeptics
- **Inspiring new players**: Listeners motivated to start their own games
- **System diversity**: Introduced various RPG systems beyond D&D
- **Demystifying the hobby**: Made TTRPGs seem approachable and fun

Many game stores and D&D groups in the late 2010s reported newcomers citing TAZ as their introduction to the hobby.

### Emotional Storytelling in Comedy

The show proved comedy actual play could deliver genuine emotional moments:
- **Balance finale**: Widely regarded as one of actual play's most emotional conclusions
- **Character deaths**: Treated with weight despite overall comedic tone
- **Relationship development**: Meaningful character relationships and growth
- **Thematic depth**: Exploration of memory, identity, sacrifice, and redemption
- **Narrative payoff**: Long-term story planning creating satisfying conclusions

This balance of comedy and drama became a template for many subsequent actual play shows.

### Mainstream Media Success

The Adventure Zone achieved success beyond the TTRPG community:
- **New York Times bestseller**: Graphic novel adaptations topped bestseller lists
- **Wide demographic appeal**: Audience beyond typical TTRPG fans
- **Critical acclaim**: Recognition in mainstream entertainment media
- **Licensed merchandise**: Wide range of official products
- **Live shows**: Sold-out tours across the United States

### MaxFun Network and Podcast Economics

TAZ demonstrated the economic viability of actual play podcasts:
- **Membership model**: MaxFun donor support system
- **Advertising revenue**: Premium podcast advertising rates
- **Merchandise**: Successful product lines
- **Graphic novels**: Publishing deals
- **Live shows**: Touring as revenue stream
- **Multi-platform presence**: YouTube, streaming, various podcast platforms

## Influence on Actual Play and Podcasting

### Editing Philosophy

TAZ established that edited actual play could compete with live streams:
- **Quality over quantity**: Shorter, tighter episodes
- **Post-production value**: Music and sound design as integral elements
- **Narrative focus**: Editing to emphasize story beats
- **Accessibility**: Removing barrier-to-entry moments for new listeners

Many subsequent actual play podcasts adopted similar editing approaches.

### System Diversity

The show demonstrated value in exploring different RPG systems:
- **Beyond D&D**: Introducing listeners to indie and alternative systems
- **System as tool**: Matching system to story goals
- **Accessibility**: Making various systems approachable to audiences
- **Designer support**: Driving sales and interest in featured systems

Sales of Monster of the Week, Fate, and other featured systems reportedly spiked after TAZ coverage.

### Narrative-Focused Play

Griffin's increasingly story-driven approach influenced actual play culture:
- **Planned arcs**: Long-term story planning in actual play
- **Narrative beats**: Prioritizing dramatic moments over pure improv
- **Player collaboration**: Cast working together for story goals
- **Rule flexibility**: "Rule of cool" over mechanical precision

This approach sparked debate about player agency vs. narrative coherence in actual play.

### Music and Sound Design

The show elevated production standards:
- **Original scores**: Griffin's composed themes for characters and moments
- **Leitmotifs**: Musical themes creating emotional associations
- **Sound effects**: Enhancing immersion in audio-only format
- **Production quality**: Professional-level audio engineering

Many actual play podcasts subsequently invested heavily in music and sound design.

## Controversies and Criticisms

### Player Agency Debates

Griffin's increasingly narrative-focused DM style sparked discussion:
- **Railroading concerns**: Criticism that player choices didn't meaningfully affect outcomes
- **Story vs. Game**: Debate about prioritizing narrative over emergent gameplay
- **Entertainment value**: Defense that the approach created better listening experience
- **Different expectations**: Traditional RPG fans vs. podcast entertainment fans

The McElroys have addressed this by acknowledging the show as entertainment first, game second.

### Graduation Reception

The Graduation arc (Travis as DM) received mixed response:
- **Pacing issues**: Criticism of slow story development
- **NPC focus**: Too much attention on DMPCs vs. player characters
- **Combat**: Infrequent and sometimes unsatisfying mechanical play
- **Player agency**: Concerns about railroading exceeded Balance arc criticisms

Travis and the family addressed criticisms openly, with Travis discussing lessons learned.

### Representation and Sensitivity

Occasional missteps in representation:
- **Stereotypes**: Some NPC portrayals criticized as stereotypical
- **Cultural sensitivity**: Handling of real-world cultural elements in fantasy settings
- **Disability representation**: Some characterizations critiqued

The McElroys typically respond by acknowledging mistakes, learning, and improving future content.
