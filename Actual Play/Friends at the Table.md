# Friends at the Table

**Format**: Narrative actual play podcast
**Systems**: Dungeon World, Apocalypse World, The Sprawl, Blades in the Dark, various PbtA/FitD games
**First Episode**: August 6, 2014
**Cast**: Austin Walker (primary GM) with rotating players including Ali Acampora, Art Martinez-Tebbel, Jack de Quidt, Keith J Carberry, Janine Hawkins, Sylvia Clare, Andrew Lee Swan, and others
**Platform**: Podcast, Patreon
**Status**: Ongoing with multiple completed seasons

## Overview

Friends at the Table (FatT) is a narrative-focused actual play podcast that has distinguished itself through sophisticated worldbuilding, thoughtful political themes, diverse representation, and commitment to independent and story-focused RPG systems. Created and primarily hosted by Austin Walker, the show has been running since 2014, predating most of the "actual play boom" while maintaining a dedicated, passionate audience.

Unlike many actual play podcasts that focus on comedy or combat-heavy play, Friends at the Table emphasizes:
- **Collaborative worldbuilding**: Extensive use of worldbuilding games and shared narrative creation
- **Political storytelling**: Thoughtful exploration of power, empire, resistance, and social structures
- **Literary ambitions**: Complex narratives with thematic depth and literary influences
- **System diversity**: Exclusive focus on independent, story-focused RPG systems
- **Representation**: Intentional diversity in characters, stories, and themes
- **Production quality**: Professional editing, original music, and sound design

The show has created an interconnected science-fantasy universe called "Hieron" and explored various settings within it, while also venturing into entirely separate worlds. Each season typically uses a different RPG system chosen to support that season's thematic and narrative goals.

Friends at the Table occupies a unique space in actual play: less commercially massive than Critical Role or The Adventure Zone, but deeply influential within indie RPG circles and among creators who value narrative experimentation and political consciousness in their gaming.

## History and Development

### Origins and Season One: The Quiet Year / COUNTER/Weight (2014-2016)

Friends at the Table began in August 2014 when Austin Walker (then managing editor at Giant Bomb, later of Waypoint/Vice Gaming) gathered friends to play The Quiet Year, a map-making worldbuilding game by Avery Alder. This collaborative worldbuilding established the foundation for the show's first major arc.

The initial season, COUNTER/Weight, followed The Quiet Year with actual play using various Powered by the Apocalypse systems, primarily The Sprawl. Set in a science fiction universe with mechs, artificial intelligence, and political intrigue, COUNTER/Weight established FatT's signature style:

- **Dense worldbuilding**: Complex political factions and history
- **Thematic focus**: Questions about identity, autonomy, and divine authority
- **Character-driven narrative**: Deep character relationships and development
- **Musical identity**: Original score by Jack de Quidt
- **Long-form storytelling**: 50+ episodes building to epic conclusion

The season concluded in December 2016 with an emotional finale that set the template for FatT's approach: long arcs building to satisfying, bittersweet conclusions.

### Establishing Identity: Marielda and the Hieron Universe (2016-2017)

Between major seasons, FatT produced Marielda, a shorter arc using Blades in the Dark to explore the fantasy city from which their world of Hieron takes its name. This season established important precedents:

- **Interstitial seasons**: Shorter arcs between major campaigns
- **System experimentation**: Trying different systems for different stories
- **Worldbuilding depth**: Exploring the history and complexity of established settings
- **Heist structure**: Narrative structures beyond traditional adventure

Marielda's success validated the show's approach of creating an interconnected universe explored through different systems and timelines.

### Season Two: Seasons of Hieron (2016-2018)

Seasons of Hieron represented FatT's longest continuous campaign, using Dungeon World to explore the fantasy continent of Hieron centuries after Marielda. This season:

- **Epic fantasy scope**: Kingdoms, ancient evils, chosen ones subverted
- **Political complexity**: Exploration of nationalism, imperialism, and resistance
- **Character evolution**: Unprecedented character development and relationship arcs
- **Tragic moments**: Including the devastating Fall of Ordenna
- **Divine themes**: Investigation of gods, faith, and divine authority

The season ran for over 60 episodes and established Hieron as FatT's signature creation, a richly detailed world worthy of fantasy literature comparison.

### Season Three: PARTIZAN (2019-2021)

After Twilight Mirage (2018-2019), which explored a space opera setting with The Sprawl and other systems, FatT returned to the COUNTER/Weight universe with PARTIZAN. This season:

- **Revolutionary struggle**: Focus on resistance movements and political organization
- **Mech action**: Using Beam Saber and other mech-focused systems
- **Espionage and intrigue**: Complex plotting and factional conflict
- **COVID adaptation**: Transitioned to remote recording during pandemic
- **Political consciousness**: Deepest engagement with themes of revolution and power

PARTIZAN represented FatT's most politically engaged storytelling, examining questions of liberation, violence, and social transformation.

### Season Four: PALISADE (2022-Present)

The current season continues PARTIZAN's storyline while exploring new time periods and characters in the Divine Principality setting. Using Armour Astir: Advent and other systems, PALISADE continues FatT's tradition of sophisticated political storytelling and experimental system use.

## Format and Style

### Podcast Structure

Friends at the Table's format differs significantly from many actual play shows:

**Episode Types**:
- **Worldbuilding episodes**: Using games like The Quiet Year, Kingdom, or Microscope
- **Gameplay episodes**: Actual play using primary system for the season
- **Post-mortems**: Discussion episodes analyzing completed arcs
- **Holiday specials**: One-shots using various systems
- **Bonus content**: Patreon-exclusive episodes and discussions

**Editing and Production**:
- Professional editing removing dead space and optimizing flow
- Minimal rules discussion kept in final product
- Original music and soundscapes by Jack de Quidt and others
- Sound design enhancing narrative moments
- Episode lengths typically 90-120 minutes

### GM Philosophy and Style

Austin Walker's GM approach emphasizes:

**Collaborative Storytelling**:
- Players as co-authors of the world and story
- "Spotlight" moments ensuring each character gets focus
- Building on player ideas and improvisations
- Transparent discussion of narrative direction and themes

**Political Thoughtfulness**:
- Examining power structures and their consequences
- Avoiding colonial narratives and tropes
- Considering material conditions and class dynamics
- Exploring resistance, organization, and collective action

**System as Tool**:
- Choosing systems that support thematic goals
- Modifying and hacking systems when needed
- Teaching systems to players and audience
- Demonstrating how mechanics influence narrative

**Literary Ambitions**:
- References to literature, philosophy, and critical theory
- Thematic coherence across long arcs
- Character arcs with literary complexity
- Worldbuilding comparable to published fantasy

### Production Values

The show maintains high production standards:
- **Original music**: Composed specifically for the show
- **Sound design**: Atmospheric elements and effects
- **Editing**: Tight, professional post-production
- **Transcripts**: Full transcripts for accessibility
- **Show notes**: Detailed episode guides and content warnings

## Cultural Impact

### Indie RPG Advocacy

Friends at the Table has been instrumental in promoting independent RPG systems:

**System Spotlight**:
- Introduced audiences to PbtA and FitD games
- Demonstrated story game potential in long campaigns
- Drove sales for indie designers and small publishers
- Provided extensive examples of systems in play
- Educated listeners on game design philosophy

Designers have reported significant sales increases after their games were featured on FatT, with some crediting the show for their game's success.

### Representation and Diversity

The show set high standards for inclusive actual play:

**Character Diversity**:
- Majority non-white characters across all seasons
- LGBTQ+ characters as default, not exception
- Disabled characters treated respectfully
- Non-binary and trans characters naturally integrated
- Body diversity and varied character backgrounds

**Cast and Crew**:
- Diverse cast of players across seasons
- Conscious effort to include marginalized voices
- Platform sharing with diverse guest players
- Hiring diverse contractors and collaborators

**Content Approach**:
- Content warnings for difficult subjects
- Thoughtful handling of trauma and violence
- Avoiding colonial and racist fantasy tropes
- Consulting sensitivity readers and cultural advisors when appropriate

This commitment influenced other actual play shows to be more intentional about representation.

### Political Consciousness in Gaming

FatT demonstrated that actual play could engage seriously with political themes:

**Thematic Exploration**:
- Imperialism and resistance movements
- Class struggle and economic systems
- Identity and autonomy under oppressive systems
- Divine authority and secular power
- Collective action and organizing

**Audience Impact**:
- Introduced political analysis frameworks to gaming discussions
- Encouraged thoughtful consideration of in-game politics
- Demonstrated storytelling as political act
- Influenced how creators approach worldbuilding

The show's political engagement attracted audiences interested in thoughtful, progressive storytelling while remaining character-focused and emotionally engaging.

### Literary Actual Play

Friends at the Table elevated expectations for narrative complexity:

**Storytelling Sophistication**:
- Complex narrative structures (non-linear timelines, flashbacks, ensemble casts)
- Thematic coherence across dozens of episodes
- Literary references and influences
- Character development comparable to novels
- Worldbuilding depth rivaling published fantasy

**Intellectual Engagement**:
- References to philosophy, critical theory, and political thought
- Analysis of genre tropes and their implications
- Meta-commentary on storytelling itself
- Encouraging critical thinking about narrative choices

This approach demonstrated actual play could be intellectually substantial while remaining entertaining.

## Notable Seasons and Moments

### COUNTER/Weight (2014-2016)

The inaugural season established FatT's identity:

**Setting**: Science fiction universe with mechs, AI, and political intrigue

**Key Themes**:
- Identity and autonomy (especially for artificial beings)
- Divine authority vs. individual freedom
- Found family and loyalty
- Sacrifice and consequence

**Memorable Moments**:
- Mako Trig's character arc and ultimate fate
- The revelation about the Divines
- Orth Godlove's decisions and their consequences
- The finale's emotional conclusion

**Impact**: Proved FatT's approach could sustain long-form storytelling

### Marielda (2017)

Short season exploring Hieron's history:

**System**: Blades in the Dark
**Format**: Heist and urban adventure
**Innovation**: Demonstrated how to use different systems for different eras of same world

### Seasons of Hieron (2016-2018)

Epic fantasy campaign and many fans' favorite season:

**Setting**: Continent of Hieron, centuries after Marielda

**Key Moments**:
- The Fall of Ordenna (devastating tragedy)
- Hella's character development
- The Hitchcock reveal
- Final confrontation and bittersweet conclusion

**Significance**:
- Most traditional fantasy season, subverting genre expectations
- Deepest character development in FatT history
- Established Hieron as fully realized fantasy world

### Twilight Mirage (2018-2019)

Space opera exploring post-scarcity society:

**Setting**: The Divine Fleet, post-utopian civilization
**Systems**: The Sprawl, Mechnoir, others
**Themes**: Utopia and its discontents, decline, memory and history

### PARTIZAN (2019-2021)

Revolutionary mech campaign:

**Setting**: Divine Principality, revolutionary struggle
**Focus**: Political organizing, resistance, and transformation
**Innovation**: Most explicitly political FatT season
**Impact**: Influenced discourse about politics in TTRPGs

## Challenges and Controversies

### Accessibility vs. Density

The show's complexity can be barrier to entry:
- Dense worldbuilding requiring attention
- Political themes potentially alienating some audiences
- Long episodes requiring time investment
- Multiple connected seasons creating continuity challenges

The show has addressed this through:
- Detailed show notes and guides
- Standalone seasons for new listeners
- Community-created resources
- Transcripts and accessibility features

### Cast Changes and Consistency

Rotating cast creates challenges:
- Character attachment when players rotate
- Narrative continuity across cast changes
- Balancing established and new players
- Scheduling complexities

### Maintaining Political Nuance

Engaging with politics risks:
- Oversimplifying complex issues
- Alienating audiences with different views
- Handling sensitive topics respectfully
- Avoiding didacticism

FatT generally navigates these carefully through:
- Focusing on character perspectives
- Avoiding simple good/evil binaries
- Acknowledging complexity
- Prioritizing storytelling over messaging
