# History of RPGs 101: From D&D to Modern Indie Games

**Course Description**: This course provides a comprehensive survey of tabletop roleplaying game history from 1974 to present. Students will explore the cultural, technological, and design evolution of TTRPGs, analyzing influential games, designers, and movements. Through hands-on play, critical reading, and analytical writing, students will develop literacy in RPG design and history.

**Prerequisites**: None

**Learning Objectives**:
By the end of this course, students will be able to:
1. Trace the historical development of TTRPGs from 1974 to present
2. Identify major design innovations and their cultural contexts
3. Analyze RPG systems using design vocabulary and frameworks
4. Evaluate the cultural impact of TTRPGs on gaming and broader culture
5. Compare and contrast design philosophies across eras and traditions

## Course Structure

### Week 1: Origins (1974-1979)
**Theme**: The Birth of Roleplaying Games

**Core Games**:
- [[Dungeons & Dragons (1974)]]
- [[Advanced Dungeons & Dragons (1977)]]
- [[Traveller (1977)]]
- [[RuneQuest (1978)]]

**Topics**:
- Wargaming roots (Chainmail, Diplomacy)
- Gary Gygax and Dave Arneson's collaboration
- The OD&D three little brown books
- TSR's formation and early growth
- Fantasy vs science fiction RPGs

**Key Concepts**:
- Character creation
- Game Master role
- Dice mechanics (percentile vs d20)
- Campaign play
- Dungeon crawling

**Assignments**:
- Read: Excerpt from *Playing at the World* (Jon Peterson)
- Play: One-shot using OD&D retroclone (Swords & Wizardry)
- Discussion: What made D&D revolutionary?

---

### Week 2: TSR Dominance (1980-1989)
**Theme**: Consolidation and Expansion

**Core Games**:
- AD&D 1st Edition (continued)
- [[Call of Cthulhu (1981)]]
- [[Star Frontiers (1982)]]
- [[GURPS (1986)]]
- [[Shadowrun (1989)]]

**Topics**:
- AD&D's systematization of D&D
- Horror RPGs and Lovecraftian gaming
- Universal systems (GURPS)
- Genre fusion (Shadowrun's cyberpunk + fantasy)
- The Satanic Panic controversy

**Key Concepts**:
- BRP (Basic Roleplaying) system
- Skill-based vs class-based systems
- Point-buy character creation
- Meta-plot and setting design
- TTRPG moral panic

**Assignments**:
- Read: [[Satanic Panic (1979-1995)|Satanic Panic]] entry
- Play: Call of Cthulhu one-shot
- Essay: Compare class-based (AD&D) vs skill-based (CoC) design

---

### Week 3: The Diverse 90s (1990-1999)
**Theme**: Artistic Ambition and Alternative Voices

**Core Games**:
- [[Vampire The Masquerade (1991)]]
- [[Ars Magica (1987)]]
- [[Over the Edge (1992)]]
- [[Feng Shui (1996)]]

**Topics**:
- White Wolf and the World of Darkness
- Storyteller system innovations
- Jonathan Tweet and Robin Laws' design innovations
- Narrative-focused design
- Alternative distribution (Indie RPG zines)

**Key Concepts**:
- Storyteller/Storytelling system
- Dice pools
- Narrative vs simulationist design
- Character drama and "story games"
- Player agency and GM authority

**Assignments**:
- Read: White Wolf's *Vampire* Player's Guide excerpts
- Play: World of Darkness chronicle session
- Discussion: What is "story games" vs traditional RPG?

---

### Week 4: The d20 Revolution (2000-2007)
**Theme**: Open Gaming and Commercial Boom

**Core Games**:
- [[Dungeons & Dragons Third Edition (2000)]]
- [[Mutants & Masterminds (2002)]]
- [[D&D 3.5 Edition (2003)]]

**Topics**:
- D&D 3E's unified mechanics
- Open Game License (OGL) impact
- d20 System and third-party publishing boom
- Wizards of the Coast vs TSR design philosophy
- Character optimization culture

**Key Concepts**:
- OGL and open content
- Unified core mechanic (d20 + modifier)
- Feats and customization
- Challenge Rating system
- "System mastery" and optimization

**Assignments**:
- Read: [[Open Game License|OGL]] analysis
- Play: D&D 3.5 or Pathfinder session
- Research: Analyze three d20 System third-party products

---

### Week 5: Indie Revolution (2008-2014)
**Theme**: Narrative Games and Design Theory

**Core Games**:
- [[Dogs in the Vineyard (2004)]]
- [[Fiasco (2009)]]
- [[Apocalypse World (2010)]]
- [[Dungeon World (2012)]]
- [[Fate Core (2013)]]

**Topics**:
- The Forge and design theory
- Story games movement
- Powered by the Apocalypse (PbtA)
- GM-less and player-driven games
- Kickstarter and crowdfunding

**Key Concepts**:
- GNS Theory (Gamist, Narrativist, Simulationist)
- PbtA mechanics (2d6+stat, moves, playbooks)
- Aspects and Fate points
- GM-less play
- Indie publishing and crowdfunding

**Assignments**:
- Read: Vincent Baker's *Apocalypse World* design notes
- Play: Fiasco or Dungeon World one-shot
- Essay: Compare PbtA to traditional RPG design

---

### Week 6: Modern Landscape (2014-Present)
**Theme**: Mainstream Success and Continued Innovation

**Core Games**:
- [[Dungeons & Dragons Fifth Edition (2014)]]
- [[Blades in the Dark (2017)]]
- [[Thousand Year Old Vampire (2018)]]
- Contemporary indie games

**Topics**:
- D&D 5E's mainstream renaissance
- Actual play streaming (Critical Role, etc.)
- Forged in the Dark (FitD) system
- Solo and journaling RPGs
- Safety tools and inclusive design
- OGL 1.1 controversy (2023)

**Key Concepts**:
- Advantage/disadvantage mechanic
- Bounded accuracy
- Forged in the Dark clocks and crews
- Safety tools (X-Card, Lines & Veils, Session 0)
- Representation and diversity in gaming

**Assignments**:
- Watch: Critical Role or other actual play excerpt
- Play: Blades in the Dark or solo RPG
- Final Essay: Trace one design element through TTRPG history

## Assessment

### Participation (20%)
- Weekly discussion contributions
- Play session engagement
- Peer critique of essays

### Play Reports (30%)
- Six short (500-word) reflections on play sessions
- Analyzing mechanics and player experience
- Connecting to course readings

### Midterm Essay (20%)
- 1500 words
- Comparative analysis of two games from different eras
- Due: End of Week 3

### Final Essay (30%)
- 2500 words
- Historical analysis tracing one design element or theme
- Options:
  - Evolution of dice mechanics
  - Character creation across eras
  - Representation of race/gender/identity
  - Economic models (TSR to Kickstarter)
  - Safety and consent in play

## Course Policies

### Play Sessions
Students will play 6 different RPG systems during the course (one per week). Sessions organized during class time or arranged with local gaming groups.

### Academic Integrity
All written work must be original. Proper citation required for game texts and secondary sources.

### Accessibility
Accommodations available for students with disabilities. Game sessions can be adapted for accessibility needs.

### Content Warnings
This course discusses sensitive historical topics (Satanic Panic) and will play games with potentially intense content (horror, violence). Students may opt out of specific sessions with alternate assignments.
