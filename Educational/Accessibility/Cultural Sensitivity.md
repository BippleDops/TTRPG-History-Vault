# Cultural Sensitivity

**Category**: Accessibility / Respectful Representation
**Era**: 2000s-present (accelerating awareness)

## Overview

Cultural Sensitivity in gaming addresses respectful representation of real-world cultures in fantasy/historical settings, avoiding appropriation/stereotypes/exoticization through research, cultural consultants, own-voices creators, and thoughtful worldbuilding, with evolution from casual appropriation (early D&D's "Oriental Adventures," problematic portrayals) toward recognition that culture isn't costume, authentic representation matters, and marginalized creators should tell their own stories, demonstrating hobby's slow progress on respecting cultural complexity while ongoing work remains addressing historical harms and building inclusive future.

**Historical Problems**:
- Casual appropriation without research
- Stereotypical "exotic" cultures
- White creators defaulting to European fantasy
- Monolithic culture portrayals
- Fetishization and exoticization

**Progress**:
- Own-voices creators (Balikbayan, Thousand Arrows, Spears of the Dawn)
- Cultural consultants
- Research and respect
- Avoiding appropriation
- Sensitivity readers

**Ongoing Debates**:
- Who can tell what stories?
- Fantasy vs. appropriation line
- Research requirements
- Compensation for consultation

## Problematic Historical Examples

**Oriental Adventures** (1985): Asian cultures as monolith, stereotypes, casual appropriation

**Al-Qadim** (1992): "Arabian Nights" without cultural depth

**Maztica** (1991): Mesoamerican-inspired problematic portrayal

**Various**: Treating real cultures as exotic fantasy elements

**Harm**: Perpetuating stereotypes, reducing cultures to aesthetics, disrespecting living traditions

## Progress and Solutions

### Own-Voices Creators

**Balikbayan** (2021): Jammi Tong's Filipino diaspora game

**Thousand Arrows** (Girl by Moonlight): Respectful wuxia

**Spears of the Dawn**: Kevin Crawford's African fantasy (researched)

**Indigenous Games**: First Nations/Aboriginal creators

**Cultural Games**: Creators from cultures they're portraying

### Consultants and Research

**Cultural Consultants**: Paying experts for guidance

**Sensitivity Readers**: Reviewing for problematic content

**Extensive Research**: Beyond surface aesthetics

**Community Input**: Engaging with cultural communities

### Respectful Practices

**Research Requirements**: Understanding cultures deeply

**Avoid Monoliths**: Recognizing cultural diversity

**Not Costumes**: Cultures aren't aesthetic choices

**Living Traditions**: Respecting ongoing practices

**Compensation**: Paying consultants fairly

**Own Stories**: Prioritizing own-voices creators

## Debates and Discussions

**Who Tells Stories?**
- Some argue only own-voices
- Others support research enabling any creator
- Middle ground: own-voices prioritized, others require extensive respect/research/consultation

**Fantasy vs. Appropriation**:
- Inspired-by vs. claiming authenticity
- Fictionalized vs. direct portrayal  
- Research requirements
- Sensitivity to sacred/closed practices

**Commercial Considerations**:
- Marketability of "exotic" settings
- Own-voices creators' economic access
- Publisher responsibility

## Cultural Impact

**Awareness**: Growing recognition of sensitivity importance

**Own-Voices Success**: Creators from cultures achieving recognition

**Consultant Norm**: Cultural consulting becoming standard

**Community Accountability**: Calling out appropriation

**Evolution**: Slow progress from casual appropriation toward respect

## Best Practices

**For Designers Using Cultures**:
- Extensive research beyond Wikipedia
- Cultural consultants (paid)
- Sensitivity readers
- Avoid stereotypes and monoliths
- Respect sacred/closed practices
- Acknowledge inspirations
- Consider if you're right person to tell this story

**For Publishers**:
- Prioritize own-voices creators
- Require cultural consultation
- Sensitivity readers
- Address problematic back-catalog
- Support diverse creators

**For Community**:
- Amplify own-voices creators
- Call out appropriation
- Support cultural sensitivity
- Learn and improve
