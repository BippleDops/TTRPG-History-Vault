# Deaf and Hard of Hearing Gaming

**Category**: Accessibility
**Era**: 2010s-present

## Overview

Deaf and Hard of Hearing Gaming encompasses adaptations enabling deaf/HOH players to participate through visual communication (text chat, sign language, visual cues), captioning for audio content, reduced audio dependency, and designs emphasizing visual over auditory information, with Sign RPG (2020—Kathryn Hymes's game about creating sign language) and community advocacy demonstrating deaf gamers' active participation when games prioritize visual communication and groups accommodate communication needs.

**Key Adaptations**:
- Text chat for voice games
- Sign language at tables
- Visual cues replacing audio
- Captions for actual play
- Written game materials over audio explanations

**Community**: Deaf gamers advocating for inclusion

**Technology**: Video chat enabling sign language, text chat tools

**Games**: Sign RPG centering Deaf experiences

**Impact**: Demonstrated gaming accessible through visual communication prioritization

## Accessibility Approaches

### Visual Communication

**Text Chat**: Discord, Roll20, VTT text for voice-alternative

**Sign Language**: In-person signing at tables

**Video Chat**: Enabling sign language visibility

**Visual Cues**: Replacing audio signals

**Written Materials**: Clear text over audio-only explanations

### Online Gaming

**Video Platforms**: Zoom/Discord enabling sign language

**Text Chat Channels**: Parallel text communication

**Captions**: Live or post-production for actual play

**Visual Indicators**: Turn order, events, notifications

### Table Accommodations

**Sign Language**: Groups learning basic signs

**Whiteboards**: Visual communication aids

**Note-Passing**: Written communication

**Patience**: Slower communication pace

**Visual Focus**: Maps, props, gestures

## Games Centering Deaf Experiences

**Sign** (2020): Kathryn Hymes's game about Deaf community creating sign language—respectful centering of Deaf culture

## Challenges

**Audio Dependency**: Voice chat excluding deaf players

**Actual Play**: Most video content lacks captions

**Table Speed**: Verbal communication faster, can exclude

**Lip Reading**: Masks during pandemic complicated

**Assumptions**: Designers assuming hearing players

## Solutions

**Text Chat Parity**: Ensuring text equals voice

**Captioning**: Adding to video content

**Visual Design**: Reducing audio dependency

**Sign Language**: Learning and using

**Deaf Consultants**: Including in design/testing

## Cultural Impact

**Deaf Gaming Community**: Active visible advocacy

**Sign RPG**: Centering Deaf culture

**Captioning**: Increasing in actual play

**Awareness**: Designers considering deaf accessibility

**Video Communication**: Enabling sign language participation

## Best Practices

**Online**:
- Provide text chat options
- Caption video content
- Visual indicators for events
- Video for sign language

**In-Person**:
- Learn basic signs
- Face players when speaking (lip reading)
- Written backup communication
- Visual focus

**Design**:
- Don't require audio
- Provide written rules
- Visual communication emphasis
- Test with deaf players
