# Blind and Low Vision Gaming

**Category**: Accessibility
**Era**: 2010s-present (increased focus)

## Overview

Blind and Low Vision Gaming encompasses adaptations and designs enabling visually impaired players to participate in TTRPGs through screen reader compatibility, audio-focused play, braille materials, high-contrast designs, and theater-of-mind emphasis, demonstrating tabletop gaming can be fully accessible when designers prioritize audio descriptions, reduce visual dependencies, and create materials compatible with assistive technology, with community advocacy and designer awareness growing significantly in 2010s-2020s as accessibility became recognized priority.

**Key Adaptations**:
- Screen reader compatible PDFs
- Audio-focused gameplay
- Braille dice and materials
- High contrast text/graphics
- Theater-of-mind over maps
- Audio description techniques

**Community**: Blind gamers and advocates pushing for accessibility

**Designer Awareness**: Increasing incorporation of accessibility from design start

**Technology**: Screen readers, text-to-speech, audio tools

**Impact**: Demonstrated gaming fully accessible when properly designed

## Accessibility Approaches

### Screen Reader Compatibility

**PDF Accessibility**:
- Proper heading structure
- Alt text for images
- Logical reading order
- Tagged PDFs
- OCR-friendly text layers

**Digital Tools**:
- D&D Beyond screen reader compatible
- Roll20 accessibility improvements
- VTT keyboard navigation

### Audio-Focused Play

**Theater-of-Mind**: Emphasizing verbal description over visual maps

**Descriptive GMing**: Rich audio descriptions

**Verbal Communication**: Clear verbal gameplay

**Audio Dice**: Apps announcing results

**Sound Design**: Using audio cues for atmosphere

### Physical Adaptations

**Braille Dice**: Tactile dice with braille

**Tactile Maps**: 3D-printed or textured terrain

**Braille Character Sheets**: Accessible materials

**High-Contrast**: Large text, strong contrast for low vision

### Community Solutions

**Blind Gaming Groups**: Communities of visually impaired players

**Advocates**: Pushing for accessibility

**Resources**: Guides for accessible gaming

**Consultants**: Blind gamers advising designers

## Games/Publishers Leading

**Evil Hat**: Accessibility statements, screen-reader PDFs

**Wizards**: D&D Beyond accessibility, PDF improvements

**Indie Publishers**: Many prioritizing accessibility

**Community Games**: Grassroots accessible designs

## Challenges

**Visual Dependency**: Many games heavily visual (maps, minis)

**PDF Quality**: Many poorly formatted for screen readers

**VTT Accessibility**: Mixed screen reader support

**Assumption**: Designers assuming sighted players

**Costs**: Accessible materials sometimes more expensive

## Solutions and Progress

**Designer Education**: Teaching accessible design

**Community Advocacy**: Blind gamers vocally requesting access

**Technology**: Improving assistive tech

**Standards**: Emerging accessibility guidelines

**Inclusive Philosophy**: Designing for disability from start not retrofit

## Cultural Impact

**Awareness**: Accessibility increasingly recognized priority

**Community Voice**: Blind gamers advocating effectively

**Designer Response**: Growing incorporation of accessibility

**Technology Progress**: Better tools and standards

**Inclusion**: Moving toward universal design

## Best Practices

**For Designers**:
- Test with screen readers
- Provide alt text
- Structure PDFs properly
- Emphasize audio over visual
- Consult blind gamers

**For GMs**:
- Rich verbal descriptions
- Theater-of-mind gameplay
- Audio cues
- Collaborative mapping descriptions
- Patience and clarity

**For Groups**:
- Accommodating play styles
- Using accessible tools
- Verbal transparency
- Supporting blind players
