## Resource Overview

The Satanic Panic of the 1980s-early 1990s represents one of the most significant cultural conflicts in TTRPG history, when moral crusaders, religious organizations, and media outlets accused Dungeons & Dragons and other RPGs of causing suicide, murder, and occult activity. This documentation draws from multiple sources:

**Primary Documentation:**
- Michael A. Stackpole's "The Pulling Report" (1989) - Detailed refutation of BADD claims
- Paul Cardwell's research and legal testimony
- Court documents from D&D-related legal cases
- Contemporary media coverage (60 Minutes, news articles)
- BADD publications and materials
- TSR's public responses and internal concerns

**Academic Analysis:**
- Joseph Laycock's "Dangerous Games: What the Moral Panic over Role-Playing Games Says About Play, Religion, and Imagined Worlds" (2015)
- Studies on moral panic theory applied to RPGs
- Sociological analysis of the phenomenon

**Media Archives:**
- Television segments (60 Minutes, local news)
- Newspaper articles and editorials
- Anti-D&D pamphlets and publications
- Evangelical Christian media response

## Key Content Summary

### Origins and Key Figures (1979-1982)

**James Dallas Egbert III Case (1979):**
- Michigan State student disappeared, media blamed D&D
- Private investigator William Dear suggested "steam tunnel" D&D playing
- Truth: Egbert was struggling with personal issues unrelated to gaming
- Media narrative established: D&D causes mental instability
- Egbert's eventual suicide (1980) reinforced false narrative

**Patricia Pulling and BADD Formation (1982):**
- Pulling's son Irving committed suicide (June 1982)
- Pulling blamed D&D curse from school game
- Investigation found no D&D connection; suicide related to depression
- Pulling founded BADD (Bothered About Dungeons & Dragons)
- Began crusade claiming D&D caused teen deaths

**Dr. Thomas Radecki and NCTV:**
- National Coalition on Television Violence expanded to RPGs
- Radecki claimed D&D caused 100+ deaths
- No evidence provided; statistics fabricated or misattributed
- Later discredited (Radecki eventually lost medical license for unrelated issues)

### The Panic Spreads (1983-1985)

**Media Coverage:**
- 60 Minutes segment "Dungeons & Dragons: A Suicide?" (1985)
- Local news "investigative" reports linking D&D to crimes
- Uncritical repetition of BADD claims
- Sensationalized coverage of teen suicides and crimes

**Religious Opposition:**
- Evangelical Christian ministries condemned D&D as occult
- "Occult symbols" misidentified in game materials
- Claims of demon summoning, spell casting teaching satanism
- Church youth groups prohibited from playing
- Book burnings and game destructions

**School and Community Bans:**
- School districts banned D&D clubs
- Libraries removed game books
- Community centers prohibited play
- Boy Scout troops forbade gaming

**Legal Cases:**
- Multiple lawsuits against TSR blaming D&D for deaths
- All cases dismissed or found no D&D causation
- Legal costs and PR damage to TSR significant

### The Response (1985-1990)

**Michael A. Stackpole's "The Pulling Report" (1989):**
- Detailed investigation of BADD's claims
- Statistical analysis disproving suicide connection
- Exposure of Pulling's lack of credentials
- Documentation of fabricated statistics
- Distribution to media and legal defenders

**Paul Cardwell's Research:**
- Psychologist who studied RPG players
- Found no evidence of psychological harm
- Testified as expert witness in legal cases
- Published academic refutations

**TSR's Defensive Measures:**
- Public statements defending the hobby
- Removal of "demons" and "devils" from AD&D (controversial choice)
- Educational materials for parents and educators
- Legal defense fund for accused players

**Gaming Community Response:**
- Letter-writing campaigns
- Media engagement attempts
- Educational outreach
- Frustration with being misrepresented

### Decline and Legacy (1990-1995)

**Panic Fades:**
- Legal cases all dismissed, establishing no causation
- BADD lost credibility as claims debunked
- Media moved to other moral panics (video games, heavy metal)
- Stackpole Report widely disseminated
- Academic research showed no harm

**Lasting Effects:**
- Stigma around RPG playing persisted
- Some school bans remained decades later
- TSR suffered financial and reputational damage
- Gaming community developed siege mentality
- Changed how RPG publishers marketed games

**Cultural Analysis:**
- Classic moral panic pattern (Cohen's "Folk Devils and Moral Panics")
- Scapegoating new youth culture
- Misunderstanding of fantasy play
- Media amplification of fringe claims
- Religious anxiety about secular entertainment

## Historical Information Covered

**Timeline of Key Events:**
- **1979**: Egbert disappearance, first media D&D panic
- **1982**: Irving Pulling suicide, BADD formation
- **1983-1985**: Peak of media coverage and school bans
- **1985**: 60 Minutes segment, highest public awareness
- **1989**: Stackpole Report published
- **1990-1992**: Legal cases dismissed, panic fades
- **1994**: Final BADD newsletter, Pulling largely inactive

**Statistical Reality:**
- Teen suicide rates among gamers statistically lower than general population
- No credible evidence linking RPG playing to violence or occult activity
- BADD's claimed death statistics fabricated or misattributed
- Gamers showed normal to above-average psychological profiles

**Impact on Industry:**
- TSR removed "demon" and "devil" from AD&D 2nd Edition
- Publishers avoided controversial imagery
- Marketing emphasized "storytelling" over combat
- Industry became more cautious about content
- Delayed mainstream acceptance of gaming

**Legal Precedents:**
- Established RPGs as protected creative expression
- Set precedents against frivolous "game made me do it" lawsuits
- Clarified limits of product liability
- Reinforced First Amendment protections

## Key Quotes/Facts

**From The Pulling Report (Stackpole):**
"BADD's statistics are not just incorrect, they are impossible. The claimed number of D&D-related deaths would require every teen suicide in America to be a gamer, plus hundreds more that didn't happen."

**On Media Coverage:**
"The 60 Minutes segment interviewed Patricia Pulling and Dr. Radecki extensively, but gave only brief time to defenders and failed to fact-check any claims. This pattern repeated across media coverage."

**From Joseph Laycock (Academic):**
"The Satanic Panic around D&D reveals deep anxieties about children's play, fantasy versus reality, and parental control over teenage culture. D&D became a scapegoat for normal adolescent rebellion and tragic but unrelated deaths."

**Legal Finding (Multiple Cases):**
"No court has ever found Dungeons & Dragons or any roleplaying game to be a contributing factor in suicide, murder, or any criminal activity. All such cases were dismissed or found no causation."

**TSR Response:**
"We deeply regret that some have misunderstood our games as anything other than creative entertainment. Millions play safely and enjoyably, and no credible evidence suggests any harm."
