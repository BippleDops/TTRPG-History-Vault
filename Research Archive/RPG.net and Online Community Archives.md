## Resource Overview

Online TTRPG communities transformed how gamers connected, shared knowledge, and influenced the hobby. This documentation covers major community sites that archive decades of discussion, debate, and development:

**Major Community Sites:**
- **RPG.net** (1996-present): Longest-running major RPG community
- **EN World** (2002-present): D&D-focused news and community
- **The Forge** (1999-2012): Indie design theory (covered separately)
- **Story Games** (2006-2018): Successor to Forge
- **Giant in the Playground** (2003-present): Comic and D&D community
- **Reddit r/rpg, r/DnD, r/OSR** (2000s-present): Modern community hubs
- **Discord servers**: Current primary community spaces

**Historical Value:**
- **Two decades+ of archived discussions**
- Contemporary reactions to releases and events
- Design debates and theory development
- Community conflicts and resolutions
- Cultural shifts in real-time documentation
- Product reviews and actual play reports

**RPG.net Focus**: As the oldest and most comprehensive:
- Founded 1996, pre-dating most gaming sites
- Moderated forums with archived threads
- Reviews database (20,000+ RPG reviews)
- News archive
- Cultural touchstone for online gaming community

## Key Quotes/Facts

**On Edition Wars:**
"The 4E edition war was the most polarizing event in D&D history. RPG.net threads from 2008-2010 document every argument, from thoughtful design analysis to tribal loyalty. Reading them now is like archaeological excavation of community conflict."

**On Community Memory:**
"Online forums preserve what human memory distorts. We can see exactly what people said in 2003, not what they remember saying. The gap between contemporary discussion and current memory is often striking."

**On Designer Participation:**
"RPG.net in the 2000s had active designer participation. Monte Cook, Robin Laws, John Wick, and others engaged directly with community feedback. This dialogue shaped design evolution in real-time."

**On Historical Value:**
"The review archives capture games that have been forgotten. Dozens of 2000s indie games, small press publishers, and experimental designs exist only in these archives and scattered PDFs."

Any vault seeking to understand TTRPG history beyond official publications must engage with these community archives.
