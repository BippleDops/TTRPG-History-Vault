## Resource Overview

Kickstarter's emergence as a crowdfunding platform transformed TTRPG publishing more than any development since the OGL, enabling creators to fund projects directly through community support and fundamentally changing industry economics and power dynamics.

**Timeline:**
- **2009**: Kickstarter launches
- **2010-2011**: Early RPG projects
- **2012**: Breakthrough year for TTRPG crowdfunding
- **2013-2016**: Explosive growth and maturation
- **2017-2020**: Mainstream adoption and professionalization
- **2020-2023**: Pandemic acceleration and record campaigns

**Key Impacts:**
- **Creator empowerment**: Bypassing traditional publishing
- **Community validation**: Market testing before production
- **Risk reduction**: Funding before printing
- **Professional production**: Funding enables quality
- **Niche viability**: Long tail gets longer
- **Industry restructuring**: Traditional publishers adapting

**Platform Statistics:**
- Thousands of successful TTRPG campaigns
- Hundreds of millions of dollars raised
- Success rate significantly higher than platform average
- Tabletop games as major Kickstarter category

## Key Content Summary

### Early Crowdfunding Era (2010-2011)

**Platform Introduction:**
- Kickstarter launched 2009
- All-or-nothing funding model
- Creator-backer direct relationship
- Reward tiers structure
- Community engagement emphasis

**First RPG Projects:**
- Small-scale indie games
- PDF-focused projects
- Modest funding goals ($5K-20K)
- Experimental, testing viability
- Community enthusiasm surprising creators

**Early Successes:**
- Projects funding beyond expectations
- Community eager to support
- Traditional publishing gatekeepers bypassed
- Proof of concept established

### Breakthrough Year (2012)

**Order of the Stick Reprint (Feb 2012):**
- Rich Burlew's webcomic RPG book reprint
- Goal: $57,750
- Raised: $1.25 million
- Demonstrated community funding power
- Opened eyes across industry

**Dwimmermount (April 2012):**
- James Maliszewski's OSR megadungeon
- Funded successfully
- Later became cautionary tale about fulfillment
- Showed risks alongside rewards

**Dungeon Crawl Classics RPG:**
- Goodman Games' DCC
- Successful funding
- Professional publisher using Kickstarter
- Validated model for established companies

**Critical Role (March 2019, but representing trend):**
- $750K goal for animated special
- Raised $11.4 million
- Record-breaking campaign
- Showed massive community power
- Mainstream media attention

### Growth and Professionalization (2013-2016)

**Major Successes:**
- **Numenera** (Monte Cook, 2012): $517K
- **13th Age** (2012): $196K
- **Fate Core** (2013): $433K
- **Torment: Tides of Numenera** (video game RPG, 2013): $4.2M
- **Exploding Kittens** (gaming crossover, 2015): $8.8M

**Industry Adoption:**
- Mid-sized publishers using Kickstarter
- Launch platform for new games
- Supplement and expansion funding
- Reprint and deluxe edition campaigns

**Professionalization:**
- Marketing becoming sophisticated
- Stretch goals proliferation
- Video production quality rising
- Campaign management as skill
- Fulfillment logistics complexity

**Community Evolution:**
- Backer expectations increasing
- FOMO (Fear of Missing Out) culture
- Exclusive content debates
- Late pledge management
- Community involvement in development

### Challenges and Cautionary Tales (2013-2018)

**Fulfillment Failures:**
- Dwimmermount delays and transfer
- Far West legendary delays
- Axes & Anvils disaster
- Creator skill vs. ambition gaps
- Backer frustration and backlash

**Common Problems:**
- Underestimating costs (especially shipping)
- Scope creep from stretch goals
- Manufacturing complexity
- Communication breakdowns
- Inexperience with logistics

**Platform Responses:**
- Kickstarter introducing guidelines
- Community developing skepticism
- Due diligence by backers
- Creator reputation importance
- Transparent communication crucial

**Lessons Learned:**
- Buffer in budgets essential
- Shipping costs carefully calculated
- Realistic timelines (then add buffer)
- Communication maintains trust
- Manufacturing partners vetted carefully

### Mature Market (2017-2020)

**Mega-Campaigns:**
- **Kingdom Death: Monster 1.5** (2016): $12.4M
- **Critical Role Animated Series** (2019): $11.4M
- **Frosthaven** (2020): $12.9M
- Multi-million dollar campaigns regular

**Market Segmentation:**
- Micro-projects ($1K-5K) still viable
- Mid-tier ($20K-100K) bread and butter
- Mega-projects ($500K+) rare but visible
- Each tier different strategies

**Professional Ecosystem:**
- Campaign consultants and managers
- Fulfillment services specialized for Kickstarter
- Marketing agencies for crowdfunding
- Pledge managers (BackerKit, etc.)
- Industry within industry

**Publisher Strategies:**
- Kickstarter as primary launch
- Retail release post-campaign
- Kickstarter exclusives debate
- Community building emphasis
- Direct customer relationships

## Key Quotes/Facts

**Creator Perspective:**
"Kickstarter let me make the game I wanted, not the game a publisher thought would sell. The community voted with their wallets, and I could create with complete artistic freedom while knowing I had an audience."

**Industry Impact:**
"Crowdfunding didn't just change how games get funded - it changed who gets to make games. The gatekeepers can be bypassed if you can convince the community to support your vision."

**On Challenges:**
"Every Kickstarter creator underestimates shipping costs. Then they learn. The successful ones budget better next time. The unsuccessful ones never get a next time."

**Community Voice:**
"Backing on Kickstarter feels different than buying retail. I'm part of making this exist. I'm invested in its success. I'm patient with delays because I understand the complexity."
