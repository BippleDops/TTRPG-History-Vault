## Resource Overview

The Open Gaming License (OGL) represents one of the most significant industry transformations in TTRPG history, comparable in impact to D&D's original publication. This documentation covers two major phases:

**Phase 1: OGL Creation and Impact (2000-2022)**
- Ryan Dancey's vision for open-source gaming
- D&D 3.0 launch with OGL 1.0a
- Third-party publishing explosion
- d20 boom and bust
- Pathfinder's rise via OGL
- Long-term industry restructuring

**Phase 2: OGL Crisis (2023)**
- Wizards of the Coast attempted OGL 1.1 introduction
- Community backlash and organized resistance
- Paizo's ORC License initiative
- WotC retreat and Creative Commons decision
- Industry-wide impact and future implications

Sources include official legal documents, designer statements, community archives, industry analysis, and the extensive documentation created during the 2023 crisis.

## Key Content Summary

### OGL 1.0a Creation (1999-2000)

**Ryan Dancey's Vision:**
- Wizards of the Coast brand manager saw D&D's true value as "platform not product"
- Observed network effects: D&D valuable because everyone plays it
- Recognized third-party support strengthens core product
- Modeled on open-source software licensing (GPL)
- Goal: Make D&D mechanics universal gaming language

**Legal Structure:**
- Irrevocable, perpetual license (later contested)
- System Reference Document (SRD) containing core mechanics
- Product Identity vs. Open Game Content distinction
- Allowed commercial use without royalties
- Required attribution and license inclusion

**D&D 3.0 Launch (August 2000):**
- Core rules released under OGL simultaneously
- Third-party publishers could use d20 System immediately
- Separate d20 System Trademark License for compatibility logo
- Revolutionary industry approach

### The d20 Boom (2000-2003)

**Third-Party Explosion:**
- Hundreds of publishers emerged overnight
- d20 versions of everything: sci-fi, horror, superheroes, anime
- Supplement market flooded with content
- Innovation in settings and subsystems
- Quality highly variable

**Major Success Stories:**
- Green Ronin: Mutants & Masterminds
- Mongoose Publishing: OGL exploitation
- Necromancer Games: Old-school content
- Sword & Sorcery Studios: Licensed properties
- AEG: Licensing out properties (L5R d20)

**Market Impact:**
- D&D 3.0/3.5 became bestselling RPG by wide margin
- Third-party support created network effects
- Game stores carried d20 products heavily
- "d20 compatible" became selling point

### The d20 Bust (2003-2005)

**Market Saturation:**
- Too many products, declining quality
- Retailer shelf space exhausted
- Player budgets split among too many options
- Race to bottom on pricing
- Major returns to distributors

**Publisher Casualties:**
- Many third-party publishers failed
- Consolidation among survivors
- Shift away from d20-only focus
- Market contraction

**Lessons Learned:**
- OGL enabled entry but didn't guarantee success
- Quality still mattered
- Market had finite capacity
- Differentiation necessary

### Pathfinder's OGL Success (2008-2013)

**D&D 4E Opportunity:**
- Wizards released 4E without OGL (new GSL instead)
- 4E mechanics deliberately incompatible with 3.5
- Community split between editions
- 3.5 publishers stranded

**Paizo's Response:**
- Published Pathfinder using OGL 1.0a
- Based on D&D 3.5 mechanics (OGL-legal)
- Improved and refined 3.5 design
- Maintained compatibility with 3.5 content

**Market Impact:**
- Pathfinder outsold D&D 4E at points
- Demonstrated OGL's power
- Third-party support ecosystem transferred
- Proved OGL was "irrevocable" in practice

### OGL 1.1 Crisis (January 2023)

**Leaked Document:**
- Draft OGL 1.1 leaked to gaming press
- Attempted to "deauthorize" OGL 1.0a
- Required royalties on products over $750K revenue
- Claimed rights to third-party content
- Prohibited certain content types

**Community Response:**
- Immediate massive backlash
- D&D Beyond subscription cancellations
- Social media campaign (#OpenDnD)
- Content creators paused projects
- Organized boycotts

**Industry Response:**
- Paizo announced ORC (Open RPG Creative) License
- Major publishers committed to ORC
- Legal analysis of OGL 1.0a "irrevocability"
- Existing OGL publishers declared intent to resist

**WotC Retreat (January-February 2023):**
- Initial attempts to revise OGL 1.1
- Continued community pressure
- Eventually abandoned OGL 1.1 entirely
- Released D&D 5E SRD to Creative Commons (CC-BY 4.0)
- OGL 1.0a remained valid for existing content

### The ORC License (2023-2024)

**Paizo's Alternative:**
- Developed with legal experts
- Explicitly irrevocable
- Industry-owned (not company-specific)
- Pathfinder 2E and Starfinder content under ORC
- Multiple major publishers adopted

**Industry Restructuring:**
- Publishers choosing between OGL, ORC, Creative Commons
- Uncertainty about future WotC positions
- Increased legal scrutiny of gaming licenses
- Community awareness of license importance

## Key Quotes/Facts

**Ryan Dancey on OGL Philosophy (2000):**
"The goal is to make Dungeons & Dragons the foundation for fantasy gaming, the way Windows is the foundation for PC software. If other people are making money off D&D, that's not competition - that makes D&D more valuable."

**On OGL Impact:**
"The OGL enabled more innovation in RPG design in five years than the previous twenty. Not all of it was good, but the ecosystem it created transformed the industry permanently."

**Kyle Brink (WotC Executive, 2023):**
"We got it wrong. The rollout of OGL 1.1 was a mistake, and we have heard you. We are committing the D&D 5E SRD to Creative Commons, ensuring it will always be freely available."

**Paizo Statement (2023):**
"We cannot rely on a license that the owning company can attempt to revoke. The ORC License will be genuinely open, industry-owned, and truly irrevocable."

**Legal Reality:**
"Whether OGL 1.0a was truly 'irrevocable' remains legally untested. WotC's retreat meant no court ruled on this question, leaving some uncertainty."

**Statistics:**
- **200+** publishers released d20 products (2000-2005)
- **$50+ million** in third-party d20 sales at peak
- **40,000+** D&D Beyond cancellations during crisis (reported)
- **50,000+** signatures on open letter to WotC
