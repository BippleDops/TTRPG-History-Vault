## Resource Overview

DriveThruRPG and its parent company OneBookShelf transformed TTRPG publishing by enabling digital distribution and print-on-demand, democratizing access to both classic and new games. This documentation covers:

**Platform History:**
- **RPGNow** (2001): Original PDF storefront
- **DriveThruRPG** (2004): Competing platform
- **Merger** (2006): Combined into OneBookShelf
- **Expansion**: DriveThruComics, DriveThruFiction, Wargame Vault, etc.
- **POD Integration**: Physical books on demand

**Industry Impact:**
- Long tail economics enabling niche publishing
- Out-of-print classics digitally available
- Self-publishing platform for indies
- Alternative to traditional distribution
- Global accessibility
- Preservation of gaming history

**Market Transformation:**
- Three-tier distribution disruption
- Publisher direct sales
- Price point flexibility
- Discoverability challenges
- PDF piracy and DRM debates

## Key Content Summary

### Pre-Digital Distribution Era (Pre-2001)

**Traditional Model Problems:**
- Three-tier distribution (publisher → distributor → retailer)
- Huge distributor cuts (50-60%)
- Print run minimums and costs
- Out-of-print scarcity
- Geographic limitations
- Small publisher barriers

**Early Digital Attempts:**
- Designer websites offering PDFs
- No unified marketplace
- Payment processing challenges
- Piracy concerns
- Legitimacy questions

**Need for Solution:**
- Way to reach customers directly
- Lower barriers for small publishers
- Access to out-of-print games
- International availability

### RPGNow Launch and Early Digital Sales (2001-2004)

**Platform Introduction:**
- James Mathe founded RPGNow (2001)
- Centralized PDF marketplace
- Publisher-direct sales model
- 65/35 revenue split (publisher favored)
- Digital delivery automation

**Early Adoption:**
- Small press and indie publishers eager
- Major publishers skeptical
- PDF pricing debates
- DRM and piracy concerns
- Quality control questions

**Market Development:**
- Proving viability of PDF sales
- Building customer trust
- Watermarking vs. DRM debates
- Community formation

**First Successes:**
- Indie publishers finding audiences
- Niche products commercially viable
- Out-of-print classics resurfacing
- Long tail economics demonstrated

### DriveThruRPG and Market Competition (2004-2006)

**DTRPG Launch:**
- Competing PDF marketplace
- Similar model to RPGNow
- Publisher recruitment
- Feature competition
- Market division

**Competition Benefits:**
- Platform improvement pressure
- Publisher leverage
- Customer features
- Market validation

**Merger Decision (2006):**
- OneBookShelf parent company
- Combined catalogs
- Unified platform (mostly)
- Reduced confusion
- Market consolidation

### Print-on-Demand Integration (2008-2013)

**POD Technology:**
- Lightning Source integration
- Print books on demand, no inventory
- Publisher uploads files, customers order prints
- Hardcover and premium options
- Game accessory expansion (cards, etc.)

**Advantages:**
- No print run minimums
- No inventory carrying costs
- No out-of-print (if publisher maintains files)
- Evergreen availability
- Risk reduction for publishers

**Quality Considerations:**
- POD quality vs. offset printing
- Color accuracy challenges
- Perfect binding limitations
- Premium options emergence
- Community acceptance

**Market Impact:**
- Classic games available in print again
- Small press physical books viable
- Hybrid PDF + POD model
- Collector vs. user editions

### Major Publisher Adoption (2010-2015)

**Initial Resistance:**
- Fear of cannibalizing print sales
- Distributor relationship concerns
- Pricing control issues
- PDF piracy worries

**Gradual Adoption:**
- Wizards of the Coast (limited participation)
- Paizo (strong presence)
- Chaosium (classics and current)
- White Wolf/Onyx Path (extensive catalog)
- Small to mid-sized publishers embracing

**Catalog Depth:**
- Current products
- Backlist availability
- Out-of-print classics
- Organized play scenarios
- Accessories and supplements

**Revenue Significance:**
- Major income source for small publishers
- Backlist long tail revenue
- International sales importance
- Convention-independent sales

### Crowdfunding Integration (2012-Present)

## Key Quotes/Facts

**On Industry Impact:**
"DriveThruRPG didn't just create a marketplace - it created an entire ecosystem where small publishers could survive, classic games could remain available, and niche products could find their audiences indefinitely."

**Publisher Perspective:**
"The 65/35 split means I keep more per sale than traditional distribution where I'd get maybe 20-30% after distributor and retailer cuts. Plus, my game is always available, never out of print, and I can update files when I find errors."

**On Long Tail:**
"My game sells maybe 5 copies a month. Not enough for distribution, but over years those sales add up. DriveThruRPG's long tail means I have ongoing revenue from a game I published in 2012."

**Historical Preservation:**
