# Planescape Campaign Setting (1994)

**Type**: Campaign Setting
**Parent Game**: [[Advanced Dungeons & Dragons (1977)]] (2nd Edition)
**Publisher**: [[TSR]]
**Designers**: David "Zeb" Cook (lead), team collaboration
**Published**: April 1994
**Page Count**: 192 pages (across boxed set components)
**Historical Significance**: 5/5
**Innovation Score**: 5/5
**Critical Reception**: Groundbreaking and Acclaimed

## Overview

Planescape, published in April 1994 as a boxed campaign setting for AD&D 2nd Edition, transformed the planes from exotic high-level adventuring locations into a comprehensive campaign setting centered on philosophy, belief, and the multiverse's nature. Lead designer David "Zeb" Cook built upon Jeff Grubb's [[Manual of the Planes (1987)]] foundation, creating Sigil—the City of Doors, a neutral hub at the center of the multiverse—and populating it with philosophical Factions whose beliefs literally shaped reality.

The setting distinguished itself through distinctive voice and style: "planar cant," a unique argot where everyone was "berk" or "blood," where the multiverse was "the planes" and beliefs were "the chant." This linguistic flavor, combined with Tony DiTerlizzi's revolutionary art direction emphasizing strange beauty over traditional fantasy aesthetics, created an immediately recognizable identity. Planescape was simultaneously D&D's most cerebral setting—where philosophical debates mattered as much as combat—and its most visually distinctive, with impossible architecture and reality-bending vistas.

More than just a setting, Planescape offered a framework for understanding D&D's entire cosmology. The faction system mechanized philosophy, making characters' beliefs as important as their combat abilities. The Blood War—the eternal conflict between lawful devils and chaotic demons—provided cosmic context for alignment struggles. Sigil's impossibility (a city on the inside of a torus floating above an infinite spire, ruled by an unknowable Lady of Pain) exemplified Planescape's willingness to embrace the weird, metaphysical, and philosophically complex.

## Setting Concepts

### Sigil, the City of Doors

Planescape's central location:

**Physical Description**:
- City on inside of torus (ring shape)
- Floating above Infinite Spire in Outlands
- No sky, city curves up and around
- Impossible architecture and geometry
- "Razorvine" vegetation everywhere

**The Lady of Pain**:
- Mysterious ruler of Sigil
- Silent and unknowable
- Appears as floating woman with bladed headdress
- Absolute power in city
- No one understands her motivations

**The Lady's Rules**:
- No gods in Sigil (she prevents entry)
- Those who worship her disappear or are "mazed"
- She maintains city's neutrality
- Intervenes rarely but decisively
- Cannot be fought or understood

**Portals**:
- Thousands of portals to everywhere
- Require specific "keys" (objects, actions, thoughts)
- One-way or two-way
- Permanent or temporary
- Make Sigil hub of multiverse

**Neighborhoods**:
- **The Hive**: Slums and poverty
- **The Lady's Ward**: Wealthy and powerful
- **The Clerk's Ward**: Bureaucracy and trade
- **The Guildhall Ward**: Merchants and craftsmen
- **The Lower Ward**: Industry and gates to Lower Planes
- **The Market Ward**: Commerce and trade
- **Undersigil**: Mysterious underground

**Population**:
- Cosmopolitan beyond belief
- Every planar race present
- Constant influx of travelers
- "Clueless" (Prime Material visitors) common
- Faction members everywhere

**Design Brilliance**:
Sigil solved campaign design problems:
- Central location for planar adventures
- Access to entire multiverse
- Urban adventures and planar exploration
- Neutral ground for all factions
- Impossible to fully conquer or control

The Lady of Pain ensured no player group could "solve" Sigil while providing mysterious, omnipresent authority figure.

### The Factions

Planescape's defining feature:

**Faction Concept**:
- Philosophical organizations
- Beliefs shape reality in Planescape
- Political power in Sigil
- Membership grants benefits and restrictions
- Fifteen factions initially

**The Fifteen Factions**:

**Athar** ("Defiers" or "Lost"):
- **Belief**: The gods are frauds, true divinity lies beyond
- **Base**: Shattered Temple in Lower Ward
- **Philosophy**: Reject worship, seek truth beyond gods
- **Benefit**: Resistance to divine magic

**Believers of the Source** ("Godsmen"):
- **Belief**: All beings can ascend to godhood through trials
- **Base**: Great Foundry
- **Philosophy**: Reincarnation and spiritual evolution
- **Benefit**: Mental resilience

**Bleak Cabal** ("Bleakers" or "Madmen"):
- **Belief**: The multiverse has no meaning
- **Base**: Gatehouse (insane asylum)
- **Philosophy**: Nihilism, embrace meaninglessness
- **Benefit**: Resistance to madness

**Doomguard** ("Sinkers"):
- **Belief**: Entropy is natural, hasten decay
- **Base**: Armory
- **Philosophy**: Destruction as cosmic force
- **Benefit**: Weapon proficiency and damage

**Dustmen** ("The Dead"):
- **Belief**: Everyone is already dead, seek True Death
- **Base**: Mortuary
- **Philosophy**: Life is illusion, death releases
- **Benefit**: Undead control, resistance to undead

**Fated** ("Takers" or "Heartless"):
- **Belief**: Power to the strong, might makes right
- **Base**: Hall of Records
- **Philosophy**: Social Darwinism, self-reliance
- **Benefit**: Reroll failed saves

**Fraternity of Order** ("Guvners"):
- **Belief**: Laws govern everything, learn rules to control reality
- **Base**: City Court
- **Philosophy**: Ultimate law and order
- **Benefit**: Legal and linguistic knowledge

**Free League** ("Indeps"):
- **Belief**: Freedom above all, no faction membership
- **Base**: None (deliberately)
- **Philosophy**: Independence and self-determination
- **Benefit**: Faction benefits resistance

**Harmonium** ("Hardheads"):
- **Belief**: Peace through enforced universal law
- **Base**: City Barracks
- **Philosophy**: Authoritarian order
- **Benefit**: Combat prowess

**Mercykillers** ("Red Death"):
- **Belief**: Justice through punishment, karma enforced
- **Base**: Prison
- **Philosophy**: Absolute justice, no mercy
- **Benefit**: Combat against guilty

**Revolutionary League** ("Anarchists"):
- **Belief**: Tear down all power structures
- **Base**: Secret cells
- **Philosophy**: Anarchism, permanent revolution
- **Benefit**: Stealth and sabotage

**Sign of One** ("Signers"):
- **Belief**: Each person's consciousness creates reality
- **Base**: Hall of Speakers
- **Philosophy**: Solipsism, personal reality
- **Benefit**: Mental powers

**Society of Sensation** ("Sensates"):
- **Belief**: Experience everything to understand reality
- **Base**: Civic Festhall
- **Philosophy**: Empiricism through sensation
- **Benefit**: Skill bonuses

**Transcendent Order** ("Ciphers"):
- **Belief**: Thought limits action, unity of body and mind
- **Base**: Great Gymnasium
- **Philosophy**: Zen-like action without thought
- **Benefit**: Combat initiative and reaction

**Xaositects** ("Chaosmen"):
- **Belief**: Embrace chaos, randomness is truth
- **Base**: Hive
- **Philosophy**: Chaotic behavior, unpredictability
- **Benefit**: Confusion and randomness powers

**Faction War** (Later Development):
- Metaplot event (1998)
- Factions expelled from Sigil
- Some dissolved, others transformed
- Setting evolution

**Design Significance**:
Factions made philosophy playable:
- Character identity beyond class
- Mechanical benefits for beliefs
- Political intrigue framework
- Multiverse philosophy accessible
- Complex ideas made gameable

The faction system influenced countless later RPGs exploring philosophical character identity.

### The Blood War

Cosmic conflict given depth:

**Combatants**:
- **Demons** (Tanar'ri): Chaotic evil from the Abyss
- **Devils** (Baatezu): Lawful evil from the Nine Hells
- Eternal war across Lower Planes

**Significance**:
- Explains why fiends don't conquer Material Plane
- Alignment conflict made cosmic
- Grand campaign framework
- Moral ambiguity (both sides evil)

**The Conflict**:
- Fighting since time immemorial
- No end in sight
- Devastates Lower Planes
- Neutrals caught between

**Player Involvement**:
- Adventures exploiting Blood War
- Missions for both sides
- Preventing one side's victory
- Moral complexities

**Yugoloth** (Daemons):
- Neutral evil mercenaries
- Play both sides
- Profit from eternal conflict
- Manipulation and espionage

The Blood War provided epic-scale conflict without simple good-versus-evil.

## Conclusion

Planescape Campaign Setting represents AD&D 2nd Edition's creative zenith—a setting that proved D&D could be intellectually sophisticated, visually revolutionary, and philosophically complex while remaining playable and engaging. Zeb Cook and Tony DiTerlizzi created something genuinely unique: a campaign world where ideas mattered mechanically, where strange beauty replaced generic fantasy aesthetics, and where the multiverse itself became comprehensible through elegant faction philosophy and distinctive voice.

The setting's innovations—the faction system making beliefs playable, Sigil as impossible neutral hub, planar cant creating immediate identity, the Lady of Pain as unknowable mystery—demonstrated masterful game design. Each element served multiple purposes: mechanical function, narrative coherence, aesthetic distinctiveness, and thematic reinforcement. The result felt complete and realized in ways few settings achieve.

Beyond its immediate success, Planescape expanded RPG design's possibilities. It showed that philosophical engagement could drive campaigns, that distinctive voice and visual identity created powerful setting recognition, and that complexity could be made accessible through creative presentation. The faction system influenced countless later games exploring character philosophy mechanically.

Most significantly, Planescape demonstrated D&D's ultimate flexibility. If the game could support both Ravenloft's gothic horror and Dark Sun's post-apocalyptic brutality, if it could embrace Planescape's philosophical metaphysics alongside Forgotten Realms' traditional fantasy—then D&D was truly universal framework capable of realizing any imaginative vision. This realization fundamentally changed how designers and players conceived D&D's potential.

For modern gamers, Planescape endures through Planescape: Torment's legendary status, through the Great Wheel cosmology's return in 5th Edition, and through the devoted community preserving and expanding the setting. The multiverse still beckons, Sigil's doors still lead everywhere, and the factions still debate reality's nature. The chant continues, and the blood still remember what it means to walk the planes.
