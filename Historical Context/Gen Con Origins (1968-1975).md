# Gen Con Origins (1968-1975)

**Date**: August 24-25, 1968 (First Gen Con)
**Historical Significance**: 5/5

## Overview

On August 24-25, 1968, Gary Gygax hosted the first "Gen Con" (originally "Geneva Convention" after Lake Geneva, Wisconsin) in his home, bringing together approximately 100 wargaming enthusiasts for a weekend of gaming, discussion, and community building. What began as a small gathering of hobbyists evolved into the longest-running and most influential gaming convention in the world, establishing templates for gaming conventions, creating essential infrastructure for the nascent roleplaying game hobby, and providing the social and professional networks that would give birth to Dungeons & Dragons and the TTRPG industry. The formative years of Gen Con (1968-1975) created convention culture as we know it, demonstrated the viability of gaming as community-focused hobby, and provided the crucible in which modern tabletop gaming was forged.

Gen Con's significance extends far beyond being simply a convention. It established conventions as essential industry infrastructure for product launches, networking, organized play, and community gathering. It created the model for gaming conventions worldwide. It provided the space where designers met collaborators, publishers discovered talent, and communities formed around shared passions. Dave Arneson and Gary Gygax's collaboration that created D&D emerged from Gen Con connections. TSR was born in the context of Gen Con's community. The modern gaming industry's social and professional infrastructure traces directly to what Gen Con established in those early years.

## Background and Context

### Wargaming Hobby (Pre-1968)

Before Gen Con, wargaming existed but lacked unified community infrastructure:

**Historical Miniatures**: Hobby focused on historical military miniatures and battles.

**Scattered Communities**: Local clubs and groups existed but were geographically isolated.

**Limited Communication**: Communication through mail and occasional publications.

**No Central Gathering**: No regular event bringing wargamers together nationally.

**Avalon Hill**: Board wargames from Avalon Hill provided some structure but were different hobby segment.

**Military Focus**: Predominantly focused on historical military simulation.

**Social Isolation**: Hobbyists often felt isolated without local gaming groups.

### Gary Gygax and Lake Geneva Gaming

Gary Gygax was central to midwest wargaming:

**Local Club**: Active in Lake Geneva Tactical Studies Association (precursor to International Federation of Wargaming).

**Network Builder**: Known for organizing events and building wargaming networks.

**IFW Involvement**: Involved with International Federation of Wargaming organization.

**Publishing Ambitions**: Interest in publishing wargaming materials and rules.

**Social Vision**: Understood the social and community aspects of gaming.

**Collaborative Spirit**: Believed in sharing ideas and building community.

### The IFW and Regional Gatherings

**International Federation of Wargaming**: Organization attempting to unite wargaming community.

**Regional Meetings**: Occasional regional gatherings of wargamers.

**Communication Networks**: Newsletters and correspondence networks.

**National Need**: Recognition that a national gathering would benefit the hobby.

**Organizational Challenges**: Previous attempts at large gatherings had limited success.

### Gygax's Vision

Gary Gygax envisioned a regular national gathering:

**Annual Event**: Regular convention bringing wargamers together annually.

**Open Participation**: Welcoming anyone interested, not restricted to club members.

**Gaming Focus**: Focused on actual gaming, not just business or organization.

**Community Building**: Creating and strengthening wargaming community.

**Midwest Location**: Lake Geneva as central location accessible to many.

**Affordable**: Keeping costs low to maximize participation.

## The Event Itself

### The First Gen Con (1968)

**Dates**: August 24-25, 1968

**Location**: Gary Gygax's home in Lake Geneva, Wisconsin

**Attendance**: Approximately 100 attendees

**Activities**:
- Miniature wargaming battles
- Board wargaming
- Discussions of rules and scenarios
- Socializing and networking
- Dealers selling gaming materials

**Informal Structure**: Casual, self-organized gaming rather than formal programming.

**Community Atmosphere**: Welcoming, enthusiastic atmosphere of shared passion.

**Success**: Deemed successful enough to plan future conventions.

**Foundation**: Established the basic template for future gaming conventions.

### Early Gen Cons (1969-1973)

Each Gen Con grew and evolved:

**Gen Con II (1969)**: Expanded to Horticultural Hall in Lake Geneva; ~150 attendees.

**Gen Con III (1970)**: Continued growth; ~200 attendees; more structured programming.

**Gen Con IV (1971)**: ~250 attendees; Gary Gygax's Chainmail rules previewed and sold.

**Gen Con V (1972)**: Approximately 300 attendees; growing reputation nationally.

**Gen Con VI (1973)**: ~400-500 attendees; D&D was being developed; significant expansion.

**Progressive Growth**: Each year brought more attendees and broader geographic representation.

**Increasing Organization**: Evolved from informal gathering to organized convention.

**Dealer Support**: More vendors and publishers participating.

**Tournament Play**: Organized tournaments and competitive events emerged.

### D&D Connection

Gen Con was crucial to D&D's development:

**Arneson-Gygax Connection**: Dave Arneson and Gary Gygax met and collaborated through Gen Con and related gatherings.

**Blackmoor Demonstrations**: Arneson demonstrated his Blackmoor campaign, which would inspire D&D's development.

**Playtesting Ground**: Early D&D was playtested and refined with Gen Con attendees.

**Network Effects**: The social network at Gen Con enabled the collaborations creating D&D.

**Community Feedback**: Feedback from Gen Con community influenced D&D's development.

### Gen Con VII (1974)

**Breakthrough Year**: D&D officially released at Gen Con VII.

**August 1974**: D&D had been released earlier in the year, but Gen Con was major showcase.

**50 Copies Sold**: According to some accounts, approximately 50 copies of D&D sold at the convention.

**Word-of-Mouth Begins**: Attendees spreading word about this revolutionary new game.

**New Era**: Marked beginning of roleplaying games as distinct from wargaming.

**Community Pivot**: Convention began transitioning from pure wargaming to include RPGs.

### Gen Con VIII (1975)

**D&D Explosion**: D&D had exploded in popularity; massive interest at Gen Con.

**Attendance Surge**: Attendance increased significantly due to D&D interest.

**RPG Emphasis**: Roleplaying games became major convention focus alongside wargaming.

**Industry Emergence**: Clear emergence of RPG industry as distinct business.

**Tournament Play**: D&D tournament play established, creating organized play traditions.

**TSR Presence**: TSR had significant presence as emerging game publisher.

**Cultural Shift**: Visible shift from wargaming convention to gaming convention broadly.

## Short-Term Impact (1975-1985)

### Explosive Growth

Gen Con grew dramatically through the late 1970s and 1980s:

**D&D Effect**: D&D's popularity drove massive attendance increases.

**Thousands of Attendees**: By late 1970s, attendance in thousands.

**Venue Expansion**: Repeatedly outgrew venues, requiring larger facilities.

**University of Wisconsin-Parkside**: Eventually moved to university campus to accommodate growth.

**Multi-Day Event**: Expanded from two days to three, then four days.

**Programming Expansion**: Extensive scheduled programming, tournaments, and events.

**Dealer Hall Growth**: Dealer halls became major commercial venues.

### Convention Culture Establishment

Gen Con established conventions as essential to gaming:

**Product Launches**: Major products launched at Gen Con.

**Tournament Tradition**: D&D tournament play became Gen Con staple.

**Organized Play**: Templates for organized play established.

**Celebrity Appearances**: Game designers became celebrity guests.

**Awards**: Origins Awards (after Gen Con absorbed Origins convention) recognized excellence.

**Media Coverage**: Gaming media extensively covered Gen Con.

**Community Gathering**: Annual pilgrimage for gaming community.

### Industry Infrastructure

Gen Con became essential industry infrastructure:

**Networking**: Where publishers, designers, and distributors networked.

**Deals Made**: Business deals and partnerships formed at Gen Con.

**Talent Discovery**: Publishers discovered new talent.

**Market Research**: Companies gauged interest in products and concepts.

**Competitive Analysis**: Observing competitors and market trends.

**Retailer Engagement**: Connecting with retailers and distributors.

### TSR and Gen Con Relationship

**TSR Ownership**: TSR eventually acquired Gen Con (1976).

**Corporate Event**: Gen Con became TSR corporate event.

**D&D Showcase**: Primary showcase for D&D products and events.

**RPGA Formation**: Role-Playing Game Association founded, headquartered at Gen Con.

**Tournament Central**: D&D championships and major tournaments.

**Strategic Asset**: Gen Con was strategic asset for TSR's market position.

### Regional Conventions Inspired

Gen Con inspired numerous regional conventions:

**Model Template**: Provided model for other gaming conventions.

**Regional Events**: Local and regional conventions emerged following Gen Con template.

**DunDraCon, GaryCon, Others**: Numerous conventions using Gen Con as template.

**International Spread**: International gaming conventions adopting similar formats.

**Specialized Cons**: More specialized conventions for specific games or genres.

### Cultural Traditions Established

**Costume/Cosplay**: Early costuming that would evolve into modern cosplay.

**Tournament Play**: Competitive tournament culture.

**Seminar Culture**: Educational seminars and panels.

**Community Reunions**: Conventions as annual community reunions.

### Indianapolis Era (2003-Present)

**Relocation**: Gen Con moved from Milwaukee to Indianapolis in 2003.

**Growth Continuation**: Continued growth in Indianapolis.

**City Partnership**: Strong partnership with Indianapolis city government.

**Convention Center**: Purpose-built convention facilities.

**50,000+ Attendance**: Attendance grew to over 50,000 (pre-pandemic).

**Best Four Days in Gaming**: "The Best Four Days in Gaming" marketing became reality.

### TSR Bankruptcy and Aftermath

**Gen Con Sold**: After TSR bankruptcy (1997), Gen Con sold separately.

**Peter Adkison Ownership**: Peter Adkison (former Wizards CEO) purchased Gen Con.

**Independence**: Gen Con became independent of any single game company.

**Neutrality**: Independence enabled serving entire industry neutrally.

**Stability**: Stable ownership provided continuity.
