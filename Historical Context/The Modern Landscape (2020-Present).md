# The Modern Landscape (2020-Present)

## Overview

The Modern Landscape represents gaming's most disruptive and transformative period since the hobby's founding. When COVID-19 forced the world into lockdown in March 2020, tabletop roleplaying games—inherently social, traditionally in-person activities—faced existential challenge. The response was revolutionary: massive migration to virtual tabletops, explosive growth in digital tools, new game designs optimized for online play, and unexpected commercial success that continued even as pandemic restrictions eased.

The pandemic's forced digitization accelerated trends that had been developing gradually. [[Roll20]], which had 4 million users pre-pandemic, reached 10 million by late 2021. [[Foundry VTT]], released in 2020, offered sophisticated self-hosted alternative. [[Discord]] became essential infrastructure, replacing physical game stores as primary community gathering space. Virtual conventions using platforms like [[Hopin]] and [[Gather.town]] enabled global participation. The entire hobby's social infrastructure transformed within months.

Yet the Modern Landscape isn't just about pandemic response—it represents fundamental shifts in what games can be, who makes them, and who plays them. The era saw:

**Radical Aesthetic and Thematic Diversity**: Games like [[Mork Borg]] (2020) brought doom metal aesthetics and minimalist rules. [[Wanderhome]] (2021) offered pastoral, combat-free journeys. [[Thirsty Sword Lesbians]] (2021) celebrated queer identity explicitly. These games demonstrated that RPGs could embrace any aesthetic, any theme, any identity without compromise or apology.

**Hopepunk and Cozy Gaming** emerged as reaction to cultural anxiety. Where grimdark dominated the 2010s, games like [[Wanderhome]], [[The Quiet Year]], and [[Good Society]] emphasized community, care, and emotional safety. These "cozy" games attracted audiences seeking comfort and connection rather than challenge and conflict.

**Safety Culture Normalization**: Tools like [[X-Card]], [[Lines and Veils]], and comprehensive safety frameworks became standard practice rather than optional add-ons. [[TTRPG Safety Toolkit]] compilations, session zero discussions, and consent-focused design became expected baseline, not progressive innovation.

**Creator Economy Explosion**: [[Patreon]], [[Ko-fi]], and [[itch.io]] enabled sustainable creator careers. Designers could build audiences directly, fund ongoing creation through subscriptions, and maintain independence from traditional publishers. This democratized who could make games while creating new challenges around sustainability and burnout.

**Solo RPG Renaissance**: Games designed for single-player journaling experiences proliferated. [[Ironsworn]], [[Thousand Year Old Vampire]], [[Carta]] games, and oracle-based systems showed that RPGs didn't require groups. This expanded accessibility dramatically, reaching people without local communities or schedule flexibility.

**AI Integration Debates**: [[ChatGPT]] (2022) and similar tools created passionate debates about AI's role in gaming. Could AI assist GMs? Generate content? Replace human creativity? Ethical concerns, copyright questions, and philosophical objections clashed with pragmatic enthusiasm.

**Critical Role's Evolution**: [[Darrington Press]] (founded 2020) brought Critical Role's multimedia empire into publishing. [[Daggerheart]] (2024), their flagship game, demonstrated that actual play celebrities could become major publishers. The show's continued growth proved actual play had staying power beyond pandemic novelty.

**Mainstream Consolidation**: D&D's dominance continued growing. [[Baldur's Gate III]] (2023) became gaming phenomenon, introducing millions to D&D. [[Dungeons & Dragons: Honor Among Thieves]] (2023) brought D&D to cinema successfully. The game's cultural presence reached unprecedented levels.

**Diversity and Representation Conflicts**: Debates about race in fantasy, cultural appropriation, inclusive language, and sensitivity escalated. [[Wizards of the Coast]]'s handling of racial mechanics, Hadozee controversy, and OGL crisis created community rifts. Progressive designers pushed boundaries while traditionalists pushed back. These conflicts reflected broader cultural polarization.

**The OGL Crisis** (January 2023) saw Wizards attempt to revoke the Open Gaming License, triggering industry-wide backlash. The response—creators unifying against corporate overreach, [[Paizo]]'s [[ORC License]] alternative, massive subscription cancellations—demonstrated community power while creating lasting mistrust.

**Environmental and Climate Themes**: Games increasingly engaged climate anxiety. [[Eco Mofos]], [[Yazeba's Bed & Breakfast]], and others explored environmental collapse, community resilience, and sustainable futures. This reflected younger creators' concerns about climate crisis.

The Modern Landscape represents gaming at unprecedented scale, diversity, and cultural visibility. Yet it also faces challenges: consolidation vs. independence tensions, sustainability of creator careers, AI's disruption, cultural polarization, and questions about gaming's future direction remain unresolved as the era continues evolving.

## Cultural and Gaming Context

The Modern Landscape emerged from and responded to extraordinary cultural disruption, technological acceleration, and social transformation.

**The COVID-19 Pandemic** (March 2020-present) forced global lockdowns, social distancing, and isolation. In-person gaming became impossible or risky. This drove massive virtual tabletop adoption, online community formation, and digital tool investment. What began as emergency response became permanent infrastructure—many groups continued online play even when in-person became possible.

The pandemic's isolation increased demand for social connection. Gaming provided structure for maintaining friendships, building new communities, and engaging socially while physically isolated. RPG sales grew substantially during lockdowns—people sought entertainment, creative outlets, and meaningful interaction.

**Virtual Tabletops Explosion**: [[Roll20]]'s growth from 4M to 10M+ users, [[Foundry VTT]]'s successful launch, [[Owlbear Rodeo]]'s simple elegance, and [[Alchemy VTT]]'s innovation demonstrated that online play could equal or exceed in-person experiences. Features like dynamic lighting, integrated character sheets, and dice automation became expected.

**Discord Dominance**: The platform became essential gaming infrastructure. Servers replaced forums and physical stores as community hubs. Voice channels enabled play, text channels organized discussion, and bots automated functions. By 2024, Discord had over 200 million monthly users, with gaming communities representing substantial portion.

**Creator Economy Maturity**: [[Patreon]] demonstrated that creators could sustain careers through direct audience support. Subscription models enabled ongoing creation without traditional publisher intermediaries. However, algorithm changes, platform fees, and creator burnout created sustainability challenges. The "creator economy" promised independence but often delivered precarity.

**TikTok Culture**: The short-form video platform (banned in some regions, dominant elsewhere) created new marketing and content approaches. Gaming content thrived—rules explanations, character concepts, table moments could go viral, reaching demographics traditional marketing couldn't access. However, attention fragmentation and algorithm dependence created new challenges.

**Political Polarization**: Intensifying cultural conflicts affected gaming communities. Debates about representation, cultural appropriation, "woke" content, and creator politics became intense and often toxic. [[GamerGate]]'s legacy persisted. Social media amplified conflicts. Communities fractured along political and cultural lines.

**Social Justice Movements**: [[Black Lives Matter]] (especially post-George Floyd 2020), [[#MeToo]]'s continuation, transgender rights activism, and other progressive movements influenced gaming culture substantially. Increased awareness of representation, systemic racism in fantasy tropes, and inclusive design reshaped major publishers' approaches while creating backlash from traditionalists.

**Climate Anxiety**: Younger creators and players increasingly expressed environmental concerns through game themes. Climate fiction, solarpunk, hopepunk, and games exploring community resilience reflected existential worries about environmental collapse.

**AI Emergence**: [[ChatGPT]]'s release (November 2022) demonstrated generative AI capabilities, sparking explosive interest and concern. AI could generate adventures, NPC dialogue, images, even rules text. This created efficiency possibilities while raising ethical questions about creativity, copyright, and human artistic labor.

**The Great Resignation** (2021-2022) saw workers leaving jobs for better conditions, remote work, or independent ventures. Some became full-time creators, leveraging Patreon and Kickstarter. This fed creator economy growth while creating saturated markets.

**Streaming Maturity**: Actual play had evolved from amateur webcam streams to professional productions rivaling television. [[Dimension 20]]'s studio sets, [[Critical Role]]'s multimedia empire, and [[The Adventure Zone]]'s animated series demonstrated actual play's commercial viability. However, market saturation made standing out increasingly difficult.

**NFTs and Crypto**: Brief speculation bubble (2021-2022) saw publishers experiment with blockchain gaming and NFTs. Almost all failed commercially and created substantial backlash. The crypto crash and environmental concerns made blockchain gaming largely abandoned by mainstream publishers.

**Remote Work Normalization**: The pandemic permanently shifted many jobs remote. This freed schedules, enabling weekday gaming and global groups unconstrained by geography. However, Zoom fatigue and screen burnout created challenges for online play sustainability.

**Mental Health Focus**: Increased cultural awareness of anxiety, depression, burnout, and trauma informed safety tool adoption, content warnings, and game designs emphasizing care and comfort. Therapy language entered gaming discourse—"emotional safety," "triggers," "processing trauma."

**Subscription Fatigue**: Proliferating subscription services (streaming, software, Patreon, gaming platforms) created consumer exhaustion and budget constraints. Publishers competed for limited subscription budgets.

**The Enshittification Discourse**: Growing awareness that digital platforms deliberately degraded user experience to maximize profit (Cory Doctorow's "enshittification" concept) created skepticism toward platform-dependent business models and corporate promises.

**Post-Pandemic Transition** (2022-present): As restrictions eased, communities navigated returning to in-person play, maintaining online groups, or hybrid approaches. Some groups returned enthusiastically; others preferred online convenience. The transition revealed that pandemic changes were permanent, not temporary.

## Major Releases

**Mork Borg (2020)** by [[Pelle Nilsson]] and [[Johan Nohr]] ([[Free League Publishing]]) brought doom metal aesthetics and art-as-game design to RPGs. The Swedish game featured dying world, minimalist rules, and brutally beautiful black/pink/yellow art design that made the book itself artistic object. Character creation took minutes, lethality was extreme, and the apocalypse was inevitable.

The game's radical aesthetic influenced numerous derivatives ([[CY_BORG]], [[Pirate Borg]], [[Death in Space]]). Its open license encouraged hacking and sharing. Mork Borg demonstrated that extreme aesthetics, minimalist mechanics, and art-first design could succeed commercially and critically.

**Wanderhome (2021)** by [[Jay Dragon]] ([[Possum Creek Games]]) offered radical alternative: pastoral, combat-free journeys through anthropomorphic animal communities. Players portrayed travelers helping communities, exploring nature, and building relationships. No dice, no violence, no conflict resolution—just collaborative storytelling about kindness, care, and seasonal change.

The game's GM-less structure, token-based mechanics, and explicitly queer and trans-affirming design demonstrated that RPGs could reject violence entirely while creating meaningful play. It exemplified "hopepunk" and "cozy gaming" aesthetics responding to cultural anxiety.

**Thirsty Sword Lesbians (2021)** by [[April Kit Walsh]] ([[Evil Hat Productions]]) celebrated queer identity explicitly and joyfully. The PbtA game about sword-wielding sapphics fighting oppression and falling in love featured playbooks like The Beast, The Trickster, and The Chosen. Combat was present but emotional connections were central. The game's unapologetic queerness and focus on relationships over violence attracted passionate audiences while becoming lightning rod for culture war conflicts.

**Avatar Legends (2022)** by [[Brendan Conway]] ([[Magpie Games]]) brought the [[Avatar: The Last Airbender]] universe to gaming. The PbtA game featured bending moves, balance mechanics (representing emotional/philosophical tensions), and multi-era play spanning Avatar canon. The Kickstarter raised over $9.5M, demonstrating that licensed properties aligned with progressive design could achieve massive success.

**Daggerheart (2024)** by [[Critical Role]]'s [[Matthew Mercer]] and team ([[Darrington Press]]) represented actual play celebrities' evolution into major publishers. The game featured dual d12 dice pools (Hope and Fear dice creating dramatic tension), card-based character creation, and domain play supporting group strongholds. The open playtest engaged community extensively. While reception was mixed (some praised innovation, others found it derivative), the game demonstrated Critical Role's industry influence.

**Vaesen (2020)** by [[Nils Karlén]] ([[Free League]]) brought Nordic folklore horror to gaming. Set in mythic 19th century Scandinavia, investigators dealt with vaesen—folkloric creatures and spirits. The [[Year Zero Engine]] mechanics supported investigative horror. Beautiful production, atmospheric setting, and accessible rules made it critically successful.

**Alice is Missing (2020)** by [[Spenser Starke]] ([[Hunters Entertainment]]) created silent, text-based mystery game. Players communicated only via texts (or index cards) during exactly 90-minute timed experience searching for missing Alice. The enforced silence and real-time constraint created unique emotional intensity. The game demonstrated that radical formal constraints could create powerful experiences.

**Kids on Brooms (2020)** by [[Doug Levandowski]] and [[Jonathan Gilmour]] brought magical school adventures to gaming. Influenced by [[Harry Potter]], [[The Magicians]], and similar properties, the game featured collaborative school creation, relationship dynamics, and coming-of-age themes. Its success demonstrated continued appetite for fantasy school settings despite Harry Potter franchise controversies.

**Blades in the Dark** (2015 release but continued influence 2020-present): The game's influence accelerated during this era. Forged in the Dark derivatives proliferated—[[Scum and Villainy]] (space opera), [[Band of Blades]] (military fantasy), [[Rebel Crown]] (colony revolution). The flexible framework and elegant mechanics made it foundation for numerous innovations.

**Ironsworn: Starforged (2022)** by [[Shawn Tomkin]] brought the acclaimed solo/co-op system to science fiction. The game's oracle tables, progress tracks, and momentum mechanics supported compelling solo play or GM-less groups. The successful Kickstarter ($677K) demonstrated solo gaming's commercial viability.

**Pathfinder 2nd Edition** (continued development): [[Paizo]] continuously refined and expanded PF2E through this era. The [[Remaster]] (2023-2024) removed OGL dependence post-crisis, revising core rules substantially. Despite D&D's dominance, Pathfinder maintained devoted audience through comprehensive content and careful balance.

**D&D's Continued Evolution**: While not new edition, [[Wizards of the Coast]] released major content throughout this era: [[Tasha's Cauldron of Everything]] (2020) adding custom origins, [[Van Richten's Guide to Ravenloft]] (2021) updating horror, [[Mordenkainen Presents: Monsters of the Multiverse]] (2022) revising monster lore. The controversial [[OGL 1.1]] attempt (2023) and subsequent [[Creative Commons]] release of 5.1 SRD shaped the era significantly.

## Design Trends

The Modern Landscape features extraordinary design diversity, with competing philosophies coexisting and sometimes conflicting.

**Safety Culture Integration**: Comprehensive safety tools became baseline rather than optional. Games included sections on [[Lines and Veils]], [[X-Card]], consent discussions, and content warnings. The [[TTRPG Safety Toolkit]] compilation provided comprehensive frameworks. Safety discussions became expected session zero component.

**Solo and GM-less Design**: Games increasingly supported play without groups or GMs. Oracle tables, journaling prompts, and procedural generation enabled solo experiences. [[Ironsworn]], [[Thousand Year Old Vampire]], [[Carta]] system games demonstrated commercial viability. This radically expanded accessibility.

**Hopepunk and Cozy Mechanics**: Reaction to grimdark trends brought games emphasizing care, community, and comfort. [[Wanderhome]]'s pastoral journeys, [[Balikbayan]] (2024) inspired by Filipino culture, and [[Good Society]] (Jane Austen RPG) rejected violence for social connection and emotional support.

**Environmental and Climate Themes**: Games increasingly engaged climate crisis. [[Eco Mofos]], [[The Quiet Year]]'s resource scarcity, and solarpunk games explored environmental collapse and community resilience. This reflected creator and player anxiety about climate change.

**Digital-First Design**: Games designed primarily for virtual tabletop play featured integrated automation, digital character sheets, and online-optimized procedures. This reversed traditional design assumption that digital was adaptation of physical.

**Asynchronous Play Support**: Games enabling play-by-post, forum-based, or asynchronous Discord campaigns acknowledged that scheduling was major barrier. Design accommodations for asynchronous pacing expanded accessibility.

**Card-Based Mechanics**: Using cards rather than dice appeared increasingly. [[Daggerheart]]'s card-based character creation, [[Alice is Missing]]'s timed card prompts, and various deck-based resolution systems provided alternative randomization.

**Explicit Identity Celebration**: Games like [[Thirsty Sword Lesbians]], [[Yazeba's Bed & Breakfast]], and [[Queer Pirates]] centered LGBTQ+ identities explicitly rather than treating representation as optional inclusion. This normalized queerness in gaming while creating culture war battlegrounds.

**Doom Clocks and Progress Mechanics**: Visible countdown and progress systems appeared widely. These made complex situations mechanically trackable, creating tension and clarity about situation urgency.

**Radical Aesthetics**: [[Mork Borg]]'s doom metal design influenced numerous games prioritizing visual identity as core feature. Art direction became as important as mechanical innovation.

**Modular and Toolkit Design**: Games providing frameworks for customization rather than complete prescriptive systems. [[Fate]]'s toolkit approach, [[Cortex Prime]]'s modular design, and others empowered user creativity.

**AI Integration Experiments**: Some designers experimented with AI-assisted content generation—NPC dialogue, adventure hooks, item descriptions. This proved controversial, with ethical objections about labor, copyright, and creativity clashing with pragmatic enthusiasm.

**Minimalist Rules**: Continued trend toward rules-light design. Games could be complete in pamphlet or zine format, reducing barrier to entry and purchase cost.

**Narrative Currency Evolution**: Meta-currencies enabling player narrative control appeared in sophisticated forms. [[Daggerheart]]'s Hope, various Fate point variants, and momentum mechanics gave players structured authority over fiction.

**Collaborative Worldbuilding**: First-session collaborative setting creation became standard in many games. This distributed creative labor while building player investment.

**Accessibility Features**: Dyslexia-friendly fonts, alt-text for images, colorblind-safe design, content warnings, and other accessibility considerations became design priorities rather than afterthoughts.

## Industry Developments

The Modern Landscape saw industry transformation through technological disruption, business model evolution, and consolidation tensions.

**The OGL Crisis** (January 2023): [[Wizards of the Coast]]'s attempt to revoke the Open Gaming License and impose restrictive OGL 1.1 triggered industry-wide backlash. The proposed license would have limited third-party publishing, claimed rights over creator content, and restricted VTT functionality. Community response was immediate and fierce—subscription cancellations, creator boycotts, and public outcry forced Wizards to reverse course.

[[Paizo]] responded by creating the [[ORC License]] (Open RPG Creative License), a publisher-owned alternative overseen by nonprofit foundation. Numerous publishers committed to ORC. Wizards eventually released D&D 5.1 SRD under [[Creative Commons]], though trust was damaged permanently. The crisis demonstrated community power while revealing corporate priorities and fragility of open gaming.

**Virtual Tabletop Competition**: [[Roll20]], [[Foundry VTT]], [[Fantasy Grounds]], [[Alchemy VTT]], [[Owlbear Rodeo]], and others competed for users. Roll20's market dominance faced challenges from Foundry's sophisticated self-hosting and Owlbear's elegant simplicity. Integration with character builders, automation features, and user experience became competitive differentiators.

**[[Wizards of the Coast]] acquired [[D&D Beyond]]** (2022), integrating digital tools officially. This vertical integration raised concerns about platform lock-in and monopolization while improving user experience through official support.

**Actual Play Professionalization**: [[Critical Role]]'s evolution into [[Darrington Press]], [[Dimension 20]]'s sophisticated production, and [[The Adventure Zone]]'s multimedia empire demonstrated actual play could sustain companies. However, market saturation made new shows' breakthrough increasingly difficult. Production costs escalated as audience expectations for quality increased.

**Creator Burnout and Sustainability**: Despite creator economy promises, most designers struggled financially. Patreon algorithm changes, platform fees, and market saturation created precarity. High-profile creators discussed burnout publicly, revealing sustainability challenges.

**Kickstarter Maturity and Saturation**: While campaigns continued succeeding, market became saturated. Standing out required substantial existing audience or compelling hook. Fulfillment challenges, shipping cost explosions, and backer fatigue created industry challenges.

**Consolidation Pressures**: [[Embracer Group]]'s acquisition of [[Dark Horse Media]] (including [[Free League Publishing]]) and similar consolidation raised concerns about industry corporatization. Tension between independent creators and corporate consolidation intensified.

**Direct Sales and Platform Diversification**: Creators increasingly sold directly through [[itch.io]], [[Ko-fi]], [[Gumroad]], and own websites, reducing dependence on DriveThruRPG or Amazon. This maximized revenue but required marketing expertise.

**Subscription Models**: [[Patreon]] remained primary but [[Ko-fi]] memberships, [[Substack]] for content, and various platform-specific subscriptions proliferated. Subscription fatigue created challenges as consumers faced dozens of competing subscriptions.

**International Growth**: Gaming continued globalizing, with substantial growth in Latin America, Southeast Asia, and Eastern Europe. Translation, localization, and regional publishers expanded markets.

**Convention Evolution**: Virtual conventions during pandemic continued post-pandemic as hybrid events. [[Gen Con]], [[PAX]], and others maintained in-person events while adding digital components. This expanded access while diluting physical attendance concentration.

**Retail Struggles**: Friendly local game stores faced ongoing challenges from online retail, virtual play reducing physical product demand, and consolidation pressures. Some stores thrived through community building and events; many closed.

**Academic Legitimacy**: Scholarly attention increased substantially. Universities offered game design courses, academic conferences featured RPG tracks, and dissertations examined gaming culture. This legitimized games as worthy of serious study.

**Environmental Concerns**: Shipping costs, carbon footprints, and physical production environmental impact led some publishers toward digital-first approaches. Consumer awareness of environmental costs influenced purchasing.

**The AI Debate**: Publishers' experimentation with AI-generated art and text created passionate conflicts. [[Paizo]] banned AI art in Infinite submissions. Many creators pledged AI-free content. However, pragmatic AI tool use for drafting, editing, and inspiration complicated absolutist positions.

**Unity-Among-Creators**: The OGL crisis created unprecedented industry unity against corporate overreach. Publishers, creators, and platforms coordinated responses, demonstrating collective power when motivated.

## Legacy and Influence

The Modern Landscape's legacy continues unfolding, but several patterns appear established:

**Permanent Virtual Play Infrastructure**: The pandemic-forced digital migration proved permanent. Virtual tabletops, Discord communities, and online play became standard rather than emergency measures. Geography became less constraining for group formation.

**Safety Culture Normalization**: Comprehensive safety tools, content warnings, and consent discussions became baseline expectations rather than progressive innovations. This permanently altered how games approached player comfort and emotional impact.

**Creator Economy as Viable Path**: While challenging and often precarious, direct audience support through Patreon, Ko-fi, and subscriptions enabled sustainable (if modest) creator careers outside traditional publishing. This democratized who could make games professionally.

**Diversity as Expectation**: Representation in creators, characters, and themes became expected baseline rather than special inclusion. While backlash persisted, progressive design and inclusive content became mainstream rather than fringe.

**Actual Play as Permanent Medium**: Watching others play games remained legitimate entertainment. The medium matured beyond pandemic novelty into sustainable industry supporting professional productions, careers, and multimedia empires.

**Community Power Demonstration**: The OGL crisis proved that organized community resistance could force corporate reversals. This empowered future activism while teaching corporations limits of community patience.

**Solo Gaming Viability**: Solo and GM-less games proved commercially successful and creatively valid, permanently expanding who could engage with RPGs and when.

**Digital-Physical Coexistence**: Rather than digital replacing physical, both coexisted productively. Digital tools enhanced physical play, physical books retained appeal despite digital availability, and hybrid approaches became normal.

**Aesthetic Diversity Acceptance**: Games like [[Mork Borg]] demonstrated that radical aesthetics, experimental design, and niche appeal could succeed. This expanded creative boundaries and normalized diverse approaches.

**Climate Consciousness**: Environmental themes, solarpunk alternatives to cyberpunk, and climate anxiety engagement became recognized design space, likely to continue as climate crisis intensifies.

The Modern Landscape represents gaming at unprecedented scale, diversity, and cultural visibility. Its challenges—sustainability, consolidation, polarization, AI disruption—remain unresolved as the era continues evolving. What seems certain is that the pandemic permanently transformed gaming's infrastructure, accessibility, and cultural position in ways that will shape decades ahead.
