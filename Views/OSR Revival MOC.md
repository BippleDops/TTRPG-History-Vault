# TTRPG History: The OSR Revival (2008-2015)

The OSR Revival represents a period of unprecedented creative ferment, fierce edition conflicts, and the emergence of independent game design as a major force. This era witnessed the Old School Renaissance's explosion, the Edition Wars between D&D 4E and Pathfinder, the indie story game movement's maturation, and the foundations of crowdfunding's transformation of the industry. By 2015, the TTRPG landscape had diversified beyond recognition, with dozens of viable design philosophies competing and thriving.

---

## Overview

The OSR Revival (2008-2015) was characterized by:
- **Old School Renaissance movement** reclaiming early D&D aesthetics and philosophy
- **Edition Wars** between D&D 4E, Pathfinder, and retroclones fragmenting community
- **Indie game explosion** with story games, Powered by the Apocalypse, and experimental designs
- **Crowdfunding revolution** via Kickstarter democratizing funding and publishing
- **DIY publishing** through print-on-demand and PDF distribution
- **Pathfinder ascendancy** briefly challenging D&D's market dominance
- **Design diversity** as dozens of mechanical and philosophical approaches flourished
- **Community fragmentation** into competing camps, each with distinct values and play cultures

This era transformed TTRPGs from a market dominated by a single game and publisher into a vibrant ecosystem of competing visions, each serving different player communities and aesthetic preferences.

---

## Timeline of the Era

### 2008-2009: Edition Wars and OSR Emergence

- **June 2008**: D&D 4E released, community splits immediately
- **August 2008**: Pathfinder RPG Beta released
- **2008**: [[OSRIC]] and other retroclones establish OSR
- **2009**: [[Pathfinder RPG]] final release, commercial success
- **2009**: [[Lamentations of the Flame Princess]] launches
- **2009**: [[Swords & Wizardry]] retroclone published
- **2009**: D&D 4E vs. Pathfinder competition intensifies
- **2009**: EN World and Paizo forums become battlegrounds

### 2010-2011: Indie Revolution

- **2010**: [[Apocalypse World]] by D. Vincent Baker revolutionizes design
- **2010**: [[Fiasco]] by Jason Morningstar brings story games mainstream
- **2011**: [[Dungeon Crawl Classics]] RPG announced
- **2011**: Pathfinder outsells D&D 4E (reported by industry sources)
- **2011**: OSR blogging scene thrives
- **2011**: [[Adventurer Conqueror King System (ACKS)]] released
- **2011**: Kickstarter opens to all creators

### 2012-2013: Crowdfunding Begins

- **2012**: [[Dungeon World]] brings PbtA to fantasy
- **2012**: First major RPG Kickstarters succeed
- **2012**: [[Numenera]] Kickstarter by Monte Cook ($500k+)
- **2013**: D&D Next playtest ongoing
- **2013**: [[13th Age]] by Heinsoo and Tweet
- **2013**: [[Torchbearer]] and [[Mouse Guard]] gain prominence
- **2013**: OSR and indie scenes cross-pollinate
- **2013**: Crowdfunding becomes primary funding model

### 2014-2015: Market Maturation

- **July 2014**: [[D&D Fifth Edition]] released
- **2014**: Healing edition war wounds begins
- **2014**: Kickstarter RPG market saturated
- **2015**: Diverse market firmly established
- **2015**: [[Blades in the Dark]] development (released 2017)
- **2015**: Story games, OSR, 5E, Pathfinder coexist
- **2015**: Fragmentation complete, specialization dominant

### Landmark Titles

**[[Pathfinder RPG (2009)]]**
- Paizo's continuation and refinement of D&D 3.5
- "3.75" - fixed imbalances, streamlined complexity
- Golarion campaign setting deeply integrated
- Monthly Adventure Path releases building loyal community
- Open playtest process engaging fans
- Outsold D&D 4E by 2011
- Proved edition loyalty and community power
- Demonstrated OGL's enduring value

**[[Apocalypse World (2010)]]**
- D. Vincent Baker's post-apocalyptic RPG
- "Powered by the Apocalypse" engine
- 2d6 + stat, 10+ success, 7-9 partial success
- Playbooks defining character archetypes
- "Fiction first" design philosophy
- GM principles and moves formalizing narrative techniques
- Most influential indie game of decade
- Spawned dozens of PbtA games

**[[Dungeon Crawl Classics RPG (2012)]]**
- Joseph Goodman's "old school with new mechanics"
- Dice chain: d3, d5, d7, d14, d16, d24, d30
- Funnel adventures: 0-level character meat grinder
- Gonzo magic system with unpredictable results
- Judges ruling over rules
- OSR aesthetics with innovative mechanics
- Cult following and continuing success

**[[Dungeon World (2012)]]**
- Sage LaTorra and Adam Koebel's fantasy PbtA
- Accessible story game for D&D fans
- "Describe what you do, roll the move"
- Collaborative worldbuilding
- Massive commercial success
- Introduced story games to mainstream audience
- Gateway drug for traditional players

**[[Numenera (2013)]]**
- Monte Cook's "science fantasy" setting
- Billion years future Earth
- Cypher System: effort, pools, intrusions
- "Strange" as core aesthetic
- Massively successful Kickstarter ($500k+)
- Demonstrated designer celebrity power
- Launched MCG as major publisher

**[[13th Age (2013)]]**
- Rob Heinsoo and Jonathan Tweet collaboration
- "D&D that might have been"
- Icon relationships and One Unique Thing
- Escalation die and cinematic combat
- Narrative and mechanical hybrid
- Demonstrated designer pedigree's appeal

**[[OSRIC (2006-2008)]]**
- Old School Reference and Index Compilation
- AD&D 1E retroclone
- Enabled old-school adventure publishing
- Stuart Marshall and Matthew Finch
- Legal framework for OSR movement
- Proved OGL's versatility

**[[Swords & Wizardry (2008)]]**
- Matt Finch's OD&D retroclone
- Multiple editions: White Box, Core, Complete
- Simple, flexible framework
- "Imagine the hell out of it"
- Quick Primer for Old School Gaming influential
- Community-favorite retroclone

**[[Lamentations of the Flame Princess (2010)]]**
- James Raggi's "weird fantasy" RPG
- Art-horror aesthetics
- Simplified classes, deadly combat
- Controversial and boundary-pushing content
- European weird fiction influence
- Demonstrated OSR's artistic ambitions

**[[Stars Without Number (2010)]]**
- Kevin Crawford's old-school sci-fi
- Sandbox campaign tools and GM resources
- Free PDF with deluxe paid version
- Sector generation tools
- Demonstrated "tools over story" design
- Massive commercial success via accessibility

**[[Fiasco (2009)]]**
- Jason Morningstar's Coen Brothers RPG
- No GM, collaborative storytelling
- Playset-driven scenarios
- Dice determining outcomes
- Accessible to non-gamers
- Introduced story games to mainstream
- Tabletop episode featuring Wil Wheaton

**[[Torchbearer (2013)]]**
- Thor Olavsrud and Luke Crane
- Burning Wheel meets dungeon crawling
- Resource management and grit
- Failure driving story
- Grind and camp phases
- Demonstrated narrative dungeon crawling

**[[Blades in the Dark (2015 development, 2017 release)]]**- John Harper's Doskvol heist game
- Position and effect framework
- Flashbacks and planning abstraction
- Crew playbooks and vice
- Innovation in heist genre
- Forged in the Dark engine
- Would become hugely influential post-2015

**[[D&D Fifth Edition (2014)]]**
- "Healing the edition wars"
- Streamlined mechanics with optional complexity
- Advantage/disadvantage system
- Bounded accuracy
- Modular design: "three pillars"
- Open playtest process
- Massive commercial success
- Restored D&D's dominance

### Mechanical Innovations

**[[Powered by the Apocalypse (PbtA)]] (2010)**
- 2d6 + stat resolution
- 10+ success, 7-9 partial success, 6- failure
- Playbooks defining characters
- Moves as basic and specialized actions
- "Fiction first" - describe action, find move
- GM principles and moves
- Influenced dozens of games
- Dominant indie framework

**[[Playbooks]] (2010)**
- Character archetypes with unique moves
- Advancement and progression paths
- Questions creating backstory
- Mechanical and narrative integration
- Apocalypse World innovation
- Became standard in PbtA games

**[[Advantage/Disadvantage]] (2014)**
- D&D 5E's elegant probability shift
- Roll 2d20, take higher (advantage) or lower (disadvantage)
- Replaces situational modifiers
- Simple, intuitive, powerful
- Widely adopted even outside D&D
- One of 5E's best innovations

**[[Bonds and Flags]] (2008-2012)**
- Character relationship mechanics
- Bonds in Dungeon World
- Flags signaling player interests
- Mechanical encouragement for character interaction
- Narrative hook integration

**[[Clocks]] / **[[Progress Clocks]] (2010s)**
- Visual representation of progress
- Segments filled as conditions met
- Used for threats, projects, countdowns
- Intuitive tracking tool
- Blades in the Dark popularized
- Became ubiquitous design element

**[[Flashbacks]] (2015)**
- Blades in the Dark innovation
- Plan during execution via flashbacks
- Spend stress to declare preparation
- Eliminates boring planning scenes
- Keeps action moving
- Heist genre breakthrough

**[[Position and Effect]] (2015)**
- Blades in the Dark framework
- Position: how dangerous is action?
- Effect: how much impact on success?
- Fictional positioning mechanized
- Nuanced resolution outcomes
- Influenced many subsequent designs

**[[Funnel]] (2012)**
- DCC RPG's 0-level character gauntlet
- Multiple characters per player
- Most die, survivors become 1st level
- Immediate investment in survivors
- Deadly, chaotic, memorable
- Old-school lethality embraced

**[[Dice Chain]] (2012)**
- DCC RPG's d3, d5, d7, d14, d16, d24, d30
- Variable die types for modifiers
- Tactile, dramatic shifts
- Unusual dice as feature
- Gonzo aesthetic support

**[[Icon Relationships]] (2013)**
- 13th Age innovation
- Relationship points with major NPCs/organizations
- Rolled to generate story complications/benefits
- Character integrated into world
- Collaborative worldbuilding hook

**[[One Unique Thing]] (2013)**
- 13th Age character element
- Narrative detail mechanically irrelevant
- Player creativity and investment
- "True about your character"
- Personalization without balance concerns

**[[Burning Conditions]] (Multiple games)**
- Angry, Afraid, Exhausted, Injured, etc.
- Narrative and mechanical consequences
- Mouse Guard, Torchbearer, others
- Replaces HP abstraction with fiction
- Resource management via conditions

**[[Escalation Die]] (2013)**
- 13th Age combat pacing mechanic
- Increases each round, adds to attacks
- Speeds combat, encourages aggression
- Mechanical pacing tool
- Cinematic feel

**[[Fail Forward]] (2010s)**
- Failure doesn't stop story
- Complications instead of dead ends
- Dungeon World and PbtA emphasis
- Maintains narrative momentum
- Became standard design principle

### Pivotal Moments

**[[Edition Wars Peak (2008-2011)]]**
- D&D 4E vs. Pathfinder community split
- Online forums became toxic battlegrounds
- "Grognards" vs. "4vengers" vs. "Pathfinders"
- Tribal identity around game preference
- Designers and developers engaged in debates
- Fragmented community deeply
- Gradual cooling as niches solidified

**[[Pathfinder Outsells D&D (2011)]]**
- Industry reports: Pathfinder #1 RPG
- First time D&D not market leader since inception
- Validated OGL and edition loyalty
- Demonstrated Paizo's community strategy success
- WotC took notice, influenced 5E development
- Temporary but symbolically massive

**[[Kickstarter Opens to All Creators (2011)]]**
- Previously invitation-only
- Democratized funding access
- RPG projects immediately successful
- Numenera, Feng Shui 2, many others funded
- Transformed publishing economics
- Enabled micro-publishers and first-time creators
- Changed industry permanently

**[[Numenera Kickstarter Success (2012)]]**
- Monte Cook's return: $500k+ raised
- Demonstrated designer celebrity power
- Crowdfunding as primary funding model validated
- Premium production standards set
- Influenced countless subsequent campaigns

**[[Dungeon World Release (2012)]]**
- Story games meet D&D
- PbtA goes mainstream
- Massive sales for indie game
- Gateway drug for traditional players
- Proved accessibility of story game principles

**[[D&D Next Open Playtest (2012-2014)]]**
- Unprecedented transparency from WotC
- Community engagement and feedback
- Healing edition war wounds
- "One D&D to rule them all" goal
- Modular design accommodating play styles
- Restored trust after 4E backlash

**[[D&D 5E Launch (2014)]]**
- July 2014: Starter Set released
- August 2014: Player's Handbook
- Massive commercial success
- Critical acclaim for accessibility and design
- "Healing the edition wars" succeeded
- Restored D&D's market dominance
- Set stage for mainstream breakthrough

**[[OSR Blogosphere Peak (2009-2012)]]**
- Grognardia, Playing at the World, others
- Sandbox design discussions
- Megadungeon projects
- Theoretical and practical design writing
- Community building and philosophy articulation
- Later declined as G+ and other platforms emerged

**[[Google+ Becomes RPG Hub (2011-2015)]]**
- Communities for OSR, story games, 5E, Pathfinder
- Hangouts for online play
- Designer accessibility and engagement
- Replaced forums for many communities
- Would collapse later, communities scattered

**[[Indie Game Designer & Theory Podcast Era (2010-2015)]]**
- The Walking Eye, Jennisodes, others
- Designer interviews and theory discussion
- Accessible education in game design
- Community building across distance
- Promoted indie games effectively

**[[DriveThruRPG Dominance (Ongoing)]]**
- PDF marketplace became primary distribution
- Enabled micro-publishers
- Print-on-demand integration
- Global instant access
- Reduced barriers to entry dramatically
- Traditional distribution less critical

## Cultural Context

### Community Fragmentation

**Competing Philosophies**
- OSR: "Rulings not rules," player skill, deadly dungeons
- Story games: "Fiction first," narrative authority, collaborative
- Tactical: Grid combat, optimization, mechanical depth
- Simulationist: Realistic modeling, detail, verisimilitude
- Each developed distinct communities and values

**Tribal Identities**
- Edition loyalty as personal identity
- "What game do you play?" signaling values
- Mutual incomprehension between camps
- Occasional productive cross-pollination
- Mostly parallel communities

**Online Platforms**
- EN World (traditional, Pathfinder-friendly)
- Paizo forums (Pathfinder community)
- Story Games forum (indie/story games)
- RPG.net (broad but story game lean)
- Google+ communities (everything)
- Reddit's /r/rpg emerging
- Fragmented communication

### DIY and Zine Culture

**OSR Aesthetics**
- Old-school art and layout
- Weird fantasy and horror
- European avant-garde influence
- Small-press and handmade quality
- Anti-corporate ethos

**Print-on-Demand Revolution**
- Lulu, DriveThruRPG, Amazon CreateSpace
- No inventory risk
- Global distribution instantly
- Enabled micro-publishers
- Quality improved over time

**Zine Publishing**
- Old-school inspired zines
- Knockspell, Fight On!, others
- Community content and house rules
- Amateur and professional blur
- Nostalgia meets innovation

### Accessibility and Growth

**Mainstream Awareness Growing**
- Wil Wheaton's Tabletop (Fiasco episode)
- Penny Arcade/PvP podcasts continuing
- Community/Harmon Harmontown D&D
- Geek culture acceptance increasing
- Still niche but less marginalized

**Convention Scene**
- Gen Con attendance growing
- PAX gaming tracks
- Regional conventions proliferating
- OSR and indie game presence
- Organized play programs (Pathfinder Society, Adventurers League)

**Demographics Slowly Diversifying**
- Still predominantly white and male
- Women's participation increasing
- LGBTQ+ presence more visible
- Diversity discussions beginning
- Representation in games improving slowly

## Design Philosophy Trends

### Old School Renaissance

**Core Principles**
- Rulings over rules
- Player skill over character abilities
- Emergent story from play
- Deadly and unforgiving
- Sandbox exploration
- DM neutrality and world logic
- Resource management crucial

**Theoretical Articulation**
- Quick Primer for Old School Gaming
- Principia Apocrypha (later, 2018)
- Blog posts and community discourse
- "What makes old-school old-school?"
- Aesthetics vs. mechanics debates

### Story Games / Indie Movement

**Fiction First**
- Describe action, find mechanic
- Narrative triggers mechanical resolution
- Rules support story, not replace it
- Collaborative worldbuilding
- Player authorship and agency

**Formalized Techniques**
- GM principles and moves
- Soft moves and hard moves
- Ask questions, use answers
- "Be a fan of the characters"
- Lines and veils for safety

### Hybridization

**Narrative Mechanics in Traditional Games**
- 13th Age's Icon Relationships
- 5E's Inspiration and Ideals/Bonds/Flaws
- Fate points and metacurrencies
- Advantage/disadvantage's narrative simplicity

**Mechanical Rigor in Story Games**
- Dungeon World's stats and damage
- Blades' position and effect framework
- Mouse Guard's resource management
- Structured improvisation

### Accessibility Focus

**Simplified Core, Complex Options**
- 5E's modular design
- Basic rules free, advanced optional
- Advantage/disadvantage vs. modifier math
- Bounded accuracy reducing number inflation

**Gateway Games**
- Fiasco for non-gamers
- Dungeon World for D&D-curious
- 5E Starter Set for beginners
- Simplified retroclones (White Box, others)

## Legacy and Influence

### Lasting Contributions

**Design Diversity Legitimized**
- OSR, story games, tactical, simulationist all viable
- No single "right way" to play
- Market large enough for specialization
- Cross-pollination enriching all approaches

**Crowdfunding Revolution**
- Transformed funding and publishing
- Enabled first-time creators
- Direct creator-to-consumer relationships
- Risks: overfunding, delays, Kickstarter fatigue

**PbtA as Major Framework**
- Dozens of PbtA games post-2015
- Urban Shadows, Monster of the Week, Masks, many more
- "Fiction first" became design standard
- Accessible indie game design

**OSR Aesthetics and Philosophy**
- Influenced mainstream design
- 5E incorporated old-school elements
- Sandbox and emergent play valued
- Player skill emphasis returned

**Edition War Lessons**
- Community management crucial
- Inclusive design accommodating play styles
- Open playtest building trust
- Edition transitions require care

### Impact on Future Eras

**5E Mainstream Breakthrough Foundation**
- Healing edition wars enabled growth
- Accessible design attracting new players
- Critical Role launching 2015 using 5E
- Digital tools and online play normalized

**Indie Game Maturity**
- Burning Wheel, PbtA, Fate, others established
- Indie design no longer marginal
- Innovation outside D&D thriving
- Cross-pollination with mainstream

**Crowdfunding Dominance**
- Post-2015: nearly all RPGs Kickstarted
- Publishing model transformed permanently
- Enabled incredible creativity
- Also enabled disasters and delays

**Design Sophistication**
- Theoretical frameworks articulated
- Mechanical innovation accelerating
- Narrative and mechanical synthesis
- Safety tools and accessibility prioritized
