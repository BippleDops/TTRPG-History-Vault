# Index Card RPG

**Publisher**: Runehammer Games
**Designer**: [[Hankerin Ferinale]]
**Year**: 2017
**System**: OSR (Streamlined Original)
**Genre**: Universal (Fantasy default)
**License**: Proprietary
**Status**: In Print

## Overview

Index Card RPG (ICRPG) represents the post-OSR generation's approach to old-school gaming—stripping D&D to its absolute mechanical core while rebuilding it for maximum speed, clarity, and accessibility. Created by Hankerin Ferinale (also known as "Hankerin" or "Professor Dungeon Master"), a game designer and YouTube content creator, ICRPG emerged from practical table experience and pedagogical goals: what's the minimum mechanical framework needed for D&D-style gameplay? The answer is radical simplification paired with innovative pacing mechanics that transform how tabletop RPGs handle time, difficulty, and progression.

The system's philosophical foundation rests on "simple, fast, fun"—reduce every mechanic to its essence, accelerate gameplay through streamlined procedures, and prioritize enjoyment over simulation. Where traditional D&D accumulates subsystems for different situations, ICRPG uses one mechanic for everything: roll d20, add stat, try to beat the target. Skills, saves, attacks, spells—all use this identical resolution. The unified mechanic eliminates rules lookup, reduces cognitive load, and enables new players to begin playing within minutes rather than hours of study.

ICRPG's most significant innovation is the EFFORT system, which replaces hit points with a problem-solving framework. Instead of tracking individual enemy HP, encounters have "EFFORT pools" representing the cumulative work required to overcome challenges. Defeating enemies, solving puzzles, overcoming obstacles—all use EFFORT. This abstraction shifts focus from bookkeeping to progress, making every action feel meaningful while dramatically reducing tracking requirements. The system transforms combat from tactical wargame into cooperative problem-solving, aligning mechanical reality with how people naturally think about overcoming challenges.

The game's relationship to OSR principles proves complex and generative. ICRPG embraces old-school values—high lethality, resource management, referee authority, player skill over character stats—while rejecting old-school complexity. The result feels simultaneously like stripped-down D&D and something entirely new. Veterans recognize familiar elements (d20 rolls, six abilities, class archetypes) while experiencing gameplay that flows differently from any D&D edition. This paradox—recognizably D&D yet mechanically revolutionary—positions ICRPG as bridge between OSR and modern indie design.

The multimedia presentation distinguishes ICRPG from traditional RPG publishing. Hankerin developed the game through his YouTube channel's actual play and tutorial videos, demonstrating mechanics in action before formal publication. The core books feature visual-heavy layout emphasizing tables, reference sheets, and quick-reference cards (literal index cards in many cases). This presentation prioritizes usability at the table over comprehensive rules text, reflecting Hankerin's teaching background and commitment to accessibility. The approach treats the game book as tool rather than novel—something to use during play, not read cover-to-cover beforehand.

## Historical Context

Index Card RPG emerged in 2017 during the OSR's maturation phase, when the movement had moved beyond recreating specific editions toward exploring what "old-school" meant philosophically. First-wave retroclones (OSRIC, Labyrinth Lord, Basic Fantasy) had established legal precedents and proven market viability. Second-wave games ([[Lamentations of the Flame Princess]], [[Dungeon Crawl Classics]]) had demonstrated that OSR mechanics could serve specific creative visions. By 2017, designers were asking: what happens when you take OSR principles to logical extremes? ICRPG answered by simplifying mechanics to theoretical minimum while maintaining old-school play experience.

Hankerin Ferinale came to RPG design through unusual path combining teaching, video production, and heavy metal music. His YouTube channel "Runehammer" (later "RUNEHAMMER Games") began sharing GM advice, quick-start rules, and actual play before ICRPG's formal publication. This reversed traditional publishing: rather than designing in isolation then releasing finished product, Hankerin developed publicly, incorporating viewer feedback and demonstrating mechanics through video. The channel built community before the game book existed, ensuring audience upon publication while pressure-testing mechanics through actual play.

The decision to create entirely new system rather than another retroclone reflected frustration with D&D's accumulated complexity. Hankerin wanted system teachable in single session, playable without constant rules reference, and focused on narrative momentum rather than tactical simulation. None of the existing retroclones, no matter how streamlined, met these criteria—they all carried D&D's legacy complexity. ICRPG would start from first principles: what mechanics are actually necessary? Everything else would be eliminated.

The initial release as PDF through DriveThruRPG followed indie RPG playbook rather than OSR conventions. Rather than targeting compatibility with classic adventures or emphasizing OGL licensing, ICRPG positioned itself as standalone system with proprietary mechanics. The approach reflected post-OSR mentality: learn from old-school gaming without being bound by its conventions or legal frameworks. ICRPG would use familiar elements (d20, classes, levels) while claiming freedom to innovate without maintaining compatibility.

The 2017 release timing coincided with tabletop RPG's renaissance driven by actual play shows, crowdfunding success, and mainstream cultural acceptance. Critical Role had demonstrated RPG entertainment's appeal; Kickstarter enabled ambitious publishing; D&D 5th Edition brought new players to hobby. ICRPG capitalized on expanded audience while positioning itself as accessible alternative for players frustrated by 5e's complexity or intimidated by OSR's grognard culture. The "simple, fast, fun" pitch appealed to new players seeking lower barriers to entry.

The multimedia approach leveraging YouTube proved prescient as video content became primary RPG marketing and community-building tool. Hankerin's videos demonstrated ICRPG in action, taught GMing skills applicable across systems, and built parasocial relationships with viewers who became customers. This direct creator-audience relationship, bypassing traditional distribution channels and gatekeepers, exemplified contemporary indie publishing's possibilities. The success influenced other designers to embrace video content as development tool, community builder, and marketing channel.

## What It Clones

Index Card RPG doesn't clone specific D&D edition—instead, it abstracts core D&D concepts to their simplest possible implementation, creating system that feels like D&D while being mechanically distinct. This approach makes ICRPG "post-clone"—informed by D&D's legacy without attempting to recreate any particular edition's mechanics.

The six ability scores (Strength, Dexterity, Constitution, Intelligence, Wisdom, Charisma) derive from D&D tradition but generate bonuses differently. Rather than complex modifier tables, ICRPG simply uses the score divided by 3 (rounded down) as the bonus. A character with STR 10 has +3 STR bonus; STR 15 gives +5. This calculation is instantly comprehensible and eliminates lookup tables while maintaining D&D's familiar ability score framework.

Character classes evoke D&D archetypes: Warrior (Fighter), Priest (Cleric), Mage (Magic-User), Scout (Thief/Ranger hybrid). Each class receives distinctive abilities and specializations, maintaining party roles familiar from D&D. However, classes function more as starting templates than rigid categories—characters can acquire abilities from other classes through training and advancement. This flexibility contrasts with traditional D&D's strict class boundaries while maintaining recognizable archetypes.

Combat uses d20 attack rolls against target numbers, superficially resembling D&D. However, ICRPG inverts several conventions. Armor Class becomes defensive stat added to d20 target (usually 10 + enemy AC), making better armor harder to hit as in ascending AC systems. Damage deals "EFFORT" toward defeating enemies rather than depleting hit points, fundamentally changing how combat feels despite familiar resolution mechanics.

Magic follows Vancian principles loosely—spellcasters have limited spell slots, must choose which spells to prepare, and expend slots upon casting. However, spell effects are deliberately abstract, encouraging referee and player creativity in determining specific outcomes. A "Magic Missile" spell deals damage, but exact manifestation depends on caster's description and referee's interpretation. This narrative flexibility maintains D&D's spell-slinging fantasy while reducing rules text to minimum.

Character advancement through levels recalls D&D progression. Characters gain new abilities, improved stats, and increased effectiveness as they accumulate experience. However, ICRPG's advancement is rapid—characters might gain multiple levels in single session—focusing on dramatic improvement rather than slow grinding. The faster pace maintains progression's motivational effect while avoiding months-long campaigns between meaningful improvements.

The d20 as universal resolution die comes from d20 System and modern D&D, but ICRPG applies it more consistently than any D&D edition. Skills, saves, attacks, magic—everything uses d20 roll plus ability bonus versus target number. This unification eliminates the multiple dice types, varied subsystems, and different resolution mechanics that characterize D&D across editions.

Equipment, treasure, and dungeon exploration draw from D&D traditions while simplifying implementation. Characters carry limited equipment (using inventory slots), treasure provides experience, dungeons feature rooms connected by corridors. However, ICRPG abstracts these elements to essential features, removing detailed encumbrance calculations, treasure valuation tables, and procedural complexity in favor of quick adjudication.

The referee role follows old-school model: authoritative, improvisational, focused on rulings over rules. ICRPG explicitly positions referee as final arbiter whose interpretation supersedes written rules. This philosophy echoes original D&D's "referee decides" approach while supporting it with clearer framework for consistent adjudication.

## Innovations and Differences

The EFFORT system represents ICRPG's most revolutionary innovation, fundamentally reconceptualizing how RPGs handle conflict resolution. Rather than tracking individual hit points for each enemy, encounters have EFFORT pools representing total work required to overcome challenges. A combat might have 30 EFFORT to defeat all enemies; a puzzle might require 20 EFFORT to solve; a social challenge might need 15 EFFORT to overcome. Each successful action contributes EFFORT toward completion: a sword hit deals 3 EFFORT, a clever argument contributes 2 EFFORT, a magical explosion delivers 8 EFFORT.

This abstraction creates multiple profound effects. First, it collapses different challenge types into unified framework—combat, puzzles, social encounters, and exploration all use EFFORT, making non-combat challenges mechanically equivalent to fighting. Second, it focuses attention on progress rather than depletion, making every action feel meaningful. Third, it dramatically reduces bookkeeping by tracking single number instead of multiple enemy HP totals. Fourth, it enables team coordination as players strategize about how to most efficiently contribute EFFORT.

The TIMER mechanic provides innovative pacing mechanism absent from traditional D&D. Timers are countdown clocks—usually starting at 4 and decreasing by 1 each round—representing approaching complications, escalating dangers, or limited windows. When timer reaches 0, something bad happens: reinforcements arrive, trap triggers, opportunity closes. Timers create urgency mechanically, transforming potentially slow encounters into desperate races against time. The mechanic elegantly solves problem of endless player deliberation while maintaining tension throughout encounters.

The unified d20 resolution system extends far beyond d20 System's implementation. In ICRPG, literally every uncertain action uses d20 + stat versus target. Attacking? D20 + STR/DEX versus 10 + enemy AC. Picking lock? D20 + DEX versus difficulty set by referee. Resisting poison? D20 + CON versus effect's potency. This absolute consistency eliminates rules lookup and mental context-switching, enabling gameplay to flow at unprecedented speed.

The TARGET system brilliantly simplifies difficulty assessment. Instead of variable DCs requiring referee calculation, ICRPG uses fixed targets: EASY = 10, MODERATE = 12, HARD = 15, NEARLY IMPOSSIBLE = 18. Referees simply decide which category applies, eliminating number calibration while maintaining meaningful difficulty gradation. The approach reflects teaching insight—players internalize four numbers instantly but struggle with infinite DC possibilities.

The LOOT system gamifies treasure through immediate mechanical benefits. Rather than gold pieces converted to experience points, ICRPG's loot provides direct character improvements: +1 to stats, additional abilities, special equipment. This immediate gratification maintains D&D's treasure-as-reward philosophy while accelerating its impact. Players feel meaningful progression after every session rather than waiting to accumulate enough XP for level advancement.

Character creation speed revolutionizes accessibility. A new player can create ICRPG character in under 5 minutes: roll stats, pick class, note starting abilities and equipment, done. This rapid creation enables same-session character introduction, reduces investment loss when characters die, and lowers barriers for new players. The contrast with hours-long D&D character creation is stark.

The ROOM design framework provides innovative adventure structure. Instead of detailed area descriptions, ICRPG presents rooms as: TYPE (what kind of space), TIMER (countdown to complication), EFFORT (difficulty to overcome), LOOT (reward for success). This template enables referees to improvise entire dungeons using minimal notes, focusing preparation on interesting situations rather than exhaustive details.

The HEART mechanic represents hit points but functions differently. Characters have HEART equal to 10 (roughly), determined by class and Constitution. However, HEART doesn't increase dramatically with levels—a 10th level character might have only 15-20 HEART. This flat progression maintains lethality across character advancement, preserving old-school danger even for experienced characters. The contrast with D&D's exponentially increasing HP is dramatic.

## Community and Culture

The ICRPG community centers around Hankerin's YouTube channel and Discord server, creating parasocial collective organized around creator personality as much as game system. This represents shift from traditional RPG communities organized around game properties toward creator-focused communities common in streaming and content creation culture. Fans identify as "Runehammer community" or "ICRPG enthusiasts" interchangeably, recognizing that system and creator are inseparably linked.

Community culture emphasizes accessibility, enthusiasm, and creative hacking. Members regularly share house rules, setting adaptations, and complete conversions of other games to ICRPG framework. This hacker ethos echoes open-source culture while operating within proprietary system—Hankerin encourages adaptation and sharing despite not releasing under open license. The community treats ICRPG as toolkit for creating personalized versions rather than authoritative rules to be followed strictly.

The "simple, fast, fun" slogan becomes community mantra, repeated in discussions and used to evaluate house rules or additions. When complexity creeps in, community members invoke the slogan to question whether added rules serve core values. This creates self-regulating culture that resists feature creep and maintains design coherence even as community members experiment and modify.

YouTube integration shapes community interaction fundamentally. Hankerin's regular videos provide community gathering points, with comment sections serving as discussion forums. Video demonstrations of mechanics, adventure design, and GMing techniques provide shared reference points for community conversations. The multimedia nature creates more accessible community than text-heavy OSR forums, welcoming players intimidated by dense discussions of rules minutiae.

The community demographic skews toward newer players and those seeking low-complexity systems rather than OSR veterans. Many members came to tabletop RPGs through actual play shows, discovered Hankerin's YouTube channel, and adopted ICRPG as first system. This creates community culture distinct from OSR's often-grognard character—less focused on edition comparisons, more interested in practical GMing advice and accessible play.

The rapid innovation cycle, with Hankerin regularly releasing new content and revisions, creates dynamic community culture. Rather than treating published rules as sacred, community members expect continuous evolution and participate in testing new ideas. This treats game as living design project rather than finished product, encouraging experimental mindset and comfort with change.

Third-party content creation remains active despite proprietary licensing. Community members create adventures, character options, setting material, and complete ICRPG hacks, sharing freely through Discord, Reddit, and itch.io. While technically requiring Hankerin's permission, enforcement is non-existent for non-commercial fan content. This creates quasi-open ecosystem despite legal restrictions.

The community's attitude toward other RPG systems proves generally ecumenical. Rather than positioning ICRPG as superior to all alternatives, community members acknowledge system's specific strengths (simplicity, speed) and appropriate use cases (introducing new players, fast-paced games). Many ICRPG players actively play other systems, treating ICRPG as tool in varied toolkit rather than exclusive choice.

## Legacy and Influence

Index Card RPG's influence extends beyond its direct player base, affecting how designers think about accessibility, presentation, and mechanical minimalism. The game demonstrated that OSR principles—high lethality, resource management, referee authority—could exist in systems dramatically simpler than any D&D edition. This validation encouraged subsequent designers to pursue aggressive simplification rather than assuming complex rules were necessary for satisfying gameplay.

The EFFORT system inspired similar abstraction in other games. Shadowdark RPG (2021) uses related concepts for its approach to challenge resolution. Various indie designers cited EFFORT as inspiration for unified resolution mechanics that collapse distinct challenges into common frameworks. The system proved that traditional hit point tracking wasn't necessary for satisfying combat while potentially improving pacing and reducing bookkeeping.

The TIMER mechanic influenced how subsequent OSR games handle pacing. Designers recognized that mechanical countdown clocks could create urgency more reliably than referee description alone. Games incorporating similar timing mechanisms cite ICRPG as precedent, validating mechanical pacing tools in OSR context traditionally skeptical of "gamey" abstractions.

The presentation approach—visual-heavy layout, tables over prose, reference cards over chapters—influenced indie RPG design broadly. Designers learned that playbooks, cheat sheets, and visual reference could reduce rules complexity more effectively than clearer writing. The approach proved particularly influential in games targeting accessibility and new players.

The multimedia development model, using YouTube for playtesting, community building, and marketing, established template replicated by numerous subsequent designers. The success demonstrated that video content could launch RPG careers and build audiences, encouraging designers to develop video skills alongside writing. The model proved especially appealing for designers lacking traditional publishing connections or marketing budgets.

The rapid character creation enabled by aggressive simplification influenced subsequent rules-light designs. Games recognized that reducing creation time lowered barriers to play, enabled same-session character introduction, and reduced psychological investment that made character death devastating. The lesson extended beyond OSR to broader indie RPG scene prioritizing accessibility.

The success marketing proprietary system without open licensing challenged OSR's OGL orthodoxy. ICRPG proved that generous creator community, accessible pricing, and quality content could build thriving ecosystem without legal openness. This emboldened subsequent designers to retain proprietary control while fostering fan content through policy rather than license.

For specific games, ICRPG's influence appears in Shadowdark RPG's streamlined approach, Quest RPG's accessibility focus, and various micro-RPGs pursuing extreme simplicity. The game established that "OSR" could mean philosophical approach rather than specific mechanics, that old-school play was compatible with modern design sensibilities.

The teaching orientation—explicit focus on helping new players and GMs rather than serving experienced audience—distinguished ICRPG from many OSR games assuming familiarity with D&D. This pedagogical approach influenced how subsequent designs explained concepts, structured examples, and prioritized clarity over comprehensiveness.
