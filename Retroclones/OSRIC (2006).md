# OSRIC (Old School Reference and Index Compilation)

**Full Name**: Old School Reference and Index Compilation
**Emulates**: Advanced Dungeons & Dragons 1st Edition (1977-1989)
**Designers**: Stuart Marshall, Matt Finch
**Published**: 2006 (v1.0), 2008 (v2.0 with full art)
**License**: Open Game License 1.0a
**Pages**: 400+ (v2.0)
**Historical Significance**: 5/5 - Foundational to OSR movement
**Status**: Free PDF, print-on-demand available

## Overview

OSRIC (Old School Reference and Index Compilation) is a retroclone that emulates Advanced Dungeons & Dragons 1st Edition, published under the Open Game License 1.0a. Released in 2006, OSRIC was the first major retroclone and one of the foundational documents of the Old School Renaissance (OSR) movement. Its primary purpose was not to replace AD&D 1E but to provide a legal framework for publishers to create and sell adventures, supplements, and materials compatible with AD&D 1E without risking legal action from Wizards of the Coast.

OSRIC accomplished something previously thought impossible: it used the OGL to recreate the mechanical framework of AD&D 1E in sufficient detail that adventures and supplements marked "compatible with OSRIC" were clearly compatible with AD&D 1E itself. This legal innovation opened the floodgates for OSR publishing, preserving classic playstyles and enabling a renaissance of old-school content that continues today.

The creation of OSRIC represented a pivotal moment in TTRPG history, demonstrating that the OGL could be used to preserve and extend classic games, influencing how communities would later respond to threats to the OGL and establishing the OSR as a viable publishing ecosystem.

## Historical Context

### The OGL Era (2000-2006)

**2000**: Wizards of the Coast released D&D 3rd Edition with the Open Game License (OGL), allowing third parties to publish D&D-compatible content

**2000-2003**: OGL publishing boom with hundreds of d20 System products

**2003**: D&D 3.5 revision, continued OGL success

**Problem**: While 3E/3.5E was available under OGL, earlier editions (AD&D, Basic D&D) were not. Publishers couldn't legally create new content for these out-of-print systems without licensing deals with Wizards of the Coast.

**Community Desire**: Many players preferred earlier editions' gameplay but couldn't get new content. Wizards of the Coast wasn't supporting these editions or licensing them to third parties.

### The Retroclone Insight (2005-2006)

Matt Finch and Stuart Marshall had a crucial realization: while you couldn't copy TSR/WotC's copyrighted expression of rules, you could recreate the underlying mechanical systems using the OGL. The rules themselves (attack matrices, saving throws, class progression, etc.) aren't copyrightable - only the specific wording and presentation.

**Key Innovation**: Create a new game with AD&D 1E's mechanics but entirely rewritten text, published under OGL. This "retroclone" would:
1. Be legally publishable without WotC permission
2. Emulate AD&D 1E closely enough to run classic adventures
3. Provide OGL framework for others to publish compatible content
4. Reference point allowing publishers to say "compatible with OSRIC" (and by extension, AD&D 1E)

**Legal Strategy**: The OGL allowed publication of game mechanics. OSRIC avoided TSR's copyrighted elements:
- Rewritten all rules text from scratch
- Avoided D&D-specific terms (beholder, mind flayer, etc.)
- Changed organization and presentation
- Created original artwork
- No copying of flavor text or specific examples

This created a "clean room" implementation of AD&D 1E.

## Development and Publication

### Version 1.0 (2006)

Stuart Marshall led the OSRIC project with contributions from Matt Finch and others from the Knights & Knaves Alehouse forum community.

**Initial Release**:
- Text-only PDF
- Free download
- Complete core rules
- Minimal art
- Functional but utilitarian

**Purpose**: Prove the concept worked and provide immediate legal framework for publishers

**Reception**: Skepticism mixed with excitement
- Some doubted legal validity
- Lawyers consulted confirmed it worked
- Publishers cautiously began creating OSRIC-compatible content
- Community recognized significance

### Version 2.0 (2008)

**Major Revision**:
- Professional layout
- Original artwork by Peter Mullen and others
- Expanded content
- Improved organization
- 400+ page complete game

**Print Version**:
- Print-on-demand through Lulu
- Later through Amazon/CreateSpace
- Affordable pricing
- Professional presentation

**Impact**: Legitimized OSRIC as complete game, not just reference document

### Ongoing Development

OSRIC continues to receive minor updates:
- Errata corrections
- Clarifications
- Minor rule adjustments
- Maintaining compatibility while improving usability

Stuart Marshall remains primary maintainer, with community input incorporated through feedback and errata submissions.

## Content and Design

### What OSRIC Emulates

**AD&D 1E Core Rules**:
- Character creation (race and class system)
- Combat system including initiative, THAC0, and saving throws
- Spell lists for clerics, druids, magic-users, and illusionists
- Treasure and magic items
- Monster stat blocks
- Dungeon mastering guidelines

**Mechanical Fidelity**:
- Attack matrices match AD&D 1E
- Saving throw progressions equivalent
- Class abilities and level progressions highly similar
- Spell effects replicated (when OGL-compatible)
- Treasure tables analogous

**Differences from AD&D 1E**:
- Rewritten text throughout
- No Product Identity elements (beholders, mind flayers, etc.)
- Some reorganization for clarity
- Minor mechanical tweaks for consistency
- Different examples and flavor text

### Rules Organization

**Classes**: Fighter, Paladin, Ranger, Cleric, Druid, Thief, Assassin, Magic-User, Illusionist (plus multi-class and dual-class options)

**Races**: Humans, Elves, Half-Elves, Dwarves, Halflings, Gnomes, Half-Orcs (with racial level limits and class restrictions)

**Core Mechanics**:
- d20 + modifiers vs. target number
- THAC0 (To Hit Armor Class 0) system
- Descending Armor Class
- d6-based ability score system (3-18)
- Vancian magic (memorization-based spell casting)

**Combat**:
- Initiative by side (group initiative)
- Weapon speeds and adjustments
- Detailed armor class modifiers
- Critical hits and fumbles (optional)

**Magic**:
- Spell levels 1-9 for arcane casters
- Spell levels 1-7 for divine casters
- Material, somatic, and verbal components
- Spell books and memorization

## Legal Framework and Impact

### The OSRIC License

OSRIC uses dual licensing:

**OGL 1.0a**: Core rules content released under Open Game License
- Anyone can publish compatible material
- Must include OGL and attribute OSRIC
- Can't use Product Identity without permission
- Enables commercial publication

**OSRIC Trademark**: "OSRIC" trademark held by Stuart Marshall
- Publishers can use "compatible with OSRIC" with permission
- Permission freely granted to compliant products
- Provides legal cover: "for OSRIC" means "for AD&D 1E" without saying so

**Legal Innovation**: This combination meant publishers could:
1. Create adventures using OSRIC's OGL text
2. Label them "compatible with OSRIC"
3. Sell them commercially without licensing fees
4. Have products clearly identified as AD&D 1E compatible

### Publishing Impact

OSRIC enabled an explosion of OSR publishing:

**Immediate Effect** (2006-2010):
- Small publishers began releasing OSRIC adventures
- Print-on-demand made small print runs viable
- PDFs on DriveThruRPG and RPGNow accessible worldwide
- Commercial viability of OSR content established

**Long-term Effect** (2010-Present):
- Hundreds of OSRIC-compatible products published
- Professional publishers entering OSR space
- Kickstarter campaigns funding major projects
- Sustained market for old-school content

**Industry Transformation**:
- Demonstrated out-of-print games could have commercial aftermarket
- Proved community could preserve gaming history
- Influenced other retroclones and OGL projects
- Set precedent for community-driven game support

## Influence on the OSR Movement

### Foundational Role

OSRIC was first successful retroclone, proving the concept worked:

**Before OSRIC**:
- No legal way to publish AD&D 1E-compatible content
- Classic editions considered dead
- Old-school playstyle niche
- No commercial support possible

**After OSRIC**:
- Legal framework established
- Publishers could enter market
- Players could get new content for preferred edition
- Old-school renaissance began in earnest

### Inspiring Other Retroclones

OSRIC's success inspired numerous other retroclones:

**Direct Descendants**:
- **Labyrinth Lord** (2007) - B/X D&D emulation
- **Swords & Wizardry** (2008) - Original D&D emulation
- **OSRIC** derivatives and variants

**Broader Influence**:
- Basic Fantasy RPG (started 2006, refined after OSRIC)
- Lamentations of the Flame Princess (2010)
- Numerous other OSR games

Each retroclone enabled support for different classic editions, expanding the OSR ecosystem and preserving multiple classic playstyles.

### Community Building

OSRIC helped coalesce the OSR community:

**Central Reference Point**: "OSRIC-compatible" became shorthand for AD&D 1E-style play

**Discussion Hub**: Forums discussing OSRIC connected players interested in old-school games

**Publishing Network**: OSRIC publishers formed relationships, cross-promoted, shared resources

**Conventions**: Old-school gaming presence grew at conventions, with OSRIC games run

**Online Presence**: Blogs, podcasts, and forums discussing OSR gaming proliferated

## OSRIC vs. AD&D 1E

### What's the Same

**Core Mechanics**: Attack rolls, saving throws, ability scores, level progression nearly identical

**Character Options**: Same classes, races, multi-classing, dual-classing

**Magic System**: Spell memorization, spell lists, spell effects (where OGL allows)

**Feel**: Gameplay experience closely approximates AD&D 1E

### What's Different

**Text**: Entirely rewritten, no copying from AD&D 1E books

**Organization**: Some streamlining and reorganization for clarity

**Product Identity**: No beholders, mind flayers, or other D&D-specific monsters

**Presentation**: Different layout, art, examples

**Minor Mechanics**: Some edge case rulings differ, occasional clarifications

**Lore**: No Greyhawk, Forgotten Realms, or other D&D settings

### Running AD&D 1E Adventures with OSRIC

**Compatibility**: AD&D 1E adventures run with OSRIC with minimal conversion:
- Monster stats translate directly (except Product Identity creatures)
- Treasure and magic items compatible
- Challenge levels roughly equivalent
- Spells and classes match

**Conversion Notes**:
- Replace Product Identity monsters with equivalents
- Check specific spell details (occasionally differ)
- Verify treasure table references
- No other significant changes needed

This compatibility was OSRIC's design goal and primary achievement.

## Controversies and Debates

### Legal Gray Areas

**Debate**: Is retroclone truly legal or just untested?

**Resolution**: Years without legal challenge, lawyer approval, widespread adoption established legitimacy

**Ongoing**: WotC never officially blessed retroclones, legal risk theoretically remains

### Fragmentation Concerns

**Issue**: Dozens of retroclones creating "balkanized" OSR

**Counterpoint**: Diversity allows different preferences, OGL promotes cross-compatibility

**Reality**: Community embraced variety, cross-pollination common

### Edition Purity

**Debate**: Are retroclones "pure" old-school or modern corruption?

**Positions**:
- Purists prefer original TSR editions
- Pragmatists value accessibility and legal certainty
- Modernizers appreciate improvements and clarifications

**Consensus**: Room for all approaches, personal preference
