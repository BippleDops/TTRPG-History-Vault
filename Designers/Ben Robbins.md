# Ben Robbins

**Active Years**: 2010-present
**Birth Year**: ~1972
**Nationality**: United States

Ben Robbins created the Microscope Trilogy—Microscope (2011), Kingdom (2013), Follow (2017)—demonstrating GMless collaborative world-building through elegant frameworks where Microscope creates histories through fractal zoom (eras to periods to events to scenes), Kingdom explores community through question-answering, and Follow chronicles quests through structured fellowship, influencing how designers think about collaborative creation, fractal structures, and GMless play while maintaining accessible rules enabling immediate play without lengthy setup.

## Major Works

### Microscope (2011)

Revolutionary history-creation game using fractal zoom:

**Fractal Structure**: 
- **History**: Overall timeline (rise and fall of empire)
- **Periods**: Eras within history (golden age, decline)
- **Events**: Significant moments within periods
- **Scenes**: Zooming into specific dramatic moments

**Palette**: Group establishes yes/no for content (Yes: political intrigue. No: graphic violence)

**Lens**: Rotating focus providing structure

**Time Travel**: Non-chronological play—jump anywhere in history

**Pure Creation**: No conflict resolution, just collaborative world-building

**Impact**: Became definitive collaborative history-creation tool. Used by RPG groups for world-building and writers for story development.

**ENnie Award**: Recognition for innovative design

**Widespread Use**: Beyond gaming—used for creative writing, world-building, educational contexts

### Kingdom (2013)

Exploring communities through question-answering:

**The Kingdom**: Any community (nation, organization, family, starship crew)

**Crossroads**: Questions facing community requiring answers

**Roles**: Power (decides), Perspective (advises), Touchstone (represents values)—rotating between players

**Community Evolution**: Tracking how Kingdom changes based on decisions

**Question-Driven**: Play proceeds through addressing significant questions

**Impact**: Demonstrated community-scale gaming could work without traditional adventuring. Influenced organizational/political gaming.

### Follow (2017)

Quest completion through fellowship structure:

**The Quest**: Defined goal fellowship pursues

**Fellowship Roles**: Each player takes role (Leader, Chronicler, Peacekeeper, etc.) contributing uniquely

**Challenge Resolution**: Collaborative overcoming obstacles

**Journey Structure**: Organized progression toward quest completion

**Character Arcs**: Individual development within collective quest

**Impact**: Showed how to structure quest narratives collaboratively

## Design Philosophy

Robbins emphasizes:

**Strategic Simplicity**: Minimal rules creating maximum freedom

**GMless Frameworks**: Structured collaborative play without facilitator

**Fractal Structures**: Zooming between scales (Microscope)

**Question-Driven**: Play organized around answering questions

**Collaborative Creation**: Groups building content together

**Accessibility**: Clear simple rules enabling immediate play

**Flexible Application**: Frameworks working for diverse content

## Innovations

**Fractal Zoom** (Microscope): Navigating between historical scales

**Palette System**: Pre-establishing content boundaries

**Lens Focus**: Rotating structural focus

**Crossroads Questions** (Kingdom): Question-answering driving play

**Fellowship Roles** (Follow): Distributed quest responsibilities

**Pure World-Building**: Gaming without conflict resolution

**Microscope Phenomenon**: Became standard world-building tool

**GMless Validation**: Demonstrated pure collaborative creation worked

**Writer Tool**: Used by authors for story/world development

## Reception

Praised for elegant simplicity (Microscope especially), collaborative frameworks, GMless accessibility, world-building utility. Some want more traditional RPG elements. Widespread use beyond gaming validates design.

## Legacy

Microscope became essential world-building tool used across gaming and writing. GMless frameworks influenced collaborative design. Fractal mechanics appeared in various forms. Kingdom validated community-scale play. Follow demonstrated quest structure could work GMlessly.
