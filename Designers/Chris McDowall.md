# Chris McDowall

**Active Years**: 2014-present
**Birth Year**: Unknown (maintains privacy)
**Nationality**: British

## Biography

Chris McDowall is a British game designer whose work has significantly influenced rules-light OSR (Old School Renaissance) gaming through extremely streamlined mechanical design and distinctive industrial weird fantasy aesthetic. His games—particularly Into the Odd and Electric Bastionland—demonstrate how radical simplification can enhance rather than diminish gameplay, inspiring a wave of minimalist OSR designs.

McDowall emerged from the British OSR community in the early 2010s, initially sharing design ideas through his Bastionland blog. His design philosophy developed in response to perceived problems with traditional OSR play: extensive character creation delays actual play, complex combat mechanics slow pacing, and extensive prep requirements burden GMs.

Into the Odd (2014) crystallized his design approach: characters generate in minutes through random tables, combat resolves quickly through streamlined mechanics with no attack rolls, and play begins immediately with minimal preparation. The game presents weird industrial fantasy setting—Bastion, a Victorian-ish city in strange world—supporting exploration, oddity discovery, and creative problem-solving over combat optimization.

The game gained devoted following for its radical simplicity and focus on actual play. Rather than extensive rules covering every situation, Into the Odd provides minimal mechanical framework supporting referee rulings and player-facing decisions. This approach influenced numerous subsequent rules-light OSR designs.

Electric Bastionland (2020) refined and expanded the system, replacing traditional character attributes and classes with "Failed Careers"—characters begin the game in massive debt following failed professional ventures, creating immediate adventure motivation. The expanded Bastion setting provides thousands of tables generating weird urban encounters, locations, and complications.

McDowall's design work extends beyond his signature games to layout design, illustration, and editorial work on other projects including Silent Titans with Patrick Stuart. His distinctive visual style—combining Victorian engraving aesthetics with contemporary design sensibility—influences the visual identity of British OSR products.

As independent publisher through Bastion Press, McDowall demonstrates sustainable small-scale game publishing while maintaining strong creative vision. His work shows how self-publishing enables distinctive, personal design unconstrained by commercial pressures to conform to established conventions.

## Design Philosophy

McDowall's design philosophy emphasizes radical simplification and immediate play:

**Immediate Play**: Get to actual gaming as quickly as possible. Character creation should take minutes, not hours. Prep should be minimal, not extensive.

**Streamlined Mechanics**: Reduce mechanics to minimum necessary for enabling meaningful play. Every rule should earn its presence by significantly contributing to gameplay.

**Rulings Over Rules**: Provide framework supporting referee judgment rather than comprehensive rules attempting to cover every situation. Trust referees to make fair decisions.

**Player-Facing Decisions**: Mechanics should create meaningful choices for players, not mechanical optimization puzzles. Decisions should involve fictional positioning and creative problem-solving.

**No Whiff Factor**: Combat and action resolution should produce interesting outcomes, not "you miss" non-results. Direct damage system eliminates miss-hit-miss tedium.

**Anti-Prep Design**: Design should support minimal GM preparation by providing tools for improvisation, random generation, and simple on-the-fly content creation.

**Failed Careers Over Classes**: Character identity comes from background and situation, not mechanical class features. Begin play with immediate problems demanding solutions.

**Weird Industrial Fantasy**: Fantasy gaming need not default to medieval pseudo-Europe. Industrial revolution, Victorian technology, and urban environments create distinctive settings.

**Focus on the Odd**: Embrace weirdness, oddity, and the strange. The memorable moments come from unusual situations and creative responses.

## Major Works

### Into the Odd (2014)

Into the Odd represents McDowall's breakthrough design, demonstrating how extreme streamlining enhances gameplay. The game reduces traditional OSR mechanics to bare essentials while maintaining emergent, player-driven gameplay characteristic of old-school play.

**Character Creation**: Characters generate in 5-10 minutes through random tables determining attributes (Strength, Dexterity, Willpower) via 3d6, hit points, starting equipment based on highest attribute, and an "oddity"—a strange item with ambiguous properties. Players can immediately begin playing with complete characters.

**Combat System**: The revolutionary aspect eliminates attack rolls entirely. Combat flows: both sides roll damage dice (based on weapons), highest result deals difference to opponent, armor reduces damage, reach zero HP and make critical damage saves. Combat resolves in minutes rather than hours, maintaining tension while eliminating tedious miss-hit-miss cycles.

**Exploration and Problem-Solving**: Without complex combat mechanics or class features to optimize, play focuses on exploration, creative problem-solving, negotiation, and strategic thinking. The system supports rather than constrains player creativity.

**Setting - Bastion**: Industrial weird fantasy city in strange world. Victorianish technology, bizarre underground levels, arcades filled with oddities, and exploration opportunities beyond city. The setting suggests atmosphere without requiring extensive reading.

Into the Odd influenced rules-light OSR design dramatically, demonstrating that radical simplification could enhance rather than diminish gameplay. Its direct damage system has been adopted by numerous subsequent designs, and its instant character generation influenced how designers think about play immediacy.

### Electric Bastionland (2020)

Electric Bastionland refined and expanded Into the Odd's system while deepening Bastion setting. The game represents McDowall's mature design vision incorporating years of player feedback and continued development.

**Failed Careers**: The breakthrough innovation replaces traditional character creation with "Failed Careers." Players roll or choose from 100 careers their character attempted before the game begins. Each career provides:
- Starting equipment relevant to that profession
- Debt amount from 1-50£ thousand that drives adventure motivation
- Immediate character identity and background

This creates instant character personality, clear adventure motivation (pay off massive debt), interesting starting equipment, and varied party compositions naturally. Characters might be Failed Locomotive Engineer, Failed Celebrity Chef, or Failed Revolutionary—each with distinct equipment and flavor.

**Expanded Bastion**: The setting section provides extensive random tables for generating:
- Urban encounters and complications
- Underground levels with increasingly strange content
- Failed Businesses and ventures
- Oddities, artifacts, and strange phenomena
- NPCs with distinctive characteristics

These tables support sustained urban campaigns with minimal prep, generating content during play that maintains Bastion's weird industrial atmosphere.

**Refined Mechanics**: Electric Bastionland maintains Into the Odd's core simplicity while adding elegant refinements based on extensive play. The system remains immediately accessible while supporting sophisticated emergent play.

**Production Quality**: The book features McDowall's distinctive layout design combining Victorian engraving aesthetics with contemporary clarity. The visual design perfectly complements the industrial weird setting.

Electric Bastionland won widespread acclaim for innovative character generation, refined mechanical design, comprehensive setting support, and production quality. It solidified McDowall's reputation as leading voice in rules-light OSR design.

## Innovations and Contributions

### Mechanical Innovations

**Direct Damage System**: Revolutionary combat mechanic eliminating attack rolls, speeding resolution dramatically, maintaining tension while removing tedium, and producing interesting outcomes every round. This innovation has been widely adopted across OSR designs.

**Instant Character Generation**: Five-minute character creation enabling immediate play, random tables creating interesting characters quickly, and no extended creation sessions required.

**Failed Careers**: Character background providing immediate motivation (debt), instant personality and identity, relevant starting equipment, and natural party diversity.

**Streamlined Attributes**: Three attributes (Strength, Dexterity, Willpower) covering all necessary situations, minimal bookkeeping, and simple resolution mechanics.

### Design Philosophy Contributions

**Anti-Prep Approach**: Articulated philosophy of minimizing GM preparation through random generation tools, improvisational support, simple on-the-fly content creation, and focus on play over planning.

**Rulings Over Rules**: Demonstrated viability of minimal rules frameworks supporting referee judgment, trusting referees to adjudicate fairly, and providing guidance over prescriptive rules.

**Player-Facing Decisions**: Emphasized meaningful fictional choices over mechanical optimization, creative problem-solving over tactical combat, and narrative positioning over stat manipulation.

### Setting and Aesthetic Contributions

**Industrial Weird Fantasy**: Popularized Victorian-industrial fantasy aesthetic in OSR, combining technology and magic distinctively, urban adventure focus, and industrial revolution setting.

**Visual Design**: Distinctive layout and illustration style combining Victorian engravings with contemporary design, influencing British OSR visual aesthetics.

**Bastion Setting**: Comprehensive weird urban setting supporting sustained campaigns, random generation enabling improvisation, and evocative atmosphere through efficient design.

## Influence on Later Designers

McDowall's influence on rules-light OSR design is substantial and continuing:

**Direct Damage System**: Widely adopted by subsequent designs including Cairn, Mausritter, Liminal Horror, and numerous others. The mechanic demonstrated that eliminating attack rolls improves rather than diminishes combat.

**Minimalist Design**: Inspired wave of streamlined OSR games demonstrating that radical simplification enhances accessibility and play, encouraging designers to cut ruthlessly, and validating extremely light rule sets.

**Anti-Prep Philosophy**: Influenced how designers think about GM support through random generation tools, improvisational frameworks, and minimal preparation requirements.

**Failed Careers/Background-Focused Characters**: Approach to character identity through background rather than mechanical classes influencing character generation across indie RPGs.

**Industrial Weird Aesthetic**: Popularized non-medieval fantasy settings in OSR, particularly Victorian-industrial worlds, encouraging setting distinctiveness.

**British OSR**: Central figure in British OSR community alongside Patrick Stuart, Scrap Princess, and others, helping establish distinctive British design sensibility.

Designers frequently cite Into the Odd and Electric Bastionland as major influences, and the "Odd" family of hacks and variations demonstrates the system's flexibility and influence.

## Quotes and Interviews

"The best prep is no prep. Or at least, prep that focuses on tools you'll actually use at the table—random tables, simple maps, evocative lists. Not detailed plots that players will immediately ignore."

"Attack rolls are exciting the first few times, then they're just tedium. Roll, miss. Roll, miss. Roll, hit for 3 damage. Direct damage keeps every combat round interesting because something always happens."

"Character creation that takes an hour before you can play is a problem. People want to game, not fill out character sheets. Five minutes to create a character means you're actually playing the game."

"Failed careers give immediate character identity and motivation. You're not 'a fighter.' You're a Failed Revolutionary with a printing press and revolutionary pamphlets, in massive debt, needing to fix your life. That's a character."

"Rules should enable play, not constrain it. Every rule should earn its place by making the game better. If removing a rule doesn't hurt gameplay, remove it."

"Bastion works because it's weird but playable. Strange things happen but you can still run it without extensive prep. The oddness creates memorable moments; the structure supports actual play."

## Legacy

Chris McDowall's legacy centers on demonstrating that radical mechanical simplification can enhance rather than diminish gameplay. Into the Odd and Electric Bastionland proved that extremely streamlined systems could support sophisticated emergent play, creative problem-solving, and memorable campaigns without extensive rules infrastructure.

The direct damage system represents genuine mechanical innovation, solving the "whiff factor" problem elegantly while speeding combat dramatically. Its adoption across dozens of subsequent games demonstrates its effectiveness and influence.

McDowall's "anti-prep" philosophy influenced how designers think about GM support, shifting focus from extensive preparation to improvisational tools and simple frameworks. This approach makes refereeing more accessible while supporting spontaneous, player-driven gameplay.

Electric Bastionland's "Failed Careers" approach to character generation influenced how designers think about character identity, demonstrating that background and situation can provide personality and motivation more effectively than mechanical class features.

As independent publisher through Bastion Press, McDowall demonstrates sustainable small-scale game publishing while maintaining strong creative control. His success encourages other designers to self-publish distinctive, personal projects rather than conforming to commercial expectations.

The Bastion setting demonstrates how industrial weird fantasy can provide distinctive alternative to standard medieval fantasy, influencing setting design across indie RPGs and OSR products.

Beyond specific innovations, McDowall's work validates rules-light design as sophisticated approach rather than mere simplification. His games demonstrate that minimal mechanics can support complex emergent play when designed thoughtfully.

His influence continues growing as more designers discover his work and adopt his approaches. The proliferation of "Odd" hacks and direct damage systems shows how foundational his innovations have become to contemporary indie RPG design.

For new designers, McDowall's work demonstrates the power of focused design vision, ruthless simplification, and trusting players and referees. His example shows that innovation often comes from subtraction rather than addition, and that the best rules are often the ones you don't need.
