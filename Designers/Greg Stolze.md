# Greg Stolze

**Active Years**: 1995-present
**Birth Year**: 1970
**Nationality**: United States

Greg Stolze is one of modern RPG design's most innovative mechanical architects, best known for creating the One Roll Engine (ORE) system and co-designing Unknown Armies. Stolze's career demonstrates remarkable range, spanning transgressive horror (Unknown Armies), superhero gaming (Godlike, Wild Talents), fantasy kingdom management (Reign), and investigative horror (Delta Green). His mechanical innovations—particularly matched sets dice mechanics and organizational-scale gameplay—influenced countless subsequent designs. Beyond game design, Stolze pioneered alternative publishing models, successfully funding projects through patronage and serialized fiction long before crowdfunding became mainstream. His work balances mechanical elegance with thematic resonance, creating systems where dice mechanics reinforce narrative concerns.

## Early Life and Career

Greg Stolze grew up in the 1970s and 1980s, absorbing the formative era of roleplaying games alongside diverse literary influences ranging from postmodern fiction to occult philosophy. Unlike many designers whose formation centered exclusively on gaming, Stolze developed broad intellectual interests in philosophy, psychology, and transgressive literature that would profoundly shape his design work.

Stolze studied creative writing and philosophy, bringing literary sensibilities to game design. His fiction writing—characterized by dark humor, psychological insight, and willingness to explore uncomfortable subjects—parallels his game design approach. The same thematic concerns appear in both media: power and corruption, identity and madness, the tension between individual agency and systemic forces.

He entered professional game design in the mid-1990s, contributing to various projects before achieving recognition with Unknown Armies. This period saw Stolze developing his design voice: mechanically innovative, thematically ambitious, willing to tackle subjects other designers avoided. His early work demonstrated the intellectual sophistication and mechanical creativity that would characterize his career.

Stolze's background in fiction writing shaped his design philosophy. He approached games as storytelling engines, creating mechanics that generate specific narrative textures rather than simulating physics. This literary sensibility, combined with genuine mechanical innovation, made him distinctive among peers.

## Major Works

### Unknown Armies (1998)

Co-designed with John Tynes for Atlas Games, Unknown Armies became one of the most influential occult horror games, pioneering mechanical and thematic approaches that influenced the story games movement and modern horror gaming broadly.

**Setting and Themes**: Unknown Armies presents contemporary occult underground where magic derives from obsession, conspiracy theories might be true, and the godwalker gambit—the struggle to become living archetypes of humanity's collective unconscious—drives secret history. The game explores power through lenses of madness, obsession, and transgression, asking what you're willing to sacrifice for significance.

Unlike traditional urban fantasy, Unknown Armies grounds magic in psychology and postmodern philosophy. Adepts gain power through obsession with symbolic acts—drinking, violence, bodily transformation. Avatars embody archetypal identities like the Flying Woman or the Demagogue, gaining power by fulfilling archetypal patterns. This psychological grounding makes magic simultaneously more personal and more disturbing than traditional spell systems.

**Madness Meters**: Stolze's signature innovation was the madness meter system, tracking psychological response to violence, the unnatural, helplessness, isolation, and self-destruction. Characters possess gauges for each type of trauma, with hardened and failed notches representing psychological adaptation and breakdown.

When facing traumatic stimuli, players roll against appropriate gauge. Success means coping; failure means stress checks, potentially gaining disorders or new hardened notches. This mechanizes psychological realism: exposure hardens you to specific traumas while potentially breaking you in new ways. A combat veteran might be hardened to violence but vulnerable to supernatural horror. This nuanced approach to mental health influenced numerous subsequent games.

The system creates mechanical teeth for horror gaming. Characters can't simply shrug off trauma; psychological consequences accumulate mechanically. Yet the system also recognizes adaptation—hardening represents survival mechanisms even as they transform characters. This sophisticated model transcends simple "sanity as hit points."

**Obsession Skills**: Characters define obsession skills—singular focuses providing broad competency within specific areas. An obsessed chef might use their obsession for cooking tasks, knife fighting, and understanding hierarchies (from kitchen brigade structure). This freeform approach to competency anticipated trait-based systems while maintaining concrete mechanical implementation.

**Magic Systems**: Multiple magic systems reflect different philosophical approaches. Adepts practice obsessive symbolic behaviors; Avatars embody archetypes; Rituals manipulate cosmic symbols. This plurality demonstrates Stolze's worldbuilding sophistication—magic isn't monolithic but rather diverse philosophical approaches to power.

**Cultural Impact**: Unknown Armies influenced story games and modern horror gaming profoundly. The madness meters inspired psychological consequence systems across numerous games. The urban occult setting anticipated contemporary weird fiction movements. The game's willingness to engage transgressive subject matter expanded design boundaries.

### Godlike (2001) and Wild Talents (2006)

Stolze's superhero games demonstrated the One Roll Engine's versatility while exploring superhuman power through unexpected lenses.

**Godlike** presented World War II superheroes ("Talents") fighting in historical battles. Unlike conventional superhero gaming emphasizing four-color adventure, Godlike explored how superhuman power would function in actual warfare's brutal context. Talents died to artillery barrages, moral compromises, and friendly fire. The game asked difficult questions: How do you maintain humanity while possessing godlike power? What does "heroism" mean in mechanized warfare? How do extraordinary individuals function within military hierarchies?

The historical grounding made superhuman power visceral and troubling rather than escapist fantasy. Characters might be bulletproof yet still experience psychological trauma. Powers provided tactical advantages yet couldn't prevent war's moral degradation. This mature approach to superhero gaming influenced subsequent serious treatments of the genre.

**Wild Talents** expanded the system to other eras and power levels, from Victorian psychics to contemporary four-color heroes. The expanded ORE system provided granular customization, letting players build nearly any conceivable superpower through unified framework.

**Company Rules**: Stolze developed rules for playing entire organizations—superhero teams, military units, corporations. Players managed organizational resources, internal politics, and strategic operations alongside individual character actions. This dual-scale play let campaigns address both personal stories and organizational dynamics, expanding roleplaying's scope beyond individual adventurers.

The Company rules influenced organizational play systems across numerous games, demonstrating how RPGs could address collective action and institutional dynamics.

### Reign (2007)

Reign showcased Stolze's ambition to create fantasy gaming where characters ruled kingdoms, commanded armies, and shaped civilizations rather than merely adventuring.

**The Setting**: The fantasy world of Helusia and the Milieu served primarily as demonstration setting for the game's real innovation—playing rulers rather than adventurers. Characters might be monarchs, generals, revolutionary leaders, or merchant princes operating at civilization scale.

**Company Rules Refined**: Reign elaborated Unknown Armies' proto-organizational rules into sophisticated system for managing kingdoms, armies, churches, and other institutions. Organizations possessed statistics, took actions, competed with rivals, and evolved over time. Players made decisions about taxation, military campaigns, diplomatic relations, and infrastructure development.

This shifted fantasy gaming's focus from personal heroics to strategic and political leadership. A session might involve commanding armies, negotiating treaties, managing resources, and dealing with internal dissent alongside traditional adventuring. The system enabled campaigns spanning years of in-game time and geographic scales traditional gaming couldn't address.

**One Roll Engine Evolution**: Reign featured the ORE system's most refined iteration, with streamlined mechanics and extensive customization options. The system handled everything from individual combat to mass battles to organizational conflicts through unified framework.

**Influence**: Reign influenced kingdom management and organizational play across subsequent designs. Games like Blades in the Dark's crew rules and various OSR domain management systems acknowledge Reign's innovations.

### Delta Green

Stolze contributed extensively to Delta Green, the Cthulhu Mythos game about modern conspiracy and the cost of fighting cosmic horror.

His work on Delta Green emphasized institutional dysfunction, moral compromise, and psychological cost. Characters serve a conspiracy fighting extraterrestrial and supernatural threats while maintaining civilian cover identities, manipulating federal agencies, and committing crimes in service of protecting humanity. The game explores how good people become monsters when fighting monstrous threats.

Stolze co-authored Delta Green: The Role-Playing Game (2016) for Arc Dream Publishing, adapting the setting to a standalone system. His design emphasized the tension between duty and humanity, career and conspiracy, sanity and mission requirements. The sanity system built on Unknown Armies' psychological sophistication, tracking how horror and moral transgression degrade agents.

His Delta Green work exemplifies mature horror gaming: emphasizing consequences, moral complexity, and psychological realism rather than combat tactics or cosmic trivia.

### Other Notable Works

**A Dirty World** (2008): Noir RPG using modified ORE, exploring moral degradation and identity in noir contexts.

**Better Angels** (2013): Superhero/villain game where players control each other's demonic possessors, creating conflicts between heroic impulses and villainous compulsions.

**The Kerberos Club** (2010): Victorian-era superpowered game exploring Strange powers in Imperial Britain.

## The One Roll Engine (ORE)

Stolze's signature mechanical innovation is the One Roll Engine, appearing in Godlike, Wild Talents, Reign, A Dirty World, and other games.

**Core Mechanic**: Players roll pools of d10s, looking for matched sets—multiple dice showing the same number. The height (the number shown) indicates quality; the width (how many dice match) indicates speed/effect. A roll might generate multiple matched sets, each enabling different actions or effects.

Example: Rolling 10d10 yielding 2, 2, 5, 5, 5, 7, 8, 9, 10, 10 produces:
- A 2×2 (two 2s): low quality, fast action
- A 3×5 (three 5s): medium quality, moderate speed
- A 2×10 (two 10s): high quality, slower action

The player chooses which set to use based on tactical situation. Speed determines initiative; height determines effect. Multiple sets might allow multiple simultaneous actions.

**Innovations**:

**Single Roll Resolution**: One roll determines initiative, success quality, and effect simultaneously, accelerating play and creating dramatic uncertainty.

**Tactical Depth**: The multiple-set possibility creates meaningful tactical choices. Do you use the fast low-quality action or wait for high-quality effect? Can you act multiple times with multiple sets?

**Scalability**: The same mechanic handles individual actions, vehicle combat, mass battles, and organizational conflicts by adjusting dice pools and interpretation frameworks.

**Customization**: Particularly in Wild Talents, the system enables granular effect customization. Superpowers combine qualities, extras, and limitations to create nearly any conceivable ability through unified framework.

**Narrative Resonance**: The matched-sets mechanic feels distinctive, creating memorable rolls. Finding multiple sets in single roll generates exciting moments; failing to match any dice creates dramatic setbacks.

**Influence**: While ORE didn't achieve ubiquity like d20 or PbtA, it influenced designers seeking alternatives to linear dice probabilities. The concept of extracting multiple data points from single roll inspired various subsequent mechanics.

## Design Philosophy

Stolze's design philosophy balances mechanical innovation with thematic integrity:

**Mechanics Must Reinforce Theme**: Rules should generate appropriate narrative texture. Unknown Armies' madness meters make psychological horror mechanical reality. ORE's speed/quality trade-offs create cinematic action. Company rules enable political gameplay. Mechanics aren't neutral—they shape play toward intended experiences.

**Sophistication Without Condescension**: Games can address mature, complex subjects while remaining accessible. Unknown Armies tackles transgression and madness without sensationalism. Delta Green explores moral compromise without preaching. Godlike examines superhuman power's troubling implications. Stolze trusts players with difficult material.

**Innovation Within Accessibility**: Revolutionary mechanics should enhance rather than obstruct play. ORE is innovative yet learnable; madness meters are sophisticated yet functional; Company rules are ambitious yet playable. Innovation serves play experience, not designer ego.

**Systemic Thinking**: Design systems, not just discrete elements. Unknown Armies' magic systems interlock with madness mechanics and setting metaphysics. Reign's Company rules integrate with individual advancement and combat. Everything should connect to coherent whole.

**Player Agency and Consequences**: Meaningful choices should have meaningful consequences. Characters pursuing obsession risk madness; rulers making bad decisions see kingdoms crumble; Delta Green agents choosing mission over morality lose their humanity. Agency matters because choices matter.

**Embrace Complexity When Justified**: Some subjects demand complex systems. Kingdom management, organizational conflict, superhero power construction—these justify sophisticated mechanics. Complexity should emerge from necessity, not ornamentation.

**Literary Sensibility**: Games are storytelling engines. Design should enable specific story types—noir degradation, political intrigue, cosmic horror, superhuman warfare. Literary awareness informs mechanical choices.

## Innovations and Contributions

### Mechanical Innovations

**One Roll Engine**: Matched-sets dice pool extracting multiple data points from single roll, influencing various subsequent experimental dice mechanics.

**Madness Meters**: Nuanced psychological consequence system tracking trauma types, hardening, and breaking points. Influenced psychological systems across modern horror gaming.

**Obsession Skills**: Freeform competency system based on singular character focuses, anticipating various trait-based approaches.

**Company Rules**: Organizational-scale gameplay enabling campaigns about kingdoms, armies, and institutions rather than just individual adventurers.

**Height/Width Mechanics**: Extracting quality and speed from single roll through matched sets, creating tactical depth from unified mechanic.

**Multiple Magic Systems**: Showing how different philosophical approaches to power can coexist systematically within single setting.

**Spray Attacks and Called Shots**: ORE's granular combat options enabling cinematic tactical choices.

### Conceptual Contributions

**Psychology in Horror**: Sophisticated treatment of trauma, obsession, and identity in horror gaming, transcending simple sanity mechanics.

**Mature Superhero Gaming**: Demonstrating superhero gaming could address serious subjects—warfare, moral compromise, power's corrupting nature—without abandoning genre excitement.

**Organizational Gaming**: Proving RPGs could handle collective action, institutional dynamics, and strategic-scale decision-making.

**Magic as Psychology**: Grounding supernatural power in character psychology and philosophy rather than mere spell lists.

**Literary Game Design**: Bringing literary sophistication and awareness to game design, treating games as worthy of artistic ambition.

### Publishing Innovations

**Patronage Model**: Long before Kickstarter, Stolze funded projects through direct patronage, with supporters funding serialized fiction and game development. This pioneered creator-owned sustainable development.

**Serialized Game Content**: Releasing fiction and game material episodically, creating ongoing reader/player engagement.

**Creator Independence**: Maintaining creative control while building sustainable audience relationships, modeling alternatives to traditional publishing.

## Legacy and Influence

Greg Stolze's influence on modern RPG design manifests across multiple domains:

**Mechanical Innovation**: The One Roll Engine demonstrated alternatives to conventional dice mechanics. While not achieving universal adoption like d20, ORE influenced designers seeking innovative resolution systems. The height/width concept appears in various forms across experimental designs.

**Psychological Horror**: Unknown Armies' madness meters influenced how games mechanize psychological consequences. Modern horror games from Delta Green to various story games acknowledge this innovation. The shift from simple sanity mechanics to nuanced trauma systems partially derives from Stolze's work.

**Organizational Play**: Reign's Company rules influenced organizational gaming across numerous subsequent designs. From Blades in the Dark's crew mechanics to various kingdom management systems, designers learned from Stolze's systematic approach to collective action.

**Mature Themes**: Stolze's willingness to engage transgressive subjects, moral complexity, and psychological realism expanded what games could address. His work demonstrated commercial viability of sophisticated, adult-oriented gaming without sacrificing accessibility or fun.

**Superhero Gaming**: Godlike and Wild Talents showed superhero gaming could transcend four-color escapism to explore power's implications seriously. This influenced subsequent mature superhero games and demonstrated genre's flexibility.

**Publishing Models**: His patronage approach pioneered creator-owned sustainable development, anticipating crowdfunding culture and demonstrating alternatives to traditional publishing.

**Literary Gaming**: Stolze helped legitimize treating game design as literary craft worthy of artistic ambition. His work bridges genre fiction, postmodern literature, and game design.

Contemporary designers from Jason Morningstar to John Harper acknowledge Stolze's influence. His games remain in print and active development decades after publication. The Unknown Armies 3rd Edition (2017) brought his innovations to new audiences while expanding the madness meter system.

Stolze proved mechanical innovation and thematic sophistication could coexist, creating accessible yet ambitious games addressing complex subjects through elegant systematic frameworks. His legacy ensures future designers continue exploring psychology, power, and consequences through mechanically innovative lenses.

## Quotes and Interviews

"The madness meters came from frustration with sanity as hit points. Real psychological trauma is more complex—you adapt to specific stressors while potentially breaking in other ways. The system needed to model that nuance."

"The One Roll Engine happened because I wanted one roll to tell me everything: who acts first, how well they succeed, what effects they cause. Looking for matched sets in dice pools achieved that elegance."

"Unknown Armies asks what you'd sacrifice for significance. Magic requires obsession—you gain power by distorting your life around symbolic behavior. That's not comfortable, but it's honest about power's cost."

On Godlike: "Superheroes in World War II shouldn't feel like comic books. Artillery doesn't care if you're bulletproof. We wanted the moral complexity and horror of actual warfare with superhuman power added—not superhero escapism with a WWII coat of paint."

"Company rules emerged from wanting to play rulers, not just adventurers. Most fantasy gaming assumes you're wandering mercenaries. But what if you're the king? How do you mechanize tax policy, military strategy, and diplomatic relations while maintaining playability?"

On design philosophy: "Mechanics aren't neutral. Your dice system creates specific play experiences. Random tables generate different stories than drama-based resolution. Design must align mechanics with intended experience."

"I trust players with difficult material. Unknown Armies addresses transgression and madness; Delta Green deals with moral compromise and institutional evil. These are legitimate subjects for art, and games are art. Treating players as adults means engaging difficult subjects seriously."
