# Alex Roberts

**Active Years**: 2016-present
**Birth Year**: ~1985
**Nationality**: United States

Alex Roberts created For the Queen (2019—card-prompted GMless collaborative storytelling) and Star Crossed (2019—two-player doomed romance using Jenga tower), demonstrating how elegant minimal frameworks could create compelling narratives accessible to non-gamers, with For the Queen becoming breakout success (Evil Hat's best-seller) spawning dozens of "Descended from the Queen" hacks, influencing card-based GMless design and showing story games could achieve mainstream commercial success through accessibility and clever design.

## Major Works

### For the Queen (2019)

Card-prompted GMless storytelling game where players draw cards with questions about the Queen, your journey, and relationships, collaboratively building narrative toward Queen's fate:

**Accessibility**: Playable by anyone, no RPG experience needed

**Card Prompts**: Questions on cards guide narrative ("The Queen knows your secret. What is it?")

**GMless**: Pure collaborative storytelling, no facilitator

**The Queen Card**: When drawn, triggers endgame—do you protect or betray the Queen?

**Viral Success**: Became Evil Hat's best-selling title, outselling many traditional RPGs

**"Descended from the Queen"**: Spawned 50+ hacks using card-prompt framework for different premises

**Cultural Phenomenon**: Played at parties, non-gaming contexts, introduced thousands to narrative gaming

**Impact**: Demonstrated ultra-accessible story games could achieve massive commercial success

### Star Crossed (2019)

Two-player doomed romance using Jenga tower:

**Jenga Mechanic**: Every interaction requires pulling Jenga block—when tower falls, consummate or separate

**Two-Player Romance**: Minimal prep instant play for couples/pairs

**Doomed Love**: Romance is fated, question is ending (together or apart)

**Tension Building**: Physical Jenga tension mirrors romantic tension

**Accessible**: Non-gamers can play immediately

**Impact**: Demonstrated physical props could enhance emotional gaming. Validated two-player romance games.

### Tension (2016)

Earlier game exploring interpersonal tension mechanics, presaging Star Crossed's success.

## Design Philosophy

Roberts emphasizes:

**Accessibility**: Making story games welcoming to non-gamers

**Minimal Frameworks**: Simple elegant structures enabling creativity

**Card-Based Design**: Using cards for prompting and pacing

**Romance Gaming**: Validating romantic relationships as compelling content

**GMless Accessibility**: Removing GM barrier for entry-level play

**Physical Props**: Jenga, cards creating tangible engagement

**Diverse Audiences**: Designing for beyond traditional hobby demographics

## Innovations

**Card-Prompt Framework**: For the Queen's question-card system

**Jenga Romance**: Physical tower tension mirroring emotional tension

**Ultra-Accessible GMless**: Story games playable at parties by non-gamers

**Descended from Queen**: Creating hackable framework spawning ecosystem

**For the Queen Phenomenon**: Evil Hat's best-seller, massive mainstream reach

## Reception

Praised for brilliant accessibility, For the Queen's viral success, Star Crossed's Jenga innovation, welcoming design. Some traditional gamers find it "not a real RPG" but that misses point—it's successfully reaching different audiences.

## Legacy

For the Queen became phenomenon. Descended from the Queen framework produced thriving ecosystem. Demonstrated accessible design could achieve unprecedented commercial success. Influenced how designers think about accessibility and non-gamer audiences.
