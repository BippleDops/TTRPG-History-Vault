# Sean Patrick Fannon

**Active Years**: 1989-present
**Birth Year**: 1967
**Nationality**: American
**Status**: Active

## Biography

Sean Patrick Fannon (born 1967) is an American game designer, writer, and industry advocate best known for creating Shaintar, extensive work with Savage Worlds, writing The Fantasy Roleplaying Gamer's Bible, and decades of community building through organized play, conventions, and gaming advocacy. His career spans multiple game lines and publishers, always emphasizing accessibility, storytelling, and building inclusive gaming communities.

Born in 1967, Fannon discovered Dungeons & Dragons in the early 1980s during the game's explosive growth. Like many teenagers of the era, he was immediately captivated by roleplaying's creative possibilities. However, Fannon also recognized gaming's potential for community building, education, and social connection beyond mere entertainment. This understanding would shape his career's trajectory toward advocacy and community development alongside design work.

Fannon pursued higher education studying communications and writing, developing skills that would serve his game writing and advocacy work. During college, he ran games extensively, organized gaming groups, and began thinking about how games could be designed to facilitate storytelling and community rather than merely providing mechanical frameworks for tactical play.

In 1989, Fannon entered the gaming industry professionally, initially freelancing for various publishers. His early work demonstrated versatility across game lines and strong writing skills. However, his breakthrough came with The Fantasy Roleplaying Gamer's Bible (1996), a comprehensive reference guide to fantasy roleplaying games.

The Gamer's Bible represented ambitious project—surveying fantasy RPG landscape comprehensively, providing guidance for players and gamemasters, reviewing major game systems and settings, discussing design philosophy and play styles, and serving as educational resource for newcomers and veterans alike. The book demonstrated Fannon's encyclopedic knowledge of the hobby and commitment to making gaming accessible.

While not a game itself, the Gamer's Bible established Fannon's reputation as industry expert and advocate. The book helped players discover games beyond D&D, educated newcomers about the hobby's diversity, and provided framework for thinking about different gaming approaches. It represented Fannon's belief that gaming benefits from broad accessibility and informed consumer education.

Throughout the 1990s and early 2000s, Fannon contributed to numerous game lines. He wrote supplements for GURPS, Shadowrun, and other major games. He designed adventures, created settings, and developed mechanical content across various systems. This breadth of experience gave him understanding of different design philosophies and mechanical approaches.

However, Fannon's defining creative work began in the mid-2000s when he created Shaintar, a high fantasy setting initially designed for multiple game systems but eventually finding its home with Savage Worlds. Shaintar represented Fannon's vision for heroic fantasy emphasizing cooperation, redemption, and fighting against darkness threatening civilization.

Shaintar's setting borrowed from classic high fantasy—multiple races working together, ancient evil threatening the world, heroes rising to defend civilization, magic and technology coexisting, and emphasis on heroism and sacrifice. However, Fannon developed the world with extensive detail and thematic coherence. The setting featured the Kal-A-Nar Empire (evil force threatening the free peoples), the Shaintar Confederation (alliance of races and nations opposing darkness), detailed cultural development for each race and nation, and comprehensive history explaining current conflicts.

What distinguished Shaintar was Fannon's commitment to making it living world through organized play. Rather than static setting waiting for individual groups to activate through home campaigns, Shaintar existed as shared campaign world where organized play events contributed to evolving metaplot. Players' actions in convention games and organized play sessions affected official setting developments, creating investment and community ownership.

This organized play approach built active Shaintar community. Players returned to conventions anticipating how their previous missions affected the setting. Game masters collaborated on maintaining consistency across multiple play groups. The setting evolved based on aggregate player choices, creating unique collaborative storytelling across distributed play groups.

Shaintar's partnership with Savage Worlds proved synergistic. Savage Worlds' fast-paced mechanics and emphasis on pulp action aligned perfectly with Fannon's vision for heroic fantasy. The system's accessibility allowed focusing on story and character development rather than mechanical mastery. Savage Worlds' popularity provided platform for Shaintar to reach wider audience than many independent fantasy settings.

Fannon developed extensive Shaintar content—campaign books detailing regions and cultures, adventure collections providing missions, player's guides offering character options, game master resources providing tools and guidance, and fiction establishing setting's tone and history. This comprehensive support created complete campaign framework rather than merely describing static world.

Beyond Shaintar, Fannon contributed extensively to Savage Worlds' development and promotion. He wrote adventures, created settings, designed supplements, and served as advocate promoting Savage Worlds' virtues. His work helped establish Savage Worlds as major alternative to d20 System dominance, demonstrating that fast-paced, story-focused systems could succeed commercially.

Fannon also worked on Necessary Evil, a superhero setting for Savage Worlds where players control supervillains forced to defend Earth after alien invasion defeats superheroes. The setting explored moral complexity—villains becoming heroes, cooperation despite mutual antagonism, and redemption through necessity. This demonstrated Fannon's interest in stories transcending simple good versus evil dynamics.

Throughout his career, Fannon has been tireless gaming advocate. He organized conventions, ran organized play programs, mentored new gamers and designers, promoted inclusivity in gaming communities, and advocated for gaming's educational and social benefits. This advocacy work distinguished him from designers focused solely on creative output.

Fannon's convention work particularly impacted gaming communities. As organizer and frequent guest, he created welcoming environments for diverse participants. He ran games for newcomers, educated players about different game systems, facilitated networking among industry professionals, and promoted gaming as inclusive hobby rather than exclusive subculture.

In recent years, Fannon has worked with Evil Hat Productions, contributing to Fate system products and continuing his advocacy work. He maintains active presence on social media, podcasts, and streaming platforms, discussing gaming, interviewing designers, and promoting positive gaming culture.

Currently, Fannon continues designing, writing, and advocating while developing new projects. His career demonstrates that game design encompasses more than creating mechanics—it includes community building, education, advocacy, and working to make gaming accessible and welcoming to diverse participants.

## Design Philosophy

Sean Patrick Fannon's design philosophy emphasizes accessibility, storytelling, and community building over mechanical complexity or simulation.

**Fast-Paced Gameplay**: Games should maintain narrative momentum rather than bogging down in mechanical resolution. Fannon favors systems like Savage Worlds that resolve actions quickly, allowing focus on story development rather than combat tactics or rules adjudication. Long combat scenes or extensive mechanical resolution break immersion and slow storytelling pacing.

**Story Over Mechanics**: Rules should facilitate storytelling rather than dominating play experience. Mechanical systems exist to support narrative, not vice versa. When rules get in the way of good story, they should be modified or ignored. This philosophy prioritizes dramatic appropriateness over mechanical consistency.

**Accessibility and Inclusion**: Games should welcome newcomers rather than intimidating them with complexity. Fannon designs for broad audiences, avoiding unnecessary complexity and providing clear guidance. He believes gaming's growth depends on accessibility—making games learnable, affordable, and welcoming to diverse participants.

**Heroic Fantasy**: Fannon gravitates toward heroic fantasy where protagonists fight against genuine evil, make meaningful sacrifices, and work together toward common goals. While acknowledging moral complexity, he values stories about heroism, redemption, and hope rather than grimdark pessimism or morally ambiguous protagonists.

**Collaborative Storytelling**: The best gaming experiences emerge from collaboration between gamemaster and players. Fannon's designs emphasize shared narrative authority rather than gamemaster dictating story. Players should contribute meaningfully to setting development and narrative direction through character choices and actions.

**Living Worlds**: Settings should evolve based on player actions rather than remaining static backdrops. Fannon's organized play approach treats settings as shared spaces where player choices affect canon development. This creates investment and ownership beyond individual gaming groups.

**Community Building**: Game design should facilitate community formation. Organized play, convention events, and shared campaign worlds create communities that sustain gaming beyond individual gaming tables. Fannon believes strong communities support hobby growth and provide social benefits beyond entertainment.

**Character Development**: Mechanical advancement should reflect character growth narratively. Experience shouldn't merely increase numbers—it should represent character learning, growth, and development. Fannon values systems where mechanical progression connects to narrative arc rather than existing as separate gamification.

**Multiple Systems, Consistent Worlds**: Good settings should work across different mechanical systems. Shaintar's initial multi-system approach reflected Fannon's belief that setting and mechanics are somewhat separable. While eventually focusing on Savage Worlds, he maintained that world-building should prioritize narrative consistency over mechanical requirements.

**Redemption and Hope**: Fannon's stories frequently explore redemption—villains becoming heroes, fallen cultures restored, darkness overcome through sacrifice. He believes gaming should offer hope and possibility rather than merely reflecting real-world cynicism.

**Education and Advocacy**: Designers should educate players about gaming's diversity and advocate for inclusive communities. Fannon's reference works, convention organizing, and community building reflect belief that designers have responsibility beyond creating products to supporting healthy gaming culture.

## Notable Works

### Shaintar (2004)

Shaintar represents Fannon's vision for heroic high fantasy combined with organized play creating living campaign world.

**Setting Overview**:

Shaintar presents classic high fantasy world where multiple races unite against darkness threatening civilization. The Kal-A-Nar Empire—ancient evil empire—threatens the Shaintar Confederation's free peoples. Heroes rise to defend their homes, seek redemption for past failures, and fight against seemingly overwhelming odds.

The setting features diverse races including humans, elves, dwarves, dregordians (dragonkin), goblins seeking redemption from evil heritage, and other fantasy species. Each race has distinct culture, history, and role in broader geopolitical landscape.

**Thematic Elements**:

Cooperation over conflict: Races must work together to survive
Redemption: Characters can overcome dark pasts through heroism
Sacrifice: Victory requires personal cost and difficult choices
Ancient evil: Kal-A-Nar represents existential threat requiring heroism
Magic and technology: Both coexist creating varied adventure possibilities
Heroic fantasy: Characters are heroes fighting genuine evil

**Organized Play Innovation**:

Shaintar's organized play program created living campaign where player actions in convention games and organized sessions affected setting canon. Game masters received missions contributing to evolving metaplot. Players' successes and failures influenced which regions fell to darkness, which heroes rose to prominence, and how major conflicts resolved.

This created investment beyond individual gaming sessions. Players followed setting developments eager to see how their actions contributed. Convention attendance grew as players returned for continuation of shared story. The setting became collaborative creation rather than author's fixed vision.

**Comprehensive Support**:

Fannon developed extensive support materials:
- Core campaign setting books detailing geography, cultures, history
- Regional supplements exploring specific areas in depth
- Adventure collections providing missions integrated into metaplot
- Character options expanding race and class choices
- Game master guides offering tools and advice
- Fiction establishing tone and bringing setting alive

**Savage Worlds Integration**:

Shaintar found ideal mechanical home in Savage Worlds. The system's fast-paced combat supported heroic action. Character creation's flexibility allowed varied concepts. The simple core mechanics kept focus on story rather than rules mastery. Wild Cards and Bennies mechanized protagonists' dramatic importance and heroic determination.

Fannon adapted Savage Worlds mechanics to support setting themes—specific Edges for racial abilities, magical systems reflecting setting's approach to magic, rules for fighting Kal-A-Nar corruption, and mechanical frameworks supporting heroic fantasy tone.

**Community Building**:

Shaintar built dedicated community through organized play, online forums, convention events, and social media engagement. Players formed lasting friendships through shared campaigns. Game masters collaborated on maintaining consistency. The setting demonstrated how games could create communities transcending individual gaming groups.

**Commercial and Critical Reception**:

While never achieving mainstream commercial success of major game lines, Shaintar succeeded within its niche. It demonstrated that independent fantasy settings could sustain commercial viability through community building and organized play. Critical reception praised the setting's depth, organized play innovation, and comprehensive support.

### The Fantasy Roleplaying Gamer's Bible (1996)

The Gamer's Bible established Fannon as industry expert and gaming advocate through comprehensive survey of fantasy roleplaying.

**Content Overview**:

The book provided extensive coverage:
- History of roleplaying games and hobby development
- Comprehensive reviews of major fantasy RPG systems
- Setting surveys covering published campaign worlds
- Advice for players improving their gameplay
- Guidance for gamemasters running better games
- Discussion of play styles and gaming philosophies
- Resource listings for products, publishers, and organizations

**Educational Mission**:

The Gamer's Bible aimed to educate players about gaming's diversity beyond D&D. Many gamers in the 1990s knew only D&D or their local gaming group's chosen system. Fannon's book introduced readers to alternatives—GURPS, Shadowrun, Earthdawn, numerous other systems offering different approaches to fantasy gaming.

This educational mission helped expand players' horizons, supporting smaller publishers by increasing awareness of their products and encouraging experimentation with different systems and play styles.

**Reference Resource**:

The book served as reference for finding games matching specific preferences. Want classless skill-based system? Consult the reviews. Prefer lightweight narrative systems? Check the recommendations. The book helped players discover games suited to their interests rather than assuming D&D was only option.

**Industry Advocacy**:

The Gamer's Bible represented advocacy for gaming hobby overall. By promoting diverse games and publishers, Fannon supported industry health and diversity. His approach assumed rising tide lifts all boats—growing hobby awareness benefits everyone, not just dominant publishers.

**Legacy**:

While predating internet's universal information access, the Gamer's Bible served important role in pre-internet gaming community. It introduced countless players to games they might never have discovered otherwise. The book exemplified Fannon's commitment to education and accessibility.

### Necessary Evil (2004)

Necessary Evil presented morally complex superhero setting where players control villains becoming reluctant heroes.

**Premise**:

Alien invasion defeats Earth's superheroes. Only supervillains remain to defend humanity. Players control villains forced into heroic roles—not because they've reformed, but because their world is at stake and no one else can stop the invaders.

**Thematic Exploration**:

The setting explores moral complexity:
- Can villains become heroes even temporarily?
- Does motivation matter if actions save lives?
- Can cooperation emerge from mutual self-interest?
- Is redemption possible for those who've done terrible things?

These questions created roleplay opportunities beyond typical superhero gaming's clear morality. Characters must work together despite antagonism, make heroic sacrifices for pragmatic reasons, and potentially find redemption through necessity.

**Mechanical Support**:

Necessary Evil adapted Savage Worlds for superhero gaming, providing:
- Power construction rules for creating varied supervillain abilities
- Rules for team dynamics despite mutual antagonism
- Mission structure supporting organized resistance
- Guidelines for running morally complex scenarios

**Reception**:

Necessary Evil earned acclaim for fresh take on superhero gaming. By inverting typical hero premise, it created unique storytelling opportunities. The setting influenced subsequent superhero games exploring moral ambiguity and complex protagonist motivations.

## Impact and Legacy

Sean Patrick Fannon's influence operates through community building, advocacy, and design work emphasizing accessibility and storytelling.

**Organized Play Innovation**: Shaintar's living campaign approach demonstrated how organized play could create investment and community beyond individual gaming groups. This influenced how other games approached organized play as community building tool rather than merely marketing strategy.

**Gaming Advocacy**: Fannon's decades of convention organizing, community building, and advocacy work helped make gaming more welcoming and accessible. His efforts contributed to hobby's growth and diversification.

**Educational Resources**: The Gamer's Bible and other educational works helped players discover gaming's diversity, supporting smaller publishers and encouraging experimentation with different systems.

**Savage Worlds Support**: Fannon's extensive Savage Worlds content helped establish the system as major alternative to d20 dominance. His settings demonstrated Savage Worlds' versatility and attracted players to the system.

**Community Focus**: Fannon's emphasis on community building over pure design innovation influenced how designers think about games' social context. His work demonstrated that successful game design includes community cultivation, not just mechanical creation.

**Accessibility Emphasis**: Throughout his career, Fannon championed accessible gaming—mechanically simple systems, welcoming communities, educational resources, and inclusive environments. This advocacy contributed to hobby's gradual movement toward greater accessibility.
