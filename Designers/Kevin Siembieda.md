# Kevin Siembieda

**Active Years**: 1981-present
**Birth Year**: 1956
**Nationality**: American
**Status**: Active

Kevin Siembieda is the founder of [[Palladium Books]] and creator of [[Rifts (1990)|Rifts]], one of gaming's most distinctive and polarizing game settings. As both designer and publisher, Siembieda built an independent RPG company that has survived over four decades despite industry consolidation, maintaining creative control through direct ownership. His maximalist design philosophy—combining every possible genre, power level, and concept into sprawling kitchen-sink universes—creates love-it-or-hate-it experiences that have sustained passionate fanbases while frustrating critics. Siembieda represents independent publishing's possibilities and challenges: creative freedom enabling unique visions alongside business struggles, controversial decisions, and design choices that defy modern conventions.

## Biography

### Early Life and Career Beginnings

Kevin Siembieda was born in 1956 and grew up during the early days of tabletop roleplaying. Discovering Dungeons & Dragons in the 1970s, he quickly became fascinated with game design and world-building. Unlike many early designers who entered the industry through TSR or established publishers, Siembieda chose the independent path from the beginning.

In 1981, Siembieda founded Palladium Books and published his first game, *The Mechanoids*, a science fiction RPG featuring humanity fleeing alien machines in generation ships. The game showcased early versions of design approaches that would characterize his later work: detailed background, extensive character options, and ambitious scope. While The Mechanoids found modest success, it established Palladium Books as a functioning independent publisher.

### Palladium Fantasy and System Development

In 1983, Siembieda released *Palladium Fantasy Role-Playing Game*, which would become the foundation for the Palladium Megaversal system. The game competed directly with Advanced Dungeons & Dragons during the height of TSR's market dominance—a bold move for an independent publisher.

Palladium Fantasy featured extensive character creation with numerous races and occupational character classes (O.C.C.s in Palladium terminology), detailed combat mechanics, and a skill system providing customization beyond class abilities. The game's mechanics prioritized detail and options over streamlined elegance, creating complex character creation and combat resolution that rewarded system mastery.

The game established Siembieda's design aesthetic: maximize player options, provide extensive detail, create rich world-building, and don't sacrifice content for simplicity. This philosophy attracted players seeking depth and customization while alienating those preferring streamlined mechanics.

### Licensed Game Success

Throughout the 1980s, Palladium Books achieved significant success adapting popular licenses to the Palladium system:

**Teenage Mutant Ninja Turtles & Other Strangeness** (1985): This adaptation of the then-underground comic became Palladium's first major commercial success. The game appeared just as TMNT was exploding into mainstream popularity through the animated series. Palladium's RPG introduced many young players to tabletop roleplaying, exposing a new generation to Palladium's mechanics and design philosophy.

The TMNT RPG featured the innovative "After the Bomb" setting where various animals gained human intelligence and abilities. The character creation system enabled creating mutant animals of virtually any species with different power levels and mutations, demonstrating Siembieda's commitment to comprehensive options and creative freedom.

**Robotech** (1986): Palladium's adaptation of the Robotech anime franchise became another major success. The game covered the three Robotech sagas (Macross, Southern Cross, Sentinels) with detailed mecha combat, character advancement, and setting material. Robotech RPG introduced many anime fans to tabletop gaming and demonstrated Palladium could handle complex licensed properties.

**Heroes Unlimited** (1984): This superhero RPG provided extensive power creation and character customization, enabling everything from street-level vigilantes to cosmic-powered beings. The game exemplified Siembieda's maximalist approach—if something could exist in superhero fiction, Heroes Unlimited tried to include rules for it.

These licensed games established Palladium as successful independent publisher with proven track record, creating financial foundation for Siembieda's most ambitious project.

### Rifts: The Kitchen-Sink Masterpiece

In 1990, Siembieda released *Rifts*, which would become his signature creation and Palladium's flagship product. Set in post-apocalyptic Earth where dimensional rifts connect to infinite realities, Rifts combined every genre imaginable: magic and technology, dragons and power armor, ancient mythology and science fiction, psychic powers and cybernetics, fantasy races and mutant animals.

The setting's premise justified unlimited creative scope: rifts in space-time connect Earth to countless dimensions, allowing literally anything to appear. Medieval knights could fight alongside cyber-enhanced soldiers; dragons could battle giant robots; practitioners of magic could duel psychic mutants. This kitchen-sink approach polarized gaming: some loved the unlimited possibility; others found it incoherent and unfocused.

**The Mega-Damage System**: Rifts introduced mega-damage, representing damage orders of magnitude greater than normal. A mega-damage point equals 100 normal hit points, separating ordinary humans from armored vehicles, power armor, and supernatural beings. This system enabled god-like power levels while maintaining mechanical functionality, though it created balance challenges and complexity.

**Setting Development**: Rifts Earth featured detailed regions including the Coalition States (fascist human supremacist nation-states), magical kingdoms like Tolkeen and Lazlo, wilderness filled with monsters and ancient evils, and ruins of pre-Rifts civilization. Each area received extensive development through sourcebooks, providing virtually unlimited campaign possibilities.

**Occupational Character Classes (O.C.C.s)**: Character creation offered dozens of O.C.C.s from Ley Line Walkers (magical practitioners) to Glitter Boys (pilots of iconic power armor) to City Rats (urban survivors) to Cyber-Knights (techno-magical warriors). The extensive options enabled precise character concepts but required navigating hundreds of pages of material.

**Commercial Success**: Despite (or because of) its kitchen-sink nature, Rifts became Palladium's bestselling product. The core rulebook and extensive supplement line sustained the company through decades, creating passionate fanbase that embraced the setting's creative chaos.

### Publishing Challenges and Controversies

Siembieda's tenure as publisher has involved numerous challenges and controversies:

**Freelancer Payment Issues**: Throughout the 1990s and 2000s, reports emerged of delayed or non-existent payments to freelance writers and artists. Former Palladium freelancers publicly described months or years waiting for payment, with some never receiving compensation for published work. These controversies damaged Palladium's reputation and created hesitancy among professional creators to work with the company.

**Legal and Financial Difficulties**: Palladium has faced various legal and financial challenges over the decades, including:
- Embezzlement by a trusted employee in the late 1990s that severely impacted company finances
- Multiple periods of financial instability requiring community support
- Legal issues with licensors over various properties
- Difficulties fulfilling Kickstarter obligations

**Kickstarter Controversies**: Palladium's Kickstarter campaigns have generated significant controversy:
- *Robotech RPG Tactics* (2013): Raised over $1.4 million but faced years of delays, quality issues, and unfulfilled pledge rewards. Many backers never received complete rewards, generating extensive negative publicity.
- Communication breakdowns and extended silence periods during difficult production periods
- Questions about fund management and project scope

**Resistance to Modernization**: Siembieda has consistently resisted updating Palladium's core mechanics, maintaining systems largely unchanged since the 1980s despite industry evolution. While this consistency pleases existing fans, it limits new player acquisition as modern audiences find the mechanics dated and cumbersome.

**Creative Control vs. Community Feedback**: Siembieda maintains tight creative control over Palladium properties, often resisting community feedback or suggestions for mechanical updates. This independence enables his unique creative vision but sometimes conflicts with player desires for mechanical improvement.

### Continued Activity

Despite challenges, Siembieda continues publishing Palladium products. Recent years have seen continued Rifts support, reprints of classic products, and occasional new releases. Palladium maintains devoted fanbase that values the company's unique properties and independent status.

The COVID-19 pandemic created additional challenges for Palladium, like many small publishers, but the company persists through Siembieda's determination and community support.

## Design Philosophy

Kevin Siembieda's design philosophy emphasizes maximalist creativity and player empowerment:

**Unlimited Possibility**: If something exists in fiction, try to include it. Settings should enable unlimited creative expression, not constrain imagination through narrow genre focus.

**Kitchen-Sink Settings**: Combining every possible element creates unique experiences impossible in genre-restricted games. Why choose between fantasy and sci-fi when you can have both simultaneously?

**Extensive Character Options**: Provide hundreds of character creation choices. Players should customize characters precisely to their vision through detailed options.

**Power Fantasy**: Enable epic-scale gameplay with god-like power levels. Players should feel mighty and capable of incredible feats.

**Detail Over Simplicity**: Comprehensive rules covering specific situations create immersive gameplay. Don't sacrifice completeness for streamlined mechanics.

**Independent Publishing**: Maintain creative control through independent ownership. Publishers should answer to their vision and community, not corporate shareholders.

**Setting Continuity**: Develop settings extensively over decades, creating deep lore and interconnected universe. Long-term players should see their game world evolve and expand.

**Cross-System Compatibility**: The Megaversal system enables using material from different Palladium games together, creating infinite combination possibilities.

## Notable Works

### Rifts (1990)

Rifts represents Siembieda's masterwork—a sprawling post-apocalyptic setting where dimensional rifts connect Earth to infinite realities. The game's kitchen-sink approach enables literally any character concept or encounter, from ancient dragons to giant robots to magical practitioners to alien invaders.

**Setting**: In 2098, ley lines (mystical energy pathways) erupted during planetary alignment, creating dimensional rifts that transformed Earth. Magic returned; monsters emerged; civilization collapsed; new societies arose from chaos. By the game's default setting (approximately 100 years post-Cataclysm), Earth features magical kingdoms, technological city-states, wilderness filled with supernatural threats, and ruins of lost civilization.

**The Coalition States**: The game's primary antagonist nation consists of human supremacist city-states viewing magic, non-humans, and literacy as threats. This fascist society provides clear villain for campaigns while raising questions about humanity, prejudice, and post-apocalyptic survival.

**Iconic Elements**:
- Glitter Boy power armor with boom gun (railgun devastating to mega-damage creatures)
- Ley Line Walkers channeling magical energy from mystical pathways
- Dragons, aliens, demons, and gods coexisting on transformed Earth
- The Megaverse concept connecting infinite dimensions

**Impact**: Rifts sustained Palladium Books for over three decades, generating hundreds of sourcebooks covering Earth regions, alien civilizations, alternate dimensions, and new character options. The setting influenced how games approach kitchen-sink settings and high-power gameplay.

### Palladium Fantasy RPG (1983)

The foundational game establishing the Megaversal system, Palladium Fantasy competed with AD&D during TSR's dominance. The game featured:

**O.C.C. System**: Occupational Character Classes combining D&D's class concept with skill-based customization. Players selected O.C.C. (like Wizard, Warrior, Thief) then customized through skill selection and character development.

**Detailed Combat**: Combat mechanics included strikes, parries, dodges, automatic dodge, strike bonuses, critical hits, and damage types. The system emphasized tactical options through detailed rules.

**The Palladium World**: The game's fantasy setting featured detailed regions, histories, cultures, and conflicts, demonstrating Siembieda's commitment to extensive world-building.

**Megaversal Foundation**: The mechanics established in Palladium Fantasy were adapted across Palladium's game line, enabling cross-system compatibility.

### Robotech RPG (1986)

Palladium's Robotech adaptation brought anime mecha gaming to American audiences, featuring:

**Mecha Combat**: Detailed rules for Veritech fighters, Destroids, and enemy mecha, capturing anime-style robot combat with transformations, missile barrages, and aerial dogfights.

**Three Sagas**: Coverage of Macross, Masters, and New Generation eras, providing decades of setting history and campaign possibilities.

**Character Development**: Rules for pilots, soldiers, scientists, and civilians, enabling various character types within military sci-fi setting.

**Cultural Impact**: The game introduced many players to tabletop gaming through the Robotech anime's popularity, creating entry point for anime fans.

### Teenage Mutant Ninja Turtles & Other Strangeness (1985)

The TMNT RPG arrived at perfect timing as the franchise exploded into mainstream popularity:

**Mutant Animal Creation**: Detailed rules for creating mutant animals of virtually any species, with random mutation tables, animal characteristics, and power levels creating unique characters.

**After the Bomb Setting**: Post-apocalyptic world where mutant animals coexisted with humans provided alternative to the core TMNT setting.

**Accessibility**: The game's connection to popular franchise introduced young players to tabletop roleplaying, expanding the hobby's audience.

**Innovation**: The mutant creation system demonstrated creative character generation possibilities and influenced subsequent games handling transformation and mutation.

## Key Innovations

### Mega-Damage System

The mega-damage system enabled god-like power levels by creating damage tiers. Normal damage affected ordinary beings; mega-damage harmed vehicles, power armor, and supernatural creatures. One mega-damage point equals 100 regular hit points, separating human-scale combat from epic-scale battles.

While controversial for creating power disparities and complexity, the system enabled Rifts' kitchen-sink approach where dragons fought robots without mechanical breakdown.

### Kitchen-Sink Multiverse

Rifts' premise—dimensional rifts connecting infinite realities—justified unlimited creative scope. The Megaverse concept enabled any character type, monster, or setting element through dimensional travel, creating infinitely expandable setting.

This approach influenced later kitchen-sink settings and demonstrated viability of genre-mixing in commercial products.

### O.C.C. System

Occupational Character Classes combined class-based structure with skill customization. Rather than rigid D&D classes, O.C.C.s provided frameworks that players customized through skill selection, creating middle ground between class and skill systems.

### Independent Publishing Model

Siembieda demonstrated independent publishers could survive long-term despite industry consolidation. Palladium Books operated outside TSR/WotC dominance and later d20 system boom, maintaining unique mechanical identity through direct ownership.

### Cross-System Compatibility

The Megaversal system enabled using material from different Palladium games together. Rifts characters could visit Palladium Fantasy's world; Heroes Unlimited heroes could encounter Mechanoid invasions. This interconnected universe created unique possibilities.

## Impact and Legacy

### Influence on Gaming

Kevin Siembieda's influence extends through several areas:

**Kitchen-Sink Settings**: Rifts demonstrated commercial viability of genre-mixing settings, influencing games that combine disparate elements rather than maintaining genre purity.

**Power Fantasy Gaming**: The mega-damage system and Rifts' epic scope showed market existed for high-power gameplay beyond standard power curves.

**Independent Publishing**: Palladium's survival through decades demonstrates independent publishers can maintain unique identities despite industry consolidation.

**Licensed Adaptations**: Palladium's work on Robotech and TMNT showed how licenses could expand into gaming, creating models for later licensed RPGs.

### Critical Reception

Siembieda's work generates polarized responses:

**Supporters** value:
- Unlimited creative possibility in kitchen-sink settings
- Extensive character options enabling precise customization
- Epic power levels and god-like gameplay
- Independent vision unconstrained by corporate considerations
- Decades of setting development creating deep lore

**Critics** cite:
- Dated, cumbersome mechanics resistant to modernization
- Power balance issues and systemic complexity
- Editing and organization problems in rulebooks
- Business practices including freelancer payment issues
- Resistance to community feedback and mechanical updates

### Contemporary Relevance

Modern gaming has largely moved away from Palladium's design philosophy. Streamlined mechanics, balanced power curves, and careful editing dominate contemporary design. However, Palladium maintains devoted fanbase valuing the company's unique approach.

The OSR movement and recent interest in older games has created some renewed appreciation for Palladium's maximalist philosophy, though the company hasn't experienced significant revival like some other older systems.

## Controversies

### Freelancer Payment Issues

Multiple former Palladium freelancers have publicly discussed delayed or missing payments for work that appeared in published products. These issues span decades and damaged the company's reputation among professional creators.

Siembieda has attributed payment delays to financial difficulties from embezzlement, market challenges, and cash flow problems. However, the pattern of non-payment created significant industry criticism.

### Kickstarter Fulfillment Problems

The *Robotech RPG Tactics* Kickstarter became one of tabletop gaming's most controversial crowdfunding campaigns:

- Raised over $1.4 million in 2013
- Experienced years of production delays
- Delivered products with quality issues
- Left many backer rewards unfulfilled
- Generated extensive negative publicity and legal threats
- Damaged Palladium's reputation with crowdfunding community

### Creative Control vs. Community Desires

Siembieda's tight creative control and resistance to mechanical updates frustrates many fans seeking modernized versions of Palladium games. While maintaining creative vision is legitimate, the gap between Palladium's mechanics and modern design conventions limits new audience growth.

## Quotes and Perspective

"Rifts is about unlimited possibility. If you can imagine it, it can exist somewhere in the Megaverse. That's the point—nothing is off limits."

On the mega-damage system: "We needed a way to make dragons fighting robots make sense mechanically. Mega-damage solves that problem—it creates tiers of power while keeping the math manageable."

"Independent publishing means I answer to the fans and my vision, not corporate shareholders. That freedom enables creating games I believe in rather than what market research suggests."

On Palladium's longevity: "We've survived over forty years because we create games people love. The fans keep us going through tough times, and we keep creating for them."

"Palladium Fantasy was my love letter to fantasy gaming with the rules I wanted. Some people prefer simpler systems, and that's fine—but there's audience for depth and detail too."

## Legacy

Kevin Siembieda's legacy is complex and contradictory. As designer, he created distinctive games with passionate followings, demonstrating that kitchen-sink settings and maximalist design could sustain commercial products. Rifts particularly stands as unique achievement—a game that shouldn't work by conventional design wisdom yet has entertained players for over thirty years.

As publisher, Siembieda demonstrated independent publishing's viability while also exemplifying its challenges. Palladium Books survived decades of industry consolidation but also struggled with business issues that damaged its reputation and limited its growth.

His resistance to mechanical modernization preserves Palladium's distinctive character while limiting appeal to new audiences. The Megaversal system remains largely unchanged since the 1980s—commendable consistency or stubborn refusal to adapt, depending on perspective.

Contemporary designers rarely cite Siembieda as direct influence, as modern design philosophy emphasizes streamlining, balance, and accessibility rather than Palladium's maximalist complexity. However, kitchen-sink settings and high-power gameplay demonstrate lasting influence of concepts Rifts popularized.

For players who love Palladium games, Siembieda represents independent vision creating unique experiences unavailable elsewhere. For critics, he exemplifies resistance to improvement and business practices damaging to creators and customers. Both perspectives contain truth, making Siembieda one of gaming's most polarizing figures—respected for persistence and creative vision, criticized for business decisions and design stagnation.

Palladium Books continues operating, creating new products for devoted fanbase while remaining outside mainstream gaming's evolution. Whether this represents admirable independence or stubborn isolation depends on one's perspective. Regardless, Kevin Siembieda's four-decade commitment to his creative vision and independent publishing demonstrates dedication few can match, creating gaming experiences that, for their fans, remain irreplaceable.
