# Matt Colville

**Active Years**: 2016-present (MCDM), 2000-present (gaming industry)
**Birth Year**: 1974
**Nationality**: American
**Status**: Active

Matt Colville is a game designer, YouTube educator, and founder of MCDM Productions who has become one of the most influential voices in modern Dungeon Master education and D&D 5th Edition design. Through his "Running the Game" YouTube series, Colville has taught hundreds of thousands of viewers the craft of game mastering, demystifying DM techniques and making the role accessible to new and experienced Game Masters alike. As designer, he created innovative supplements including *Strongholds & Followers* and *Kingdoms & Warfare*, which add domain-level play and mass combat to 5E while introducing action-oriented monster design that fundamentally improves encounter dynamics. Colville's combination of clear teaching, practical design, and transparent community engagement makes him uniquely influential in contemporary tabletop gaming.

## Biography

### Early Life and Gaming Background

Matthew Colville was born in 1974 and discovered Dungeons & Dragons during the game's early years, becoming an avid player and Dungeon Master throughout his youth. His extensive DM experience across multiple editions, combined with deep engagement with fantasy literature and storytelling, formed the foundation for his later educational and design work.

Colville pursued creative writing and storytelling professionally, authoring fantasy novels including *Priest* (2015) and *Thief* (2016), which demonstrated his narrative skills and understanding of fantasy conventions. This literary background informs his game design and educational approach, emphasizing story structure, character development, and dramatic pacing.

### Video Game Industry Career

Before founding MCDM, Colville worked extensively in the video game industry for over fifteen years, contributing to several notable projects:

**Obsidian Entertainment**: Worked as designer and writer on projects for the acclaimed RPG studio known for *Fallout: New Vegas*, *Pillars of Eternity*, and other narrative-focused games.

**Turtle Rock Studios**: Contributed to *Evolve* (2015), a asymmetric multiplayer game featuring monster-versus-hunters gameplay.

**Schell Games**: Worked on various projects at the independent game studio.

This video game experience exposed Colville to professional game design processes, iteration methodologies, playtesting protocols, and team-based development. The skills and perspectives gained in video game development—particularly regarding player engagement, difficulty curves, and systemic design—directly inform his tabletop design work.

### Running the Game: YouTube Educator

In 2016, Colville began the "Running the Game" YouTube series, creating educational videos about Dungeon Master techniques, game design philosophy, and tabletop RPG history. The series transformed DM education by:

**Accessible Teaching**: Colville explains complex DM concepts in clear, engaging language without condescension. His teaching style assumes intelligence while explaining fundamentals, making content valuable for both new and experienced DMs.

**Specific Techniques**: Rather than vague advice like "be creative," Colville teaches specific, actionable techniques: how to create engaging NPCs, structure adventures, pace sessions, handle player choices, design encounters, and resolve common table problems.

**Transparency and Honesty**: Colville discusses his own mistakes, learning processes, and ongoing challenges, demonstrating that DM skill develops through practice rather than innate talent.

**Historical Context**: Many videos explore RPG history, design evolution, and industry developments, educating viewers about gaming's past and how it informs contemporary practice.

**Community Building**: The series created engaged community of DMs sharing techniques, discussing concepts, and supporting each other's growth. Colville's comment sections and associated communities became valuable resources.

The "Running the Game" series has accumulated millions of views and is widely considered one of the best DM education resources available. Colville's videos appear in countless recommendation lists for new DMs and have demonstrably improved how thousands run games.

### MCDM Productions and Kickstarter Success

The YouTube channel's success enabled Colville to found MCDM Productions, an independent tabletop RPG publishing company. MCDM's business model combines crowdfunding through Kickstarter with traditional sales, enabling ambitious projects while maintaining community engagement.

**Strongholds & Followers Kickstarter (2017)**: MCDM's first major project raised over $2 million, far exceeding its initial goal. This campaign demonstrated Colville's community reach and validated demand for his design work. The overwhelming success enabled hiring a development team and establishing MCDM as professional publisher.

**Kingdoms & Warfare Kickstarter (2019)**: The second major supplement campaign raised over $2.1 million, further establishing MCDM's commercial viability and community support.

**Flee, Mortals! Kickstarter (2022)**: The monster manual campaign raised over $4.7 million, becoming one of the most successful RPG Kickstarters and demonstrating MCDM's growing influence.

These campaigns showcased transparent project management, regular communication, realistic timelines, and quality fulfillment—addressing common crowdfunding criticisms. Colville's openness about challenges, costs, and development processes educated backers about publishing realities while building trust.

## Design Philosophy

Matt Colville's design philosophy emphasizes DM empowerment, player agency, and practical utility:

**DM Craft as Teachable Skill**: Game mastering is craft with learnable techniques, not mysterious talent. Specific, actionable advice enables improvement through practice.

**Action-Oriented Design**: Every element should create engagement and meaningful choices. Static scenes where nothing happens waste table time; dynamic encounters with escalating stakes maintain engagement.

**Player Agency Paramount**: Design should maximize meaningful player choices. Railroading frustrates; multiple viable approaches empower. Respect player decisions and make consequences matter.

**Retroactive Continuity in Service of Fun**: Internal consistency matters less than current enjoyment. If retconning something makes the current game better, do it. Continuity serves fun, not vice versa.

**Tools Over Prescription**: Provide frameworks, tables, and tools enabling DM creativity rather than prescribing specific narratives. DMs should customize content for their tables.

**Domain Play Integration**: D&D benefits from domain-level play where characters establish organizations, manage holdings, and influence regions. Integrate these systems into core gameplay.

**Challenging but Fair**: Design should create genuine challenge without unfair gotchas. Players should succeed or fail based on decisions, not DM fiat or arbitrary difficulty.

**Clear Communication**: Explain design reasoning explicitly. Transparency helps users understand intent and customize effectively.

**Historical Awareness**: Understanding RPG history informs better design. Study what worked, what failed, and why.

**Iterative Improvement**: Playtesting and feedback inform design. Iterate based on actual play rather than theory.

## Notable Works

### Strongholds & Followers (2018)

*Strongholds & Followers* adds domain-level play to D&D 5th Edition, enabling characters to establish bases, attract followers, and exercise regional influence. The supplement addresses gap in 5E's rules, which largely ignore domain management despite its presence in earlier D&D editions.

**Stronghold System**: Characters can establish strongholds (castles, towers, temples, guildhalls, etc.) providing mechanical benefits, narrative opportunities, and campaign hooks. Each stronghold type offers unique abilities reflecting its nature—temples provide divine aid; wizard towers offer magical research; castles enable military might.

Strongholds develop through investment, providing escalating benefits as characters upgrade facilities. This creates resource sink for high-level characters while generating narrative content around construction, defense, and management.

**Follower System**: Characters gain followers—loyal subordinates who assist adventures, manage strongholds, and execute missions. The follower rules enable delegating tasks while maintaining abstraction preventing the game from becoming management simulator.

Followers die in service, creating emotional stakes. The system balances utility (followers help accomplish goals) with cost (their deaths matter), generating authentic domain-level play experience.

**Organization Classes**: Different character classes attract different follower types reflecting their nature. Fighters attract soldiers; clerics attract acolytes; rogues attract scoundrels. This class-specific content ensures all characters benefit from systems.

**Impact**: *Strongholds & Followers* demonstrated significant market appetite for domain-level 5E content. The system influenced how designers think about integrating organizational play into campaigns and provided proof-of-concept for MCDM's design approach.

### Kingdoms & Warfare (2020)

*Kingdoms & Warfare* expanded MCDM's domain-level design, adding mass combat, diplomacy, and kingdom management to D&D 5E. The supplement enables campaigns featuring large-scale warfare, political intrigue, and realm management.

**Warfare System**: The mass combat rules enable resolving battles between armies as dramatic encounters alongside traditional D&D combat. Players command units, employ tactics, and influence battle outcomes through character actions.

The system balances abstraction (preventing tedious detail) with meaningful choices (players affect outcomes). Battles resolve in reasonable time while maintaining dramatic stakes and tactical interest.

**Diplomacy and Intrigue**: Rules for diplomatic actions, intelligence gathering, and political maneuvering enable intrigue-focused campaigns. Characters can negotiate treaties, conduct espionage, and influence factions through non-violent means.

**Organization Management**: Expanded rules for running organizations (kingdoms, mercenary companies, guilds, etc.) provide frameworks for domain management without overwhelming complexity.

**Domain Action System**: The supplement introduces domain actions—special abilities organizations can execute during downtime, enabling kingdom-level play integrated with adventure-level activity.

**Impact**: *Kingdoms & Warfare* filled another 5E gap, providing mass combat and intrigue rules many DMs wanted. The system's success validated ongoing domain-level development and demonstrated MCDM's design sophistication.

### Action-Oriented Monsters

Colville's most influential design innovation appears throughout MCDM products: action-oriented monster design. This approach fundamentally improves 5E encounters by making enemies more dynamic and engaging.

**The Problem**: Standard 5E monsters often feel static. They act once per round, doing the same thing repeatedly until defeated. This creates boring encounters where optimal strategy is "hit it until it dies."

**The Solution**: Action-oriented monsters have multiple action types:

**Villain Actions**: Occur at specific initiative counts (like lair actions) separate from the monster's turn. These extra actions increase monsters' action economy, making solo bosses competitive with full party action counts.

**Bonus Actions**: Special abilities usable as bonus actions create dynamic encounters where monsters do multiple interesting things each round.

**Phase-Based Design**: Powerful monsters change behavior at certain health thresholds, creating encounter phases with different challenges. This prevents repetitive combat where optimal first-round strategy remains optimal until victory.

**Legendary Actions**: Extending 5E's existing legendary action concept, action-oriented design emphasizes using these abilities to create dynamic, multi-action monster turns.

**Impact**: Action-oriented monster design has been widely adopted across 5E community. Many DMs retrofit existing monsters using these principles; third-party designers incorporate the approach; community discussions reference "action-oriented" as shorthand for dynamic enemy design. The concept represents Colville's most significant mechanical contribution to modern D&D.

### Flee, Mortals! (2023)

*Flee, Mortals!* is MCDM's monster manual for D&D 5E, featuring hundreds of creatures designed using action-oriented principles and MCDM's design philosophy.

**Action-Oriented Throughout**: Every monster incorporates dynamic abilities, multiple action types, and engaging mechanics rather than static stat blocks.

**Encounter Building**: The book emphasizes monsters as encounter-building tools rather than isolated creatures, providing advice for combining creatures effectively.

**Evocative Design**: Monster abilities suggest tactics, personality, and flavor, helping DMs understand how to run creatures effectively.

**Production Quality**: The book features extensive original artwork, clear layout, and high production values demonstrating MCDM's commitment to professional quality.

**Success**: The Kickstarter raised over $4.7 million and the book won ENnie Award for Best Monster/Adversary, validating MCDM's design approach and establishing the company as major 5E publisher.

### The Chain of Acheron

Colville's ongoing campaign setting, showcased through actual play videos and developed for eventual publication, demonstrates his world-building and narrative design:

**Collaborative World-Building**: The setting develops through play, with player choices shaping history and geography. This demonstrates Colville's philosophy of retroactive continuity and emergent world-building.

**Multiple Campaign Frameworks**: The setting supports various campaign types from traditional adventuring to domain management to large-scale warfare, showcasing system integration.

**Actual Play as Design Tool**: Using actual play to develop and showcase setting demonstrates design-through-play methodology and provides marketing through entertainment.

## Quotes and Philosophy

"The job of the DM is to make the game fun. That's it. Everything else—rules, consistency, realism—serves that goal."

On action-oriented monsters: "The problem with most D&D boss fights is the boss acts once and the party acts four or five times. Of course the fight is boring—the monster can't compete. Give bosses more actions and suddenly fights become dynamic."

"Running the Game is about teaching specific techniques. Don't tell new DMs 'be creative.' Give them actual tools they can use at their table tonight."

On player agency: "The players will surprise you. Your job isn't controlling the story—it's responding to their choices in interesting ways. That's where the magic happens."

"Strongholds aren't about detailed economics simulation. They're about giving players something to care about and defend. When the villain attacks their castle, suddenly it's personal."

On retroactive continuity: "If changing some background detail makes the current game more fun, change it. Your players won't know and wouldn't care if they did. Continuity serves fun, not the other way around."

"The best compliment MCDM could receive is someone saying our book helped their table have more fun. That's why we do this."

On Kickstarter transparency: "We're detailed about costs, timelines, and challenges because backers deserve to understand what their money funds. Transparency builds trust."
