# Avery Alder

**Active Years**: 2012-present
**Birth Year**: 1989
**Nationality**: Canadian

Avery Alder is one of the most innovative and influential contemporary indie RPG designers, best known for creating Monsterhearts, The Quiet Year, and the Belonging Outside Belonging framework. Alder's work pioneered queer representation in gaming, GMless collaborative storytelling, and community-building game structures. Their designs consistently push boundaries while remaining accessible, creating games that explore marginalization, desire, community formation, and identity through elegant mechanical frameworks. As a queer game designer, Alder brought LGBTQ+ perspectives to gaming in unprecedented ways, creating space for stories about queer adolescence, chosen families, and communities existing outside dominant power structures. Their influence extends across story games, PbtA design, and GMless gaming, making them one of the most important voices in contemporary independent game design.

## Early Life and Design Beginnings

Avery Alder (born 1989) grew up in Canada during the 1990s and 2000s, coming of age alongside the internet's expansion and indie gaming's emergence. Unlike designers from previous generations, Alder's formation occurred within established indie scene, learning from PbtA, story games, and Nordic larp traditions.

Alder's identity as queer person profoundly shaped their design perspective. Growing up queer meant experiencing marginalization, developing community outside mainstream structures, and understanding how identity and desire complicate social dynamics. These lived experiences became design foundations, enabling Alder to create games exploring subjects often absent from mainstream gaming.

Their design education came through playing indie games, reading theory, and participating in online design communities. Alder absorbed PbtA principles, GMless games like Fiasco, Nordic larp's emotional intensity, and story games' emphasis on character relationships. This diverse foundation enabled innovative synthesis.

Alder studied education and community organizing, bringing pedagogical awareness and understanding of group dynamics to game design. Their academic background informs how games teach themselves, facilitate difficult conversations, and build communities through play.

They founded Buried Without Ceremony as publishing vehicle for their games, maintaining creative control while building sustainable independent practice. This DIY approach follows punk and zine traditions of self-publishing as political and creative act.

## Major Works

### Monsterhearts (2012)

Alder's breakthrough game, Monsterhearts explores the messy lives of teenage monsters navigating high school, identity, desire, and power. Built on the Powered by the Apocalypse framework, Monsterhearts applied PbtA mechanics to queer adolescent horror, creating genre-defining work.

**Setting and Themes**: The game casts players as teenage monsters—vampires, werewolves, ghosts, fae, infernals, and others—attending high school and dealing with supernatural powers, complicated desires, social hierarchies, and emerging identities. Unlike traditional monster games emphasizing combat and supernatural politics, Monsterhearts focuses on relationships, sexuality, identity formation, and emotional intensity.

The game explicitly centers queer experience. Monsters serve as metaphor for queer adolescence—feeling different, hiding true nature, experiencing desires that complicate social belonging, existing outside normative expectations. This metaphorical framework allows exploring queer themes whether characters are explicitly LGBTQ+ or not.

**Mechanical Innovations**:

**Strings**: The game's signature mechanic represents emotional leverage between characters. When you manipulate someone or they choose to give you power over them, you gain a String. Strings can be spent to add dice to rolls against someone, refuse their conditions, or make them do something reasonable.

This mechanizes emotional manipulation, power dynamics in relationships, and how intimacy creates vulnerability. The system makes visible the invisible dynamics of attraction, coercion, and influence. Strings represent messy reality that emotional connections grant power over us.

**The Sex Move**: Each playbook includes a Sex Move—special ability triggered when characters become physically intimate. These moves mechanize how intimacy changes relationships and creates consequences. The explicitness challenged gaming's often puritanical approach to sexuality while creating meaningful mechanical integration.

The Sex Moves normalize discussing sexuality in gaming contexts, demonstrate how intimacy has mechanical consequences, and create safe framework for exploring mature themes. They're not gratuitous but rather essential to the game's examination of desire and identity.

**Conditions**: Instead of hit points, characters gain Conditions—emotional states like "shamed," "terrified," or "obsessed." Conditions provide mechanical modifiers while tracking emotional consequences. This shifts focus from physical damage to psychological and social harm.

**The Skin**: Each character chooses a Skin (playbook)—Chosen, Fae, Ghost, Ghoul, Infernal, Mortal, Queen, Vampire, Werewolf, Witch. Each represents archetypal teenage monster with unique moves, stats, and narrative implications. The Skins explore different aspects of adolescent experience through monstrous metaphor.

**Darkest Self**: Each Skin has a Darkest Self—monstrous state triggered by trauma or extremity. The Darkest Self represents losing control to monstrous nature, mechanizing how pressure causes destructive behavior. Players roleplay Darkest Self according to prescribed framework until specific conditions end it.

**Turn On/Shut Down**: Moves for expressing attraction (turning someone on gains String) and rejecting advances (shutting someone down causes harm). These mechanics normalize exploring desire and rejection, making visible the power dynamics in attraction.

**Thematic Achievement**: Monsterhearts successfully creates space for stories about:

- Queer adolescence and coming out
- Complicated desire and attraction across genders
- Social hierarchies and marginalization
- Identity formation and self-discovery
- The messiness of teenage relationships
- Power dynamics in intimacy and friendship
- Chosen family and finding belonging

**Impact**: Monsterhearts became landmark game demonstrating:

- Queer games could achieve mainstream indie success
- Sexuality could be mechanized meaningfully
- PbtA framework could explore diverse genres and themes
- Games could create safe containers for exploring difficult subjects
- Representation matters and creates new audiences

The game won multiple awards, achieved commercial success, and influenced countless subsequent designers. Monsterhearts 2 (2017) refined the system while expanding Skins and options.

### The Quiet Year (2013)

Radically different from Monsterhearts, The Quiet Year is GMless map-drawing game about community building during temporary peace before certain catastrophe.

**Structure**: The game uses standard playing card deck to structure play over four seasons. Each week (card draw), one player takes turn making discovery, starting project, or discussing community concern. The community draws map, tracking geography, resources, projects, and threats. Dice determine project success, contempt tokens represent simmering conflicts, and discussion cards force difficult conversations.

At year's end, certain disaster (The Frost King) arrives, ending play. The Quiet Year chronicles the community's brief flowering before inevitable conclusion.

**Key Innovations**:

**GMless Structure**: No single facilitator; authority rotates based on card draws. This distributes narrative power and teaches collaborative storytelling.

**Map as Play Artifact**: The evolving map serves as physical record of collective creativity, external memory, and beautiful artifact. Drawing together creates shared investment.

**Contempt Tokens**: When disagreeing with decision, players silently place contempt token rather than arguing. This mechanizes unspoken resentment, creating tension that erupts later. The silent disagreement rule forces accepting collective decisions while acknowledging dissent.

**Discussion Restraints**: The game forbids certain conversation topics until specific cards appear. This creates rhythms of silence and revelation, building tension through what cannot yet be said.

**Inevitability**: Knowing disaster comes creates poignancy. Every achievement is temporary; every conflict ultimately doesn't matter. This structures play toward specific emotional register.

**No Character Sheets**: Players embody community collectively rather than individual characters, creating unusual perspective on collaborative storytelling.

**Thematic Focus**: The Quiet Year explores:

- Community building under pressure
- Collective decision-making and disagreement
- Environmental precarity and resource management
- What we build knowing it won't last
- Conflict between individual desire and collective good
- The politics of small communities

**Impact**: The Quiet Year demonstrated:

- GMless games could have structure without rigid rules
- Map-drawing creates powerful collaborative experiences
- Card decks can structure narrative elegantly
- Games can explore political themes accessibly
- Constraint (silence, contempt tokens) creates interesting play

The game became widely played in diverse contexts—game nights, design workshops, therapy, education—demonstrating indie games' versatility.

### Dream Askew / Dream Apart (2018-2019)

Alder's most sophisticated mechanical innovation, Dream Askew crystallized the Belonging Outside Belonging framework—GMless structure for collaborative storytelling about communities existing outside or against dominant power structures.

**Dream Askew Setting**: Queer community in post-apocalyptic enclave, building chosen family and sanctuary outside collapsing civilization. Players portray queer survivors creating space for themselves amid ruin—finding belonging outside the heteronormative structures that excluded them.

The setting explicitly centers queerness not as individual identity but as communal practice. The enclave exists because its members were pushed outside dominant society; they build new ways of relating, organizing, and surviving.

**Belonging Outside Belonging Framework**:

**No Facilitator**: Unlike PbtA's MC or traditional GM, BoB distributes facilitator responsibilities through Setting Elements—domains like "the enclave," "the psychic maelstrom," "the outside world." Players take Setting Elements alongside characters, framing scenes and introducing complications from their domains.

**Token Economy**: Players have token pools. Most moves cost tokens; some generate tokens. This creates resource economy encouraging varied action types and rhythmic play. Tokens ensure players sometimes make risky weak moves and sometimes take strong action.

**No Dice**: Resolution uses tokens instead of dice. Weak moves cost tokens; strong moves generate tokens but create complications. This creates different probability feel than dice—deterministic yet strategically interesting.

**Playbooks and Setting Elements**: Character playbooks (the Muscle, the Hawker, the Watcher, etc.) provide unique moves and fictional positioning. Setting Elements (the enclave, the psychic maelstrom, the outside world, etc.) give framing responsibilities and complication prompts.

**Lenses**: Rather than traditional stats, characters have Lenses—different ways of approaching situations (forcefully, slyly, playfully, etc.). Choosing lens frames how you engage situations, creating varied approaches to challenges.

**Belonging and Struggle**: The framework's name—Belonging Outside Belonging—captures core tension. Characters belong to their community (the enclave) while existing outside dominant structures. They struggle against external threats and internal conflicts while building chosen family.

**Design Philosophy**: BoB framework assumes:

- Communities, not just individuals, deserve focus
- Marginalized people create culture, not just survive
- GMless play can have structure without centralized authority
- Queerness is lens for understanding community and resistance
- Comfort and safety coexist with meaningful conflict

**Dream Apart**: Benjamin Rosenbaum's companion game adapts BoB to Jewish shtetl stories, demonstrating framework's flexibility. This two-game collection showed how single framework could explore different marginalized communities.

**Impact**: Belonging Outside Belonging:

- Provided replicable GMless framework for other designers
- Centered marginalized communities in unprecedented ways
- Demonstrated token economies' design potential
- Influenced GMless gaming broadly
- Created structure for stories about chosen family and resistance
- Won multiple major awards including ENnie Gold

## Design Philosophy

Alder's design philosophy centers queer perspectives and community:

**Center Marginalized Experience**: Games should explore perspectives and communities often excluded from gaming. Queerness, marginalization, and resistance deserve mechanical and thematic focus, not just representation.

**Mechanics Create Experience**: Specific mechanical choices generate specific play experiences. Strings mechanize power in relationships; contempt tokens create simmering conflict; token economies structure GMless play. Design mechanics intentionally for desired experience.

**Safety and Boundaries**: Games exploring difficult content need safety tools and clear boundaries. Alder advocates explicit consent mechanics, safety tools, and thoughtful design around potentially harmful content.

**Accessible Innovation**: Radical mechanics should remain learnable and playable. Dream Askew's GMless structure and token economy are innovative yet accessible; Monsterhearts explores sexuality within familiar PbtA framework.

**Community Over Individual**: Games can focus on communities and collective action, not just individual heroes. The Quiet Year and Dream Askew center communities; even Monsterhearts emphasizes relationships over solo achievement.

**Intimacy and Vulnerability**: Games can create space for emotional intimacy, difficult conversations, and vulnerability. Monsterhearts' Sex Moves, The Quiet Year's contempt tokens, and Dream Askew's community focus all encourage emotional engagement.

**DIY Publishing**: Self-publishing maintains creative control, builds sustainable practice, and models alternatives to corporate gaming. Buried Without Ceremony demonstrates indie viability.

**Generous Teaching**: Share design knowledge freely through resources like Simple World, essays, and talks. Build design community by teaching, not hoarding knowledge.

**Political Gaming**: Games are never politically neutral. Design choices reflect values; Alder chooses to center queer liberation, community building, and resistance to oppression.

## Innovations and Contributions

### Mechanical Innovations

**Strings**: Emotional leverage mechanic making power dynamics in relationships visible and playable.

**Belonging Outside Belonging**: Complete GMless framework with token economy, distributed facilitation through Setting Elements, and diceless resolution.

**Token Economies**: Sophisticated resource management creating strategic decisions and play rhythms without dice randomness.

**Sex Moves**: Mechanizing intimacy's consequences within safe framework, normalizing sexuality in gaming.

**Conditions Over Hit Points**: Emotional/social consequences replacing physical damage, focusing play on psychological stakes.

**Contempt Tokens**: Silent disagreement mechanic creating tension through what cannot be said.

**Setting Elements**: Distributing GM responsibilities through playable roles tied to setting aspects.

**Lenses**: Approach-based character options replacing traditional stats, focusing on how you act rather than numerical competencies.

### Conceptual Contributions

**Queer Games Movement**: Pioneering queer representation as central design element, not just character option.

**Community-Focused Gaming**: Demonstrating games can explore collective identity, chosen family, and community building.

**GMless Structure**: Providing replicable frameworks for GMless play beyond pure improvisation.

**Safety-Conscious Design**: Advocating for and implementing safety tools, consent mechanics, and thoughtful content design.

**Marginalization as Theme**: Centering experiences of marginalized communities—queer people, diaspora communities, outsiders—as rich game material.

**Desire and Power**: Exploring how attraction, intimacy, and desire create power dynamics worth mechanizing.

**Post-Apocalyptic Queerness**: Imagining queer futures and communities outside dominant structures.

### Cultural Impact

**Representation**: Creating space for LGBTQ+ players to see themselves centered in gaming.

**Design Accessibility**: Teaching resources like Simple World lower barriers to design entry.

**Community Building**: Games like The Quiet Year used in diverse contexts—education, therapy, community organizing—demonstrating gaming's reach beyond entertainment.

**Industry Change**: Demonstrating queer games achieve commercial and critical success, encouraging publishers to support diverse voices.

**Safety Tools Adoption**: Advocacy for safety tools influenced their mainstream adoption across gaming.

## Legacy and Influence

Avery Alder's influence on contemporary indie gaming is profound and multifaceted:

**Queer Gaming**: Alder pioneered centering queer experience in game design, creating space for LGBTQ+ designers and players. Monsterhearts demonstrated queer games could achieve mainstream indie success, encouraging publishers to support diverse voices and designers to explore queer themes.

**GMless Innovation**: Dream Askew's Belonging Outside Belonging framework provided replicable structure for GMless play, influencing numerous subsequent GMless designs. The framework appears in For the Queen, other BoB games, and influenced broader GMless design thinking.

**Emotional Gaming**: Alder's games normalize exploring difficult emotions, complicated relationships, and vulnerability. This influenced how indie games approach emotional content, safety tools, and intimate subjects.

**Community Focus**: Games centering communities rather than individual heroes influenced various subsequent designs exploring collective action, chosen families, and communal identity.

**Mechanical Accessibility**: Demonstrating innovative mechanics can remain accessible influenced how designers balance experimentation with playability.

**DIY Publishing**: Buried Without Ceremony models sustainable independent publishing, encouraging designers to self-publish rather than seek corporate backing.

**Safety and Consent**: Advocacy for safety tools influenced their mainstream adoption across indie and increasingly mainstream gaming.

Contemporary designers regularly cite Alder as influence. Games like Wanderhome (Jay Dragon), For the Queen (Alex Roberts), and countless others build on Alder's innovations. The BoB framework spawned entire design subgenre.

Alder's work proves games can be politically engaged, emotionally sophisticated, mechanically innovative, and commercially successful simultaneously. They established queer game design as vibrant movement rather than niche curiosity.

## Conclusion

Avery Alder represents the best of contemporary indie game design: mechanically innovative, thematically sophisticated, politically engaged, and fundamentally humane. Their games create space for queer stories, emotional vulnerability, community building, and meaningful choice within elegant mechanical frameworks.

From Monsterhearts' exploration of queer adolescence through Strings and Sex Moves, to The Quiet Year's map-drawing community building, to Dream Askew's Belonging Outside Belonging framework for chosen family stories, Alder's work consistently innovates while remaining accessible and emotionally resonant.

Their influence reshapes indie gaming—normalizing queer perspectives, demonstrating GMless potential, advocating for safety, and centering communities over individuals. Contemporary designers build on Alder's foundations, creating diverse games exploring marginalization, identity, desire, and resistance.

Alder proves games can be radical and accessible, innovative and emotionally direct, politically engaged and deeply fun. Their work establishes that the most meaningful innovations often come from centering perspectives traditionally excluded from design—and that games genuinely inclusive and emotionally sophisticated reach broader audiences and create more resonant experiences.

For understanding contemporary indie gaming, queer game design, GMless innovation, or emotionally engaged storytelling games, Avery Alder's work is essential—not just historical foundation but living practice continuing to evolve and inspire.
