# C.J. Carella

**Active Years**: 1999-present
**Birth Year**: ~1965
**Nationality**: United States

C.J. Carella created the Unisystem engine powering WitchCraft (1999), All Flesh Must Be Eaten (1999—zombie survival), Buffy/Angel RPGs (2002-2003—licensed Whedonverse games using Cinematic Unisystem), demonstrating how single flexible engine could serve multiple horror/urban fantasy genres while making licensed adaptations that faithfully captured source material, influencing unified system design and establishing Eden Studios as major 2000s horror publisher.

## Major Works

### WitchCraft (1999)

Modern occult urban fantasy creating Unisystem:

**Unisystem Engine**: Flexible d10-based system

**Modern Occult**: Contemporary witches and supernatural politics

**Classic Unisystem**: Crunchy detailed version

**World of Darkness Alternative**: Competing urban fantasy option

**Impact**: Created engine used across multiple games

### All Flesh Must Be Eaten (1999)

Zombie survival horror:

**Zombie Gaming**: Before Walking Dead made zombies ubiquitous

**Flexible**: Any zombie scenario (Romero, fast zombies, comedic)

**Survival Emphasis**: Resource management and desperate escape

**Deadworlds**: Multiple zombie apocalypse settings

**Commercial Success**: Eden's flagship horror title

**Impact**: Established definitive zombie RPG pre-Walking Dead

### Buffy the Vampire Slayer RPG (2002)

Licensed Whedonverse using Cinematic Unisystem:

**Cinematic Unisystem**: Streamlined faster-playing Unisystem variant

**Drama Points**: Heroic bennies for cinematic action

**Faithful Adaptation**: Captured show's tone perfectly

**Episode Structure**: Organized like TV episodes

**Character Types**: Slayers, Watchers, White Hats, etc.

**Commercial Hit**: Licensed success

**Impact**: Demonstrated Cinematic Unisystem worked. Template for Angel RPG.

### Angel RPG (2003)

Buffy companion covering Angel series using same system.

## Design Philosophy

Carella emphasizes:

**Unified System**: One engine for multiple genres

**Horror Focus**: Various horror subgenres

**Licensed Respect**: Faithful adaptations

**Cinematic vs. Classic**: Two Unisystem variants for different play styles

**Urban Fantasy**: Modern supernatural settings

## Innovation

**Unisystem Engine**: Flexible unified system

**Cinematic/Classic Split**: Two complexity levels

**Drama Points**: Cinematic heroic moments

**Licensed Success**: Buffy/Angel as exemplar adaptations

## Awards

- **Origins Awards**: Multiple Eden Studios games

- **Commercial Success**: Strong sales 2000s

## Cultural Impact

**Unisystem Establishment**: Widely-used system across multiple games

**Zombie Gaming**: AFMBE before Walking Dead zombies saturated culture

**Licensed Quality**: Buffy/Angel set standards for TV adaptations

**Eden Studios**: Helped build major horror publisher

**Urban Fantasy**: Alternative to World of Darkness dominance

## Reception

Praised for Unisystem flexibility, licensed faithfulness (Buffy especially), zombie gaming before saturation. Some found Classic Unisystem complex; Cinematic addressed this.

## Legacy

Unisystem powered multiple successful games. Buffy RPG remains beloved licensed adaptation. AFMBE pre-zombie-saturation classic. Demonstrated single designer could create flexible engine and multiple successful implementations.
