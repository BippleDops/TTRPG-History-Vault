# Zak Smith / Zak S

**Active Years**: 2009-2019
**Birth Year**: 1978
**Nationality**: American
**Also Known As**: Zak Sabbath, Zak S

## Content Warning

This profile discusses a designer whose work significantly influenced OSR gaming but who was expelled from the RPG industry in 2019 following serious allegations of abuse. This profile focuses on the historical design contributions while acknowledging the controversy that ended this designer's industry career. The work remains influential in OSR circles while the creator is persona non grata in organized gaming.

## Biography

Zak Smith (born 1978), also known as Zak Sabbath and Zak S, was a professional artist, adult film performer, and game designer whose RPG work between 2009 and 2019 significantly influenced OSR (Old School Renaissance) aesthetics and design. His career combined fine art training, unconventional life experiences, and innovative game design, creating distinctive products that won multiple awards while generating significant controversy even before allegations ended his industry involvement.

Smith trained as a fine artist and worked professionally in that field while also working in adult entertainment. He discovered old-school D&D and began blogging about gaming in the late 2000s, quickly gaining attention for articulate game theory writing, beautiful artwork, and combative online persona that generated both admirers and critics.

His first major RPG product, Vornheim: The Complete City Kit (2011), published by Lamentations of the Flame Princess, showcased innovative approach combining practical DM tools with artistic presentation. The book won ENnie Awards and established Smith as significant OSR voice.

A Red & Pleasant Land (2014), his Alice in Wonderland-inspired setting for Lamentations of the Flame Princess, won multiple ENnie Awards including Best Setting and Product of the Year. The book demonstrated how literary influences and fine art aesthetics could create distinctive RPG content, featuring Smith's paintings and intricate design throughout.

In 2014, Wizards of the Coast consulted Smith for D&D 5th Edition development, generating controversy due to his adult entertainment work and confrontational online behavior. WotC defended the choice, citing his game design expertise, but the controversy highlighted tensions around who belongs in mainstream RPG industry.

Smith continued producing acclaimed products including Maze of the Blue Medusa (2016, with Patrick Stuart) and various supplements for Lamentations of the Flame Princess. His work consistently demonstrated innovative design, beautiful production, and practical utility for DMs.

However, Smith's online behavior generated ongoing controversy. His confrontational style, aggressive responses to criticism, and tendency toward lengthy public disputes created polarized opinions within the RPG community. Supporters valued his design innovations and theoretical contributions; critics found his behavior toxic and divisive.

In February 2019, multiple allegations of long-term abuse were made public by his former romantic partner and corroborated by others. The allegations detailed a pattern of manipulative, controlling, and abusive behavior. The RPG industry responded swiftly with publishers, conventions, and community organizations distancing themselves from Smith and removing his work from sale or consideration.

Since 2019, Smith has been effectively expelled from the organized RPG industry, with his products removed from major sales platforms and his name removed from industry consideration. While some continue engaging with his design work separately from the creator, he remains persona non grata in organized gaming.

## Design Philosophy

Smith's design philosophy, as demonstrated in his published work, emphasized:

**Practical Utility**: Game books should provide immediately useful tools for DMs. Random tables, generators, and reference materials should facilitate play, not just provide reading.

**Artistic Presentation**: RPG books are visual objects. Beautiful artwork, thoughtful layout, and aesthetic consideration enhance the gaming experience.

**Surrealism and Literary Influence**: Draw from literature, fairy tales, and surrealist art rather than generic fantasy. Create distinctive, memorable settings through unusual combinations and imaginative recombination.

**Player Agency**: Design should maximize meaningful player choice. Information should be available through investigation, and multiple solutions should exist for problems.

**Improvisational Support**: Provide tools enabling DM improvisation rather than prescribing specific narratives. Random tables, generators, and flexible frameworks support spontaneous play.

**Old-School Principles with Modern Sensibility**: Embrace OSR philosophy (player skill over character abilities, deadly environments, emergent narrative) while applying modern design sophistication and presentation quality.

**Intellectual Rigor**: Game design deserves serious theoretical consideration. Write clearly, argue precisely, and bring intellectual engagement to the craft.

## Major Works

### Vornheim: The Complete City Kit (2011)

Vornheim revolutionized city-based gaming through innovative random generation tools and practical DM utilities. Rather than presenting a detailed city requiring extensive preparation, Smith provided flexible tools for generating city content during play.

The book included random tables for NPC encounters, shops, building interiors, and urban complications. Notably, it featured techniques using the physical book as tool—dropping dice on pages to generate results, using table edges for number generation, and incorporating book structure into random generation.

Vornheim's minimalist approach to city content influenced how designers think about urban adventures. Instead of exhaustive detail, provide generation tools. Instead of prescribed locations, enable improvisation.

The book won ENnie Awards for Best Aid and Best Rules, validating its innovative approach. It demonstrated that practical utility and beautiful presentation could coexist in compact, immediately useful format.

### A Red & Pleasant Land (2014)

A Red & Pleasant Land reimagined Alice in Wonderland as dark fantasy RPG setting, combining Lewis Carroll's surrealism with vampire mythology and Smith's artistic vision. The setting featured Voivodja, a demiplane ruled by vampire courts where nothing quite makes sense and everything might be dangerous.

The book showcased Smith's painting throughout, with nearly every page featuring original artwork. The production quality and artistic integration set new standards for OSR products, demonstrating that indie publishers could achieve premium presentation.

Mechanically, the book provided the setting, random encounter tables, new monsters and magic items, and practical tools for running the location. The content balanced weirdness with usability, giving DMs strange material they could actually implement.

A Red & Pleasant Land won multiple ENnie Awards including Best Setting, Best Adventure, Best Writing, Best Art, Interior, and Product of the Year. It represented peak achievement in combining artistic vision with practical game content.

The book influenced how OSR designers approach setting design, demonstrating viability of literary adaptation with distinctive artistic vision. It showed that strange, surreal settings could work in play when supported by proper tools and framework.

### Maze of the Blue Medusa (2016)

Maze of the Blue Medusa, co-created with Patrick Stuart, presented a massive dungeon-as-art-gallery with 300+ rooms across multiple levels. The maze featured surreal locations, strange inhabitants, complex factions, and beautiful artwork throughout.

The design implemented non-linear exploration with multiple entrances, interconnections, and routes through the space—applying Jaquaysing principles to surreal fantasy environment. The location-based content emphasized discovery and interaction rather than combat-focused challenges.

The book's production quality continued raising standards for OSR products, with full-color artwork, thoughtful layout, and attention to usability. It won ENnie Awards including Best Adventure, demonstrating continued industry recognition of Smith's design work.

### Frostbitten & Mutilated (2014)

This supplement provided arctic adventure content with random tables for wilderness encounters, environmental hazards, new monsters, and survival challenges. Like Vornheim, it emphasized practical utility, providing tools DMs could use immediately rather than content requiring extensive preparation.

The book demonstrated Smith's approach to environmental adventure design: create distinctive atmosphere through careful detail, provide mechanical support for environmental challenges, offer random generation for sustained play, and maintain artistic quality throughout.

## Innovations and Contributions

### Practical DM Tools

Smith advanced design of practical play aids through random table innovation, physical book integration, improvisation support frameworks, and compact, dense information presentation.

### OSR Aesthetics

His work raised production quality standards for OSR publishing, demonstrated fine art could integrate with game content, showed literary influences could create distinctive settings, and proved indie products could achieve premium presentation.

### Urban Adventure Design

Vornheim influenced how designers approach cities by providing generation tools over exhaustive detail, supporting improvisation, creating flexible frameworks, and designing for actual play utility.

### Surreal Setting Design

A Red & Pleasant Land showed how surrealism works in gaming, literary adaptation for RPG format, balancing strangeness with usability, and creating distinctive voice in settings.

### Art-Game Integration

Smith demonstrated integration of fine art and game design, using artwork as design element not decoration, applying artistic principles to layout and presentation, and creating visually distinctive products.

## Legacy

Zak Smith's legacy is deeply complicated by his expulsion from the industry. His design contributions between 2009 and 2019 significantly influenced OSR gaming through practical tools innovation, aesthetic standards, literary and artistic influences, and production quality advancement. Works like Vornheim and A Red & Pleasant Land demonstrated new possibilities for OSR products and influenced numerous subsequent designers.

However, these contributions exist within context of behavior that led to industry expulsion. The 2019 allegations and response represent important moment in RPG industry history regarding accountability, community safety, and professional standards.

The gaming community continues grappling with questions about this legacy: How do we discuss influential work created by problematic figures? Can design innovations be separated from designer behavior? How does industry address accountability while acknowledging historical influence?

Many designers influenced by Smith's work have continued developing those innovations while distancing from the creator. The practical tools approach, artistic presentation standards, and surreal setting design demonstrated in his work continue influencing OSR design through those who built upon these foundations.

The situation serves as case study in how industry addresses serious allegations, the importance of community safety, and ongoing discussions about accountability and behavior in gaming. It demonstrates that even significant creative contributions do not excuse harmful behavior, and that industry can enforce community standards through collective action.

For historical documentation purposes, Smith's work between 2009 and 2019 represents significant OSR design contributions, while his 2019 expulsion represents important moment in industry accountability. Both are part of tabletop RPG history and inform current discussions about community standards, accountability, and how industry addresses problematic behavior.
