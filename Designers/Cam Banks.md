# Cam Banks

**Active Years**: 1996-present
**Birth Year**: 1972

## Biography

Cameron "Cam" Banks (born 1972) is a highly influential game designer, developer, and creative director known for innovative narrative-focused game systems and contributions to beloved licensed properties. Originally from New Zealand and later based in the United States, Banks has worked across multiple gaming companies and systems, consistently pushing the boundaries of narrative mechanics, relationship systems, and flexible game design. His work on the Cortex system family, particularly Cortex Plus, established him as a leading voice in modern narrative game design.

Banks discovered roleplaying games in New Zealand during the 1980s, falling in love with D&D, RuneQuest, and other early systems. His international perspective—experiencing gaming culture in both New Zealand and later the United States—provided unique insights into how different communities approached roleplaying.

His professional gaming career began in the late 1990s when he became involved with the Dragonlance community, contributing to fan sites and unofficial materials. This involvement led to official work when Margaret Weis and Tracy Hickman established Sovereign Press to produce licensed Dragonlance materials using the d20 System. Banks joined as developer and designer, working on Dragonlance's return to active publication under the 3rd Edition D&D framework.

Banks's work on Dragonlance demonstrated talent for balancing established setting continuity with fresh approaches. He helped modernize Dragonlance for 3rd Edition while respecting its history, working closely with Weis and Hickman to ensure canonical consistency.

When Sovereign Press evolved into Margaret Weis Productions (MWP), Banks became central to the company's creative direction. MWP focused on licensed properties using innovative game systems rather than d20, allowing creative experimentation. This shift enabled Banks to explore narrative-focused design approaches that would define his career.

His breakthrough came with the *Serenity RPG* (2005), based on Joss Whedon's *Firefly* television series, using the Cortex System. The game emphasized cinematic action and character-driven stories, features that aligned perfectly with the source material. Banks's work demonstrated how mechanics could reinforce genre and setting rather than imposing generic frameworks.

The *Battlestar Galactica RPG* (2007) further developed these ideas, using Cortex to capture the show's tension, moral dilemmas, and character conflicts. Banks refined mechanics supporting dramatic tension and difficult choices.

His most innovative work came with Cortex Plus, an evolved version of Cortex emphasizing modularity and customization. Different games using Cortex Plus could feel dramatically different while sharing core concepts. This family of systems included:

**Smallville RPG** (2010): Revolutionary character creation through relationship mapping. Rather than choosing statistics, players defined their character through connections, values, and relationships with other characters and setting elements. The Pathways character creation system became highly influential, demonstrating that relationships could mechanically define characters. Smallville won the Diana Jones Award, one of gaming's most prestigious honors.

**Marvel Heroic Roleplaying** (2012): Brought Marvel's superheroes to life through plot-point driven campaigns and effect-based powers. Rather than simulating powers mechanically, the system focused on narrative effects and dramatic stakes. Despite critical acclaim and commercial success, licensing issues led to the game's discontinuation, though it influenced subsequent superhero RPG design.

**Leverage RPG** (2010): Based on the heist television series, featured flashback mechanics and team-focused play reflecting the show's ensemble dynamics and planning-execution structure.

Banks's work established Cortex Plus as a leading narrative game system family, inspiring designers and demonstrating how flexible frameworks could support diverse genres while maintaining mechanical coherence.

After Margaret Weis Productions, Banks worked with various publishers including Free League Publishing (contributing to *Tales from the Loop* and other titles), Evil Hat Productions, and independent projects. He became creative director at Critical Role, helping develop their gaming products and narrative experiences before eventually departing to pursue independent work.

Throughout his career, Banks has been prolific designer, developer, and consultant, contributing to dozens of games across multiple systems and genres. He remains active in game design, teaching, and mentoring newer designers.

## Design Philosophy

Banks's design philosophy emphasizes narrative flexibility, character relationships, and player agency:

**Fiction First**: Mechanics should emerge from fictional positioning and narrative logic. What makes sense in the story should guide mechanical resolution, not vice versa.

**Relationships Define Character**: Who characters know, what they value, and how they relate to the world define them as much as skills and abilities. Mechanical systems should reflect this.

**Flexible Frameworks**: Game systems should be modular and customizable, allowing groups to emphasize aspects matching their preferences. One-size-fits-all design constrains creativity.

**Genre Emulation**: Licensed games should capture source material's feel through mechanics. Playing *Battlestar Galactica* should feel like being in the show; *Marvel Heroic* should feel like comics.

**Player Investment Through Choice**: Character creation and development should involve meaningful choices reflecting player priorities. Mechanical decisions should connect to character concept and story.

**Effect-Based Rather Than Simulation**: Focus on narrative effects rather than detailed simulation. In superhero gaming, what matters is dramatic effect, not physics simulation.

**Plot Points and Story Currency**: Give players mechanical resources enabling narrative influence. This creates collaborative storytelling while maintaining structure.

**Fail Forward**: Failure should advance story, not halt it. Unsuccessful rolls should create complications, costs, or new challenges rather than dead ends.

**Accessibility**: Complex ideas should be approachable. Innovative mechanics shouldn't require excessive rules mastery to enjoy.

## Innovations and Contributions

### Mechanical Innovations

**Relationship-Based Character Creation (Smallville)**: The Pathways character creation system represented fundamental innovation—characters emerged through defining relationships, values, and connections rather than choosing skills. Players placed characters on relationship map, established connections to other PCs and setting elements, and derived abilities from these relationships. This made character creation collaborative, ensured group cohesion, and mechanically reinforced that characters are defined by their connections.

**Plot Point Campaigns**: Developed structured campaign frameworks using "plot points"—modular story events that could trigger in various orders based on player choices. This balanced narrative structure with player agency, ensuring story progression while respecting choice.

**Effect-Based Powers (Marvel Heroic)**: Rather than simulating superhero powers mechanically, used effect-based approach—powers defined by narrative effects and dramatic implications. This allowed flexible interpretation while maintaining mechanical framework.

**Flashback Mechanics (Leverage)**: Incorporated planning flashbacks as mechanical feature, allowing players to retroactively establish preparations. This captured heist genre's "it was part of the plan all along" moments.

**Distinctions as Complications**: In Cortex Plus, character distinctions could be invoked for benefits or complications, with complications providing plot points. This mechanically encouraged players to embrace character flaws and complications for narrative currency.

**Graduated Dice Pools**: Cortex's use of different die types (d4 through d12) in pools created intuitive sense of varying importance and capability, with smaller dice representing weaknesses and larger dice strengths.

**Stress Tracks Instead of Hit Points**: Various stress tracks (physical, mental, emotional, social) replaced traditional hit points, creating nuanced approach to character condition and enabling social and emotional conflicts to have mechanical weight.

### Conceptual Contributions

**Licensed Game Innovation**: Demonstrated licensed RPGs could innovate mechanically rather than simply applying generic systems to IPs. Each licensed game should use mechanics reinforcing source material.

**Modular System Design**: Cortex Plus's modularity showed systems could share core concepts while playing very differently. This influenced later toolbox systems like Forged in the Dark and Powered by the Apocalypse.

**Character Relationships as Mechanics**: Pioneered making relationships primary mechanical component rather than roleplaying flavor. This influenced games like Hillfolk, Tenra Bansho Zero, and numerous story games.

**Narrative Currency**: Advanced use of plot points and similar currencies giving players mechanical resources for narrative influence, balancing GM authority with collaborative storytelling.

### Industry Contributions

**Small Publisher Innovation**: Work at Margaret Weis Productions demonstrated small publishers could compete through innovation and quality rather than market dominance. MWP's critical success validated narrative-focused design commercially.

**Licensed Property Respect**: Set standards for respecting source material while creating mechanically interesting games. His licensed work showed fidelity to IP and innovative design weren't contradictory.

**Design Education**: Through blogs, convention panels, and mentorship, Banks has educated designers about narrative mechanics, licensed game design, and system development.

**New Zealand-US Connection**: Banks's career bridged New Zealand and United States gaming communities, facilitating cross-pollination and demonstrating gaming's international nature.

## Influence on Later Designers

Banks's influence appears across modern narrative game design:

**Relationship Mechanics**: Smallville's Pathways influenced how designers approach character relationships mechanically. Games like *Hillfolk*, *Tenra Bansho Zero*, *Wrath of the Autarch*, and numerous Powered by the Apocalypse games incorporate relationship mechanics as core components.

**Modular System Design**: Cortex Plus's flexibility influenced system families like Forged in the Dark and Powered by the Apocalypse, demonstrating how shared frameworks could support diverse implementations.

**Licensed Game Design**: His work established templates for respecting source material while innovating mechanically. Later licensed games show his influence in balancing fidelity and creativity.

**Effect-Based Design**: The Marvel Heroic approach influenced how superhero and high-power games handle abilities, with *Masks*, *City of Mist*, and others using narrative effects over simulation.

**Plot Point Structures**: Campaign frameworks using modular plot points appear in various systems, providing structured flexibility in adventure design.

**Fail Forward Philosophy**: Banks's advocacy for failure advancing story rather than halting it influenced mainstream acceptance of this principle in modern design.

Designers including John Harper, Brendan Conway, Sean Nittner, and many others cite Banks's influence, particularly regarding narrative mechanics and licensed properties.

## Legacy

Cam Banks's legacy centers on proving narrative mechanics could be sophisticated, systematic, and commercially viable. Before Cortex Plus, narrative-focused games often felt mechanically light or improvisation-dependent. Banks demonstrated that narrative priorities could combine with mechanical rigor.

Smallville's Pathways character creation represents genuine innovation—approaching character creation from entirely fresh angle. The Diana Jones Award recognition validated its significance, and its influence appears throughout contemporary indie game design.

Marvel Heroic Roleplaying's success—both critical and commercial—proved superhero games could move beyond simulation toward narrative emulation. Its discontinuation due to licensing complications remains frustrating for fans, but its design influence persists in subsequent superhero games.

The Cortex system family, particularly Cortex Plus and later Cortex Prime, continues evolving through community development and licensed implementation. Banks's foundational work created flexible framework that designers continue exploring.

His contributions to Dragonlance during its d20 era maintained the setting's vitality during transition periods, bridging classic era to modern iterations. His respect for established continuity while advancing the setting earned appreciation from longtime fans.

As educator and mentor, Banks has influenced designers through convention panels, blog posts, and personal mentorship. His willingness to discuss design process and share knowledge has elevated design discourse.

His career demonstrates designer can work across multiple publishers, systems, and genres while maintaining consistent creative vision. The throughline of narrative focus, player agency, and mechanical innovation appears whether he's working on Dragonlance, Marvel superheroes, or science fiction settings.

Banks remains active in gaming, continuing to design, consult, and contribute to the hobby's evolution. His influence on narrative game design ensures his place among the most important designers of the modern era.
