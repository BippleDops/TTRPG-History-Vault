# Clinton R. Nixon

**Active Years**: 2003-present
**Birth Year**: ~1975
**Nationality**: United States

Clinton R. Nixon designed The Shadow of Yesterday (2004—fantasy using revolutionary Keys advancement where experience comes from pursuing specific character goals) and Donjon (2002—dungeon-crawling parody where success lets players establish dungeon facts), demonstrating how narrative authority distribution and goal-driven advancement could serve traditional fantasy gaming, with Keys mechanic becoming foundational indie design tool influencing how designers think about character-driven advancement beyond XP accumulation.

## Major Works

### The Shadow of Yesterday (2004)

Fantasy RPG with revolutionary Keys advancement:

**Keys Innovation**: Experience points earned by pursuing specific character goals (Keys)
- Key of the Guardian: XP when protecting others
- Key of the Mission: XP when advancing quest
- Key of Unrequited Love: XP when love creates complications

**Player-Driven**: Advancement incentivizes playing character truthfully

**Near (Setting)**: Detailed fantasy world recovering from catastrophe

**Pool System**: Simple d6 pools

**Crunch-Light**: Rules-light compared to D&D but with tactical depth

**Impact**: Keys became standard narrativist tool. Influenced character-driven advancement across indie design.

**Open License**: Creative Commons release encouraged hacking

**Legacy**: Keys appeared in Apocalypse World (as Hx gains), Lady Blackbird, countless other games

### Donjon (2002)

Dungeon-crawling parody where success gives narrative authority:

**Success = Control**: Better rolls let players establish dungeon facts ("I rolled well—there's a convenient rope bridge")

**Wealth = Hit Points**: Abstract satirical health system

**Parody**: Mocking but lovingly D&D conventions

**Impact**: Demonstrated narrative control through success-level

## Design Philosophy

Nixon emphasizes:

**Keys Advancement**: XP from pursuing character goals not killing monsters

**Player Authority**: Success giving narrative control

**Goal-Driven**: Character motivations mechanically central

**Fantasy with Purpose**: Traditional settings with modern mechanics

**Open Licensing**: Encouraging hacks and derivatives

## Innovation

**Keys Mechanic**: Revolutionary advancement through character-goal pursuit

**Success-Level Narrative Control** (Donjon): Better rolls = more authority

**Character-Driven XP**: Experience from roleplay not combat

## Awards

- **Indie RPG Awards**: Multiple nominations
- **Critical Acclaim**: Keys especially praised
- **Design Influence**: Widely cited

## Cultural Impact

**Keys Proliferation**: Advancement mechanic appeared across indie gaming

**Character-Driven Design**: Influenced how designers think about motivation and advancement

**TSOY Legacy**: Open license enabled multiple hacks (Lady Blackbird, others)

**Narrativist Advancement**: Template for XP-from-character-goals

## Reception

Praised for Keys brilliance, Near setting, accessible fantasy with narrative mechanics. Some wanted more crunch. Keys universally recognized as innovative.

## Legacy

Keys became standard indie advancement tool. Influenced Apocalypse World, Lady Blackbird, countless others. Demonstrated character-goal-driven XP superior to murder-hobo incentives. Open license enabled derivative works.
