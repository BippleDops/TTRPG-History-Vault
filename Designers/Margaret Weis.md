# Margaret Weis

**Active Years**: 1983-present
**Birth Year**: 1948

## Biography

Margaret Edith Weis (born March 16, 1948) is one of fantasy literature's most successful authors and a transformative figure in gaming fiction. As co-creator of the Dragonlance saga with Tracy Hickman, Weis helped elevate game-derived fiction to mainstream literary success, proving that RPG-based novels could achieve critical and commercial acclaim. Her character-focused storytelling, nuanced characterization, and literary craftsmanship distinguished Dragonlance from typical genre fiction and established templates for gaming fiction that endure today.

Born in Independence, Missouri, Weis grew up with passion for reading and creative writing. She studied creative writing and literature at the University of Missouri, developing skills she would later apply to fantasy fiction. After college, she worked various jobs including teaching and bookselling before discovering career opportunities in publishing.

In the early 1980s, Weis joined TSR as an editor and designer, working on various projects including books based on TSR's gaming properties. When TSR leadership decided to develop a major new campaign setting supported by coordinated novel series, they assigned Tracy Hickman to design the world and needed a writer to develop the novels.

Hickman had created Dragonlance's world framework, major historical events, and basic plot structure. TSR management paired him with Weis to develop the narrative. Their collaboration proved remarkably synergistic—Hickman excelled at plotting and world-building, while Weis brought characters to life with psychological depth and emotional resonance.

The partnership faced skepticism. Game-derived fiction previously achieved limited success, and TSR's investment in coordinated multimedia campaign was unprecedented. However, *Dragons of Autumn Twilight* (1984), the first Dragonlance novel, became an unexpected New York Times bestseller. The sequels followed suit, ultimately selling millions of copies and launching Weis into literary stardom.

The Dragonlance Chronicles introduced readers to complex characters like Raistlin Majere (the morally ambiguous mage), Tanis Half-Elven (struggling with identity), and Goldmoon (spiritual leader confronting faith). Weis's characterization made them memorable beyond typical fantasy archetypes. Raistlin particularly became iconic—a physically frail, morally complex protagonist whose ambition and ruthlessness made him fascinating rather than simply sympathetic.

Following the Chronicles' success, Weis and Hickman continued with Dragonlance Legends, exploring time travel and Raistlin's attempt to achieve godhood. The trilogy deepened character development and featured mature themes of sacrifice, redemption, and the corruption of power. It demonstrated game-derived fiction could tackle sophisticated literary themes.

In the late 1980s and 1990s, Weis and Hickman expanded beyond Dragonlance, creating original fantasy series including the Death Gate Cycle (seven-volume epic fantasy), Rose of the Prophet, and Star of the Guardians (space opera). These demonstrated their appeal transcended licensed properties—readers followed them for their storytelling, not just D&D association.

Weis also authored independent works without Hickman, including the Star of the Guardians series and collaborations with other authors like Don Perrin and Tracy Hickman's son Curtis. Her versatility across fantasy subgenres showcased range beyond Dragonlance.

In 2001, Weis and Hickman launched Sovereign Press, their own publishing company, to produce the Sovereign Stone RPG system and related fiction. This venture allowed creative control and business ownership, though it eventually ceased RPG production while continuing fiction publishing.

Weis returned to Dragonlance periodically as Wizards of the Coast licensed new novels, most notably writing the War of Souls trilogy (2000-2002), which advanced Dragonlance's timeline and introduced new generations to Krynn. She and Hickman continued producing Dragonlance novels into the 2010s.

Throughout her career, Weis remained involved in gaming, attending conventions, engaging with fan communities, and mentoring aspiring writers. Her success paved the way for other authors in gaming fiction and demonstrated women could achieve major commercial success in fantasy publishing.

## Design Philosophy

Weis's creative philosophy emphasizes character depth, emotional authenticity, and literary craftsmanship:

**Character First**: Compelling characters drive compelling stories. Readers invest in well-developed, psychologically complex characters with understandable motivations and meaningful growth.

**Moral Complexity**: Characters should exist beyond simple good/evil binaries. Heroes have flaws, villains have comprehensible motivations, and moral choices should feature genuine dilemmas.

**Emotional Authenticity**: Fantasy characters should feel emotionally real despite magical settings. Their relationships, struggles, and growth should resonate with human experience.

**The Ambiguous Hero**: Protagonists need not be traditionally heroic. Raistlin's ambition and ruthlessness made him fascinating. Characters can be selfish, damaged, or morally compromised while remaining compelling.

**Female Agency**: Female characters should have agency, complexity, and narrative importance. They should be protagonists, not prizes or supporting players.

**Literary Quality**: Game-derived fiction deserves literary craftsmanship. Prose quality, thematic depth, and narrative sophistication matter regardless of genre or origin.

**Collaborative Synergy**: Effective collaboration balances different strengths. Weis acknowledged Hickman's plotting skills complemented her character development, creating better work than either achieved alone.

**Accessibility with Depth**: Fantasy should welcome new readers while offering depth for sophisticated readers. Complex themes should be accessible, not obscure.

## Innovations and Contributions

### Literary Innovations

**Character-Driven Gaming Fiction**: Weis demonstrated game-derived fiction could feature literary-quality characterization. Her characters had psychological depth, internal conflicts, and meaningful development rather than serving as mere action figures.

**The Morally Ambiguous Protagonist**: Raistlin Majere became template for complex fantasy protagonists—brilliant, damaged, ambitious, ruthless, yet compelling. His moral ambiguity predated grimdark fantasy's popularity, influencing how fantasy approaches heroism.

**Ensemble Cast Dynamics**: The Companions of Dragonlance featured diverse personalities, conflicting motivations, and complex relationships. Their interactions felt authentic, with friction and friendship coexisting naturally.

**Female Characters with Agency**: Weis created strong female characters—Goldmoon, Tika, Laurana, Kitiara—who drove narratives, made consequential choices, and exhibited complexity. This countered fantasy's typical marginalization of women.

**Redemption Arcs**: Explored genuine redemption through character evolution, particularly in characters like Caramon (Raistlin's brother) confronting codependency and developing self-worth.

**Disabled Characters**: Raistlin's physical frailty and magical curse (seeing mortality's decay) made him rare disabled protagonist in fantasy, challenging genre's emphasis on physical prowess.

### Industry Contributions

**Mainstream Gaming Fiction Success**: The Dragonlance novels' bestseller status proved gaming fiction could achieve mainstream literary success, opening publishing opportunities for game-based novels industry-wide.

**Author-Publisher Collaboration**: Demonstrated effective collaboration between authors and gaming companies, establishing business models for licensed fiction that continue today.

**Female Leadership in Gaming Publishing**: As senior editor and author at TSR, Weis provided representation for women in gaming industry leadership, helping diversify traditionally male-dominated field.

**Independent Publishing**: Sovereign Press demonstrated designers and authors could maintain creative control through independent publishing while leveraging gaming industry connections.

**Long-Form Series Development**: The extended Dragonlance saga—spanning decades with consistent quality—demonstrated viability of long-running fantasy series tied to gaming properties.

### Design Contributions

While primarily known for fiction, Weis contributed to game design:

**Sovereign Stone RPG**: Co-designed with Hickman and Don Perrin, featuring innovative magic system and setting integrating their world-building with game mechanics.

**Editorial Direction**: As TSR editor, shaped numerous gaming products, bringing literary sensibility to game writing and development.

**Setting Development**: Contributed to Dragonlance setting development, ensuring game materials aligned with narrative continuity and character consistency.

## Influence on Later Designers and Authors

Weis's influence extends across gaming fiction and fantasy literature:

**Gaming Fiction Authors**: R.A. Salvatore, Elaine Cunningham, Ed Greenwood, and countless others cite Dragonlance as influence. Weis demonstrated gaming fiction deserved literary quality, raising standards industry-wide.

**Character-Focused Fantasy**: The emphasis on character depth over pure action influenced fantasy writing broadly. Raistlin's complexity predated and influenced later morally ambiguous protagonists in works by Joe Abercrombie, Mark Lawrence, and others.

**Female Authors in Gaming**: Weis's success helped legitimize female authorship in gaming fiction, opening opportunities for subsequent generations of women writers in fantasy and gaming.

**Multimedia Franchises**: The Dragonlance model—coordinated novels and games—influenced Magic: The Gathering, WarHammer, World of Warcraft, and other properties balancing fiction with gaming.

**Ensemble Fantasy**: The Companions' group dynamics influenced ensemble fantasy from Robert Jordan's *Wheel of Time* to Brandon Sanderson's works, demonstrating power of diverse character groups with distinct personalities.

**Disability Representation**: Raistlin's portrayal influenced how fantasy approaches disabled characters, showing physical limitations need not preclude compelling protagonists.

## Quotes and Interviews

"Gaming fiction deserves the same literary quality as any fantasy novel. The D&D association shouldn't excuse poor writing. We owed readers our best work."

"Readers connected with the Companions because they felt real—they argued, made mistakes, grew. Their relationships mattered as much as the epic plot."

"The Death Gate Cycle let us explore ideas we couldn't in Dragonlance—darker themes, complex magic systems, ambitious worldbuilding. Creative freedom produced some of our best work."

On Sovereign Press: "Owning our creative work mattered. It gave us control over Sovereign Stone's direction and demonstrated authors could be publishers."

## Legacy

Margaret Weis's legacy centers on proving gaming fiction could achieve literary excellence and commercial success. Before Dragonlance, game-derived novels were modest ventures. After, they became major publishing category.

Her characterization—particularly Raistlin—demonstrated fantasy characters could exhibit psychological complexity, moral ambiguity, and literary depth regardless of genre origin. This elevated gaming fiction's literary aspirations.

As prominent female author in traditionally male-dominated gaming and fantasy fields, Weis provided representation and inspiration for subsequent generations of women creators. Her success helped normalize female authorship in gaming fiction.

The Dragonlance saga's endurance—continuously in print for 40 years, with new generations discovering the Chronicles—validates Weis and Hickman's creative vision. The characters remain beloved, the novels continue selling, and the setting retains devoted fan base.

Her influence extends beyond direct imitators to broader fantasy publishing—the character-focused, ensemble-cast, morally complex approach she pioneered became mainstream fantasy rather than exception.

The collaborative model she established with Hickman—author partnerships balancing complementary strengths—influenced how collaborative fiction projects approach creative division of labor.

Weis remains active, engaging with fans, attending conventions, and continuing to write. Her contributions to fantasy literature and gaming culture ensure her place among the most influential figures in both fields.
