# Hamish Cameron

**Active Years**: 2013-present  
**Birth Year**: ~1975
**Nationality**: New Zealand

Hamish Cameron designed The Sprawl (2016), bringing accessible cyberpunk to PbtA through mission-structured shadowrunning where players execute corporate espionage while managing Heat (attention) and Debt, demonstrating Apocalypse World framework could serve Shadowrun-style gameplay without that game's notorious complexity, providing streamlined alternative for cyberpunk heisting that influenced mission-based PbtA design.

## Major Work: The Sprawl (2016)

Cyberpunk PbtA featuring:

**Mission Structure**: Legwork → Action → Fallout framework

**Heat Mechanics**: Corporate attention tracking creating pressure

**Debt**: Financial/social obligations driving complications

**Playbooks**: Classic cyberpunk archetypes (Killer, Hacker, Driver, Fixer, etc.)

**Streamlined**: Shadowrun-style play without complex rules

**Successful**: Strong sales, multiple printings, established cyberpunk PbtA niche

**Impact**: Demonstrated mission-structure worked with PbtA. Provided accessible cyberpunk alternative to crunchier games.

## Design Philosophy

Cameron emphasizes:

**Accessibility**: Simpler than traditional cyberpunk games

**Mission Framework**: Structured but flexible

**Genre Emulation**: Capturing cyberpunk feel through PbtA

**Streamlining**: Removing unnecessary complexity

## Innovation

**Mission Structure**: Three-phase framework for shadowruns

**Heat System**: Corporate attention mechanics

**Cyberpunk PbtA**: First major accessible cyberpunk using Apocalypse World

## Cultural Impact

Provided accessible Shadowrun alternative. Demonstrated cyberpunk worked with PbtA. Mission structure influenced heist-based PbtA. New Zealand indie presence.

## Legacy

The Sprawl became default accessible cyberpunk recommendation. Mission structure appeared in other PbtA games. Demonstrated streamlining could serve genre emulation.
