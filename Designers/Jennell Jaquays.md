# Jennell Jaquays

**Active Years**: 1977-2024
**Birth Year**: 1956 (died 2024)

## Biography

Jennell Jaquays (October 14, 1956 – January 10, 2024) was one of the most influential adventure designers in tabletop roleplaying history, whose innovations in dungeon design fundamentally changed how designers approach adventure creation. Her work established principles of non-linear, interconnected environments that remain the gold standard for adventure design today. The term "Jaquaysing" entered the design lexicon as shorthand for sophisticated, player-choice-driven dungeon layouts.

Born in Michigan, Jaquays developed early interests in art and fantasy, studying illustration and discovering roleplaying games in the mid-1970s. She began contributing artwork and cartography to early gaming publications while still a student, quickly demonstrating exceptional talent for both visual and game design.

Her breakthrough came with *Dark Tower* (1979) and *Caverns of Thracia* (1979), published by Judges Guild while she was in her early twenties. These adventures revolutionized dungeon design by rejecting the linear, single-entrance layouts typical of early modules. Instead, Jaquays created complex, multilevel environments with multiple entrances, interconnecting passages, vertical movement between levels, and meaningful player choices about approach and navigation.

The sophistication of her early work caught Chaosium's attention, leading to her work on RuneQuest materials including *Griffin Mountain* (1981) and *Griffin Island* (1983). These wilderness-focused adventures demonstrated her design principles scaled beyond dungeon crawls to open-world exploration.

In the mid-1980s, Jaquays transitioned to video game design, joining Coleco and later working at numerous game companies including Ensemble Studios (Age of Empires III), Raven Software (Quake II, Hexen II), and id Software (Quake III Arena). Her tabletop design principles influenced her video game level design, creating sophisticated 3D spaces emphasizing player choice and multiple approaches.

Throughout her career, Jaquays remained involved with tabletop gaming through freelance work, convention appearances, and mentorship. She was an outspoken advocate for diversity in gaming and shared her journey as a transgender woman to help others in the community.

Jaquays passed away in January 2024, prompting widespread tributes from both tabletop and video game communities recognizing her foundational contributions to adventure design across gaming media.

## Design Philosophy

Jaquays's design philosophy emphasized player agency, environmental complexity, and meaningful choice:

**Multiple Approaches**: Adventures should provide several ways to enter and navigate environments. Players should choose their approach based on strategy, not follow a predetermined path.

**Interconnected Spaces**: Levels and areas should connect in multiple ways, creating loops, shortcuts, and alternative routes. This enables strategic movement and retreat.

**Vertical Integration**: Multi-level dungeons should feature vertical connections (pits, chimneys, stairs, shafts) allowing movement between levels at multiple points, not just at endpoints.

**Environmental Logic**: Dungeons should make logical sense as places. Who built this? For what purpose? How do inhabitants use the space? Environmental storytelling emerges from thoughtful design.

**Informed Choice**: Provide information allowing players to make meaningful decisions. Rumors, scouting, and investigation should reveal options and consequences.

**Risk-Reward Balance**: Greater challenges should offer greater rewards, but clever play should enable weaker parties to succeed through strategy rather than brute force.

**Functional Beauty**: Maps and environments should be both beautiful and functional, with visual design serving gameplay clarity.

**Player Respect**: Design should respect player intelligence and agency, providing challenges requiring thought rather than arbitrary difficulty.

## Innovations and Contributions

### Mechanical Innovations

**Non-Linear Dungeon Design**: Jaquays pioneered dungeon layouts featuring multiple entrances, creating immediate player choice about approach. Rather than single entrance leading to linear progression, her dungeons offered strategic decisions from the start.

**Multilevel Interconnections**: Created vertical connections between dungeon levels at multiple points, not just staircases at level endpoints. Pits, chimneys, shafts, and magical portals allowed dynamic movement through three-dimensional space.

**Loop Design**: Incorporated loops and alternate paths within dungeon levels, enabling tactical retreats, flanking maneuvers, and strategic positioning. Players could approach areas from multiple directions.

**Information Ecology**: Designed comprehensive rumor tables and information sources providing genuine intelligence about adventure locations. Players could gather useful reconnaissance before entering, enabling informed strategic planning.

**Functional Wilderness Design**: Extended sophisticated design principles to wilderness adventures, creating open environments with multiple locations, interconnecting relationships, and player choice about exploration order.

**Environmental Keying**: Developed detailed location descriptions emphasizing environmental features, inhabitant behavior, and spatial relationships, enabling DMs to run locations dynamically rather than reading scripted text.

### Design Principles ("Jaquaysing")

The term "Jaquaysing" was coined by Justin Alexander in his analysis of adventure design, identifying principles demonstrated in Jaquays's work:

- **Multiple entrances** to major adventure areas
- **Multiple paths** through locations
- **Loops** allowing circular movement
- **Secret and unusual paths** rewarding exploration
- **Sub-levels and side-treks** expanding vertical and horizontal complexity
- **Discontinuous level connections** (level 1 doesn't only connect to level 2)
- **Divided levels** creating distinct regions
- **Nested dungeons** (dungeons within dungeons)
- **Minor elevation shifts** adding tactical terrain
- **Continuous backgrounds** (consistent world logic)

These principles became fundamental to adventure design, influencing megadungeons, sandbox adventures, and modern adventure design across tabletop and video games.

### Artistic Contributions

Jaquays was an accomplished fantasy illustrator and cartographer. Her maps combined aesthetic beauty with functional clarity, establishing standards for adventure cartography. Her art appeared throughout Judges Guild products and other early gaming materials, contributing to tabletop RPG visual identity.

### Video Game Design

Jaquays's transition to video games brought tabletop design principles to digital environments:

- **Quake II level design**: Created interconnected 3D spaces with multiple routes and vertical complexity
- **Age of Empires III**: Contributed to map design and campaign structures
- **Hexen II**: Designed hub-based level structures emphasizing exploration and choice

Her influence on first-person shooter level design particularly reflects her dungeon design philosophy—complex, interconnected spaces rewarding exploration and offering tactical choices.

## Influence on Later Designers

Jaquays's influence on adventure design cannot be overstated. The principles she pioneered became standard practice:

**Megadungeon Design**: Every significant megadungeon since the 1980s shows Jaquays influence—Undermountain, Rappan Athuk, Stonehell Dungeon, and countless others feature multiple entrances, interconnected levels, and non-linear exploration.

**OSR Movement**: The Old School Renaissance heavily emphasizes Jaquays-style design, with designers like Justin Alexander explicitly teaching her principles. Her work represents the gold standard for old-school dungeon design.

**Modern D&D Adventures**: Contemporary adventure design incorporates her principles, with multiple approaches, interconnected locations, and player choice emphasized in official and third-party publications.

**Sandbox Design**: Her wilderness design influenced how designers approach open-world adventures, providing frameworks for player-driven exploration.

**Video Game Level Design**: First-person shooters, immersive sims, and exploration games apply her principles to 3D space design. Games like Dark Souls, Dishonored, and modern immersive sims demonstrate Jaquays-style interconnected level design.

Designers across media cite her influence, and adventure design discussions routinely reference "Jaquaysing" as shorthand for sophisticated environmental design.

## Quotes and Interviews

"A good dungeon should give players meaningful choices. Where do we enter? Which path do we take? Do we go up or down? Those decisions matter."

"When I designed Dark Tower and Caverns of Thracia, I was thinking about how real fortresses and cave systems work. Multiple entrances make tactical sense. Interconnections create emergent gameplay."

"Players should feel clever, not railroaded. If they scout carefully, gather information, and plan well, they should have advantages. Good design rewards preparation."

"The transition to video games taught me that the same principles work in any medium—give players meaningful choices, respect their intelligence, and create spaces that reward exploration."

On being transgender in gaming: "I hope my visibility helps others. Gaming should welcome everyone. I've been fortunate to work in communities that, by and large, have been supportive."

"Environmental design is storytelling. A dungeon's layout tells you who built it, why, and how it's used. Form follows function, and both serve narrative."

## Community Impact and Advocacy

Beyond her design contributions, Jaquays made significant impact as advocate for diversity and inclusion in gaming. As a transgender woman who transitioned publicly in the 2010s, she provided visibility and support for LGBTQ+ individuals in gaming communities that historically could be unwelcoming.

Her openness about her transition journey helped educate others and demonstrated that gaming should welcome everyone regardless of gender identity. She spoke at conventions about diversity in gaming, mentored other transgender designers, and used her platform to advocate for inclusive community practices.

Jaquays's advocacy work occurred during important period for LGBTQ+ visibility in gaming. Her willingness to be public about her identity while maintaining successful career across tabletop and video games showed that transgender individuals could thrive in gaming industry. This visibility encouraged others and contributed to gradually improving attitudes within gaming communities.

Her passing in 2024 prompted widespread tributes not only acknowledging her design genius but celebrating her advocacy work and positive impact on community culture. Many designers and gamers shared stories of how her visibility and support helped them feel welcome in gaming spaces.

## Legacy

Jennell Jaquays's death in January 2024 prompted widespread recognition of her foundational contributions to adventure design. Her innovations in dungeon layout, environmental design, and player agency became so fundamental that many modern designers apply her principles without realizing their origin.

The term "Jaquaysing" entering standard design vocabulary represents rare achievement—having your design philosophy named after you. Adventure designers routinely evaluate dungeons by how well they're "Jaquaysed," measuring against standards she established 45 years ago. This terminology ensures her name and contributions remain recognized by new generations of designers.

Her dual career in tabletop and video game design demonstrates how core design principles transcend medium. Whether on graph paper or in 3D engines, her philosophy of interconnected spaces, multiple approaches, and player choice creates compelling experiences. First-person shooter level designers studying her tabletop work discovered principles directly applicable to 3D spaces. Immersive sim designers building complex environments reference her emphasis on player choice and multiple solutions.

As a transgender woman in gaming, Jaquays provided crucial visibility and advocacy, helping create more inclusive community. Her openness about her journey and support for other transgender gamers contributed to positive cultural change in gaming. The outpouring of support and celebration of her life following her death demonstrated how many people her advocacy touched.

Her work continues influencing new generations of designers across tabletop RPGs, board games, and video games. Every interconnected megadungeon, every level with multiple approaches, every environment rewarding exploration reflects principles she pioneered. Modern megadungeons like Stonehell, massive video game levels in Dark Souls or Dishonored, and complex board game maps all show her influence.

The preservation and continued discussion of her design principles ensures that Jennell Jaquays's contributions to game design will be studied, celebrated, and built upon for generations. Her legacy encompasses not only specific mechanical innovations but a fundamental shift in how designers think about space, player agency, and environmental design across all gaming media.
